Founding: Federation — the Commonwealth of Australia — 1 January 1901. The modern nation was created on 1 January 1901, when six self-governing British colonies — New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, and Tasmania — federated under a written Constitution to form the Commonwealth of Australia, proclaimed at a ceremony in Sydney's Centennial Park. This founding rests on a far deeper human history: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have inhabited the continent for at least 65,000 years, making theirs the world's oldest continuous living cultures. British colonisation began with the arrival of the First Fleet at Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788, which established a penal colony in New South Wales and set in motion the dispossession of Aboriginal peoples that accompanied the new nation's creation.

Australia's human history is among the longest on Earth: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have lived on the continent for at least 65,000 years, sustaining the world's oldest continuous cultures. European contact transformed that world from 1770, when James Cook charted the east coast and claimed it for Britain, and from 1788, when the First Fleet founded a penal colony at Sydney Cove. Over the nineteenth century the colonies expanded across the continent, driven by pastoralism and gold rushes, while frontier violence and disease devastated Aboriginal peoples and dispossessed them of their lands. Six self-governing colonies federated to form the Commonwealth of Australia on 1 January 1901, a new nation that soon enshrined a restrictive 'White Australia' immigration policy. The twentieth century brought the trauma of two world wars, mass post-war immigration that reshaped society, and a slow reckoning with the dispossession of, and the removal of children from, Aboriginal families. Modern Australia is a prosperous, multicultural democracy still working toward reconciliation, marked by the 1967 referendum, the Mabo native-title judgment, and the 2008 national apology to the Stolen Generations.

## Aboriginal Australia: the world's oldest continuous cultures — c. 65,000 BCE – 1770 CE

For more than 65,000 years, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have lived across the Australian continent, developing the world's oldest continuous living cultures, hundreds of languages, and deep systems of law, kinship, and connection to Country.

- c. 65,000+ years ago: **Aboriginal peoples settle the continent** — Aboriginal peoples reached Australia at least 65,000 years ago, crossing from Southeast Asia to the joined landmass of Sahul and beginning the world's oldest continuous human occupation of a single territory. [brit-aboriginal, nma-aboriginal]
- c. 50,000–45,000 years ago: **Settlement across the whole continent** — Within tens of thousands of years, Aboriginal peoples had spread across the entire continent, from tropical north to the cold south, developing diverse cultures adapted to every environment. [brit-aboriginal]
- before European contact: **Hundreds of nations and languages** — By the time of European contact the continent was home to hundreds of distinct nations speaking around 250 languages, sustaining complex law, kinship, trade, art, and spiritual systems centred on connection to Country. [brit-aboriginal, nma-aboriginal]

## European contact and the First Fleet — 1770 – 1820

James Cook's charting of the east coast in 1770 opened the way for British colonisation, and in 1788 the First Fleet founded a penal colony at Sydney Cove — the beginning of a settlement that would dispossess Aboriginal peoples of their lands.

- 22 August 1770: **Cook charts and claims the east coast** — Lieutenant James Cook charted the eastern coast of the continent aboard HMB Endeavour and claimed it for Britain as New South Wales, opening the way to British colonisation. [nma-cook, brit-australia]
- 26 January 1788: **The First Fleet founds the penal colony** — Captain Arthur Phillip's First Fleet established a British penal colony at Sydney Cove, beginning permanent European settlement and the dispossession of Aboriginal peoples of the Sydney region. [nma-first-fleet, brit-australia]
- 1789: **Smallpox devastates Aboriginal peoples** — A smallpox epidemic spread among Aboriginal peoples around Sydney, killing a large proportion of the Eora and neighbouring peoples and compounding the impact of land seizure and conflict. [brit-australia, nma-aboriginal]

Figures:

- Arthur Phillip (1738–1814) — First Governor of New South Wales. Commanded the First Fleet and founded the penal colony at Sydney Cove in 1788, serving as the first governor of New South Wales.
- James Cook (1728–1779) — Royal Navy lieutenant and explorer. Charted Australia's east coast in 1770 and claimed it for Britain as New South Wales, opening the continent to British colonisation.

## Colonial expansion, gold rushes, and frontier conflict — 1820 – 1900

As the colonies spread across the continent on the back of wool and gold, settlers and Aboriginal peoples clashed in a long frontier war, while gold rushes from 1851 transformed the population, economy, and politics of the Australian colonies.

- 1820s–1900s: **The frontier wars** — Across the continent, the expansion of pastoral settlement met Aboriginal resistance in a long, dispersed frontier conflict of raids, reprisals, and massacres that, with introduced disease, devastated Aboriginal peoples and dispossessed them of their land. [brit-australia, nma-aboriginal]
- 1851: **Gold rushes begin** — Discoveries of gold in New South Wales and Victoria triggered rushes that drew hundreds of thousands of migrants, transforming the colonies' population, economy, and society within a decade. [brit-gold-rushes, nma-eureka]
- 3 December 1854: **Eureka Stockade** — Goldminers at Ballarat rebelled against oppressive licensing and the denial of political rights; troops stormed their stockade, killing more than twenty, but the revolt helped secure democratic reforms. [nma-eureka, brit-gold-rushes]

## Federation and the new nation — 1880s – 1914

On 1 January 1901 six colonies united to form the Commonwealth of Australia under a written Constitution — a democratic milestone that was also founded on the exclusionary 'White Australia' policy and offered nothing to Aboriginal peoples.

- 1 January 1901: **Federation creates the Commonwealth of Australia** — Six self-governing colonies federated under a written Constitution to form the Commonwealth of Australia, a new federal nation with a parliament in which power was shared between the Commonwealth and the states. [nma-federation, naa-federation, brit-australia]
- 1901: **Immigration Restriction Act and 'White Australia'** — One of the new parliament's first laws established the cornerstone of the 'White Australia' policy, using a dictation test to exclude non-European migrants and keep the nation overwhelmingly white and British. [naa-immigration-restriction, brit-australia]
- 1902: **Women win the federal vote** — The Commonwealth Franchise Act gave most (non-Aboriginal) women the right to vote and to stand for the federal parliament, making Australia one of the first nations to enfranchise women nationally. [brit-australia, nma-federation]

Figures:

- Edmund Barton (1849–1920) — First Prime Minister of Australia. A leading advocate of federation who became Australia's first prime minister in 1901 and later sat on the High Court; his government also enacted the White Australia legislation.

## The world wars, immigration, and the twentieth century — 1914 – 1972

Two world wars cost Australia heavily and forged a powerful national mythology at Gallipoli, while post-war mass immigration transformed a once narrowly British society — even as Aboriginal children continued to be taken from their families.

- 25 April 1915: **Gallipoli landing and the Anzac legend** — Australian and New Zealand troops landed at Gallipoli in a costly campaign that, despite ending in withdrawal, became a foundational national story commemorated each year on Anzac Day. [awm-immigration-restriction, brit-australia]
- c. 1910–1970s: **The Stolen Generations** — Under official assimilation policies, governments and churches forcibly removed many thousands of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children from their families over decades, causing profound and lasting harm. [nma-apology, brit-australia]
- 19 February 1942: **Bombing of Darwin** — Japanese aircraft bombed Darwin during the Second World War, the largest attack ever mounted on Australia, prompting Australia to turn increasingly toward the United States for security. [brit-australia]
- 1945–1970s: **Post-war mass immigration** — A large post-war immigration programme brought millions of migrants, first from Europe and later from Asia and beyond, transforming Australia into a far more diverse society. [naa-immigration-restriction, brit-australia]
- 27 May 1967: **The 1967 referendum** — Australians voted by nearly 91 per cent to let the Commonwealth make laws for Aboriginal people and to include them in the census, a landmark in the struggle for Aboriginal rights and recognition. [moadoph-1967, brit-australia]

Figures:

- John Curtin (1885–1945) — Prime Minister of Australia. Led Australia through much of the Second World War, turned to the United States for defence as Japan advanced, and reshaped the nation's wartime strategy and alliances.

## Modern Australia: multiculturalism, reconciliation, and the apology — 1972 – present

Embracing multiculturalism and full legal independence, modern Australia has reckoned with its past through the Mabo native-title judgment and the 2008 national apology to the Stolen Generations, while reconciliation with First Peoples remains unfinished.

- 3 March 1986: **Australia Act and full legal independence** — The Australia Act 1986, building on the Statute of Westminster adopted in 1942, ended the British Parliament's power to legislate for Australia and abolished appeals to the Privy Council, completing Australia's legal independence. [brit-australia]
- 3 June 1992: **Mabo decision overturns terra nullius** — The High Court ruled in Mabo v Queensland that the continent had not been terra nullius and that Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples could hold native title, leading to the Native Title Act 1993. [aiatsis-mabo, brit-australia]
- 13 February 2008: **National Apology to the Stolen Generations** — Prime Minister Kevin Rudd delivered a formal apology in parliament to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, and especially to the Stolen Generations harmed by past policies of forced child removal. [nma-apology, brit-australia]
- 14 October 2023: **Voice to Parliament referendum** — Australians voted against a constitutional amendment to recognise First Peoples and establish an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Voice to Parliament, leaving questions of recognition and reconciliation unresolved. [brit-australia]

Figures:

- Eddie Mabo (1936–1992) — Torres Strait Islander land-rights campaigner. Led the legal challenge that produced the 1992 High Court decision bearing his name, overturning terra nullius and establishing native title in Australian law.
- Gough Whitlam (1916–2014) — Prime Minister of Australia. His reformist government of the 1970s dismantled the last of the White Australia policy, embraced multiculturalism, and advanced Aboriginal land rights before its dramatic dismissal in 1975.
