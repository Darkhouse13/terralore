# The Chronicle of Mali: From the gold of Wagadou to the Sahelian republic

History of Mali: From the gold of Wagadou to the Sahelian republic. 6 eras and 22 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/MLI/chronicle
Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from France — 22 September 1960. After the short-lived Mali Federation with Senegal broke apart, the former French Soudan declared itself the independent Republic of Mali, with Modibo Keïta as its first president.

On the bend of the upper Niger rose three of Africa's greatest medieval states — the Ghana (Wagadou), Mali and Songhai empires — grown wealthy on the trans-Saharan gold and salt trade and famed for the scholarship of Timbuktu and Djenné. Conquered piecemeal in the nineteenth century and absorbed into French Soudan, the territory became the independent Republic of Mali on 22 September 1960. A democratic opening in 1991 gave way after 2012 to insurgency in the north and a return to military rule.

## The Ghana Empire (Wagadou) — c. 8th–11th century CE

A Soninke trading state on the desert's edge grows rich as the broker of West Africa's gold.

- c. 950: **Ibn Hawqal records Ghana's wealth** — The Arab geographer Ibn Hawqal, visiting the western Sudan, describes Ghana's ruler as among the richest kings in the world on account of his gold. [met-transsaharan, ebsco-ghana]
- by c. 1000: **Apogee of the gold-and-salt trade** — Ghana controls a broad swathe of the western Sahel, acting as the broker between southern goldfields and North African salt caravans. [met-transsaharan, lumen-ghana]
- 1076: **Almoravids take Kumbi Saleh** — Berber Almoravids from present-day Mauritania capture Ghana's chief city, weakening the empire and the trade routes it controlled. [met-transsaharan, lumen-ghana]

## The Mali Empire — c. 1235–1450s

Sundiata Keita forges an empire on the upper Niger; Mansa Musa makes its gold legendary.

- c. 1235: **Battle of Kirina and the founding of Mali** — Sundiata Keita defeats Sumanguru Kanté of the Soso, unifying the Malinke and establishing the Mali Empire with its capital at Niani. [britannica-sundiata, natgeo-mali, wiki-mali-empire]
- 1324: **Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca** — Mansa Musa journeys to Mecca with a vast retinue and quantities of gold so great his almsgiving is said to have disrupted Egypt's gold prices, spreading Mali's fame abroad. [ebsco-mansamusa, natgeo-mali, wiki-mali-empire]
- 14th century: **Timbuktu and Djenné flourish** — Under Mali's mansas the Niger cities grow into major centres of trans-Saharan trade and Islamic scholarship, attracting merchants and scholars. [unesco-timbuktu, natgeo-mali]

Figures:

- Sundiata Keita (c. 1217 – c. 1255) — Founder and first mansa of Mali. United the Malinke chiefdoms, defeated Sumanguru Kanté at Kirina around 1235, and founded the Mali Empire; his life is celebrated in the Epic of Sundiata.

## The Songhai Empire and Its Fall — c. 1464–1591

From Gao, the Songhai build the Sahel's largest empire — until Moroccan muskets end it at Tondibi.

- 1468: **Sonni Ali takes Timbuktu** — Sonni Ali captures Timbuktu, and in 1473 Djenné, bringing the Niger trade cities under Songhai control and laying the foundation of the empire. [saylor-songhai, wiki-sonni-ali]
- 1493: **Askia Muhammad founds the Askiya dynasty** — A former general seizes power as Askia Muhammad, reorganising the empire and ushering in the golden age of Timbuktu's scholarship. [saylor-songhai, wiki-askia]
- April 1591: **Battle of Tondibi ends the Songhai Empire** — Moroccan forces under Judar Pasha, armed with firearms, rout the Songhai army at Tondibi near Gao, collapsing the last of the great Niger empires. [ebsco-songhai-fall, wiki-moroccan-invasion]

Figures:

- Askia Muhammad I (reigned 1493–1528) — Emperor of Songhai, founder of the Askiya dynasty. Reorganised Songhai's administration, made the pilgrimage to Mecca, and patronised the scholarship that made Timbuktu's golden age.

## Segou, the Tukulor and Samori — c. 1712–1898

Bambara kings, a jihadist empire and a Malinke resister contest the upper Niger as France advances.

- c. 1712: **Bitòn Coulibaly consolidates the Bambara kingdom of Segou** — Mamari (Bitòn) Coulibaly transforms a youth society into an army, founding the centralised Bambara state of Segou on the middle Niger. [newworld-bamana, wiki-segou]
- 1861: **Umar Tal's Tukulor empire conquers Segou** — Al-Hajj Umar Tal, leading the Tijaniyya jihad begun in the 1850s, overthrows the Bambara kingdom of Segou and extends the Tukulor empire across the upper Niger. [britannica-umartal, wiki-segou]
- 1864: **Death of Umar Tal** — The Tukulor leader dies in 1864 near Hamdallahi amid revolt; his successors face mounting French pressure. [britannica-umartal]
- 29 September 1898: **Capture of Samori Touré** — French forces seize Samori Touré, founder of the Wassoulou Empire and Mali's last great armed resister, ending nearly two decades of war. [blackpast-samori, britannica-samory, wiki-samori]

Figures:

- Samori Touré (c. 1830 – 1900) — Founder of the Wassoulou Empire; resistance leader. Malinke commander who built an empire across present-day Guinea, Mali and Côte d'Ivoire and resisted French conquest for almost twenty years before his capture in 1898.

## French Soudan and Independence — 1898–1968

Colony of French Soudan, then a brief federation, then the Republic of Mali under Modibo Keïta.

- early 20th century: **Colony of French Soudan** — The conquered upper Niger is administered as French Soudan within French West Africa, its economy reoriented toward the colonial metropole. [britannica-modibo, wiki-mali-federation]
- January 1959: **The Mali Federation with Senegal** — French Soudan and Senegal join in the short-lived Mali Federation, with Modibo Keïta as a leading figure; it dissolves in August 1960. [wiki-mali-federation, britannica-modibo]
- 22 September 1960: **Independence of the Republic of Mali** — The former French Soudan becomes the independent Republic of Mali; Modibo Keïta becomes its first president the following day. [blackpast-modibo, britannica-modibo, wiki-mali-federation]
- 19 November 1968: **Military coup ousts Modibo Keïta** — Lieutenant Moussa Traoré leads a coup against the socialist one-party government, beginning 23 years of his rule. [wiki-moussa-traore, aljazeera-timeline]

Figures:

- Modibo Keïta (1915–1977) — First President of Mali. Pan-Africanist and socialist who led French Soudan to independence in 1960 and governed as a one-party president until his overthrow in 1968.

## Dictatorship, Democracy and Crisis — 1968–present

From Traoré's long rule to the 1991 opening, then northern insurgency and a return to military government.

- March 1991: **Fall of Moussa Traoré and democratic transition** — After a deadly crackdown on protesters on 22 March, officers led by Amadou Toumani Touré oust Traoré; a transition leads to multiparty rule under Alpha Oumar Konaré in 1992. [wiki-1991-coup, nvc-mali-1991, aljazeera-timeline]
- 2012: **Tuareg rebellion, coup and jihadist seizure of the north** — The MNLA's January rebellion and a 22 March coup precipitate collapse; AQIM, Ansar Dine and allied jihadists take Timbuktu, Gao and Kidal. [aljazeera-timeline, ctc-serval, unesco-timbuktu]
- 11 January 2013: **France launches Operation Serval** — As jihadist forces drive toward Bamako, France intervenes militarily, stopping the advance and recapturing the northern cities. [ctc-serval, aljazeera-timeline]
- 18 August 2020: **Coup ousts President Ibrahim Boubacar Keïta** — Amid mass protests and a worsening insurgency, military officers arrest President Keïta, who resigns; a transitional government is formed. [wiki-2020-coup, crisisgroup-mali, aljazeera-timeline]
- May 2021: **Second coup brings Assimi Goïta to power** — Colonel Assimi Goïta removes the transitional civilian president and prime minister and takes the presidency himself. [crisisgroup-mali, aljazeera-timeline]

Figures:

- Amadou Toumani Touré (1948–2020) — Soldier and President of Mali. Led the 1991 coup that ended Traoré's rule and oversaw the transition to democracy, then served as elected president (2002–2012) until himself ousted in the 2012 coup.
