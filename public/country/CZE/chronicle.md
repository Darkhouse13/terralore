# The Chronicle of Czechia: From Great Moravia and the crown of Saint Wenceslas to the heart of Europe, by way of Hus, White Mountain and two peaceful revolutions.

Founding: The peaceful birth of the modern Czech state (the Velvet Divorce) — 1 January 1993. The modern Czech state came into being on 1 January 1993, when Czechoslovakia dissolved peacefully into two sovereign successor states, the Czech Republic and Slovakia, in a separation so amicable that it became known as the 'Velvet Divorce'. After the Velvet Revolution of 1989 ended communist rule, growing political and economic differences between Czech and Slovak leaders led the federal legislature to vote in November 1992 to divide the country; separate constitutions were drafted and the two republics came into existence at the start of 1993 without bloodshed, in sharp contrast to the violent breakup of Yugoslavia at the same time. The Czech statehood proclaimed in 1993 was, however, the latest chapter of a far older story: a continuity of Czech political identity that reaches back through the Kingdom of Bohemia, the duchy of the Premyslids and Saint Wenceslas, to the ninth-century Slavic state of Great Moravia.

The history of the Czech lands runs from the Slavic state of Great Moravia, where Saints Cyril and Methodius first brought Christianity and a written Slavic liturgy in the ninth century, to the modern republic at the centre of the European Union. The Premyslid dukes and their patron Saint Wenceslas forged a duchy that became the Kingdom of Bohemia, which reached its zenith under Charles IV, who made Prague an imperial capital and founded central Europe's first university in 1348. Bohemia produced one of Europe's earliest religious reformations under Jan Hus, whose burning in 1415 set off the Hussite Wars; two centuries later the Defenestration of Prague in 1618 helped ignite the Thirty Years' War, and defeat at the Battle of White Mountain in 1620 brought three centuries of Habsburg and Catholic domination. A nineteenth-century National Revival rebuilt the Czech language and nation, and in 1918 the multi-ethnic state of Czechoslovakia was founded under Tomas Garrigue Masaryk. That state was dismembered by the Munich Agreement of 1938 and occupied by Nazi Germany; after the war some three million Sudeten Germans were expelled, a communist coup followed in 1948, the Prague Spring of 1968 was crushed by Warsaw Pact tanks, and the Velvet Revolution of 1989 restored democracy. On 1 January 1993 Czechoslovakia divided peacefully into the Czech Republic and Slovakia, and the new state joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.

## Great Moravia, Saint Wenceslas and the Premyslid Dukes — c. 833 – 1212

From the first Slavic state of Great Moravia and the mission of Cyril and Methodius rose the Premyslid dukes of Bohemia and their martyred patron, Saint Wenceslas.

- c. 863: **Mission of Cyril and Methodius to Great Moravia** — At the request of Prince Rostislav, the Byzantine brothers Cyril and Methodius came to Great Moravia, translated the liturgy into Old Church Slavonic and devised the Glagolitic alphabet, bringing Christianity to the Slavs. [cyril-methodius-britannica, great-moravia-britannica]
- early 10th c.: **Fall of Great Moravia** — Weakened by internal division and overwhelmed by the Magyars, Great Moravia disintegrated in the early tenth century, and leadership of the western Slavs shifted to the Premyslid dukes of Bohemia. [great-moravia-britannica]
- c. 929: **Martyrdom of Saint Wenceslas** — Wenceslas (Vaclav), Premyslid duke of Bohemia and promoter of Christianity, was murdered by his brother and was at once venerated as a martyr, becoming the patron saint of the Czech nation. [wenceslas-britannica]
- 1212: **Golden Bull of Sicily and the Kingdom of Bohemia** — The Golden Bull of Sicily, issued by Emperor Frederick II, confirmed the royal title of the Premyslid rulers as hereditary, formally raising Bohemia from a duchy to a kingdom within the Holy Roman Empire. [otakar-britannica]

Figures:

- Saints Cyril and Methodius (9th century) — Byzantine missionaries, 'Apostles of the Slavs'. Brothers from Thessalonica sent to Great Moravia about 863, they translated Christian texts into Old Church Slavonic and created the Glagolitic alphabet, the first script for a Slavic language, shaping the religious and cultural life of the Slavs.
- Saint Wenceslas (Vaclav) (c. 907 – c. 929) — Duke of Bohemia and patron saint. A devout Premyslid duke who fostered Christianity in Bohemia, Wenceslas was murdered by his brother and venerated as a martyr almost at once. He became the patron saint of the Czech nation, and the royal crown of Bohemia bears his name.

## The Golden Age of Bohemia under Charles IV — 1212 – 1378

Under the Luxembourg king and emperor Charles IV, Prague became an imperial capital, a Gothic city of cathedral, bridge and university at the very heart of Europe.

- 1306: **End of the Premyslid dynasty** — The murder of Wenceslas III extinguished the male line of the Premyslids; after a brief contest the Bohemian crown passed to the House of Luxembourg, the dynasty of Charles IV. [czechoslovak-history-britannica]
- 1348: **Foundation of Charles University** — Charles IV founded the university in Prague that bears his name, the first university in central Europe, endowed with the same rights and liberties as the universities of Paris and Bologna. [charles-university-britannica, prague-unesco]
- 1355: **Charles IV crowned Holy Roman Emperor** — Already king of Bohemia, Charles was crowned Holy Roman Emperor in Rome in 1355, making Prague the principal residence of the Empire and the centre of European politics and culture. [charles-iv-britannica]
- 1357: **Building of Charles Bridge and Gothic Prague** — Charles IV laid the foundation of the stone bridge over the Vltava and advanced St Vitus Cathedral and the New Town, creating the Gothic core of Prague later recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage site. [prague-unesco, charles-iv-britannica]

Figures:

- Charles IV (1316 – 1378) — King of Bohemia and Holy Roman Emperor. The greatest of Bohemia's medieval rulers, Charles IV made Prague an imperial capital, founded Charles University in 1348, built the cathedral and bridge that define the city, and issued the Golden Bull of 1356 governing imperial elections.
- Arnost of Pardubice (1297 – 1364) — First Archbishop of Prague. Confidant and adviser of Charles IV, Arnost became the first archbishop of Prague when the see was elevated in 1344 and served as the founding chancellor of Charles University in 1348, helping to make Prague an ecclesiastical and intellectual centre.

## Jan Hus, the Hussite Wars and the Bohemian Reformation — 1378 – 1526

A century before Luther, the Prague preacher Jan Hus challenged the Church; his burning in 1415 unleashed the Hussite Wars, in which Bohemia defied crusade after crusade.

- 1402: **Jan Hus preaches at the Bethlehem Chapel** — Appointed preacher at the Bethlehem Chapel in Prague, Hus began to denounce church corruption and indulgences and to champion reform and vernacular preaching, drawing on the ideas of John Wycliffe. [jan-hus-britannica]
- 6 Jul 1415: **Jan Hus burned at the Council of Constance** — Condemned as a heretic despite a promise of safe conduct, Hus was burned at the stake at Constance on 6 July 1415, becoming a martyr whose death set off the Hussite movement. [jan-hus-britannica, hussite-britannica]
- 1419–1436: **The Hussite Wars** — Followers of Hus fought a series of wars against the Catholic crown and papal crusades; under Jan Zizka the Hussite armies proved nearly invincible before the conflict ended in compromise. [hussite-britannica, czechoslovak-history-britannica]
- 1436: **Compact of Iglau** — The moderate Utraquist Hussites secured a peace settlement, the Compact of Iglau, that granted communion in both kinds and an independent Bohemian Catholic church, ending the main phase of the wars. [hussite-britannica]

Figures:

- Jan Hus (c. 1369 – 1415) — Religious reformer and martyr. The foremost Czech reformer of the fifteenth century, Hus preached against church corruption and for vernacular Scripture a century before Luther. Burned at the Council of Constance in 1415, he became a national martyr and gave his name to the Hussite movement.
- Jan Zizka (c. 1360 – 1424) — Hussite military commander. The general of the radical Hussites, Zizka pioneered the use of fortified war-wagons and fielded a disciplined peasant army that defeated crusade after crusade, fighting on even after he was completely blind, and was never beaten in the field.

## Habsburg Bohemia: White Mountain, Counter-Reformation and National Revival — 1526 – 1918

The Defenestration of Prague helped ignite the Thirty Years' War; defeat at White Mountain brought three centuries of Habsburg rule, against which a nineteenth-century revival rebuilt the Czech nation.

- 1526: **Bohemia under the Habsburgs** — The Bohemian estates elected Ferdinand I of Habsburg as king, binding the kingdom to the Habsburg monarchy and to Austria and Hungary for nearly four centuries. [czechoslovak-history-britannica]
- 23 May 1618: **Defenestration of Prague** — Protestant nobles threw two Catholic imperial regents and their secretary from a window of Prague Castle, sparking the Bohemian revolt against Ferdinand II and helping to launch the Thirty Years' War. [defenestration-britannica]
- 8 Nov 1620: **Battle of White Mountain** — Imperial and Catholic League armies crushed the Bohemian rebels at White Mountain near Prague, ending Czech estates' power and ushering in Habsburg absolutism and forced Counter-Reformation. [defenestration-britannica, czechoslovak-history-britannica]
- 19th century: **The Czech National Revival** — From the late 18th century, scholars led by the historian Frantisek Palacky rebuilt and codified the Czech language and national history, transforming a cultural awakening into a political movement within Austria-Hungary. [palacky-britannica, czechoslovak-history-national-awakening]

Figures:

- Frantisek Palacky (1798 – 1876) — Historian and 'Father of the Nation'. The founder of modern Czech historiography, Palacky wrote a vast history of the Czech people, placed the Hussite era at the heart of national memory, and became the leading political voice of the nineteenth-century National Revival.
- Ferdinand II (1578 – 1637) — Habsburg Holy Roman Emperor and king of Bohemia. A zealous Catholic, Ferdinand II provoked the Bohemian revolt of 1618 and, after the victory at White Mountain in 1620, imposed Counter-Reformation and absolutist rule on the Czech lands.

## Czechoslovakia, the Munich Betrayal and Nazi Occupation — 1918 – 1945

Masaryk's multi-ethnic republic was Europe's surviving interwar democracy until the Munich Agreement of 1938 ceded the Sudetenland to Hitler and Nazi Germany occupied the Czech lands.

- 28 Oct 1918: **Foundation of Czechoslovakia** — With Austria-Hungary collapsing, independence was proclaimed in Prague on 28 October 1918; Tomas Garrigue Masaryk became the first president of the new multi-ethnic democratic republic. [masaryk-britannica, foundation-national-museum]
- 29–30 Sep 1938: **The Munich Agreement** — Germany, Italy, Britain and France agreed at Munich that Czechoslovakia must cede the German-majority Sudetenland to Nazi Germany; Czechoslovakia was not a party and yielded under Anglo-French pressure. [munich-britannica, munich-ushmm]
- 1–10 Oct 1938: **German annexation of the Sudetenland** — Following the Munich Agreement, German forces occupied the Sudetenland, stripping Czechoslovakia of its border defences and around three million inhabitants in its German-speaking borderlands. [munich-ushmm, sudetenland-britannica]
- 15 Mar 1939: **Occupation and the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia** — In breach of Munich, Germany occupied the Czech lands on 15 March 1939 and proclaimed the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia, while a separate Slovak state was set up under German control. [czechoslovakia-ushmm, munich-ushmm]

Figures:

- Tomas Garrigue Masaryk (1850 – 1937) — Founder and first President of Czechoslovakia. Philosopher and statesman, Masaryk organised the campaign for Czechoslovak independence from exile during the First World War and won Allied recognition. Proclaimed president in 1918, he became the moral founder of the interwar republic.
- Edvard Benes (1884 – 1948) — President of Czechoslovakia. Masaryk's close collaborator and foreign minister, Benes succeeded him as president in 1935. He resigned after Munich, led the government-in-exile during the war and returned to office in 1945, issuing the decrees that expelled the Sudeten Germans.

## Expulsion, Communism, the Velvet Revolution and the Modern Republic — 1945 – present

After the expulsion of the Sudeten Germans and a communist coup, the Prague Spring was crushed by tanks; the Velvet Revolution then restored democracy and the Velvet Divorce created the Czech Republic.

- 1945–1946: **Expulsion of the Sudeten Germans** — Under the Benes decrees and Allied agreement at Potsdam, Czechoslovakia stripped the citizenship and property of its Germans and expelled some three million Sudeten Germans, the largest forced migration in the history of the Czech lands. [benes-britannica, sudetenland-britannica, potsdam-britannica]
- Feb 1948: **Communist coup** — In February 1948 the Communist Party seized full power in Czechoslovakia, ending postwar democracy and bringing the country firmly into the Soviet bloc for the next four decades. [czechoslovak-history-britannica, havel-britannica]
- 20–21 Aug 1968: **Prague Spring crushed by Warsaw Pact** — Alexander Dubcek's reforms of 'socialism with a human face' were ended when roughly half a million Warsaw Pact troops, led by the Soviet Union, invaded Czechoslovakia in August 1968. [prague-spring-britannica, warsaw-pact-britannica]
- 17 Nov 1989: **The Velvet Revolution** — A suppressed student march in Prague on 17 November 1989 set off mass protests that toppled the communist regime within weeks; Vaclav Havel was elected president on 29 December. [velvet-revolution-britannica, havel-britannica]
- 1 Jan 1993: **The Velvet Divorce and birth of the Czech Republic** — After a 1992 vote to divide, Czechoslovakia split peacefully on 1 January 1993 into the Czech Republic and Slovakia; the Czech Republic later joined NATO in 1999 and the EU in 2004. [velvet-divorce-britannica, nato-membership, eu-enlargement-commission]

Figures:

- Vaclav Havel (1936 – 2011) — Dissident playwright and President. A playwright imprisoned for his dissent and a founder of Charter 77, Havel became the moral leader of the Velvet Revolution. Elected president of Czechoslovakia in 1989, he served as the first president of the Czech Republic from 1993 to 2003.
- Alexander Dubcek (1921 – 1992) — Reform Communist leader of the Prague Spring. As first secretary of the Communist Party in 1968, Dubcek led the Prague Spring reforms aimed at 'socialism with a human face'. The Warsaw Pact invasion ended the experiment, but he re-emerged in 1989 as a symbol of the Velvet Revolution.
