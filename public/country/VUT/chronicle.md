# The Chronicle of Vanuatu: Eighty islands of ash, coral and kastom

Founding: Independence as the Republic of Vanuatu — 30 July 1980. The New Hebrides, jointly governed by Britain and France, became the independent Republic of Vanuatu, with the Anglican priest Walter Lini of the Vanua'aku Pati as its first prime minister.

Vanuatu is a Melanesian archipelago of some eighty volcanic and coral islands in the southwestern Pacific, settled around three thousand years ago by Austronesian seafarers of the Lapita culture whose oldest known Pacific cemetery lies at Teouma on Efate. Its oral history reveres the paramount chief Roi Mata, whose seventeenth-century domain is now a UNESCO World Heritage site. After European contact from 1606, the islands endured the sandalwood trade, the 'blackbirding' labour traffic and catastrophic depopulation by disease, then seven decades under a famously duplicated Anglo-French Condominium known as the New Hebrides. Vanuatu became an independent republic on 30 July 1980 and today, vulnerable to cyclones and rising seas, leads the world's climate diplomacy.

## Lapita Seafarers and Chief Roi Mata — c. 1000 BCE – 1606 CE

Austronesian voyagers of the Lapita culture peopled the archipelago, and from their descendants rose the paramount chief Roi Mata, remembered in oral tradition and honoured by a World Heritage site.

- c. 1300–1000 BCE: **Lapita seafarers settle the archipelago** — Austronesian voyagers of the Lapita culture reached northern Vanuatu, carrying their distinctive pottery, crops and animals on one of history's great oceanic migrations. [britannica-history, anu-teouma, humanities-teouma]
- c. 1000 BCE: **The Teouma cemetery on Efate** — The burial ground at Teouma on Efate, dated to about 3,000 years ago, is the oldest known cemetery in the Pacific and preserves the remains of the first settlers. [anu-teouma, humanities-teouma]
- early 1600s: **Reign of the paramount chief Roi Mata** — Oral tradition records Roi Mata as a great chief of central Vanuatu who ended inter-clan warfare and whose burial on Artok is honoured by lasting prohibitions on his sites. [unesco-crmd, unesco-legendary, vanuatu-tourism-crmd]

Figures:

- Roi Mata (early 17th century (traditional)) — Paramount chief of central Vanuatu. Melanesian paramount chief remembered in oral tradition for uniting warring clans and ending bloodshed; his residence, death site and mass burial form the UNESCO World Heritage property named for him.

## European Contact and Naming — 1606 – 1773

A Spanish expedition mistook Espiritu Santo for the unknown southern continent in 1606; Bougainville and then Cook, who named the islands the New Hebrides, followed in the eighteenth century.

- May 1606: **Quirós reaches Espiritu Santo** — Pedro Fernández de Quirós, leading a Spanish expedition, landed on the largest island, named it La Austrialia del Espíritu Santo and claimed it for Spain before his short-lived colony failed. [britannica-espiritusanto, slnsw-spanish]
- 1768: **Bougainville sails through the group** — The French navigator Louis-Antoine de Bougainville charted parts of the archipelago and showed that Quirós's 'southern continent' was a group of islands. [britannica-history]
- July 1774: **Cook names the New Hebrides** — On his second voyage James Cook surveyed and charted the islands, naming them the New Hebrides — a name they kept until independence in 1980. [britannica-history, dailypost-newhebrides]

Figures:

- Pedro Fernández de Quirós (c. 1565–1614) — Portuguese-born navigator in Spanish service. Led the 1606 expedition that first brought Europeans to the islands, naming Espiritu Santo in the mistaken belief he had found the great southern continent.

## Sandalwood, Blackbirding and Missions — 1825 – 1905

Sandalwood traders, the 'blackbirding' labour traffic and introduced disease devastated island populations, while Christian missionaries — some martyred — transformed island life.

- 1839: **Missionaries arrive; Williams killed at Erromango** — Christian missions began in the islands; the LMS missionary John Williams was killed at Erromango, the first of several martyrdoms as Christianity slowly took hold. [anu-christianity, britannica-history]
- 1863–1904: **The blackbirding labour trade** — Recruiters carried over forty thousand islanders to Queensland and others to Fiji and New Caledonia for plantation labour, by indenture, deception and force. [paclii-blackbirding, anu-christianity]
- 19th century: **Depopulation by introduced disease** — Measles, dysentery, influenza and other introduced diseases, together with the labour trade, devastated island populations across the colonial period. [anu-christianity, britannica-history]

## The Anglo-French Condominium — 1906 – 1979

Britain and France governed the New Hebrides jointly under a uniquely duplicated condominium, while wartime American bases helped spawn the John Frum cargo movement on Tanna.

- 1906: **Anglo-French Condominium established** — Britain and France agreed to govern the New Hebrides jointly, creating a famously duplicated administration with two of nearly everything. [britannica-history, state-vanuatu]
- 1942: **U.S. wartime bases on Efate and Santo** — American forces built major bases in the islands during the Pacific War, flooding them with goods and personnel and transforming local society. [britannica-history]
- c. late 1930s–1940s: **Rise of the John Frum movement** — On Tanna the John Frum cargo cult emerged, awaiting a messianic 'John from America' who would bring cargo and restore custom against missionary and colonial control. [britannica-history]

## Independence and the Coconut War — 1971 – 1980

The Vanua'aku Pati under Walter Lini led the New Hebrides to independence as Vanuatu in 1980, even as a secessionist revolt on Espiritu Santo — the 'Coconut War' — challenged the new state.

- 1971: **Vanua'aku Pati founded under Walter Lini** — The party that became the Vanua'aku Pati, led by the Anglican priest Walter Lini, was formed to demand independence and the return of alienated land. [britannica-history, state-vanuatu]
- 1980: **The Coconut War on Espiritu Santo** — Jimmy Stevens's Nagriamel movement declared the breakaway 'State of Vemerana' on Espiritu Santo; the lightly armed revolt was suppressed soon after independence. [britannica-history]
- 30 July 1980: **Republic of Vanuatu proclaimed** — The New Hebrides became the independent Republic of Vanuatu, with Walter Lini as first prime minister; Papua New Guinean troops then crushed the Santo secession. [britannica-history, state-vanuatu]

Figures:

- Walter Lini (1942–1999) — First Prime Minister of Vanuatu. Anglican priest who founded the Vanua'aku Pati, led the independence movement and governed independent Vanuatu from 1980, championing non-alignment and 'Melanesian socialism'.

## Kastom, Cyclones and Climate Diplomacy — 1980 – present

Independent Vanuatu balances custom and democracy, weathers ever-fiercer cyclones and rising seas, and has made itself the global standard-bearer for climate justice.

- 2008: **Chief Roi Mata's Domain joins World Heritage List** — UNESCO inscribed the residence, death site and burial of Chief Roi Mata on Efate, Lelepa and Artok — Vanuatu's first World Heritage property. [unesco-crmd, vanuatu-tourism-crmd]
- March 2015: **Cyclone Pam devastates the islands** — Tropical Cyclone Pam, the most destructive storm in Vanuatu's history, damaged the great majority of buildings and a vast share of national output. [reliefweb-harold, uncdf-vanuatu]
- 29 March 2023: **UN asks the ICJ on climate obligations** — A Vanuatu-led campaign, begun by Pacific law students, won a UN General Assembly resolution requesting an ICJ advisory opinion on states' climate-change obligations. [vanuatu-icj, reliefweb-icj]
- 23 July 2025: **ICJ delivers landmark climate opinion** — The International Court of Justice found that states have binding legal duties to protect the climate and that failure to act may be an internationally wrongful act. [icj-case187, earthorg-icj]

Figures:

- Walter Lini (1942–1999) — Founding Prime Minister of Vanuatu. First leader of independent Vanuatu, who set the young republic's course of non-alignment, custom and Pacific solidarity that still shapes its climate diplomacy.
