Founding: Proclamation of the German Empire — 18 Jan 1871. King Wilhelm I of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles on 18 January 1871, uniting the German states into a single nation under Prussian leadership. The new empire drew on older notions of German statehood reaching back to East Francia, established by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, and the imperial coronation of Otto I in 962 that founded the Holy Roman Empire. After the catastrophe of two world wars and forty years of Cold War division, the modern German nation-state was made whole again through reunification on 3 October 1990, when the German Democratic Republic acceded to the Federal Republic of Germany.

Germany's history is one of a people long bound by language and culture yet politically fragmented for most of a millennium. From the Germanic tribes who halted Rome at the Rhine, through the loose sprawl of the Holy Roman Empire and the religious rupture of the Reformation, the German lands coalesced only in 1871 under Prussian leadership. The unified nation rose swiftly to industrial and military power, descended into two world wars and, between 1933 and 1945, into the dictatorship of the Third Reich and the genocide of European Jewry. Defeated and divided in 1945, Germany spent four decades split between a democratic west and a communist east before its peaceful reunification in 1990 restored it as a single democratic state at the centre of the European Union.

## Germania, Rome and the Frankish Inheritance — c. 9 CE – 911

Germanic peoples beyond the Rhine resisted Roman conquest, then inherited and reshaped the western Roman world through the Frankish empire of Charlemagne.

- 9 CE: **Battle of the Teutoburg Forest** — Germanic tribes under Arminius destroyed three Roman legions commanded by Varus, ending Roman attempts to conquer Germania east of the Rhine and securing the river as a permanent frontier. [teutoburg-britannica]
- 25 Dec 800: **Coronation of Charlemagne** — Pope Leo III crowned the Frankish king Charlemagne Emperor of the Romans in Rome, reviving the western imperial title and binding the German lands into a renewed Christian empire. [charlemagne-britannica]
- 843 CE: **Treaty of Verdun** — Charlemagne's grandsons divided the Carolingian empire into three kingdoms. East Francia, the German-speaking eastern share, became the foundation of the future Kingdom of Germany. [verdun-britannica]

Figures:

- Arminius (c. 18 BCE – c. 19 CE) — Cheruscan chieftain and war-leader. Trained in the Roman army and granted citizenship, Arminius turned his knowledge against Rome to forge a tribal coalition that destroyed Varus's legions in 9 CE. Later German nationalism mythologised him as 'Hermann', a founding hero, though his historical aims were tribal rather than national.
- Charlemagne (747/748 – 814) — King of the Franks and Emperor. The greatest of the Carolingian rulers, Charlemagne united much of western Europe under Frankish rule and was crowned emperor in 800. Both German and French traditions claim him; the partition of his empire in 843 set the German lands on their own path.

## The Holy Roman Empire and the Reformation — 962 – 1648

A sprawling, decentralised empire of princes, free cities and bishoprics gave the German lands their political form, until religious schism plunged them into a generation of war.

- 2 Feb 962: **Otto I crowned emperor** — Pope John XII crowned Otto I of East Francia emperor in Rome, uniting the German and Italian kingdoms and founding the Holy Roman Empire that would frame German political life for over 800 years. [otto-britannica]
- 31 Oct 1517: **Luther's Ninety-five Theses** — Martin Luther issued his Ninety-five Theses against the sale of indulgences, sparking the Protestant Reformation that split Western Christianity and reshaped German society and language. [theses-britannica]
- 25 Sep 1555: **Peace of Augsburg** — The imperial diet established the first lasting legal coexistence of Lutheranism and Catholicism, letting each prince set his territory's religion under the principle cuius regio, eius religio. [augsburg-britannica]
- 1618–1648: **The Thirty Years' War and the Peace of Westphalia** — A devastating European war fought largely in Germany ended with the Peace of Westphalia in 1648, which confirmed the autonomy of the empire's states and entrenched German fragmentation. [westphalia-britannica]

Figures:

- Otto I (Otto the Great) (912 – 973) — King of Germany and Holy Roman Emperor. By defeating the Magyars at Lechfeld and securing his coronation in Rome in 962, Otto fused royal and imperial authority and inaugurated the Holy Roman Empire, anchoring the German crown to the imperial title for centuries.
- Martin Luther (1483 – 1546) — Theologian and reformer. Luther's challenge to papal authority and his German Bible transformed religion, politics and language across the empire. Excommunicated and outlawed, he survived under princely protection, and Lutheranism became a defining force in German history.

## Prussia, Fragmentation and the Road to Unity — 1648 – 1871

The rise of Prussia, the collapse of the old empire under Napoleon and a growing national movement converged in the unification of Germany under Bismarck.

- 6 Aug 1806: **Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire** — Under pressure from Napoleon, Emperor Francis II abdicated the imperial crown and dissolved the Holy Roman Empire, ending the medieval framework of German statehood after more than eight centuries. [francis-britannica]
- 1815: **Congress of Vienna and the German Confederation** — After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna created a loose confederation of German states dominated by Austria and Prussia, leaving national unity unfulfilled. [germany-britannica]
- 1848–1849: **The Revolutions of 1848** — Liberal and nationalist uprisings swept the German states, and the Frankfurt Parliament drafted a constitution for a united Germany, but the movement collapsed when the Prussian king refused its imperial crown. [germany-britannica]
- 18 Jan 1871: **Proclamation of the German Empire** — Following victory in the Franco-Prussian War, the German states united as the German Empire, proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles with Wilhelm I as Emperor and Bismarck as Chancellor. [versailles-chateau, germany-britannica]

Figures:

- Otto von Bismarck (1815 – 1898) — Minister-President of Prussia and first Chancellor of the German Empire. The architect of German unification, Bismarck engineered a series of wars and a web of diplomacy that forged the empire in 1871. As chancellor he dominated European politics for two decades and pioneered the first modern welfare-state institutions.

## The German Empire and the First World War — 1871 – 1918

A newly unified empire rose to industrial and military pre-eminence, then plunged Europe into the First World War and emerged in defeat and revolution.

- 1871–1890: **The Bismarckian empire and industrial rise** — Under Bismarck's chancellorship the empire industrialised rapidly and introduced the world's first national social insurance, while maintaining a careful balance of power in Europe. [germany-britannica]
- 1884–1885: **German colonial expansion** — Germany acquired colonies in Africa and the Pacific, a latecomer's empire whose rule, including the genocide of the Herero and Nama in South West Africa, left a brutal legacy. [germany-britannica]
- 1914–1918: **The First World War** — Germany fought the First World War as the principal Central Power. Four years of total war devastated a generation and ended in German defeat. [germany-britannica]
- 9 Nov 1918: **Abdication of Wilhelm II** — Amid military collapse and revolution, Emperor Wilhelm II abdicated and a republic was proclaimed in Berlin; the armistice followed two days later, ending the war and the monarchy. [germany-britannica]

Figures:

- Wilhelm II (1859 – 1941) — Last German Emperor and King of Prussia. Impulsive and ambitious, Wilhelm II dismissed Bismarck and steered Germany toward a more aggressive 'world policy'. His reign ended in war, revolution and abdication, closing the chapter of imperial monarchy in Germany.

## Weimar, the Third Reich and the Second World War — 1918 – 1945

Germany's first democracy struggled and fell, giving way to the Nazi dictatorship, the Second World War and the genocide of European Jewry.

- 1919: **The Weimar Republic and its constitution** — Germany's first democracy adopted the Weimar Constitution in 1919, establishing a parliamentary republic that would be undermined by economic crisis and political extremism. [weimar-bundestag]
- 30 Jan 1933: **Hitler appointed chancellor** — President Hindenburg appointed Adolf Hitler chancellor. Within months the Nazis abolished democracy and established a totalitarian one-party dictatorship. [ushmm-keydates]
- 1 Sep 1939: **Invasion of Poland** — Germany invaded Poland, igniting the Second World War in Europe and beginning six years of conquest, occupation and total war. [ushmm-keydates]
- 1941–1945: **The Holocaust** — Nazi Germany carried out the systematic, state-organised murder of some six million European Jews, alongside the killing of Roma, disabled people, Soviet prisoners and other victims. [ushmm-keydates]
- 8 May 1945: **Unconditional surrender** — Germany surrendered unconditionally to the Allies, ending the Second World War in Europe and the Nazi regime amid the ruin of the country. [ushmm-keydates]

Figures:

- Friedrich Ebert (1871 – 1925) — First President of the Weimar Republic. A Social Democrat, Ebert helped guide Germany from monarchy to republic in 1918 and served as its first president. He defended the fragile democracy against uprisings from both extremes amid relentless instability.

## Division, the Federal Republic and Reunification — 1945 – present

Occupied and divided after 1945, Germany was split into a democratic west and a communist east before peaceful reunification in 1990 restored a single democratic state.

- 23 May 1949: **Founding of the two German states** — The Basic Law established the Federal Republic of Germany in the west on 23 May 1949; the German Democratic Republic was founded in the Soviet zone on 7 October 1949, formalising the country's division. [germany-britannica]
- 13 Aug 1961: **Construction of the Berlin Wall** — East Germany began building the Berlin Wall to stop its citizens fleeing west, creating the most potent symbol of the Cold War division of Europe. [germany-britannica]
- 9 Nov 1989: **Fall of the Berlin Wall** — Amid mass protests and the collapse of communist rule across Eastern Europe, the Berlin Wall was opened, ending the division of Berlin and clearing the path to reunification. [germany-britannica, unity-deutschland]
- 3 Oct 1990: **German reunification** — The German Democratic Republic acceded to the Federal Republic, reuniting Germany as a single democratic state, now commemorated annually as the Day of German Unity. [unity-deutschland, germany-britannica]

Figures:

- Konrad Adenauer (1876 – 1967) — First Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany. Adenauer led West Germany from 1949 to 1963, anchoring it firmly in the democratic West, securing reconciliation with former enemies and overseeing the postwar economic recovery that restored German prosperity and credibility.
- Helmut Kohl (1930 – 2017) — Chancellor of reunification. Chancellor from 1982 to 1998, Kohl seized the opening created by the fall of the Wall to drive German reunification in 1990 and championed deeper European integration, including the creation of the euro.
