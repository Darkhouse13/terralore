# The Chronicle of Malaysia: From the Malacca Sultanate and the spice straits to a multiethnic federation of peninsula and Borneo.

Founding: Formation of Malaysia — 16 Sep 1963. Malaysia was created on 16 September 1963 when the independent Federation of Malaya joined with Singapore and the former British Borneo territories of North Borneo (renamed Sabah) and Sarawak under the Malaysia Agreement signed in London on 9 July 1963. The new federation grew directly out of the Federation of Malaya, which had achieved independence (Merdeka) on 31 August 1957 under Tunku Abdul Rahman. Singapore left the federation by mutual agreement in 1965, leaving the present union of eleven peninsular states and the two Borneo states of Sabah and Sarawak.

Malaysia's history is bound to the Strait of Malacca, the maritime artery between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea, where early Malay polities and the Buddhist realm of Srivijaya gave way around 1400 to the Malacca Sultanate, the great Islamic entrepot of the Malay world. Successive European powers seized that wealth, the Portuguese in 1511, the Dutch in 1641 and the British across the nineteenth century, who knit the Straits Settlements, the Malay states and the Borneo territories of Sabah and Sarawak into British Malaya. After the Japanese occupation and the long Malayan Emergency, the Federation of Malaya won independence on 31 August 1957 and Malaysia itself was formed on 16 September 1963; Singapore separated in 1965. Today Malaysia is a multiethnic constitutional monarchy and federation shaped by the New Economic Policy, the Mahathir era and a turbulent modern democracy.

## Early Malay Polities and the Srivijaya Maritime Realm — c. 7th – 14th century CE

Long before sultans or colonisers, the wealth of the Malay world flowed through the Strait of Malacca, where the Buddhist thalassocracy of Srivijaya grew rich on the monsoon trade between India and China.

- c. 7th century CE: **Rise of the Srivijaya thalassocracy** — Based at Palembang in Sumatra, the Buddhist kingdom of Srivijaya comes to control the Strait of Malacca and the maritime trade between India and China, becoming the dominant commercial power of the Malay world. [britannica-srivijaya, ebsco-srivijaya]
- 1025: **Chola naval raid weakens Srivijaya** — A naval expedition from the south Indian Chola dynasty strikes Srivijaya's ports, contributing to the long decline of its dominance over the strait. [britannica-srivijaya]

## The Malacca Sultanate, Islam and the Spice Trade — c. 1400 – 1511

Founded around 1400 by a refugee prince, Malacca became the greatest entrepot of the Malay world, a Muslim sultanate where the spice trade and the coming of Islam met at the narrowest point of the strait.

- c. 1400: **Founding of Malacca by Paramesvara** — The Sumatran prince Paramesvara, having fled Palembang and Tumasik before Majapahit's forces, settles at Malacca and founds a city that becomes the leading port of the Malay world. [britannica-paramesvara, worldhistory-portuguese-malacca]
- early 15th c.: **Malacca, Islam and ties with Ming China** — Through marriage to a Muslim princess and the adoption of the title Sultan, Malacca's court embraces Islam, while tributary relations with Ming China, marked by Zheng He's visits, secure its trade. [britannica-paramesvara, worldhistory-portuguese-malacca]
- 15th century: **Malacca, the great entrepot of the strait** — Malacca grows into the most important trade centre in the region, where gold, spices, silk and tea pass between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea, and Malay court culture flourishes. [worldhistory-portuguese-malacca, unesco-melaka-georgetown]

Figures:

- Paramesvara (c. 1344 – c. 1414) — Founder and first ruler of Malacca. A prince of Palembang who fled the collapse of the old Sumatran order, by way of Tumasik, and founded Malacca around 1400. His court's turn to Islam helped make Malacca both the leading port of the Malay world and a centre from which the faith spread.

## European Powers and the Making of British Malaya — 1511 – 1941

Portuguese, Dutch and finally British control reshaped the strait. From Penang and Singapore the British built the Straits Settlements, drew the Malay states under protection, and absorbed the Borneo lands of Sabah and Sarawak.

- 1511: **Portuguese capture of Malacca** — Afonso de Albuquerque storms Malacca after a siege, ending the independent sultanate and beginning sustained European control of the strait and its trade. [worldhistory-portuguese-malacca, ebsco-melaka-portuguese]
- 1641: **Dutch conquest of Malacca** — The Dutch East India Company, allied with the Sultanate of Johor, takes Malacca from the Portuguese, holding it as a lesser outpost while focusing power on Batavia. [worldhistory-portuguese-malacca]
- 1786: **Penang, Singapore and the Straits Settlements** — Britain founds Penang in 1786 and Singapore in 1819 and, with Malacca, combines them in 1826 as the Straits Settlements, the base for expanding British control over the peninsula. [britannica-straits-settlements]
- 1841: **James Brooke becomes Raja of Sarawak** — The Sultan of Brunei appoints the Englishman James Brooke raja of Sarawak, founding the 'White Raja' dynasty and beginning the absorption of the Borneo territories into the British sphere. [britannica-malaya-borneo]

Figures:

- James Brooke (1803–1868) — First White Raja of Sarawak. English adventurer who helped suppress a revolt against the Sultan of Brunei and was rewarded in 1841 with the rule of Sarawak, founding a dynasty that governed the Borneo state until the Second World War and drew it into the territory that became East Malaysia.

## Occupation, the Emergency and Merdeka — 1941 – 1957

The Japanese occupation shattered British prestige; the failed Malayan Union spurred Malay nationalism; and through the long communist Emergency the Federation of Malaya marched to independence on 31 August 1957.

- 15 Feb 1942: **Japanese occupation and the fall of Singapore** — Japanese forces overrun Malaya and capture Singapore on 15 February 1942, beginning a three-and-a-half-year occupation that shatters British prestige and deepens communal divisions. [britannica-political-transformation, nlb-fall-singapore]
- Mar 1946: **Malayan Union opposition and the founding of UMNO** — Malay opposition to the centralising Malayan Union proposal gives rise to the United Malays National Organisation, the country's first major political party, and the union is soon replaced by the Federation of Malaya. [britannica-political-transformation]
- 1948: **The Malayan Emergency** — After the Malayan Communist Party launches a guerrilla insurgency, the government declares a state of emergency on 18 June 1948, beginning a counterinsurgency that lasts until 1960. [britannica-malayan-emergency]
- 31 Aug 1957: **Merdeka: independence of Malaya** — The Federation of Malaya achieves independence under Tunku Abdul Rahman, who proclaims a sovereign, democratic state at Merdeka Stadium in Kuala Lumpur. [britannica-federation-malaya, britannica-tunku]

Figures:

- Tunku Abdul Rahman (1903–1990) — First Prime Minister of Malaya and Malaysia. Leader of UMNO and the multiethnic Alliance who negotiated independence in London and proclaimed Merdeka in 1957. Remembered as the 'Father of Independence', he became the first prime minister of Malaya and then of Malaysia, presiding over the federation's formation in 1963.

## The Formation of Malaysia and Singapore's Separation — 1961 – 1969

On 16 September 1963 Malaya, Sabah, Sarawak and Singapore joined to form Malaysia, surviving Indonesian Konfrontasi; but communal strains soon forced Singapore out in 1965 and erupted in the riots of 1969.

- 16 Sep 1963: **Formation of the Federation of Malaysia** — Malaya joins with Singapore, Sabah (North Borneo) and Sarawak under the Malaysia Agreement to form Malaysia, the founding act of the modern federation. [britannica-federation-malaya, britannica-political-transformation]
- 1963–1966: **Indonesian Confrontation (Konfrontasi)** — Indonesia's President Sukarno opposes the new federation and wages an undeclared campaign of armed confrontation against Malaysia until 1966, drawing in Britain, Australia and the United States. [history-state-frus-malaysia, britannica-political-transformation]
- 9 Aug 1965: **Singapore's separation from Malaysia** — Amid deepening communal and political friction, Singapore is separated from Malaysia by mutual agreement and becomes an independent state. [britannica-political-transformation, history-state-frus-malaysia]
- 13 May 1969: **The 13 May communal riots** — Racial rioting between Malays and Chinese in Kuala Lumpur follows a divisive general election, leaving many dead and leading to a suspension of parliament that transforms Malaysian politics. [britannica-political-transformation]

## The New Economic Policy, Mahathir and Modern Malaysia — 1970 – present

After the trauma of 1969, the New Economic Policy reshaped society in favour of the Malay majority; Mahathir Mohamad drove rapid industrialisation; and after 2018 Malaysia entered an era of turbulent, competitive democracy.

- 1971: **The New Economic Policy** — In the wake of the 1969 riots, the government launches a twenty-year programme of affirmative action to reduce poverty and raise the economic share of the Bumiputera majority, reshaping Malaysian society. [iseas-nep, britannica-political-transformation]
- 1981–2003: **The Mahathir era and rapid modernisation** — Mahathir Mohamad serves as prime minister for twenty-two years, driving industrialisation and signature projects such as the Petronas Twin Towers while centralising political power. [britannica-mahathir]
- 2008: **Melaka and George Town inscribed by UNESCO** — UNESCO lists Melaka and George Town, Historic Cities of the Straits of Malacca, recognising more than five centuries of trade and multicultural exchange between East and West. [unesco-melaka-georgetown]
- 9 May 2018: **1MDB scandal ends Barisan Nasional rule** — Amid the 1MDB corruption scandal, an opposition alliance led by Mahathir defeats Najib Razak, ending more than six decades of Barisan Nasional government; Najib is later convicted and jailed. [aljazeera-najib-1mdb, aljazeera-anwar-timeline]
- 24 Nov 2022: **Anwar Ibrahim becomes prime minister** — After years of imprisonment and opposition and a period of post-2020 instability, Anwar Ibrahim is appointed Malaysia's tenth prime minister following an inconclusive general election. [aljazeera-anwar-timeline]

Figures:

- Mahathir Mohamad (born 1925) — Prime Minister (1981–2003; 2018–2020). Malaysia's longest-serving prime minister, who led a sweeping industrialisation drive and assertive nationalism over twenty-two years, then returned in 2018 at age 92 to head the alliance that toppled the scandal-hit Barisan Nasional government.
- Anwar Ibrahim (born 1947) — Prime Minister of Malaysia (from 2022). Once Mahathir's deputy, dismissed and jailed in 1998, he spent two decades as a reform figurehead in opposition before becoming prime minister in November 2022, leading a multiethnic coalition pledged to clean government.
