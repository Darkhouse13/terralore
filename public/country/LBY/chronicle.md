Founding: Independence as the United Kingdom of Libya — 24 December 1951. On 24 December 1951 Libya proclaimed its independence as the United Kingdom of Libya, a federal constitutional monarchy uniting the provinces of Tripolitania, Cyrenaica and the Fezzan under King Idris al-Senussi, head of the Senussi religious order. The new state was the first country to attain independence through the United Nations: a 1949 UN General Assembly resolution had determined that the former Italian colony, placed under British and French administration after the Second World War, should become a single independent and sovereign state. Libya was also among the first former European possessions in Africa to gain independence.

Libya unites three historic regions—Tripolitania in the west, Cyrenaica in the east and the Saharan Fezzan in the south—whose pasts long ran apart. Phoenician traders founded Tripoli, Sabratha and Leptis Magna on the western coast, while Greek settlers raised Cyrene in the east; both fell to Rome, and the desert Garamantes built a kingdom in the Fezzan. The Arab-Islamic conquest of the seventh century made the country Muslim and Arabic-speaking. Ottoman rule from 1551, including the semi-independent Karamanli dynasty of Tripoli, gave way in 1911 to a brutal Italian colonisation resisted by Omar al-Mukhtar. On 24 December 1951 Libya became independent as the United Kingdom of Libya under King Idris—the first state created through the United Nations. Oil wealth, Muammar Gaddafi's 42-year rule from 1969, his fall in the 2011 uprising and the subsequent division have shaped the country since.

## Phoenician Tripolitania, Greek Cyrene and Roman Rule — c. 7th century BCE – 4th century CE

Phoenician and Greek colonists planted great cities on the Libyan coast that Rome inherited as some of its finest provincial centres.

- later 7th century BCE: **Phoenicians found Leptis Magna** — Phoenician colonists establish Leptis Magna, which with Oea (Tripoli) and Sabratha forms the 'three cities' that give Tripolitania its name. [wikipedia-leptis-magna, britannica-libya]
- 631 BCE: **Greeks found Cyrene** — Settlers from Thera, led by Battus, found Cyrene in eastern Libya; it becomes the chief city of the Cyrenaican Pentapolis and a renowned centre of Greek culture. [wikipedia-cyrene, britannica-cyrenaica]
- 96 BCE: **Cyrenaica passes to Rome** — On the death of Ptolemy Apion, Cyrenaica is bequeathed to Rome; Tripolitania likewise comes under Roman authority, opening centuries of prosperity. [wikipedia-cyrene, britannica-cyrenaica]
- 193–211 CE: **Leptis Magna's golden age under Septimius Severus** — The emperor Septimius Severus, born at Leptis Magna, lavishes monuments on his home city, making it one of the foremost ports of Roman Africa. [wikipedia-leptis-magna, unesco-leptis-magna]

## The Garamantes and the Arab-Islamic Conquest — c. 1st century BCE – 11th century CE

A desert kingdom thrived in the Fezzan before Arab armies brought Islam, Arabic and lasting cultural change to all three regions.

- 1st–4th centuries CE: **Garamantes flourish in the Fezzan** — From their capital at Garama, the Garamantes sustain a desert kingdom through foggara irrigation and command of trans-Saharan trade. [wikipedia-garamantes, britannica-fezzan]
- 642–643 CE: **Arab conquest of Cyrenaica** — Arab forces under Amr ibn al-As take Cyrenaica and advance into Tripolitania, beginning Libya's transformation into a Muslim, Arabic-speaking land. [fanack-muslim-conquest, britannica-libya]
- 660s CE: **Arab expeditions reach the Fezzan** — Campaigns associated with Uqba ibn Nafi extend Arab-Muslim authority into the Saharan Fezzan, ending the autonomy of the Garamantes' heartland. [wikipedia-garamantes, fanack-muslim-conquest]
- from the 11th century: **Banu Hilal migration** — The westward migration of Arab Bedouin tribes such as the Banu Hilal accelerates the Arabisation of Libya's interior. [britannica-libya]

## Ottoman Rule and the Karamanli Dynasty — 1551–1911

The Ottomans took Tripoli in 1551; under the Karamanli dynasty the regency became a Barbary power that fought a war with the young United States.

- 1551: **Ottomans capture Tripoli** — Ottoman admirals Sinan Pasha and Turgut Reis take Tripoli, establishing the Regency of Tripoli within the Ottoman Empire. [wikipedia-ottoman-tripolitania, britannica-libya]
- 1711: **Karamanli dynasty seizes power** — Ahmed Karamanli founds a hereditary dynasty that rules Tripoli in semi-independence for 124 years under nominal Ottoman suzerainty. [wikipedia-karamanli, wikipedia-ottoman-tripolitania]
- 1801–1805: **First Barbary War with the United States** — Yusuf Karamanli's tribute demands provoke war with the United States; the US Navy blockades Tripoli until a treaty ends the conflict in 1805. [wikipedia-first-barbary-war, wikipedia-karamanli]
- 1835: **Ottomans restore direct rule** — Sultan Mahmud II ends the Karamanli dynasty and reimposes direct Ottoman administration over Tripolitania. [wikipedia-ottoman-tripolitania]
- 1837: **Founding of the Senussi order** — Muhammad ibn Ali al-Senussi founds the Sanusi religious order, which from the 1840s becomes the leading power in Cyrenaica and the eastern Sahara. [britannica-sanusi, wikipedia-sanusiyya]

## Italian Colonisation and the Resistance — 1911–1943

Italy seized Libya from the Ottomans in 1911 and imposed a savage 'pacification', met by Omar al-Mukhtar's resistance and answered with concentration camps.

- 1911: **Italo-Turkish War and the invasion of Libya** — Italy attacks Tripoli on 3 October 1911 and seizes the Ottoman provinces; the 1912 Treaty of Lausanne cedes the territory to Italy. [wikipedia-italian-colonization, britannica-libya]
- 1920s–1932: **Pacification of Libya** — Italian forces wage a war of 'pacification', crushing resistance and deporting Cyrenaica's population into concentration camps where tens of thousands die. [wikipedia-concentration-camps, wikipedia-italian-colonization]
- 16 September 1931: **Execution of Omar al-Mukhtar** — The resistance leader Omar al-Mukhtar, the 'Lion of the Desert', is captured and publicly hanged at Soluch, breaking organised resistance in Cyrenaica. [wikipedia-omar-mukhtar, wikipedia-soluch]
- 1934: **Creation of Italian Libya** — Governor Italo Balbo merges Tripolitania and Cyrenaica into a single colony, Italian Libya, reviving the classical name. [wikipedia-italian-colonization]
- 1940–1943: **North African desert campaigns** — The Second World War's desert war sweeps across Libya; by 1943 Allied forces expel the Axis, ending Italian rule and beginning Anglo-French administration. [britannica-libya]

Figures:

- Omar al-Mukhtar (1858–1931) — Leader of the Cyrenaican resistance. A Senussi Qur'anic teacher who organised and led the guerrilla resistance against Italian colonisation in Cyrenaica for nearly twenty years. Captured and hanged at Soluch in 1931, the 'Lion of the Desert' became a national symbol of Libyan defiance.

## Independence, Monarchy and the Coming of Oil — 1951–1969

Libya won independence under King Idris as the first state created by the United Nations, then was transformed by the discovery of oil.

- 24 December 1951: **Independence of the United Kingdom of Libya** — Libya is proclaimed an independent federal monarchy under King Idris—the first state created through the United Nations. [historytoday-kingdom, state-libya-bgn]
- 1959: **Commercial oil discovered** — The discovery of oil in commercial quantities at the Zelten field transforms Libya's prospects; exports begin in 1961. [britannica-libya-oil, wikipedia-zelten]
- 1963: **Abolition of the federal system** — A constitutional reform replaces Libya's three-province federation with a unitary kingdom. [state-libya-bgn]

Figures:

- King Idris I (1889/90–1983) — First and only king of Libya. Head of the Senussi order and leader of the Cyrenaican resistance, Sayyid Muhammad Idris al-Senussi became emir and then, on independence in 1951, king of Libya. Conservative and Western-aligned, he was deposed by Gaddafi's coup in 1969 while abroad and died in exile in Egypt.

## The Gaddafi Era, Uprising and Division — 1969–present

A 1969 coup brought Muammar Gaddafi to power for 42 years; his fall in the 2011 uprising opened a decade of fracture and civil war.

- 1 September 1969: **Gaddafi's coup overthrows the monarchy** — The Free Officers under Muammar Gaddafi depose King Idris in a near-bloodless coup and proclaim a republic, beginning 42 years of Gaddafi's rule. [wikipedia-1969-revolution, history-qaddafi-coup]
- 1977: **Proclamation of the Jamahiriya** — Gaddafi declares the 'state of the masses' (Jamahiriya), his idiosyncratic political system set out in the Green Book. [wikipedia-green-book, britannica-libya]
- 15 April 1986: **US bombing of Tripoli and Benghazi** — In Operation El Dorado Canyon, US aircraft strike Libyan targets in retaliation for a Berlin disco bombing, deepening Libya's confrontation with the West. [wikipedia-1986-bombing, history-1986-bombing]
- February–October 2011: **Uprising, NATO intervention and Gaddafi's fall** — An uprising from February 2011 and a NATO air campaign from 19 March topple Gaddafi, who is captured and killed at Sirte on 20 October 2011. [britannica-2011-revolt, wikipedia-battle-sirte]
- 2014 onward: **Division and civil war** — Disputed elections split Libya between rival governments in Tripoli and the east; Haftar's 2019–2020 assault on Tripoli fails, and a 2021 unity government cannot reunite the country. [wikipedia-second-civil-war]

Figures:

- Muammar Gaddafi (1942–2011) — Ruler of Libya, 1969–2011. Leader of the 1969 coup, he ruled Libya for 42 years through a personal system he called the Jamahiriya. Confrontational abroad and repressive at home, he was overthrown and killed in the 2011 uprising amid NATO intervention.
