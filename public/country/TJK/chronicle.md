# The Chronicle of Tajikistan: Heir to Sogdiana and the Samanid golden age, reborn as a Persian-speaking republic in the mountains.

Founding: Declaration of state independence of the Republic of Tajikistan — 9 September 1991. As the Soviet Union disintegrated, the Supreme Soviet of the Tajik SSR adopted a declaration of state independence on 9 September 1991, a date now marked as Tajikistan's national day. Independence came reluctantly to a republic long dependent on Moscow, and within months the new state slid into a civil war between the post-communist government and an alliance of Islamic and democratic opposition forces. The conflict, among the bloodiest of the post-Soviet space, was ended only by a UN-brokered General Agreement signed in Moscow on 27 June 1997.

Tajikistan is the Persian-speaking heart of Central Asia, heir to the ancient civilisations of Sogdiana and Bactria whose merchants carried goods and faiths along the Silk Road. Tajiks trace their golden age to the Samanid Empire of the 9th and 10th centuries, centred on Bukhara, which nurtured the rebirth of Persian literature in the verse of Rudaki and made Ismail Samani a national symbol. After centuries of Turkic, Mongol and Bukharan rule, the region was absorbed by the Russian Empire and reorganised by the Soviet Union, which created the Tajik republic in the 1920s while leaving the historic cities of Bukhara and Samarkand in Uzbekistan. Independence on 9 September 1991 was followed by a devastating civil war (1992–1997) ended by a UN-brokered peace, and the country has since been governed by Emomali Rahmon over a mountainous, remittance-dependent economy.

## Sogdiana, Bactria and the Silk Road — c. 3500 BCE – 651 CE

Long before the Tajik nation, the river valleys of the Zeravshan and the Oxus gave rise to Sogdiana and Bactria, whose merchants made the Silk Road speak Sogdian.

- c. 3500–2000 BCE: **The proto-urban settlement of Sarazm** — In the Zeravshan valley near Penjikent, Sarazm grows into one of the oldest proto-urban settlements in Central Asia, a centre of metallurgy and long-distance trade, later inscribed as Tajikistan's first UNESCO World Heritage Site (2010). [unesco-sarazm, loc-centralasia]
- c. 329 BCE: **Alexander the Great in Sogdiana and Bactria** — After overthrowing the Achaemenid Persians, Alexander the Great campaigns through Sogdiana and Bactria, the Iranian lands of Transoxania that later peoples would regard as the Tajik homeland. [britannica-sogdiana, britannica-history-tajikistan]
- 5th–8th centuries CE: **Sogdian merchants dominate the Silk Road** — Sogdian traders from Samarkand and Penjikent become the principal caravan merchants of the Silk Road, and the Sogdian language serves as the commercial lingua franca of Central Asia. [britannica-sogdiana, loc-centralasia]

## The Samanid Golden Age — 651 – 999 CE

Under the Samanids of Bukhara, conquered Transoxania became the cradle of New Persian literature — the period Tajiks honour as their golden age.

- 819 CE: **The Samanid Empire centred on Bukhara** — The Persian Samanid dynasty rules eastern Iran and Central Asia from Bukhara, making the city a focal point of Islamic civilisation and a hub of Persianate culture. [britannica-samanid, britannica-sogdiana]
- 892–907 CE: **The reign of Ismail Samani** — Ismail I ibn Ahmad unifies the Samanid realm and rules from Bukhara as one of the dynasty's most famous sovereigns; he is revered today as a founding symbol of the Tajik nation. [britannica-ismail, loc-centralasia]
- 10th century CE: **Rudaki and the rebirth of Persian poetry** — At the Samanid court in Bukhara, the poet Rudaki composes in New Persian and is honoured as the father of Persian poetry, anchoring the literary heritage Tajiks claim as their own. [britannica-rudaki, britannica-samanid]
- 999 CE: **Karakhanid conquest of Bukhara** — The Turkic Karakhanids capture Bukhara, ending Samanid rule in Transoxania and opening centuries of Turkic and Mongol domination over the region. [britannica-samanid, britannica-history-tajikistan]

Figures:

- Ismail Samani (Ismail I ibn Ahmad) (reigned 892–907 CE) — Samanid amir, national symbol of Tajikistan. The most renowned ruler of the Samanid dynasty, who unified its lands and made Bukhara a centre of trade and learning. Independent Tajikistan has adopted him as a founding national symbol, naming its currency, highest peak and monuments after him.
- Rudaki (c. 858–941 CE) — Persian poet of the Samanid court. Court poet to the Samanid amir Nasr II at Bukhara and the first major poet of New Persian, widely regarded as the father of Persian poetry. His work is central to the literary heritage that Tajiks regard as their own.

## Turkic, Mongol and Bukharan Rule — 999 – 1868 CE

For more than eight centuries the Persian-speaking population of the mountains lived under successive Turkic and Mongol rulers, latterly within the Emirate of Bukhara.

- 1219–1221 CE: **The Mongol conquest of Transoxania** — Genghis Khan's armies sweep through Central Asia, sacking Bukhara and Samarkand, ushering in centuries of Mongol and Turko-Mongol rule over the Persian-speaking population. [britannica-history-tajikistan, loc-centralasia]
- 1370 CE: **Timur makes Samarkand his capital** — The conqueror Timur (Tamerlane) builds a Timurid empire centred on Samarkand, whose courts become renowned centres of Persianate art, science and architecture. [britannica-history-tajikistan]
- 18th–19th centuries: **Division between Bukhara and Kokand** — The territory of present-day Tajikistan is split mainly between the Emirate of Bukhara, which holds the south, and the Khanate of Kokand, which controls the northern Ferghana fringe. [loc-centralasia, britannica-history-tajikistan]

## Russian Conquest and the Soviet Republic — 1868 – 1991 CE

Russian conquest and then Soviet nation-building reshaped the region, creating a Tajik republic while leaving Bukhara and Samarkand in Uzbekistan.

- 1868 CE: **Bukhara becomes a Russian protectorate** — Defeated by Russian forces, the Emir of Bukhara accepts Russian terms in 1868; the emirate becomes a protectorate and the northern Tajik lands are absorbed into Russian Turkestan. [britannica-history-tajikistan, loc-centralasia]
- 1924 CE: **Creation of the Tajik ASSR** — In the Soviet national delimitation of Central Asia, a Tajik Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic is established within the Uzbek SSR, with Dushanbe as its capital. [loc-centralasia, britannica-history-tajikistan]
- December 1929: **The Tajik SSR and the loss of Bukhara and Samarkand** — The Tajik ASSR is detached from Uzbekistan and elevated to a full union republic, but the historic Persian-speaking cities of Bukhara and Samarkand are left within the Uzbek SSR. [loc-centralasia, britannica-history-tajikistan]
- 9 September 1991: **Declaration of independence** — As the Soviet Union disintegrates, the Supreme Soviet of the Tajik SSR declares the state independence of the Republic of Tajikistan — the country's founding moment and national day. [britannica-independence, britannica-history-tajikistan]

## Independence and Civil War — 1991 – 1997 CE

Independence brought not freedom but a brutal civil war between the post-communist government and an Islamic-democratic opposition, ended only by a UN-brokered peace.

- 1992–1997: **The Tajik civil war** — Civil war erupts between the post-communist government and an Islamic-democratic opposition alliance; it causes massive displacement and a death toll that remains contested and is best treated as an estimate. [britannica-independence, loc-centralasia]
- 1994 CE: **Emomali Rahmon elected president; UNMOT established** — Emomali Rahmon, leader of the government side since 1992, is elected president; the UN establishes the Mission of Observers in Tajikistan (UNMOT) to monitor the ceasefire. [britannica-rahmon, un-unmot]
- 27 June 1997: **UN-brokered General Peace Agreement** — President Rahmon and United Tajik Opposition leader Said Abdullo Nuri sign the General Agreement on the Establishment of Peace and National Accord in Moscow, ending the civil war under UN auspices. [un-unmot, britannica-independence]

Figures:

- Said Abdullo Nuri (1947–2006) — Leader of the United Tajik Opposition. Islamic scholar and politician who led the United Tajik Opposition during the civil war and signed the 1997 peace agreement with the government, later chairing the national reconciliation commission that implemented it.

## The Rahmon Era and the Mountain Republic — 1997 – present

Peace restored, Tajikistan has been shaped by the long rule of Emomali Rahmon, the grandeur of the Pamirs, and an economy leaning heavily on remittances.

- 2013 CE: **Tajik National Park inscribed by UNESCO** — The Tajik National Park (Mountains of the Pamirs), covering roughly 18 per cent of the country, becomes Tajikistan's first natural World Heritage Site, protecting the 'Roof of the World'. [unesco-tajiknationalpark]
- 2015 CE: **Rahmon named 'Leader of the Nation'** — Parliament grants Emomali Rahmon the title 'Founder of Peace and National Unity — Leader of the Nation', extending his authority as his long rule continues. [mfa-leader, britannica-rahmon]
- 2020s: **A remittance-dependent economy** — Remittances from Tajik labour migrants, mostly working in Russia, reach close to half of GDP — among the highest shares in the world — leaving the economy heavily exposed to external shocks. [worldbank-tajikistan, iom-labourmigration]

Figures:

- Emomali Rahmon (born 1952) — President of Tajikistan. Leader of Tajikistan since 1992, first as chairman of the Supreme Soviet and, from 1994, as president. He oversaw the end of the civil war and the 1997 peace, and has since consolidated power, being styled 'Leader of the Nation' in 2015.
