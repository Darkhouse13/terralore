Founding: Independence from Britain — 15 Aug 1947. India gained independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, ending nearly two centuries of East India Company and Crown dominance. It became a sovereign democratic republic when its constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, now celebrated as Republic Day. Yet India's civilisational roots run far deeper, to the urban Indus Valley Civilisation that flourished from around 2600 BCE.

India's recorded story stretches back more than four and a half thousand years to the Bronze Age cities of the Indus Valley, making it one of the oldest continuous civilisations on Earth. Across the millennia it gave birth to four world religions, the Sanskrit classics, the decimal numeral system and a succession of empires from the Maurya to the Gupta and the Mughals. After roughly two centuries of British commercial and then Crown rule, India won its independence on 15 August 1947 amid the trauma of Partition, and on 26 January 1950 adopted a constitution that established it as a sovereign democratic republic.

## The Indus Valley Civilisation — c. 2600 – c. 1900 BCE (roots to c. 3300 BCE)

Along the Indus and its tributaries arose one of the world's earliest urban civilisations, with planned cities, standardised weights and a script still undeciphered.

- c. 2600 BCE: **Mature urban phase of the Indus cities** — Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and hundreds of other settlements reach their peak, with grid-planned streets, advanced drainage and standardised weights across a vast trading network. [worldhistory-indus, wikipedia-indus]
- c. 2600 BCE: **Mohenjo-daro flourishes** — Mohenjo-daro, on the Indus in modern Pakistan, becomes one of the civilisation's largest cities and is today a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its Great Bath and citadel. [unesco-mohenjodaro, worldhistory-indus]
- c. 1900 BCE: **Decline of the Harappan cities** — The major cities are gradually abandoned as weakening monsoons and shifting rivers undermine the agricultural base, marking the end of the civilisation's urban phase. [worldhistory-indus, wikipedia-indus]

## The Vedic Age and the Great Religions — c. 1500 – c. 500 BCE

Out of the post-Harappan world came the Vedas, the Sanskrit language and the spiritual ferment that produced Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism.

- c. 1500 BCE: **Composition of the Rigveda** — The Rigveda, the oldest Vedic text and a foundational scripture of Hinduism, is composed in early Sanskrit and preserved through oral tradition. [wikipedia-rigveda, ebsco-vedic]
- c. 500 BCE: **Rise of the Buddha and Mahavira** — Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) founds Buddhism and Mahavira reforms Jainism, launching two enduring religions amid a wider questioning of Vedic ritual. [britannica-mahavira]

Figures:

- Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) (traditionally c. 563–483 BCE) — Founder of Buddhism. A prince of the Shakya clan who renounced worldly life and, after attaining enlightenment, taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. His teachings spread from India across Asia to become one of the world's major religions.

## Classical Empires: Maurya and the Gupta Golden Age — c. 322 BCE – c. 550 CE

India's first great pan-Indian empire under the Mauryas gave way, centuries later, to the Gupta era, remembered as a classical golden age of art, science and Sanskrit literature.

- c. 322 BCE: **Chandragupta Maurya founds the Maurya Empire** — Chandragupta Maurya overthrows the Nanda dynasty and builds the first empire to unify most of the subcontinent, with its capital at Pataliputra. [britannica-mauryan, wikipedia-chandragupta]
- c. 261 BCE: **Ashoka's conquest of Kalinga and conversion** — After the devastating Kalinga war, Emperor Ashoka embraces Buddhism, renounces conquest, and spreads an ethic of dharma through inscribed edicts across his vast empire. [britannica-mauryan]
- c. 320 CE: **Gupta golden age** — The Gupta Empire presides over a classical flowering of Sanskrit literature, mathematics and astronomy, including the works of Kalidasa and Aryabhata and the maturing of the decimal system. [wikipedia-gupta, britannica-gupta]

Figures:

- Ashoka (reigned c. 268–232 BCE) — Maurya emperor. The greatest Maurya ruler, who after the carnage of the Kalinga war turned to Buddhism and non-violence, promoting moral governance through rock and pillar edicts. His lion capital is today the national emblem of India.
- Aryabhata (476 – c. 550 CE) — Mathematician and astronomer. A Gupta-era scholar who worked with positional notation, accurately approximated pi, and proposed that the Earth rotates on its axis, embodying the scientific achievements of classical India.

## The Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire — 1206 – 1707 (Mughal decline to 1857)

From 1206 a series of Muslim dynasties ruled from Delhi, culminating in the Mughal Empire, which fused Persian, Islamic and Indian cultures into one of the wealthiest realms of its age.

- 1206: **Founding of the Delhi Sultanate** — Qutb al-Din Aibak establishes independent rule at Delhi, beginning a 320-year succession of Islamic dynasties that reshape the politics and culture of northern India. [wikipedia-qutb, wikipedia-delhisultanate]
- 1526: **Babur founds the Mughal Empire** — Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat, ending the Delhi Sultanate and establishing the Mughal Empire, which would rule much of the subcontinent for over two centuries. [wikipedia-delhisultanate, britannica-mughal]
- 1556: **Reign of Akbar** — Akbar the Great consolidates and expands the empire, reforms its administration with the mansabdari system, and pursues a policy of religious tolerance and cultural synthesis. [britannica-mughal]
- 1707: **Death of Aurangzeb and Mughal decline** — Aurangzeb dies after extending the empire to its greatest extent; his costly wars and religious policies leave the Mughal state weakened, and its authority rapidly fragments thereafter. [britannica-mughal]

Figures:

- Akbar (1542–1605) — Mughal emperor. The third and arguably greatest Mughal emperor, who consolidated the empire across northern and central India, built an efficient administration, and promoted dialogue among India's religions.

## The East India Company and the British Raj — 1757 – 1947

A trading company became the master of a subcontinent, and after the rebellion of 1857 the British Crown ruled India directly until the rising tide of nationalism forced its withdrawal.

- 23 Jun 1757: **Battle of Plassey** — Robert Clive's East India Company forces defeat the Nawab of Bengal, marking the beginning of British political and military dominance in India. [britannica-plassey, britannica-raj]
- 1858: **British Crown rule begins** — After the suppression of the 1857 rebellion, the Crown abolishes East India Company rule and assumes direct governance of India, beginning the British Raj. [britannica-raj]
- 12 Mar 1930: **The Salt March** — Gandhi leads a 24-day march to the sea to make salt in defiance of the British salt monopoly, galvanising mass non-violent resistance against colonial rule. [britannica-saltmarch]
- 1943: **Bengal famine** — An estimated two to three million people die in Bengal in a man-made famine driven by wartime inflation, export priorities and colonial policy rather than any major shortfall in food production. [britannica-bengalfamine, wikipedia-bengalfamine]

Figures:

- Mohandas (Mahatma) Gandhi (1869–1948) — Leader of the independence movement. The pre-eminent leader of Indian nationalism, who developed satyagraha — non-violent civil resistance — and led mass campaigns such as the Salt March and Quit India that mobilised the country against British rule.

## Independence, Partition and the Modern Republic — 1947 – present

Freedom in 1947 came with the agony of Partition; three years later India became a republic, and has since grown into the world's most populous democracy.

- 15 Aug 1947: **Independence and Partition** — India becomes independent from Britain under Prime Minister Nehru as the subcontinent is partitioned into India and Pakistan, unleashing mass migration and communal violence. [wikipedia-partition, britannica-republicday]
- 1947–1948: **Partition violence and mass migration** — Up to 12–20 million people are uprooted and an estimated one to two million die in massacres and forced migration along the new India–Pakistan border. [wikipedia-partition]
- 26 Jan 1950: **Constitution and the Republic** — The Constitution of India comes into force, making India a sovereign democratic republic with universal suffrage and fundamental rights; the day is celebrated as Republic Day. [britannica-republicday, knowindia-republic]

Figures:

- Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964) — First Prime Minister of India. A leading figure of the independence movement and India's first prime minister, who shaped the new republic's democratic institutions, secular constitution and non-aligned foreign policy.
- B. R. Ambedkar (1891–1956) — Chief architect of the Constitution. A jurist, economist and social reformer who chaired the drafting committee of the Constitution and championed the rights of the Dalits and other marginalised communities.
