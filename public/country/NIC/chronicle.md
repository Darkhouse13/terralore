# The Chronicle of Nicaragua: From the Nicarao and the colonial rival cities of León and Granada to a republic shaped by intervention, revolution, and contested power.

Founding: Independence from Spain — 15 Sept 1821. On 15 September 1821 the provinces of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, Nicaragua among them, proclaimed their independence from Spain in the Act of Independence of Central America, signed at Guatemala City without bloodshed. Nicaragua was briefly annexed to the Mexican empire of Agustín de Iturbide, then joined the Federal Republic of Central America in 1823. The federation fractured amid regional rivalry, and Nicaragua withdrew to become a fully separate sovereign state in 1838.

Nicaragua takes its name from the Nicarao, one of the indigenous peoples the Spanish encountered on its Pacific lowlands alongside the Chorotega. From 1524 the conquistador Francisco Hernández de Córdoba founded León and Granada, the rival cities whose Liberal and Conservative elites would dominate national politics for generations, while the Caribbean Mosquito Coast developed separately under British influence. Independence from Spain came on 15 September 1821 and, after the short-lived Federal Republic of Central America, Nicaragua endured the filibuster William Walker, repeated United States intervention, Augusto César Sandino's resistance, and the long Somoza family dictatorship. The Sandinista Revolution of 1979 and the Contra war of the 1980s defined the modern era, followed by a democratic interlude after 1990 and the return and consolidation of power under Daniel Ortega to the present.

## Indigenous Peoples of the Isthmus — c. 800 – 1522

Before the Spanish arrival, the Pacific lowlands were home to the Nicarao and Chorotega, peoples linked by migration and trade to the wider civilisations of Mesoamerica and lower Central America.

- c. 800 CE: **Nicarao and Chorotega settle the Pacific lowlands** — Nahua-speaking Nicarao and Oto-Manguean Chorotega establish chiefdoms in western Nicaragua, farming the volcanic lowlands around Lakes Managua and Nicaragua. [britannica-nicarao, britannica-nicaragua]
- 1522 CE: **First Spanish contact** — The expedition of Gil González Dávila reaches the Pacific coast and encounters the Nicarao, opening the way to Spanish conquest. [britannica-history-nicaragua, britannica-nicaragua]

## Conquest, León, and the Mosquito Coast — 1524 – 1821

Spain founded the rival cities of León and Granada within the Captaincy General of Guatemala, while the Caribbean Mosquito Coast developed apart under British influence — a colonial division that would mark Nicaragua for centuries.

- 1524 CE: **Founding of León and Granada** — Francisco Hernández de Córdoba founds León and Granada, the rival cities whose Liberal and Conservative elites would shape Nicaraguan politics for generations. [unesco-leon-viejo, britannica-leon, britannica-history-nicaragua]
- 1578 & 1610: **León Viejo abandoned** — The original León, struck by the Momotombo eruption of 1578 and the earthquake of 1610, is abandoned; its buried ruins later become Nicaragua's first UNESCO World Heritage Site. [unesco-leon-viejo]
- 17th c.: **British protectorate over the Mosquito Coast** — Great Britain allies with the Miskito kings and treats the Caribbean Mosquito Coast as a protectorate, leaving the region distinct from Hispanic Pacific Nicaragua. [britannica-mosquito-coast]

Figures:

- Francisco Hernández de Córdoba (c. 1475–1526) — Spanish conquistador and founder. Conquistador who in 1524 founded León and Granada, the two cities at the heart of colonial and republican Nicaragua. The national currency, the córdoba, bears his name.

## Independence, Rivalry, and the Filibuster — 1821 – 1893

Independence in 1821 gave way to the bitter León–Granada feud, the chosen compromise capital of Managua, and the extraordinary episode of the American filibuster William Walker, who briefly made himself president of Nicaragua.

- 15 Sept 1821: **Independence from Spain** — Nicaragua, with the other provinces of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, declares independence from Spain in the Act of Independence of Central America. [britannica-history-nicaragua, britannica-nicaragua]
- 1838 CE: **Separation from the Central American federation** — Amid regional rivalry, Nicaragua withdraws from the Federal Republic of Central America to become a fully sovereign state. [britannica-history-nicaragua]
- 1855–1857: **William Walker's filibuster** — Hired by León's Liberals, the American filibuster William Walker seizes Granada and makes himself president of Nicaragua in 1856 before a coalition of Central American states expels him. [britannica-william-walker, history-state-filibuster]
- 1860 CE: **Treaty of Managua** — Britain cedes its claims on the Caribbean coast to Nicaraguan sovereignty, creating a self-governing Mosquito Reserve. [britannica-mosquito-coast]

Figures:

- William Walker (1824–1860) — American filibuster, self-proclaimed president. Tennessee adventurer who led a mercenary expedition into Nicaragua's civil war, seized Granada, and ruled as president in 1856–57. Driven out by a Central American coalition, he was executed in Honduras in 1860.

## Intervention, Sandino, and the Somoza Dynasty — 1893 – 1979

Decades of United States intervention and Marine occupation provoked the nationalist resistance of Augusto César Sandino — and, after his murder, the rise of the Somoza family, whose dictatorship endured for more than four decades.

- 1894 CE: **Incorporation of the Mosquito Coast** — President José Santos Zelaya militarily incorporates the British-influenced Caribbean coast into the Nicaraguan state. [britannica-mosquito-coast, britannica-history-nicaragua]
- 1912–1933: **United States Marine occupation** — US Marines are stationed in Nicaragua to support Conservative governments and oversee its finances, with only a brief interruption, until 1933. [britannica-history-nicaragua, history-state-filibuster]
- 1927–1933: **Sandino's resistance** — Augusto César Sandino wages guerrilla war against the Marines and National Guard, becoming a hemispheric symbol of resistance to US intervention. [britannica-sandino]
- 1934 CE: **Assassination of Sandino** — Sandino is lured to Managua and murdered by National Guard officers under Anastasio Somoza García, opening the way to the Somoza dictatorship. [britannica-sandino, britannica-somoza-family]
- 1936 CE: **Rise of the Somoza dynasty** — Anastasio Somoza García seizes the presidency, founding a family dictatorship that, directly or through proxies, rules Nicaragua until 1979. [britannica-somoza-family, britannica-somoza-garcia]

Figures:

- Augusto César Sandino (1893–1934) — Nationalist guerrilla leader. Led an armed resistance against US Marines and the National Guard from 1927. Assassinated in 1934 on the orders of Somoza García, he became the namesake and inspiration of the later Sandinista movement.
- Anastasio Somoza García (1896–1956) — Dictator, founder of the dynasty. National Guard commander who seized power in 1936 and ruled Nicaragua directly or through chosen presidents until his assassination in 1956, founding a dynasty that lasted until 1979.

## Revolution and the Contra War — 1979 – 1990

The Sandinista Revolution of 1979 toppled the Somoza dictatorship and set out to remake Nicaraguan society; the United States–backed Contra war that followed turned the country into a Cold War battleground until the election of 1990.

- 19 July 1979: **Sandinista Revolution** — The FSLN overthrows Anastasio Somoza Debayle, ending the dynasty; the insurrection is estimated to have cost about 50,000 lives. [britannica-sandinista, britannica-sandinista-government, britannica-somoza-debayle]
- early 1980s: **The Contra war** — The United States finances and arms the Contra insurgency against the Sandinista government; the war kills an estimated tens of thousands and devastates the economy. Casualty figures vary and are estimates. [britannica-contra, britannica-sandinista-government]
- Aug 1987: **Esquipulas II peace accord** — Central American presidents sign a regional peace plan committing to democratisation and an end to support for insurgencies. [britannica-sandinista-government]
- 25 Feb 1990: **Chamorro defeats Ortega** — Violeta Barrios de Chamorro wins the presidency for an opposition coalition; Ortega cedes power in a peaceful transfer that ends the war. [britannica-ortega, britannica-sandinista-government]

Figures:

- Daniel Ortega (born 1945) — Sandinista leader and president. Member of the FSLN junta that took power in 1979 and elected president in 1984. Defeated in 1990, he returned to office in 2007 and consolidated control over the state in the years that followed.
- Violeta Barrios de Chamorro (1929–2025) — President of Nicaragua. Newspaper publisher's widow and opposition candidate whose 1990 victory ended Sandinista rule and the Contra war, making her the first woman elected president in the Americas.

## Democratic Interlude and Ortega's Return — 1990 – present

After a decade and a half of multiparty government, Daniel Ortega returned to the presidency in 2007 and steadily consolidated power, a course that culminated in the deadly crisis of 2018 and a tightly controlled state.

- Jan 2007: **Ortega returns to power** — Having won the 2006 election, Daniel Ortega is inaugurated president again, beginning a long second period in office. [britannica-ortega, britannica-ortega-return]
- 2009–2014: **Re-election limits removed** — A 2009 court ruling and 2014 constitutional reform abolish term limits, enabling Ortega's continued re-election and the consolidation of FSLN control. [britannica-ortega-return]
- Apr 2018: **The 2018 crisis** — Protests against social-security cuts become a mass anti-government movement met with deadly force; the UN and IACHR document a death toll estimated upward of 300 (figures are estimates). [britannica-ortega-return, ohchr-nicaragua-2018, iachr-nicaragua-2018]
- Nov 2021: **Contested re-election** — Ortega wins a further term after leading opposition candidates are detained, in an election widely criticised internationally. [britannica-ortega, britannica-ortega-return]

Figures:

- Rosario Murillo (born 1951) — Vice-president of Nicaragua. Wife of Daniel Ortega and a dominant figure in his government, she became vice-president in 2017 and a central architect of the post-2018 consolidation of power.
