Founding: Independence from Spain — 15 Sept 1821. On 15 September 1821 a council of notables in Guatemala City signed the Act of Independence of Central America, severing the Captaincy General of Guatemala from Spanish rule after nearly three centuries. The former kingdom was briefly annexed to the Mexican empire of Agustin de Iturbide before joining the United Provinces of Central America, which in 1824 adopted the constitution of the Federal Republic of Central America, a federation of Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica. The federation dissolved amid civil war by 1840, leaving Guatemala a sovereign republic.

Guatemala is the heartland of Maya civilisation, home to the great Classic city of Tikal and to the K'iche' Maya whose sacred Popol Vuh survives as a masterwork of Mesoamerican literature. The Spanish conquest under Pedro de Alvarado in 1524 opened three centuries of colonial rule from Santiago de los Caballeros, today's Antigua Guatemala, until independence from Spain on 15 September 1821. A coffee economy and a succession of Liberal dictatorships gave way to the democratic October Revolution of 1944, ended by a CIA-backed coup in 1954; there followed a thirty-six-year civil war in which, by the United Nations-backed truth commission's estimate, some 200,000 people were killed or disappeared, the great majority of them Maya. Peace accords in 1996 closed the conflict, and Guatemala remains a young democracy contending with the legacy of war, inequality, and corruption.

## The Maya Heartland — c. 900 BCE - 900 CE

Across the lowland forests of the Peten, the Maya built one of the great civilisations of the ancient world, whose largest city, Tikal, rose to dominance before the Classic collapse.

- c. 900-300 BCE: **Tikal settled in the Peten** — A village forms at Tikal in the Middle Formative period and grows into a ceremonial centre of the lowland Maya. [britannica-tikal, unesco-tikal]
- c. 600-800 CE: **Tikal at its height** — Tikal reaches its architectural and artistic peak as one of the dominant city-states of the Classic Maya world. [unesco-tikal, britannica-tikal]
- c. 900 CE: **Classic Maya collapse** — Tikal and the southern lowland centres are depopulated and abandoned as the Classic Maya order disintegrates. [britannica-maya, unesco-tikal]

## Postclassic Kingdoms and Spanish Conquest — c. 900 - 1543

In the highlands the K'iche' and other Maya peoples built new kingdoms and preserved the Popol Vuh, until Pedro de Alvarado's invasion of 1524 brought them under Spanish rule.

- 1524 CE: **Conquest under Pedro de Alvarado** — Alvarado invades from Mexico, defeats the K'iche', and burns their capital Q'umarkaj, beginning Spanish rule in Guatemala. [britannica-alvarado, britannica-guatemala-history]
- 1543 CE: **Santiago de los Caballeros re-founded** — After a flood destroys the earlier capital, the colonial seat of the Captaincy General is established at present-day Antigua Guatemala. [britannica-antigua, unesco-antigua]
- c. 1554-1558: **The Popol Vuh set down** — The K'iche' creation epic, the Popol Vuh, is written in alphabetic K'iche', preserving Maya mythology and dynastic history. [britannica-popol-vuh]

Figures:

- Pedro de Alvarado (c. 1485-1541) — Spanish conquistador. Lieutenant of Cortes who led the conquest of Guatemala in 1524, defeating the K'iche' and Kaqchikel and founding the first Spanish capital as captain general.

## Colony, Independence, and Federation — 1543 - 1871

For nearly three centuries Guatemala was the seat of Spanish power in Central America, until independence in 1821 led through a short-lived federation to a conservative republic.

- 1773 CE: **Santa Marta earthquakes ruin Antigua** — Powerful earthquakes devastate the colonial capital, leading to the transfer of government to present-day Guatemala City. [britannica-antigua, unesco-antigua]
- 15 Sept 1821: **Independence from Spain** — Guatemala City signs the Act of Independence of Central America, ending nearly three centuries of Spanish rule. [britannica-central-america, loc-guatemala]
- 1823-1824: **Federal Republic of Central America** — The former captaincy general forms a federation of five states, with Guatemala City as its capital. [britannica-central-america]
- 1847 CE: **Republic of Guatemala proclaimed** — With the federation dissolved, Guatemala declares itself a sovereign republic under Conservative rule. [britannica-guatemala-history, britannica-central-america]

## Coffee and the Liberal Dictatorships — 1871 - 1944

The Liberal revolution of 1871 remade Guatemala around coffee exports, foreign capital, and a succession of strongmen, deepening the inequalities that would later erupt in revolution.

- 1871 CE: **Liberal revolution** — Justo Rufino Barrios and the Liberals seize power, launching coffee-based modernisation and the expropriation of communal and Maya lands. [britannica-guatemala-history]
- from 1870s: **The coffee economy** — Coffee becomes Guatemala's dominant export, financing railways and ports while binding the Maya to plantation labour. [britannica-guatemala-history]
- early 20th c.: **Rise of the United Fruit Company** — United Fruit acquires extensive lands, railways, and the main port, becoming Guatemala's largest landowner and a dominant economic force. [britannica-guatemala-history, history-state-guatemala]
- 1931-1944: **Dictatorship of Jorge Ubico** — General Ubico rules for thirteen years, repressing labour and opposition while sustaining the coffee oligarchy and ties to United Fruit. [britannica-guatemala-history]

Figures:

- Justo Rufino Barrios (1835-1885) — Liberal president and reformer. Architect of the 1871 Liberal revolution who modernised Guatemala around coffee exports, curtailed the Church, and expropriated communal lands, entrenching a plantation economy and an authoritarian state.

## Revolution, Coup, and Civil War — 1944 - 1996

A decade of democratic reform was cut short by a CIA-backed coup in 1954, opening a thirty-six-year civil war marked by a campaign of genocide against Maya communities.

- 1944 CE: **October Revolution** — A popular uprising ends the Ubico dictatorship and ushers in democratic reform under Juan Jose Arevalo. [britannica-guatemala-history, history-state-guatemala]
- 1952 CE: **Agrarian reform under Arbenz** — President Arbenz's Decree 900 expropriates idle lands, including United Fruit estates, for the landless, alarming Washington. [history-state-guatemala, britannica-guatemala-history]
- Jun 1954: **CIA-backed coup overthrows Arbenz** — The covert operation PBSUCCESS pressures Arbenz to resign on 27 June 1954, ending the reform era and installing military rule. [history-state-guatemala, britannica-guatemala-history]
- 1960-1996: **Civil war and the Maya genocide** — A thirty-six-year conflict leaves some 200,000 dead or disappeared, mostly Maya, with the state held by the CEH to have committed genocide. [unesco-peace-accords, britannica-rios-montt, britannica-guatemala-history]
- 29 Dec 1996: **Firm and Lasting Peace accords** — The government and the URNG sign the final peace agreement in Guatemala City, ending the civil war. [unesco-peace-accords]

Figures:

- Jacobo Arbenz (1913-1971) — President of Guatemala. Reformist army officer elected in 1950 whose agrarian reform, redistributing idle land including United Fruit holdings, led to his overthrow in the 1954 CIA-backed coup.
- Efrain Rios Montt (1926-2018) — Military dictator. General who seized power in 1982 and ruled until 1983 during one of the bloodiest phases of the civil war. In 2013 a Guatemalan court convicted him of genocide against the Ixil Maya, though the verdict was later annulled.

## Guatemala Since the Peace — 1996 - present

Since the peace accords Guatemala has been a fragile democracy, struggling with the legacy of war, indigenous rights, corruption, and migration.

- 1999 CE: **Memory of Silence report** — The UN-backed Commission for Historical Clarification publishes its findings, attributing the bulk of wartime atrocities to the state and finding acts of genocide against the Maya. [unesco-peace-accords]
- 2013 CE: **Rios Montt convicted of genocide** — A Guatemalan court convicts the former dictator of genocide against the Ixil Maya, the first such verdict by a national court, though it is soon annulled. [britannica-rios-montt]
- 2023-2024: **Election of Bernardo Arevalo** — Anti-corruption candidate Bernardo Arevalo wins the presidency and, despite attempts to block him, is inaugurated in January 2024. [usip-guatemala-2023]

Figures:

- Rigoberta Menchu (born 1959) — Indigenous-rights activist, Nobel laureate. K'iche' Maya activist whose family was killed during the civil war and who won the 1992 Nobel Peace Prize for her struggle for the rights of indigenous peoples. She later founded Guatemala's first indigenous political party.
