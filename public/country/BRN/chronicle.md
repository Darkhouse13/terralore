# The Chronicle of Brunei: From a maritime empire that once ruled all of Borneo to an oil-rich sultanate, the abode of peace under the world's longest-reigning monarch.

Founding: Resumption of full independence from Britain — 1 January 1984. At midnight on 1 January 1984, Brunei Darussalam resumed full sovereignty after nearly a century as a British protectorate, and Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah proclaimed the national philosophy of Melayu Islam Beraja, the Malay Islamic Monarchy. The moment was a restoration as much as a beginning: Brunei had been an independent sultanate since the 14th century and a maritime power that once dominated Borneo, before the loss of Sarawak, Sabah, and Labuan and the British residency reduced it to a small enclave on the island's north coast. Britain had retained control of Brunei's defence and foreign affairs since the protectorate treaty of 1888; independence in 1984 returned those powers to the sultan, who also became prime minister, and the United States recognised the new state the same day.

Brunei's history runs from an early Borneo trading port that paid tribute to China in the 6th century to one of the wealthiest small states on Earth. Out of that trade rose the Bruneian Sultanate, which embraced Islam in the 14th century and reached a golden age under Sultan Bolkiah around 1500, when its writ extended over the whole island of Borneo, the Sulu Archipelago, and parts of the Philippines. Centuries of decline followed as the sultanate ceded Sarawak to the English adventurer James Brooke, lost Sabah and Labuan, and in 1888 became a British protectorate, transformed only by the discovery of oil at Seria in 1929. After the Japanese occupation, a 1959 constitution, and the suppressed revolt of 1962, Brunei resumed full independence on 1 January 1984 as an absolute monarchy under the Malay Islamic Monarchy ideology and the long reign of Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah.

## Early Borneo: Trade, Tribute, and the Rise of Po-ni — c. 6th century - 14th century CE

Long before the sultanate, a trading port on the north coast of Borneo grew rich on camphor, spices, and forest products, sending tribute to China and bending for a time to the Hindu empire of Majapahit in Java.

- 6th century CE: **Brunei trades with China as Po-ni** — Chinese sources record a trading kingdom on the north coast of Borneo, called Po-ni, exchanging tribute and goods with the Chinese empire from around the 6th century. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-brunei]
- 13th century: **Boni described as a wealthy maritime kingdom** — A Chinese account portrays Boni as a prosperous state with a fleet to protect its trade in camphor, spices, and forest products drawn from the Bornean interior. [britannica-history-brunei]
- late 13th-14th century: **Tributary ties to Majapahit** — Brunei comes under the cultural and political influence of the Hindu Javanese empire of Majapahit, to which it pays tribute, while maintaining its long-standing relationship with China. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-majapahit]

## The Coming of Islam and the Birth of the Sultanate — c. 1363 - 1485 CE

When the ruler of Brunei embraced Islam and took the title Sultan Muhammad Shah, a trading port became an Islamic kingdom, the foundation of a dynasty that still reigns more than six centuries later.

- c. 1363 (traditional): **Conversion to Islam and the first sultan** — Awang Alak Betatar embraces Islam, takes the title Sultan Muhammad Shah, and becomes the first sultan of Brunei, refounding the state as an Islamic sultanate. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-brunei]
- 14th century: **Founding of the ruling dynasty** — The line descended from Muhammad Shah, later the House of Bolkiah, establishes itself as Brunei's royal house, beginning a continuous monarchy that endures to the present. [britannica-history-brunei, un-brunei-facts]

Figures:

- Sultan Muhammad Shah (reigned 14th century, traditionally from c. 1363) — First Sultan of Brunei. Remembered in Bruneian tradition as the chief Awang Alak Betatar who converted to Islam and took the title Sultan Muhammad Shah, founding the Islamic sultanate and the dynasty that still reigns over Brunei today.

## The Golden Age of the Maritime Empire — c. 1485 - 1600 CE

Under Sultan Bolkiah, the singing admiral, Brunei reached its zenith, its power spanning the whole of Borneo, the Sulu Archipelago, and the islands of the Philippines, until internal strife and European arrivals began to erode it.

- c. 1485: **Accession of Sultan Bolkiah** — Bolkiah, the fifth sultan, comes to the throne and presides over Brunei's golden age, expanding the sultanate's dominion across Borneo and the surrounding seas. [britannica-history-brunei]
- c. 1500: **Empire at its zenith** — Under Bolkiah, Brunei's power extends over the whole of Borneo, the Sulu Archipelago, and into the Philippines, with Bruneian influence reaching as far as Manila. [britannica-history-brunei, un-brunei-facts]
- 1521: **Magellan's expedition reaches Brunei** — The ships of Ferdinand Magellan's circumnavigation anchor off Brunei and record a wealthy capital ruled by the great Bolkiah, who controls Borneo and neighbouring islands. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-magellan]
- 1578: **Spanish attack on the capital** — A Spanish expedition from the Philippines briefly captures Brunei's capital during a war with the sultanate before being forced to withdraw, signalling the new European challenge. [britannica-history-brunei]

Figures:

- Sultan Bolkiah (reigned c. 1485-1524) — Fifth Sultan of Brunei, the Singing Admiral. Brunei's most celebrated ruler, a renowned seafarer whose conquests carried the sultanate to its greatest extent over Borneo, Sulu, and the Philippines; his golden-age reign coincided with the first European contact at Magellan's visit in 1521.

## Decline, the White Rajahs, and the British Protectorate — 1600 - 1929 CE

Through three centuries of contraction Brunei lost Sarawak to the adventurer James Brooke, surrendered Labuan and Sabah, and in 1888 placed itself under British protection, shrinking from an empire to an enclave.

- 1841: **Cession of Sarawak to James Brooke** — After helping put down a revolt, the English adventurer James Brooke is made rajah of Sarawak, founding the White Rajah dynasty and beginning the dismemberment of Brunei's territory. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-james-brooke, britannica-sarawak]
- 1846: **Labuan ceded to Britain** — Brunei cedes the island of Labuan in Brunei Bay to Great Britain, which establishes it as a crown colony, further reducing the sultanate's holdings. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-james-brooke]
- 17 September 1888: **Brunei becomes a British protectorate** — Sultan Hashim signs a treaty placing Brunei under British protection, transferring control of its foreign affairs to Britain while preserving the sultanate from absorption. [britannica-history-brunei, un-brunei-facts]
- 1906: **British Residency system imposed** — A supplementary agreement installs a British Resident to advise the sultan on all matters but Malay custom and Islam; in practice the Resident comes to run the administration. [britannica-history-brunei, un-brunei-facts]

Figures:

- James Brooke (1803-1868) — First White Rajah of Sarawak. British adventurer who, after aiding Brunei against rebels, was made rajah of Sarawak in 1841; his dynasty of White Rajahs steadily expanded at Brunei's expense, stripping the sultanate of much of its Bornean domain.
- Sultan Hashim Jalilul Alam Aqamaddin (reigned 1885-1906) — Sultan who secured British protection. Faced with the loss of his territories to Sarawak and British North Borneo, he signed the 1888 protectorate treaty with Britain that preserved Brunei's existence as a state at the cost of its foreign sovereignty.

## Oil, the Japanese Occupation, and the Road to Self-Government — 1929 - 1962 CE

The strike of oil at Seria in 1929 remade Brunei's fortunes; war, occupation, and recovery followed, and a 1959 constitution restored self-rule, only for an armed revolt in 1962 to test the new order.

- 1929: **Oil struck at Seria** — The British Malayan Petroleum Company, a Shell enterprise, makes the first commercial oil discovery at Seria, launching the petroleum industry that would make Brunei rich. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-brunei]
- 16 December 1941: **Japanese occupation begins** — Japanese forces land at Kuala Belait and quickly occupy Brunei, seizing the strategically vital Seria oil fields; the occupation lasts until 1945. [britannica-history-brunei]
- 10 June 1945: **Allied liberation** — Australian troops land in Brunei Bay during the Borneo Campaign, ending the Japanese occupation; the capital and much of the country lie in ruins from the fighting and must be rebuilt after the war. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-bandar-seri-begawan]
- 29 September 1959: **Constitution grants self-government** — A written constitution gives Brunei internal self-government and ends the British Residency, with Britain keeping control of defence and foreign affairs. [britannica-history-brunei, britannica-omar-ali-saifuddin, un-brunei-facts]

Figures:

- Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III (1914-1986; reigned 1950-1967) — Architect of modern Brunei. Used the wealth of Seria's oil to modernise the country and negotiated the 1959 constitution that restored internal self-government; he abdicated in 1967 in favour of his son Hassanal Bolkiah and is remembered as the founder of modern Brunei.

## The 1962 Revolt, Independence, and the Modern Sultanate — 1962 - present

A crushed rebellion, a choice to stay out of Malaysia, and decades of preparation led to full independence in 1984, since when an oil-rich absolute monarchy under Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah has governed by the creed of Malay Islamic Monarchy.

- 8 December 1962: **The Brunei revolt** — The Parti Rakyat Brunei's armed wing launches an uprising against the sultanate and the Malaysia plan; British forces crush it within days, and the party is banned. [britannica-history-brunei]
- 1963: **Brunei stays out of Malaysia** — Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III decides not to join the new Federation of Malaysia, keeping Brunei on a separate course toward eventual independence. [britannica-history-brunei]
- 1967: **Accession of Hassanal Bolkiah** — Omar Ali Saifuddien III abdicates in favour of his son Hassanal Bolkiah, who becomes the 29th sultan and would go on to be the world's longest-reigning living monarch. [britannica-hassanal-bolkiah, britannica-history-brunei, britannica-brunei]
- 1 January 1984: **Full independence and Malay Islamic Monarchy** — Brunei resumes full sovereignty from Britain; the sultan becomes prime minister and proclaims Melayu Islam Beraja, the Malay Islamic Monarchy, as the national philosophy. [history-state-brunei, un-brunei-facts]
- 2014: **Sharia penal code phased in** — Brunei begins implementing a strict Sharia penal code in stages, with later phases drawing international condemnation, while pursuing the Wawasan 2035 plan to diversify beyond oil. [britannica-brunei]

Figures:

- Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah (born 1946; reigned since 1967) — 29th Sultan of Brunei and head of state. Acceding in 1967 and leading Brunei to independence in 1984, he rules as one of the world's last absolute monarchs, combining the offices of sultan and prime minister and presiding over an oil-rich state guided by the Malay Islamic Monarchy ideology.
- A. M. Azahari (1928-2002) — Leader of the Brunei People's Party. Founder of the Parti Rakyat Brunei, whose armed wing launched the failed 1962 revolt against the sultanate and the Malaysia plan in pursuit of a unified North Kalimantan; he spent the rest of his life in exile.
