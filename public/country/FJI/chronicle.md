# The Chronicle of Fiji: Crossroads of the Pacific, from Lapita voyagers to a republic of two peoples

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 10 October 1970. Fiji became an independent sovereign state within the Commonwealth on 10 October 1970, exactly 96 years after the Deed of Cession of 1874. Ratu Sir Kamisese Mara, leader of the Alliance Party, became its first prime minister; Fiji later became a republic in 1987.

Fiji was settled around 1000 BCE by Austronesian Lapita voyagers whose descendants built powerful, sea-going chiefdoms across more than 300 islands. European contact began with Abel Tasman in 1643 and deepened through the sandalwood and bêche-de-mer trades, drawing the warlord-chief Seru Epenisa Cakobau toward Britain, to whom Fiji was ceded on 10 October 1874. To work its sugar plantations the colonial government imported more than 60,000 indentured labourers from India between 1879 and 1916, the girmitiyas, creating a large Indo-Fijian community and an enduring communal divide. Independent since 10 October 1970, Fiji has been shaken by a cycle of coups rooted in indigenous-Fijian and Indo-Fijian tension, returning to elected government under the 2013 constitution and emerging as a leading Pacific voice on climate change.

## Lapita Voyagers & the Sea-Going Chiefdoms — c. 1000 BCE – 1643 CE

Austronesian navigators bearing distinctive pottery reached Fiji around 1000 BCE, founding a society of warring, alliance-building chiefdoms long before Europeans appeared.

- c. 1000 BCE: **Lapita settlement of Fiji** — Austronesian Lapita voyagers, master navigators with distinctive pottery, reach Fiji by around 1000 BCE, founding the islands' first societies. [eb-lapita, eb-fiji-history]
- 1st millennium BCE onward: **Rise of Fijian chiefdoms** — Fijian society develops into a hierarchy of competing chiefdoms bound by lineage, land and warfare, at the crossroads of Melanesia and Polynesia. [eb-fiji-history, statedept-fiji]

## European Contact & the Rise of Cakobau — 1643 – 1874

From Tasman's distant sighting to the sandalwood and bêche-de-mer rushes, European trade armed the chiefs and lifted the warlord of Bau, Seru Epenisa Cakobau, toward a fragile kingdom.

- 1643: **Abel Tasman sights Fiji** — The Dutch navigator Abel Tasman becomes the first European to sight the Fijian islands, without landing among them. [eb-fiji-history]
- 1789: **Bligh charts Fijian waters** — Cast adrift after the Bounty mutiny, Captain William Bligh navigates his open boat between Fiji's main islands, through what is now Bligh Water. [eb-fiji-history]
- early 19th century: **Sandalwood and bêche-de-mer trades** — A sandalwood rush at Bua Bay, then the bêche-de-mer trade with China, draw traders, missionaries and muskets that reshape chiefly power. [eb-fiji-history]
- 1854: **Cakobau converts to Christianity** — Seru Epenisa Cakobau, paramount chief of Bau and self-styled Tui Viti, accepts Christianity, bringing many Fijians under Methodist influence. [eb-cakobau, eb-fiji-history]

Figures:

- Seru Epenisa Cakobau (c. 1815–1883) — Paramount chief of Bau; Tui Viti ('King of Fiji'). Warlord-chief who made Bau the dominant power in Fiji, adopted the title King of Fiji, converted to Christianity in 1854, and ultimately ceded the islands to Britain in 1874.

## Cession, Colony & the Girmitiyas — 1874 – 1916

Britain accepted Fiji by cession in 1874; a measles epidemic devastated the population the next year, and a sugar economy worked by indentured Indians remade the islands' people for good.

- 10 October 1874: **Deed of Cession to Britain** — Cakobau and other chiefs cede Fiji to Queen Victoria at Levuka, which becomes the first colonial capital of the new Crown colony. [eb-fiji-history, unesco-levuka, statedept-fiji]
- 1875: **Measles epidemic devastates Fiji** — A measles epidemic with no prior immunity kills roughly a quarter of the Fijian population, striking the chiefly class especially hard. [eb-fiji-history]
- 14 May 1879: **Arrival of the Leonidas — first girmitiyas** — The first ship of indentured Indian labourers reaches Fiji, beginning a system that brings over 60,000 girmitiyas to the sugar plantations. [eb-fiji-history, usp-sukuna]
- 1882: **Capital moves to Suva** — The colonial capital is relocated from Levuka on Ovalau to Suva on Viti Levu, the larger harbour that remains Fiji's capital. [unesco-levuka]

## Late Colony & the Road to Independence — 1916 – 1970

As the two communities grew side by side, leaders like Ratu Sir Lala Sukuna built indigenous institutions, and a carefully balanced constitution carried a plural colony to independence in 1970.

- 1940: **Native Land Trust Board established** — Ratu Sir Lala Sukuna helps create the board that entrenches indigenous control of most Fijian land, shaping the colony's economy and politics. [usp-sukuna, eb-fiji-history]
- 10 October 1970: **Independence from the United Kingdom** — Fiji becomes independent within the Commonwealth under a power-sharing constitution, with Ratu Sir Kamisese Mara as first prime minister. [eb-mara, fiji-parliament, statedept-fiji]

Figures:

- Ratu Sir Lala Sukuna (1888–1958) — Fijian chief, soldier and colonial statesman. The first iTaukei university graduate and a decorated war veteran, regarded as the forerunner of modern Fijian leadership; architect of the Native Land Trust Board protecting indigenous land.
- Ratu Sir Kamisese Mara (1920–2004) — First Prime Minister of Fiji. Oxford-educated high chief and Alliance Party leader who led Fiji to independence in 1970 and dominated its politics for most of the post-independence era, later serving as president.

## Independence Tested: The Cycle of Coups — 1970 – 2009

Four times in two decades the army or armed men overturned elected governments, as the unresolved tension between indigenous-Fijian political dominance and Indo-Fijian equality erupted into crisis.

- May & September 1987: **Rabuka's coups; Fiji becomes a republic** — Lieutenant Colonel Sitiveni Rabuka leads two military coups to assert indigenous Fijian control, ending the monarchy and declaring a republic. [eb-rabuka, statedept-fiji]
- 1999: **First Indo-Fijian prime minister** — Mahendra Chaudhry, leader of the Fiji Labour Party, becomes the country's first Indo-Fijian prime minister after a landslide election. [eb-chaudhry]
- 19 May 2000: **Speight hostage crisis** — Armed men led by George Speight seize parliament and hold the Chaudhry government hostage for 56 days, triggering a constitutional crisis. [eb-speight, eb-chaudhry]
- 5 December 2006: **Bainimarama's coup** — Commodore Frank Bainimarama overthrows the Qarase government in Fiji's fourth coup, beginning an extended period of military-led rule. [eb-bainimarama, eb-qarase]

Figures:

- Sitiveni Rabuka (born 1948) — Coup leader, later Prime Minister. Army officer who led the 1987 coups in the name of indigenous Fijian paramountcy; later served as elected prime minister in the 1990s and again from 2022.
- Mahendra Chaudhry (born 1942) — First Indo-Fijian Prime Minister. Trade unionist and Fiji Labour Party leader whose 1999 election as Fiji's first Indo-Fijian prime minister was cut short by the 2000 hostage crisis.

## New Constitution, Democracy & the Climate Frontline — 2013 – present

A 2013 constitution that abolished communal voting returned Fiji to elected government, ushered in a peaceful transfer of power in 2022, and made the islands a leading global voice on climate change.

- 2013: **New constitution adopted** — Fiji promulgates a 2013 constitution that abolishes ethnic communal voting in favour of a single common electoral roll. [statedept-fiji]
- 2014: **Return to elected government** — Elections deemed broadly credible by observers restore parliamentary democracy; Frank Bainimarama becomes elected prime minister. [statedept-fiji]
- 2017: **Fiji presides over COP23** — Fiji becomes the first small-island developing state to lead a UN climate conference, promoting the Talanoa Dialogue and the 1.5°C target. [un-cop23, worldbank-fiji, eb-fiji]
- December 2022: **Peaceful change of government** — Bainimarama's party loses its majority; a coalition led by Sitiveni Rabuka takes power through the ballot box, ending 16 years of his rule. [statedept-fiji]

Figures:

- Frank Bainimarama (born 1954) — Military commander and Prime Minister. Naval commodore who led the 2006 coup, governed by decree, then won elected office in 2014 under the new constitution before losing power in 2022.
