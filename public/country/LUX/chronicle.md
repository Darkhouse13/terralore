Founding: Count Siegfried acquires the rock of Lucilinburhuc — 963. Luxembourg traces its origin to the year 963, when Siegfried, a count of the Ardennes, obtained a rocky promontory above the valley of the Alzette on which a small fortified site called Lucilinburhuc, the 'little fortress', already stood. The castle Siegfried built on this Bock promontory is regarded as the birthplace of the city, the country and the nation, and the name of the stronghold became the name of the realm. From his descendants grew the County of Luxembourg, raised to a duchy in 1354, and the House of Luxembourg that supplied the Holy Roman Empire with several emperors. The rock's command of trade and military routes between western and central Europe made it one of the continent's strongest fortresses, fought over for nine centuries until its neutralisation and dismantling were ordered by the Treaty of London in 1867.

Luxembourg's history is that of a tiny territory whose strategic rock made it one of Europe's most contested places. The story begins in 963, when Count Siegfried of the Ardennes acquired the promontory of Lucilinburhuc on the Alzette and raised the castle that gave the country its name. From his line sprang the House of Luxembourg, which in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries gave the Holy Roman Empire emperors Henry VII, Charles IV and Sigismund. The rock grew into a fortress so formidable it was nicknamed the 'Gibraltar of the North', and for centuries it passed between Burgundian, Spanish, French and Austrian hands. Luxembourg was repeatedly partitioned: France took a slice at the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659; the Congress of Vienna in 1815 raised it to a Grand Duchy under the Dutch king while placing it in the German Confederation; the Treaty of London of 1839 handed the larger French-speaking half to the new kingdom of Belgium; and the Treaty of London of 1867, ending the Luxembourg Crisis between France and Prussia, guaranteed the country's perpetual neutrality and ordered the great fortress demolished. The crown separated from the Netherlands in 1890 under the Nassau-Weilburg dynasty. Twice occupied by Germany in the World Wars, Luxembourg abandoned neutrality after 1945 and became a founding member of Benelux, NATO, the European Coal and Steel Community and the European Union, its capital one of the three official seats of the EU.

## Siegfried's Rock and the County of Luxembourg — 963 – 1308

A count of the Ardennes seized a Roman-era rock above the Alzette and founded a dynasty whose fortress would dominate the surrounding country for a thousand years.

- 963: **Siegfried acquires Lucilinburhuc** — Count Siegfried of the Ardennes obtained the rocky promontory of Lucilinburhuc above the Alzette and built a castle on the Bock, the founding act of the city, the country and the nation. [vdl-history, luxcity-history]
- c. 1150: **The town is walled** — The settlement gathered beneath Siegfried's castle was enclosed with stone ramparts around the middle of the twelfth century, beginning its long evolution into a fortified city. [luxcity-history, vdl-history]
- 1308: **Henry of Luxembourg elected King of the Romans** — Count Henry, head of the House of Luxembourg, was elected King of the Romans, opening the dynasty's rise to the imperial throne of the Holy Roman Empire. [monarchie-nassau, luxcity-history]

Figures:

- Siegfried, Count of the Ardennes (c. 922 – 998) — Founder of Luxembourg. By acquiring the rock of Lucilinburhuc in 963 and building his castle on the Bock, Siegfried founded the stronghold that gave Luxembourg its name and became the cradle of the country and its ruling house.
- Henry VII (c. 1278/79 – 1313) — Count of Luxembourg and Holy Roman Emperor. Count of Luxembourg elected King of the Romans in 1308 and crowned Holy Roman Emperor in 1312, the first emperor of the House of Luxembourg, who carried the small dynasty to the height of imperial power.

## The House of Luxembourg and the Imperial Duchy — 1308 – 1443

From a small county the House of Luxembourg rose to give the Holy Roman Empire emperors and kings of Bohemia, raised its homeland to a duchy, and then lost it to Burgundy.

- 1310: **John the Blind becomes King of Bohemia** — John of Luxembourg, son of Henry VII, became king of Bohemia in 1310 and count of Luxembourg in 1313, a renowned warrior-prince later celebrated as a national hero of Luxembourg. [monarchie-nassau, luxcity-history]
- 26 Aug 1346: **Death of John the Blind at Crecy** — Blind in his final years, John of Luxembourg insisted on joining the fighting and was killed at the Battle of Crecy on the side of France, becoming an emblematic figure of Luxembourg's chivalric past. [luxcity-history, monarchie-nassau]
- 1354: **Luxembourg raised to a duchy** — Emperor Charles IV of the House of Luxembourg elevated the County of Luxembourg to the rank of a duchy, entrusting it to his half-brother Wenceslaus as its first duke. [luxcity-history, monarchie-nassau]
- 1443: **Burgundy seizes Luxembourg** — Philip the Good, duke of Burgundy, took Luxembourg in 1443, ending the rule of the House of Luxembourg and absorbing the duchy into the Burgundian dominions. [luxcity-history, vdl-history]

Figures:

- John the Blind (1296 – 1346) — Count of Luxembourg and King of Bohemia. Count of Luxembourg and king of Bohemia, blinded yet undaunted, he died charging the enemy at Crecy in 1346 and remains one of the most celebrated heroes of Luxembourg's history.
- Charles IV (1316 – 1378) — Holy Roman Emperor of the House of Luxembourg. King of Bohemia and Holy Roman Emperor, author of the Golden Bull of 1356, he raised his ancestral county of Luxembourg to a duchy in 1354 and made Prague a great capital of the Empire.

## The Gibraltar of the North and the First Partition — 1443 – 1797

For three centuries the great fortress passed between Burgundy, Spain, France and Austria, and at the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659 Luxembourg was partitioned for the first time.

- 7 Nov 1659: **Treaty of the Pyrenees: the First Partition** — The peace between France and Spain ceded a southern band of the duchy, including Thionville, Montmedy and Stenay, to Louis XIV's France, the first partition of Luxembourg's territory. [pyrenees-ebsco, luxcity-history]
- 1684: **Louis XIV takes the fortress; Vauban rebuilds it** — French forces captured Luxembourg City in 1684, and the engineer Vauban remodelled its fortifications before the stronghold was later returned to Spanish rule. [luxcity-history, vdl-history]
- 1697: **Return to the Habsburgs** — Luxembourg reverted to Habsburg rule, passing in the eighteenth century from the Spanish to the Austrian branch of the dynasty while remaining a frontier fortress. [luxcity-history]
- 1795: **Revolutionary France captures the fortress** — After a long blockade, French Revolutionary forces took the fortress in 1795 and Luxembourg was annexed to the French Republic, ending the era of Habsburg rule. [vdl-history, luxcity-history]

Figures:

- Philip the Good (1396 – 1467) — Duke of Burgundy. Duke of Burgundy who seized Luxembourg in 1443, drawing the duchy into the Burgundian, and then Habsburg, orbit that would make it a contested frontier fortress for three hundred years.
- Louis XIV (1638 – 1715) — King of France. The French king whose wars against Spain won Luxembourg's first partition at the Pyrenees in 1659 and whose armies took the fortress city itself in 1684, when Vauban remodelled its defences.

## The Grand Duchy, the Second and Third Partitions, and Neutrality — 1815 – 1890

The Congress of Vienna made Luxembourg a Grand Duchy, but two further partitions cut away most of its land before the Treaty of London of 1867 made it a perpetually neutral state and ordered its fortress razed.

- 1815: **Congress of Vienna creates the Grand Duchy** — Luxembourg was raised to a Grand Duchy under William I of the Netherlands in personal union, placed within the German Confederation with a Prussian garrison, and lost an eastern slice to Prussia in a second partition. [monarchie-nassau]
- 1839: **Treaty of London: the cession to Belgium** — The 1839 Treaty of London partitioned the Grand Duchy a third time, ceding its larger French-speaking western half to the new kingdom of Belgium while confirming the independence of the German-speaking remainder. [monarchie-nassau, luxcity-history]
- 1842: **Entry into the Zollverein** — The reduced Grand Duchy joined the German customs union in 1842, binding its economy closely to the neighbouring German territories for the rest of the century. [ww1-handbook]
- 11 May 1867: **Treaty of London: neutrality and the razing of the fortress** — After the Luxembourg Crisis between France and Prussia, the powers guaranteed the Grand Duchy's perpetual neutrality, withdrew the Prussian garrison and ordered the fortress dismantled, a demolition completed in 1883. [monarchie-nassau, luxcity-history]
- 9 Dec 1890: **Separation of the crown from the Netherlands** — On the death of William III the personal union ended; the Dutch crown passed to Wilhelmina while Luxembourg, under male-line succession, passed to Adolphe of Nassau-Weilburg, founding a national dynasty. [monarchie-nassau]

Figures:

- William I of the Netherlands (1772 – 1843) — First Grand Duke of Luxembourg. King of the Netherlands and Prince of Orange-Nassau, to whom the Congress of Vienna granted the new Grand Duchy of Luxembourg in 1815 in personal union, beginning eight decades of shared rule with the Dutch crown.
- Adolphe of Nassau-Weilburg (1817 – 1905) — Grand Duke, founder of the national dynasty. On the end of the union with the Netherlands in 1890 he became the first Grand Duke of the House of Nassau-Weilburg, the dynasty that still reigns, taking his constitutional oath on 9 December 1890.

## Two World Wars and the End of Neutrality — 1890 – 1945

Neutral on paper, Luxembourg was invaded by Germany in both world wars; the second occupation brought annexation, forced conscription and the first general strike in Nazi-occupied Europe.

- 2 Aug 1914: **German invasion in the First World War** — German forces occupied neutral Luxembourg on 2 August 1914 to control its railways for the advance on France, holding the country until November 1918 despite its formal protest. [ww1-occupation, ww1-handbook]
- Jan 1919: **Abdication of Marie-Adelaide** — Criticised for her conduct under the German occupation, Grand Duchess Marie-Adelaide abdicated in January 1919 in favour of her sister Charlotte, who reigned until 1964. [ww1-occupation, monarchie-nassau]
- 10 May 1940: **Second German invasion and annexation** — The Wehrmacht overran Luxembourg on 10 May 1940; the government and Grand Duchess fled into exile, and the territory was placed under a German civil administration aimed at its full integration into the Reich. [ww2-fasos, nvdatabase-strike]
- 31 Aug 1942: **Conscription and the general strike** — When the occupier imposed German citizenship and Wehrmacht conscription in August 1942, a general strike spread from 31 August, the first in Nazi-occupied Europe; the Germans declared martial law and executed twenty-one strikers. [nvdatabase-strike, ww2-fasos]
- 16 Dec 1944: **Liberation and the Battle of the Bulge** — Allied forces liberated Luxembourg in September 1944, but the German Ardennes offensive launched on 16 December 1944 overran the north of the country, which had to be liberated a second time before war's end. [ww2museum-bulge, nvdatabase-strike]

Figures:

- Grand Duchess Charlotte (1896 – 1985) — Grand Duchess of Luxembourg. Reigning from 1919 to 1964, she led the nation in exile during the Second World War, broadcasting to her occupied people over the BBC and becoming an enduring symbol of Luxembourg's resistance and independence.
- Marie-Adelaide (1894 – 1924) — Grand Duchess of Luxembourg. Luxembourg's first reigning Grand Duchess from 1912, she was accused of excessive accommodation toward the German occupier during the First World War and abdicated in 1919 in favour of her sister Charlotte.

## A Founder of the New Europe — 1945 – present

Abandoning the neutrality forced on it in 1867, post-war Luxembourg became a founding member of Benelux, NATO and the European Communities, and its capital a seat of the Union it helped to build.

- 5 Sep 1944: **The Benelux customs convention** — The exiled governments of Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg signed a customs union in London on 5 September 1944, launching the Benelux that entered into force in 1948 and was deepened by the 1958 Economic Union. [benelux-gouv, benelux-int, cvce-benelux]
- 4 Apr 1949: **Founding member of NATO** — Having renounced its enforced neutrality, Luxembourg was one of the twelve states that signed the North Atlantic Treaty in Washington on 4 April 1949, founding the Atlantic alliance. [nato-founding, cvce-benelux]
- 1951: **Founding member of the ECSC** — Following the Schuman Declaration of 9 May 1950, Luxembourg joined Belgium, France, Italy, the Netherlands and West Germany as a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community, operating from 1952. [eu-schuman, eu-1945-59]
- 1 Jan 1958: **Founding member of the European Communities** — Under the Treaties of Rome, Luxembourg became a founding member of the European Economic Community, joining what is now the European Union from 1 January 1958 and later adopting the euro. [eu-luxembourg, eu-1945-59]
- 14 Jun 1985: **The Schengen Agreement** — On 14 June 1985 five states signed, at the village of Schengen on the Luxembourg Moselle, the agreement abolishing internal border controls that gave a Luxembourg name to Europe's borderless travel area. [cvce-schengen, eu-luxembourg]

Figures:

- Robert Schuman (1886 – 1963) — French statesman, born in Luxembourg. Born in Luxembourg City and a chief architect of European integration, his declaration of 9 May 1950 launched the European Coal and Steel Community of which Luxembourg was a founding member.
- Grand Duke Jean (1921 – 2019) — Grand Duke of Luxembourg. Son of Grand Duchess Charlotte, he served as a volunteer in the Allied liberation of Europe and reigned from 1964 to 2000, presiding over Luxembourg's emergence as a prosperous founder-state of the European Union.
