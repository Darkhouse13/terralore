Founding: Separation from Colombia — 3 November 1903. Panama declared its independence from Colombia on 3 November 1903, with the tacit support of the United States and the presence of U.S. naval forces in nearby waters. Within weeks, on 18 November 1903, the Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty granted the United States rights to a Canal Zone across the isthmus and guaranteed the new republic's independence. The treaty, signed by U.S. Secretary of State John Hay and the French promoter Philippe Bunau-Varilla acting for Panama, would shape the nation's politics for the rest of the century.

Panama is the narrow land bridge where the Americas meet and where, in 1513, Vasco Nunez de Balboa first sighted the Pacific from the New World. Under Spain the isthmus became the artery of empire, its Pacific terminus at Panama Viejo and its Caribbean ports of Portobelo and Nombre de Dios carrying Peruvian silver to the fleets of Seville. After winning independence from Spain in 1821 it joined Gran Colombia and remained a province of Colombia until, with United States backing, it separated on 3 November 1903 and granted the rights that built the Panama Canal, opened in 1914. The twentieth century was defined by the struggle over that canal, from the 1964 Flag Riots and the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties to the 1989 U.S. invasion and the canal's transfer to Panama on the last day of 1999.

## Crossroads and Conquest — Pre-1513 - 1538

Home to the Cueva, Guna, and Ngabe peoples, the isthmus was already a crossroads of the Americas when Spaniards arrived and Balboa, in 1513, became the first European to sight the Pacific from the New World.

- 1501-1502: **Spanish reach the isthmus** — Rodrigo de Bastidas and then Christopher Columbus explore the Caribbean coast of the isthmus, opening the region to Spanish colonisation. [loc-panama, britannica-panama]
- 25 Sept 1513: **Balboa sights the Pacific** — Vasco Nunez de Balboa crosses the isthmus and becomes the first European to see the Pacific Ocean from the Americas, claiming it for Castile. [britannica-balboa]
- 1519: **Balboa executed** — Accused of treason by the governor Pedrarias Davila, Balboa is beheaded, the same year a new Pacific-coast city is founded. [britannica-balboa]

Figures:

- Vasco Nunez de Balboa (c. 1475-1519) — Spanish explorer and conquistador. The first European to sight the Pacific Ocean from the New World, in 1513, after crossing the isthmus of Panama. He claimed the South Sea for Spain but was later executed for treason by a rival governor.

## Artery of the Spanish Empire — 1519 - 1821

Panama Viejo on the Pacific and the Caribbean ports of Nombre de Dios and Portobelo made the isthmus the gold and silver route of the Spanish empire, coveted, fortified, and sacked.

- 1519: **Founding of Panama Viejo** — Pedrarias Davila founds Panama on the Pacific coast, the oldest European city on the Pacific shore of the Americas and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [unesco-panama-viejo, britannica-panama]
- 1597: **Trade shifts to Portobelo** — Spain moves its Caribbean treasure port to Portobelo, whose fairs and fortifications anchor the trans-isthmus gold and silver route. [unesco-portobelo, loc-panama]
- 1671: **Henry Morgan sacks Panama** — The buccaneer Henry Morgan crosses the isthmus and takes Panama Viejo, which burns to the ground; the city is refounded on a new site in 1673. [britannica-morgan, unesco-panama-viejo]

Figures:

- Henry Morgan (c. 1635-1688) — Welsh buccaneer and privateer. Caribbean buccaneer who crossed the isthmus in 1671 and captured Panama Viejo, which was destroyed by fire during the raid. He was later knighted and made lieutenant governor of Jamaica.

## A Province of Colombia — 1821 - 1903

Independent of Spain in 1821, Panama joined Gran Colombia and then Colombia; the California Gold Rush, the trans-isthmus railroad, and a failed French canal revived its destiny as a route between the seas.

- 28 Nov 1821: **Independence from Spain and union** — Panama declares independence from Spain and voluntarily joins Gran Colombia, remaining tied to Colombia after Gran Colombia dissolves. [britannica-panama, loc-panama]
- 1855: **Panama Railroad completed** — Spurred by the California Gold Rush, a U.S. company completes the trans-isthmus railroad, the shortest route between the oceans. [loc-panama, britannica-panama]
- 1880-1889: **French canal attempt fails** — Ferdinand de Lesseps's company begins a sea-level canal but is defeated by disease and cost, collapsing in financial scandal. [state-building-canal, britannica-panama]

Figures:

- Ferdinand de Lesseps (1805-1894) — French diplomat and canal promoter. Builder of the Suez Canal whose company began a French canal across Panama in 1880. The effort was overwhelmed by tropical disease and engineering difficulty and collapsed in scandal, clearing the way for the United States.

## The Canal and the Zone — 1903 - 1968

Backed by the United States, Panama separated from Colombia in 1903 and granted the Canal Zone; the canal opened in 1914, but the perpetual American enclave bred grievance that erupted in the 1964 Flag Riots.

- 3 Nov 1903: **Separation from Colombia** — With U.S. backing, Panama declares independence from Colombia and is swiftly recognised by the United States. [state-panama-canal, britannica-hay-bunau-varilla]
- 18 Nov 1903: **Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty** — The treaty grants the United States a Canal Zone across the isthmus in perpetuity and guarantees Panama's independence. [britannica-hay-bunau-varilla, state-panama-canal]
- 15 Aug 1914: **Panama Canal opens** — After a decade of U.S. construction, the lock canal opens to traffic, joining the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. [state-building-canal, britannica-panama]
- 9 Jan 1964: **The Flag Riots** — Clashes over flying the Panamanian flag in the Canal Zone leave roughly two dozen Panamanians and several Americans dead, hardening demands to revise the 1903 treaty. [state-panama-canal, britannica-panama]

Figures:

- Philippe Bunau-Varilla (1859-1940) — French engineer and Panama's treaty negotiator. Former French canal engineer who, acting as Panama's diplomatic representative, signed the 1903 Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty granting the United States the Canal Zone, a settlement many Panamanians regarded as unjust.

## Torrijos, the Treaties, and Noriega — 1968 - 1999

Omar Torrijos's regime won the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties returning the canal; after his death, Manuel Noriega's rule ended in the 1989 U.S. invasion, and Panama regained the canal at the close of 1999.

- 1968: **Torrijos takes power** — A military coup brings General Omar Torrijos to power; his government makes recovery of the canal its central aim. [britannica-torrijos, loc-panama]
- 7 Sept 1977: **Torrijos-Carter Treaties** — Torrijos and President Carter sign treaties abolishing the Canal Zone and committing to transfer the canal to Panama by the end of 1999. [state-panama-canal, britannica-torrijos]
- 20 Dec 1989: **U.S. invasion ousts Noriega** — Operation Just Cause removes General Manuel Noriega; casualty estimates vary, and Panamanian deaths are reported from several hundred to around a thousand. [britannica-just-cause]
- 31 Dec 1999: **Canal transferred to Panama** — At noon, the United States hands the Panama Canal to Panama, ending nearly a century of foreign control. [state-panama-canal, britannica-panama]

Figures:

- Omar Torrijos (1929-1981) — Military leader of Panama. De facto ruler of Panama from 1968 who negotiated the 1977 treaties returning the canal to Panama. A populist reformer, he died in a plane crash in 1981 before the transfer he secured was completed.

## The Canal Nation — 2000 - present

Master of its waterway at last, Panama built a service and logistics economy around the canal, doubling its capacity with the 2016 expansion while contending with the politics of governance and global trade.

- 2000: **Panama administers the canal** — The Panama Canal Authority assumes full management of the waterway, anchoring a service and logistics economy. [state-panama-canal, britannica-panama]
- 26 June 2016: **Expanded canal opens** — New Agua Clara and Cocoli locks open, roughly doubling capacity and admitting larger Neopanamax vessels. [britannica-canal-expansion, britannica-panama]
