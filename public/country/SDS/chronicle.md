# The Chronicle of South Sudan: From the cattle camps of the Nile to the world's newest nation

Founding: Independence from Sudan — 9 July 2011. Following a January 2011 referendum in which 98.83% voted to secede, South Sudan declared independence from Sudan at midnight on 9 July 2011, becoming the world's newest widely recognised state and Africa's 54th country.

South Sudan is built on the Nilotic pastoral societies of the upper White Nile and the vast Sudd wetland, peoples such as the Dinka, Nuer, Shilluk and Azande whose cattle camps, divine kingships and segmentary clans long predate any state. Nineteenth-century slave raiding, Turco-Egyptian conquest and the Mahdist wars devastated the region, after which British rule under the Anglo-Egyptian Condominium administered the largely Christian and traditionalist south apart from the Arab-Muslim north. Marginalised at Sudan's 1956 independence, the south fought two long civil wars (1955-1972 and 1983-2005) before the 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement and a 2011 referendum delivered statehood on 9 July 2011. Independence was quickly followed by a ruinous civil war from 2013 and a fragile, repeatedly tested peace.

## Peoples of the Upper Nile — c. 3000 BCE – c. 1700 CE

Cattle-herding Nilotic societies and the Sudd wetland shape a world of pastoral clans and divine kings.

- c. 3000 BCE: **Cattle-herding cultures in the Sudd** — Archaeological evidence indicates transhumant cattle-raising societies established in the Sudd marshlands of the upper White Nile. [wiki-history-ss, oxford-precolonial]
- c. 14th century: **Nilotic expansion across the upper Nile** — Western Nilotic peoples including the Dinka and Nuer spread out from the Sudd, a dispersal linked to the decline of the Christian Nubian kingdoms of Makuria and Alodia. [wiki-history-ss, oxford-precolonial]
- c. mid-15th century: **Founding of the Shilluk Kingdom** — The Shilluk establish a centralised kingdom on the White Nile under the sacred kingship of the Reth, traditionally traced to the founder-figure Nyikang. [wiki-shilluk, oxford-precolonial]
- c. 1690s: **Fashoda made the Shilluk royal seat** — King Tugo founds Fashoda as the permanent residence of the Shilluk kings, formalising the kingdom's investiture rituals and divine kingship. [wiki-shilluk]

## Conquest, Ivory and the Slave Raids — 1821 – 1898

Turco-Egyptian expansion, the zariba traders and Mahdist war devastate the southern Nile peoples.

- 1821: **Turco-Egyptian conquest reaches Sudan** — Muhammad Ali's forces conquer the Funj lands and extend Egyptian rule up the Nile, seeking gold, ivory and slaves and making Khartoum the seat of administration. [wiki-turco-egyptian, blackpast-khartoum]
- 1850s–1870s: **Zariba slave and ivory raids** — Armed traders such as al-Zubayr Rahma Mansur establish fortified zaribas across the south; contemporary estimates put captives taken at tens of thousands a year by the mid-1870s. [wiki-history-ss, wiki-zubayr]
- 1869: **Equatoria province established** — Samuel Baker is appointed governor of Equatoria, an Egyptian southern province later led by Charles Gordon, partly to suppress the slave trade. [wiki-history-ss]
- 1885: **Mahdist revolt and the fall of Khartoum** — The Mahdist uprising overruns Sudan and captures Khartoum, ending Turco-Egyptian rule and bringing further upheaval to the southern Nile peoples. [britannica-condominium]
- 1898: **Mahdist state crushed at Omdurman** — An Anglo-Egyptian army defeats the Mahdist forces at Omdurman, clearing the way for the reconquest of Sudan, including the south. [britannica-condominium]

## The Condominium and the Southern Policy — 1899 – 1955

British rule administers the south apart from the north, then hands it to Khartoum without its consent.

- 1899: **Anglo-Egyptian Condominium established** — Britain and Egypt establish joint rule over Sudan, with the largely Christian and traditionalist south administered separately from the Arab-Muslim north. [britannica-condominium, wiki-history-ss]
- 1922: **Closed District ordinances** — Colonial 'closed district' rules restrict travel and trade between north and south, isolating the southern provinces and curbing northern and Arabic influence. [lse-southern-policy, wiki-history-ss]
- 1930: **The Southern Policy formalised** — A government memorandum directs administrators to develop the south as a distinct bloc, favouring local languages and English over Arabic and limiting northern contact. [lse-southern-policy, wiki-history-ss]
- 1946: **Britain reverses course** — Colonial authorities abandon the Southern Policy and move to unite the south with the north, without meaningful consultation of southerners. [wiki-history-ss]
- 1947: **Juba Conference** — Northern and southern representatives meet at Juba to discuss the south's place in a future Sudan, but southern delegates have little leverage over the outcome. [wiki-history-ss]

## Marginalisation and Two Civil Wars — 1955 – 2005

From the Torit mutiny to the SPLM/SPLA, the south fights two long wars against rule from Khartoum.

- Aug 1955: **Torit mutiny begins the First Civil War** — Southern soldiers of the Equatorial Corps mutiny at Torit over fears of northern domination, an event marking the start of the First Sudanese Civil War. [wiki-history-ss, britannica-addis]
- 1 Jan 1956: **Sudan's independence; the south sidelined** — Sudan becomes independent under a northern-dominated government, with southern demands for federalism largely unmet. [wiki-history-ss]
- 27 Feb 1972: **Addis Ababa Agreement** — The Addis Ababa Agreement ends the seventeen-year first war, granting the south a single autonomous region and integrating Anyanya fighters into Sudan's forces. [britannica-addis, wiki-addis]
- 1983: **Second Civil War and the rise of the SPLM/SPLA** — Khartoum abolishes southern autonomy and moves to impose sharia; the SPLM/SPLA forms under John Garang, beginning the Second Sudanese Civil War. [wiki-history-ss, britannica-cpa]
- 1983: **An estimated two million dead** — Over more than two decades the war kills around two million people through combat, famine and disease and uproots millions, among the deadliest conflicts of its time. [britannica-cpa, cfr-cpa]

Figures:

- John Garang de Mabior (1945–2005) — Founder and leader of the SPLM/SPLA. Led the Sudan People's Liberation Movement and Army through the Second Sudanese Civil War, championing a reformed, united 'New Sudan'. He signed the 2005 peace agreement and became first vice-president of Sudan, only to die in a helicopter crash weeks later.

## Peace, Referendum and a New State — 2005 – 2011

The Comprehensive Peace Agreement leads to an overwhelming vote for independence and statehood in 2011.

- 9 Jan 2005: **Comprehensive Peace Agreement signed** — Khartoum and the SPLM/SPLA sign the CPA, ending the second war and providing for southern autonomy and a self-determination referendum within six years. [britannica-cpa, cfr-cpa]
- Jul 2005: **Death of John Garang** — Weeks after becoming Sudan's first vice-president, John Garang dies in a helicopter crash; Salva Kiir Mayardit succeeds him as SPLM leader and southern president. [britannica-cpa, cfr-cpa]
- 9–15 Jan 2011: **Self-determination referendum** — In a week-long vote, 98.83% of southern Sudanese choose independence on a turnout of about 99%. [un-referendum, wiki-history-ss]
- 9 Jul 2011: **Independence of South Sudan** — At midnight on 9 July 2011 South Sudan becomes an independent state, the world's newest widely recognised nation, recognised the same day by the United States and others. [state-ss-relations, wiki-history-ss]
- 14 Jul 2011: **Admission to the United Nations** — South Sudan is admitted as the 193rd member state of the United Nations, and soon after joins the African Union. [wiki-history-ss]

Figures:

- Salva Kiir Mayardit (born 1951) — First President of South Sudan. A founding member of the SPLM, he succeeded John Garang in 2005 and led South Sudan to independence in 2011 as its first president. His rivalry with Riek Machar would help plunge the young state into civil war.

## A Young Nation at War — 2013 – present

Within two years of independence the new state collapses into a civil war marked by famine and atrocity.

- Dec 2013: **Civil war erupts in Juba** — A power struggle between President Salva Kiir and former vice-president Riek Machar ignites fighting in Juba that spreads nationwide and takes on an ethnic character. [commons-primer, wiki-history-ss]
- Feb 2017: **Famine declared in Unity state** — The UN declares famine in parts of Unity state, with millions needing emergency aid; experts warn that famine used as a weapon of war may be an international crime. [un-famine, wiki-famine]
- 2018: **An estimated 383,000 dead** — A London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine study estimates about 383,000 conflict-related deaths by April 2018, roughly half from direct violence. [lshtm-mortality, commons-primer]
- 12 Sep 2018: **Revitalised peace agreement (R-ARCSS)** — After repeated failed ceasefires, the government and opposition sign the R-ARCSS, providing for a unified army, a new constitution and elections. [accord-rtgonu, commons-primer]
- 22 Feb 2020: **Unity government formed** — A Revitalised Transitional Government of National Unity is sworn in, with Riek Machar again first vice-president under President Salva Kiir, though the peace remains fragile. [accord-rtgonu]
