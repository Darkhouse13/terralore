# The Chronicle of Comoros: An Indian Ocean archipelago of Swahili-Muslim sultanates, drawn into the spice and slave trades, colonised by France, and independent since 1975 amid coups, secessions and an unresolved dispute over Mayotte.

Founding: Unilateral declaration of independence from France — 6 July 1975. After a referendum held throughout the islands on 22 December 1974 returned an overwhelming majority for independence—while voters on Mayotte favoured remaining within France—the Chamber of Deputies in Moroni, led by Ahmed Abdallah, unilaterally proclaimed the independence of the Comoros on 6 July 1975. The new state, comprising Grande Comore (Ngazidja), Anjouan (Ndzuwani) and Mohéli (Mwali), was admitted to the United Nations on 12 November 1975 by General Assembly resolution 3385 (XXX), which reaffirmed the territorial integrity of the archipelago including Mayotte. France, however, retained administration of Mayotte, and Abdallah himself was overthrown within a month, opening a long era of coups and instability.

The Comoros are a volcanic archipelago in the northern Mozambique Channel, between the East African coast and Madagascar. Settled from around the eighth century CE by Austronesian seafarers and Bantu-speaking peoples from the African mainland, the islands were woven into the trade networks of the Swahili coast and the wider Indian Ocean; the Dembeni-phase site shows long-distance commerce with the Abbasid and Fatimid worlds by the ninth to twelfth centuries. Islam took deep root and remains the defining feature of Comorian life. From roughly the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, Muslim sultanates of Swahili culture—legitimised through Arab, Persian (Shirazi) and Hadhrami genealogies—rose on the three islands of Ngazidja (Grande Comore), Ndzuwani (Anjouan) and Mwali (Mohéli), trading in slaves, rice and spices. France took Mayotte in 1841 and placed the other three islands under protectorate in 1886; the archipelago was formally made a French possession in 1908 and long administered through Madagascar, becoming an overseas territory in 1947 and gaining internal autonomy in 1961. In a referendum on 22 December 1974 the three western islands voted overwhelmingly for independence while Mayotte voted to remain French; Ahmed Abdallah declared independence on 6 July 1975. The decades that followed brought extraordinary instability, with some twenty coups or attempted coups, several involving the French mercenary Bob Denard. Anjouan and Mohéli attempted to secede in 1997, and a 2001 Union constitution introduced a federal structure with a presidency rotating among the islands. France administers Mayotte, which the Comoros and the UN General Assembly regard as Comorian territory.

## Settlement and the Coming of Islam — c. 8th – 14th century CE

Settled by Austronesian seafarers and Bantu-speaking Africans, the islands were drawn into the trade of the Swahili coast and the wider Indian Ocean, and Islam took root among their peoples.

- by the 8th century CE: **Earliest settlement of the islands** — Austronesian seafarers and Bantu-speaking peoples from the African mainland settle the archipelago, occupying the islands no later than the eighth century CE and embedding them in Swahili-coast networks. [wikipedia-history-comoros, wikipedia-comoros, africanhistory-ngazidja]
- 9th–12th century: **Dembeni phase and Indian Ocean trade** — The Dembeni-phase settlement flourishes as one of the richest sites of its age in the region, trading rock crystal and other goods with the Abbasid and Fatimid worlds and importing Persian and Chinese ceramics. [wikipedia-dembeni, jia-rockcrystal, wikipedia-history-comoros]
- c. 11th–12th century: **Islam takes root** — Islam, carried by Indian Ocean merchants and migrants, becomes established across the islands, tying the Comoros to the religious culture of the Swahili coast. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros]

## The Swahili-Muslim Sultanates — c. 15th century – 1841

Muslim sultanates of Swahili culture rose on Ngazidja, Ndzuwani and Mwali, legitimised by Arab, Persian and Hadhrami genealogies and enriched by Indian Ocean trade—and, increasingly, by slavery.

- 15th–16th century: **Rise of the island sultanates** — Muslim sultanates of Swahili culture emerge on the islands, with ruling houses claiming Arab, Persian (Shirazi) and Hadhrami descent to legitimise their authority. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros, africanhistory-ngazidja]
- early 16th century: **Portuguese and European contact** — Portuguese navigators reach the islands in the early sixteenth century; the Comoros become a provisioning station for ships crossing the western Indian Ocean. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- c. 1785–1820: **Malagasy slaving raids** — Sakalava and Betsimisaraka war-fleets from Madagascar raid the Comoros for captives over several decades, devastating communities across the archipelago. [wikipedia-slavery-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- mid-19th century: **Slave society on the islands** — The slave trade and plantation labour expand greatly, and by the mid-nineteenth century enslaved people are estimated to make up a very large proportion of the islands' population. [wikipedia-slavery-comoros, britannica-comoros]

Figures:

- The Sultans of the Medinas — Rulers of the pre-colonial Comorian city-states. The sultans of Ngazidja, Ndzuwani, Mwali and Maore built the coral-stone medinas — mosques, palaces and walled quarters — that UNESCO inscribed in 2026 as the archipelago's first World Heritage site.

## French Colonisation — 1841 – 1946

Beginning with Mayotte in 1841, France brought the archipelago under its control, imposed a plantation economy of vanilla, cloves and ylang-ylang, and administered the islands through Madagascar.

- 1841: **Mayotte ceded to France** — The ruler of Mayotte cedes the island to France, giving France its first foothold in the Comoro archipelago. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- 1886: **Protectorates over the three islands** — France establishes protectorates over Grande Comore, Anjouan and Mohéli, dealing with sultans including Said Ali bin Said Omar of Grande Comore. [wikipedia-history-comoros, wikipedia-saidali, britannica-comoros]
- 1908: **Annexation and administration via Madagascar** — The islands are formally brought under French possession and attached to the colony of Madagascar; the last sultan relinquishes power by 1912. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros, wikipedia-saidali]
- late 19th–20th century: **Plantation economy** — Colonial rule reorganises the economy around export crops—vanilla, cloves, copra and especially ylang-ylang—making the Comoros a leading supplier of perfume essence. [wikipedia-economy-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- 1946: **Separate overseas territory** — The Comoros are detached from Madagascar and constituted as a distinct French overseas territory, beginning to acquire their own institutions. [wikipedia-history-comoros, britannica-comoros]

## Autonomy and Independence — 1946 – 1975

Granted representation and then internal autonomy, the islands voted for independence in 1974—except Mayotte—and Ahmed Abdallah declared the Comoros independent on 6 July 1975.

- 1947: **Overseas territory with representation** — The Comoros are confirmed as a French overseas territory and given representation in the French Parliament and a local assembly. [britannica-comoros, wikipedia-history-comoros]
- 1961: **Internal autonomy granted** — A year after Madagascar's independence, the Comoros are granted internal autonomy, with their own governing council and capital at Moroni. [britannica-comoros, wikipedia-history-comoros]
- 22 December 1974: **Independence referendum** — Voters back independence by about 95 per cent across the archipelago, but on Mayotte a majority of roughly 63 per cent votes to remain French, splitting the islands. [wikipedia-1974referendum, britannica-comoros]
- 6 July 1975: **Unilateral declaration of independence** — The Chamber of Deputies in Moroni, led by Ahmed Abdallah, declares the Comoros independent, claiming all four islands; Abdallah becomes first president. [wikipedia-state-comoros, britannica-comoros, sahistory-comoros]
- 12 November 1975: **Admitted to the United Nations** — The Comoros joins the UN under General Assembly resolution 3385 (XXX), which reaffirms the territorial integrity of the archipelago including Mayotte. [euacademic-mayotte, wikipedia-state-comoros]

## Coups, Mercenaries and Instability — 1975 – 1996

The young republic endured one of the world's most coup-prone histories, with Ali Soilih's radical rule, the assassination of Ahmed Abdallah, and the recurring shadow of the French mercenary Bob Denard.

- 3 August 1975: **Abdallah overthrown** — A coalition aided by mercenaries led by Bob Denard ousts President Ahmed Abdallah weeks after independence, beginning a long era of coups. [wikipedia-state-comoros, wikipedia-bobdenard, britannica-comoros]
- 1976–1978: **Ali Soilih's revolution** — Radical leftist Ali Soilih takes power and launches a confrontational revolution against the sultans, religious notables and old order, alienating France and the elite. [wikipedia-state-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- May 1978: **Denard restores Abdallah** — Bob Denard returns with mercenaries, overthrows Soilih—who is soon killed—and restores Ahmed Abdallah; the state becomes the Federal Islamic Republic of the Comoros. [wikipedia-state-comoros, wikipedia-bobdenard, wikipedia-1978coup]
- 26 November 1989: **Assassination of Ahmed Abdallah** — President Abdallah is shot dead in his office in Moroni in disputed circumstances; Denard is widely suspected but later acquitted, and is pressured to leave. [wikipedia-bobdenard, wikipedia-abdallah]
- September–October 1995: **Operation Kaskari and French intervention** — Bob Denard seizes Moroni with mercenaries in his final coup attempt; France intervenes in 'Operation Azalée' and removes him within days. [wikipedia-bobdenard, britannica-comoros]

Figures:

- Ali Soilih (1937-1978) — President of the Comoros. Seized power in 1975 and attempted a radical social revolution against the sultans, the clergy and the customary order; overthrown by Bob Denard's mercenaries in 1978 and killed shortly afterwards.
- Ahmed Abdallah (1919-1989) — First President of the independent Comoros. Declared independence unilaterally in July 1975, was deposed within a month, restored by mercenaries in 1978, and was assassinated in 1989 while the same mercenaries guarded his palace.
- Bob Denard (1929-2007) — French mercenary. Took part in four coups in the archipelago between 1975 and 1995, commanded the presidential guard for a decade, and made the Comoros the standing example of a state small enough to be captured by a few dozen armed men.

## The Union and the Mayotte Question — 1997 – present

Secession crises on Anjouan and Mohéli were resolved by a 2001 Union constitution with a rotating presidency, while the status of Mayotte—claimed by the Comoros and the UN but administered by France—remains unresolved.

- 1997: **Anjouan and Mohéli secede** — Anjouan and Mohéli declare separation from the Comoros; a federal attempt to retake Anjouan by force is defeated, pushing the archipelago towards fragmentation. [britannica-comoros, encyclopedia-union-comoros]
- 1999: **Azali Assoumani seizes power** — Army chief Azali Assoumani takes power in a coup amid the secession crisis, casting himself as the guarantor of national unity. [britannica-comoros, encyclopedia-union-comoros]
- 2001: **Union constitution and rotating presidency** — Following the Fomboni accords, a new constitution approved by referendum creates the Union of the Comoros, giving each island autonomy and rotating the Union presidency among the three islands. [encyclopedia-union-comoros, britannica-comoros]
- 29 March 2009: **Mayotte votes to become a French department** — In a referendum, Mayotte votes overwhelmingly to become a fully integrated French department, a status it assumes in 2011 as France's 101st department. [wikipedia-2009referendum, britannica-mayotte]
- since 1976: **UN affirms Comorian sovereignty over Mayotte** — The UN General Assembly has repeatedly affirmed Comorian sovereignty over Mayotte and the archipelago's territorial integrity; the Comoros claims the island while France administers it. [euacademic-mayotte, unga-mayotte-resolution]
- 2026: **The medinas of the sultanates inscribed** — The Medinas of the Historic Sultanates of the Comoros become the archipelago's first World Heritage site — the stone towns of the pre-colonial sultanates recognised on criteria (iii) and (iv). [unesco-comoros-medinas]
