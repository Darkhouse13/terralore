# The Chronicle of Serbia: From the medieval Nemanjić kingdom and the field of Kosovo to a modern Balkan republic at the centre of Europe's last great territorial dispute.

Founding: International recognition of Serbian independence at the Congress of Berlin — 1878. Modern Serbia's independence was recognised by the great powers at the Congress of Berlin in 1878, three centuries after the Ottoman conquest had extinguished the medieval state. The Serbian Revolution had begun in 1804 with the First Uprising under Karađorđe; the Second Uprising of 1815 under Miloš Obrenović won autonomy, formalised when the Ottoman government granted the principality self-government in 1830. De facto independence followed in 1867 with the withdrawal of the last Ottoman garrisons, and the Treaty of Berlin of 1878 confirmed it in international law, enlarging the principality, which became the Kingdom of Serbia in 1882. This statehood had far older roots in the medieval kingdom and empire of the Nemanjić dynasty, whose memory — above all the Battle of Kosovo of 1389 — remained central to Serbian national identity through the long centuries of Ottoman rule.

Serbia's history runs from a powerful medieval Orthodox kingdom, through nearly five centuries of Ottoman rule, to a modern state reborn in revolution and still defined by unresolved questions of territory. Under the Nemanjić dynasty, founded by Stefan Nemanja in the twelfth century, Serbia became a major Balkan power whose church won autocephaly under Saint Sava in 1219 and whose ruler Stefan Dušan was crowned emperor in 1346. That golden age ended at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389 and the Ottoman conquest of the Despotate in 1459. A national revolution beginning in 1804 won autonomy and, at the Congress of Berlin in 1878, full international recognition; the Kingdom of Serbia led the creation of Yugoslavia in 1918. The violent breakup of Yugoslavia in the 1990s under Slobodan Milošević culminated in the Kosovo War, the NATO bombing of 1999 and UN Security Council Resolution 1244, which placed Kosovo under United Nations administration. Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence, found by the International Court of Justice in 2010 not to have violated international law, is still rejected by Serbia, which regards Kosovo as its autonomous province; the dispute remains live, addressed through the EU-facilitated Brussels and Ohrid dialogue as Serbia pursues membership of the European Union.

## The Medieval Serbian State under the Nemanjić Dynasty — c. 1166 – 1346

A grand prince of Raška founded a dynasty that raised Serbia into a kingdom, won its church independence, and culminated in the imperial coronation of Stefan Dušan.

- c. 1166–1196: **Stefan Nemanja founds the Nemanjić state** — As grand chieftain of Raška, Stefan Nemanja united the Serb principalities and founded the Nemanjić dynasty, which would develop Serbia into a major medieval Balkan power. [stefan-nemanja-britannica, nemanjic-britannica]
- 1190s: **Foundation of Studenica Monastery** — Stefan Nemanja founded the marble monastery of Studenica, retiring there around 1196; its churches became a masterpiece of the Raška school and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [studenica-unesco]
- 1217: **Stefan the First-Crowned becomes king** — Stefan Nemanjić obtained recognition as king, raising the grand principality of Raška to the rank of a kingdom and earning the name Prvovenčani, the 'First-Crowned'. [stephen-firstcrowned-britannica, nemanjic-britannica]
- 1219: **Autocephaly of the Serbian Orthodox Church** — Saint Sava obtained from the patriarch at Nicaea the status of autocephalous archbishop, founding an independent Serbian church that bound religious and royal authority together. [saint-sava-britannica]
- Apr 1346: **Imperial coronation of Stefan Dušan** — Having expanded the realm across the southern Balkans, Stefan Dušan was crowned 'Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks' and raised the church of Peć to a patriarchate, the high point of medieval Serbia. [stefan-dusan-britannica, nemanjic-britannica]

Figures:

- Stefan Nemanja (c. 1113 – 1199/1200) — Grand chieftain, founder of the Nemanjić dynasty. Grand župan of Raška from about 1166, Nemanja united the Serb lands and founded the dynasty that would rule medieval Serbia. He abdicated to become a monk and was later venerated as Saint Simeon, founder with his son of the Serbian monastic tradition.
- Saint Sava (c. 1175 – 1235/1236) — First archbishop of the autocephalous Serbian Church. The monk Rastko, youngest son of Stefan Nemanja, secured autocephaly for the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1219 and became its first archbishop. Lawgiver, diplomat and patron of letters, he is revered as the founder of Serbian national identity.

## The Battle of Kosovo and the Ottoman Conquest — 1346 – 1459

Dušan's empire fragmented after his death, and on the field of Kosovo in 1389 Serbia met the advancing Ottomans in a battle that became the centre of national memory before the state was finally conquered.

- 1355: **Death of Dušan and the empire's collapse** — The death of Stefan Dušan in 1355 led to the rapid disintegration of the Serbian Empire into rival principalities, leaving the Balkans divided as the Ottomans advanced. [stefan-dusan-britannica, serbia-history-britannica]
- 28 Jun 1389: **The Battle of Kosovo** — On the Field of Blackbirds the army of Prince Lazar fought the Ottoman sultan Murad I; both leaders were killed and Serbian resistance was broken, making Kosovo the central event of national memory. [battle-kosovo-britannica]
- 1402–1459: **The Serbian Despotate** — A reduced Serbian state survived as the Despotate, ruled latterly from Smederevo and balancing between the Ottomans and Hungary, prolonging Serb statehood for another half-century. [serbia-history-britannica]
- 1459: **Ottoman conquest of Serbia** — The Ottomans captured the fortress of Smederevo and extinguished the Despotate, absorbing the Serb lands into the empire and beginning nearly four centuries of Ottoman rule. [serbia-history-britannica]
- from 1346: **Patriarchate of Peć** — Elevated under Dušan, the patriarchate of Peć became the spiritual heart of the Serbs; its medieval churches in Kosovo survive as UNESCO World Heritage monuments of the Serbian Orthodox tradition. [kosovo-monuments-unesco]

Figures:

- Stefan Dušan (c. 1308 – 1355) — King and Emperor of Serbia. The greatest ruler of medieval Serbia, Dušan doubled the size of the kingdom, was crowned emperor in 1346 and issued a famous law code. His death in 1355 was followed by the swift collapse of the empire he had built.
- Lazar Hrebeljanović (c. 1329 – 1389) — Prince of Serbia, martyr of Kosovo. Prince Lazar led the Serbian and allied army against the Ottomans at Kosovo in 1389 and was killed in or after the battle. Canonised by the Serbian Church, he became the central heroic and martyr figure of the Kosovo legend.

## The Serbian Revolution and the Road to Independence — 1804 – 1914

Two uprisings against Ottoman rule won autonomy, the Congress of Berlin granted full independence in 1878, and a confident Kingdom of Serbia helped ignite the First World War.

- 1804–1813: **First Serbian Uprising** — Led by Karađorđe, Serbs rose against Ottoman rule and for nearly a decade governed a self-proclaimed state, the opening act of the Serbian Revolution before its suppression in 1813. [karadjordje-britannica, serbia-ottoman-britannica]
- 1815: **Second Uprising and autonomy under Miloš Obrenović** — Miloš Obrenović led the Second Uprising; by 1830 the Ottomans granted the principality full autonomy under him as hereditary prince, establishing self-governing Serbia. [serbia-ottoman-britannica]
- 1878: **Independence recognised at the Congress of Berlin** — The Treaty of Berlin confirmed Serbia's full independence in international law and enlarged the principality, which became the Kingdom of Serbia in 1882. [serbia-ottoman-britannica, statedept-serbia]
- 1912–1913: **The Balkan Wars** — Serbia drove the Ottomans from most of their European lands and, after the Second Balkan War, gained Kosovo, Metohija and much of Macedonia, nearly doubling its territory and alarming Austria-Hungary. [serbia-history-britannica]
- 28 Jun 1914: **Assassination at Sarajevo** — The Bosnian Serb Gavrilo Princip assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo; Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia, and its declaration of war triggered the First World War. [serbia-history-britannica]

Figures:

- Karađorđe Petrović (1768 – 1817) — Leader of the First Serbian Uprising. A former soldier and livestock trader, Karađorđe ('Black George') led the 1804 uprising that founded modern Serbia and gave his name to the Karađorđević dynasty. Murdered in 1817 amid the feud with Miloš Obrenović, he became a national hero.
- Miloš Obrenović (1780 – 1860) — Prince of Serbia. Leader of the Second Uprising of 1815, Miloš negotiated the autonomy granted in 1830 and became hereditary prince of Serbia, founding the Obrenović dynasty that contended with the Karađorđević line through the nineteenth century.

## The Birth of Yugoslavia and the World Wars — 1918 – 1980

Victorious but devastated, Serbia led the creation of a South Slav kingdom; after the catastrophe of the Second World War, Serbia became a republic within Tito's socialist Yugoslavia.

- 1 Dec 1918: **Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes proclaimed** — Prince Regent Alexander proclaimed the South Slav kingdom in Belgrade, uniting Serbia and Montenegro with former Habsburg lands under the Serbian crown; it was renamed Yugoslavia in 1929. [ksc-britannica, serbia-yugoslav-kingdom-britannica]
- 1929: **Royal dictatorship and renaming as Yugoslavia** — Amid party deadlock King Alexander suspended the constitution, imposed a royal dictatorship and renamed the state the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, deepening tensions among its nations. [ksc-britannica]
- Apr 1941: **Axis invasion and occupation** — Germany and its allies invaded and dismembered Yugoslavia in April 1941, placing Serbia under occupation and unleashing a multi-sided war of resistance and civil conflict. [tito-britannica, serbia-history-britannica]
- 1945: **Socialist Yugoslavia under Tito** — Tito's Partisans triumphed and built a socialist federation of six republics; after breaking with Stalin in 1948, Yugoslavia pursued non-alignment, with Serbia its largest republic. [tito-britannica]
- 1974: **Autonomy for Vojvodina and Kosovo** — The federal constitution of 1974 granted wide autonomy to the Serbian provinces of Vojvodina and Kosovo, a settlement resented by many Serbs that would ignite conflict after Tito's death. [serbia-history-britannica]

Figures:

- Alexander I Karađorđević (1888 – 1934) — King of Yugoslavia. As prince regent he proclaimed the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes in 1918 and as king imposed a centralising royal dictatorship in 1929, renaming it Yugoslavia. He was assassinated in Marseille in 1934.
- Josip Broz Tito (1892 – 1980) — Partisan leader and President of Yugoslavia. Leader of the communist Partisans in the Second World War, Tito built and ruled socialist Yugoslavia from 1945 until his death. His break with Stalin and policy of non-alignment gave the federation a distinctive place in the Cold War.

## The Breakup of Yugoslavia and the Kosovo War — 1987 – 2000

Slobodan Milošević rose on the Kosovo question, the federation dissolved in war, and the conflict in Kosovo ended with NATO's bombing and the placing of the province under United Nations administration.

- 1989: **Milošević curtails Kosovo's autonomy** — Riding a wave of Serbian nationalism, Slobodan Milošević revoked the autonomy of Kosovo and Vojvodina, deepening the crisis that would break Yugoslavia apart. [milosevic-britannica, serbia-tenyears-britannica]
- 1991–1995: **Wars in Croatia and Bosnia** — As Yugoslavia disintegrated, war engulfed Croatia and Bosnia amid ethnic cleansing and atrocity, with Belgrade backing Serb forces and the federation collapsing into a rump Yugoslavia. [serbia-tenyears-britannica, milosevic-britannica]
- 24 Mar – Jun 1999: **Kosovo War and NATO bombing** — After fighting between Yugoslav forces and the Kosovo Liberation Army and the displacement of Albanian civilians, NATO bombed Yugoslavia for some eleven weeks until Milošević agreed to withdraw from Kosovo. [kosovo-conflict-britannica]
- 10 Jun 1999: **UN Security Council Resolution 1244** — The Security Council placed Kosovo under an international civil (UNMIK) and security presence, granting substantial autonomy while reaffirming the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Yugoslavia. [unsc1244-unmik]
- 5 Oct 2000: **Fall of Milošević** — After disputed elections, mass protests in Belgrade forced Milošević from power; he was later tried at the ICTY in The Hague but died in 2006 before a verdict was reached. [milosevic-britannica, icty-milosevic]

Figures:

- Slobodan Milošević (1941 – 2006) — President of Serbia and of Yugoslavia. A communist functionary turned nationalist leader, Milošević dominated Serbia from 1989 through the wars of Yugoslav succession. Driven from power in 2000, he was tried for war crimes and genocide at the ICTY in The Hague, where he died in 2006 before judgment.
- Vojislav Koštunica (born 1944) — President of Yugoslavia. A moderate nationalist and constitutional lawyer, Koštunica defeated Milošević in the 2000 elections; the popular uprising that confirmed his victory ended Milošević's rule and opened Serbia's democratic transition.

## Modern Serbia and the Kosovo Dispute — 2000 – present

A democratic Serbia parted from Montenegro and turned toward Europe, but the unresolved status of Kosovo — declared independent in 2008 and still rejected by Belgrade — remains the defining question of its statehood.

- Jun 2006: **Montenegro's independence and the end of the State Union** — Following a referendum on 21 May 2006, Montenegro declared independence; Serbia accepted the result and declared itself the successor state, dissolving the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro. [montenegro-independence-britannica, statedept-serbia]
- 17 Feb 2008: **Kosovo declares independence** — Kosovo's assembly declared independence from Serbia, a step recognised by many Western states but rejected by Serbia, which continues to regard Kosovo as its autonomous province. [icj-kosovo, kosovo-conflict-britannica]
- 22 Jul 2010: **ICJ advisory opinion on Kosovo** — The International Court of Justice found that Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence did not violate international law; Serbia continued nonetheless to withhold recognition. [icj-kosovo]
- 19 Apr 2013: **Brussels Agreement** — In the EU-facilitated dialogue, Belgrade and Pristina reached the First Agreement of Principles, integrating Serb-majority northern Kosovo into Kosovo's institutions without constituting Serbian recognition. [brussels-ohrid-eeas]
- Feb–Mar 2023: **Agreement on the Path to Normalisation (Ohrid)** — Serbia and Kosovo agreed to develop normal relations and recognise each other's documents and symbols, with an implementation annex agreed at Ohrid, while leaving the question of status unresolved. [brussels-ohrid-eeas]

Figures:

- Boris Tadić (born 1958) — President of Serbia. President during Montenegro's secession, Kosovo's 2008 declaration and the referral to the ICJ, Tadić steered a pro-European course, oversaw Serbia's EU membership application and rejected Kosovo's independence while pursuing dialogue.
- Aleksandar Vučić (born 1970) — President of Serbia. Dominant in Serbian politics since the 2010s as prime minister and then president, Vučić has led the EU-facilitated dialogue with Pristina, agreeing the 2023 normalisation framework at Ohrid while maintaining Serbia's non-recognition of Kosovo.
