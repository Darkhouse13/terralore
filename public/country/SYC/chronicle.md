# The Chronicle of Seychelles: From empty islands to Creole republic

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 29 June 1976. The Republic of Seychelles became an independent state within the Commonwealth, with James Mancham as its first president and France-Albert René as prime minister.

Seychelles is an archipelago of granitic and coral islands in the western Indian Ocean that had no native human population. Sighted by European navigators in the sixteenth century and long a haunt of pirates, it was claimed by France in 1756, settled from 1770 with enslaved Africans on its plantations, and ceded to Britain in 1814. Out of slavery and the later settlement of Africans rescued from slave ships grew a distinct Creole society. Seychelles became independent on 29 June 1976, passed through a 1977 coup and a one-party socialist era, returned to multiparty democracy in 1993, and today is a high-income nation built on tourism and tuna.

## Empty Islands and Pirates — 1502 – 1755

Sighted by passing Europeans but settled by none, the islands lay empty for two more centuries, frequented chiefly by pirates.

- 1502: **Portuguese navigators sight the islands** — Vasco da Gama's second voyage to India recorded the Amirantes group; further Portuguese sightings followed in the early 1500s, giving Europe its first knowledge of the archipelago. [wiki-history, seyvillas]
- 19 January 1609: **First recorded landing by the Ascension** — Crew of the English East India Company ship Ascension landed and found the islands fertile and uninhabited, but the Company chose not to settle. [wiki-history, holidify]
- early 1700s: **A haunt of pirates** — For a century and a half the empty islands were used by pirates as a hideout and watering place while they preyed on Indian Ocean shipping. [holidify, seyvillas]

## The French Claim and First Settlers — 1756 – 1793

France claimed and named the islands, then planted a settlement of colonists and enslaved Africans on spice and food plantations.

- 1 November 1756: **France claims and names the islands** — Corneille Nicholas Morphey laid a stone of possession and named the archipelago after finance minister Jean Moreau de Séchelles; the main island was named Mahé. [wiki-history, kreol-mag]
- 12 August 1770: **First permanent settlement** — Colonists, enslaved Africans and Indians established the first lasting settlement near Mahé to begin spice cultivation. [wiki-history, seyvillas]
- c. 1771: **Spice and plantation economy** — Pierre Poivre promoted spice gardens to challenge the Dutch monopoly; cotton, coconut, maize and cinnamon followed, all worked by enslaved Africans. [kreol-mag, liquisearch]

Figures:

- Pierre Poivre (1719–1786) — French administrator and naturalist. Intendant of Isle de France who championed Seychelles as a base for spice cultivation to break the Dutch monopoly on cloves and nutmeg.

## British Rule and Emancipation — 1794 – 1902

Captured during the Napoleonic Wars and ceded in 1814, the islands abolished slavery and absorbed Africans freed from slave ships.

- 1794–1811: **Capitulations during the Napoleonic Wars** — Administrator Quéau de Quincy surrendered the islands to British squadrons several times, restoring French rule whenever they left. [wiki-history, seyvillas]
- 1814: **Cession to Britain by the Treaty of Paris** — Seychelles was formally ceded to Britain with Mauritius and governed thereafter as a dependency of Mauritius. [wiki-history, seyvillas]
- 1835: **Abolition of slavery** — Emancipation freed the several thousand enslaved Africans who formed the majority of the colony's population. [wiki-history, sey-museum-slavery]
- 1861–1874: **Liberated Africans resettled** — The Royal Navy landed thousands of Africans rescued from intercepted slave dhows, deepening the islands' Creole society. [sey-museum-slavery, sna-creole]
- 31 August 1903: **Separate Crown Colony** — Seychelles was detached from Mauritius and constituted as a British Crown Colony in its own right. [wiki-history, state-gov-old]

Figures:

- Jean-Baptiste Quéau de Quincy (1748–1827) — French commandant of Seychelles. Pragmatic administrator who repeatedly capitulated to British warships during the Napoleonic Wars while preserving the colony's neutrality and French character.

## An Extraordinary Natural Heritage — 1881 – present

Two World Heritage sites — Aldabra Atoll and the Vallée de Mai — preserve the giant tortoise and the coco de mer.

- 1982: **Aldabra Atoll inscribed by UNESCO** — The raised coral atoll, home to the world's largest giant-tortoise population, was added to the World Heritage List for its outstanding natural value. [unesco-aldabra, sna-aldabra]
- 1983: **Vallée de Mai inscribed by UNESCO** — Praslin's palm forest, where the coco de mer grows wild, was recognised as a World Heritage Site for its unique flora. [unesco-valleedemai, sif-vdm]

## Independence, Coup and One-Party State — 1976 – 1992

Independence in 1976 was followed by a 1977 coup, a one-party socialist state and a failed mercenary raid in 1981.

- 29 June 1976: **Independence from the United Kingdom** — Seychelles became an independent republic within the Commonwealth, with James Mancham as first president and France-Albert René as prime minister. [wiki-history, uca-seychelles]
- 5 June 1977: **Coup brings René to power** — Supporters of France-Albert René overthrew Mancham in an almost bloodless coup while he was abroad; René took the presidency. [wiki-history, uca-seychelles]
- 1979: **One-party socialist state** — A new constitution made the Seychelles People's Progressive Front the sole legal party, with nationalisations and restrictions on the press. [uca-seychelles, ascleiden-mancham]
- 25 November 1981: **Mike Hoare's mercenary raid fails** — Mercenaries disguised as a beer-drinkers' club and backed from South Africa tried to seize power; the plot collapsed and they hijacked an airliner to flee. [wiki-1981coup]

Figures:

- James Mancham (1939–2017) — First President of Seychelles. Pro-Western lawyer and writer who led Seychelles to independence in 1976 and was deposed in the 1977 coup; he returned from exile to help restore multiparty politics.

## Democracy and Prosperity — 1993 – present

A return to multiparty democracy in 1993 underpinned modern Seychelles, a high-income economy built on tourism and tuna.

- 1993: **Return to multiparty democracy** — A new constitution restored multiparty politics; the first competitive presidential elections were held, won by René. [uca-seychelles, ascleiden-mancham]
- c. 2010s: **High-income tourism and tuna economy** — Tourism became the dominant sector and tuna fishing a leading export, lifting Seychelles to high-income status. [wiki-economy, cia-factbook]

Figures:

- France-Albert René (1935–2019) — President of Seychelles (1977–2004). Took power in the 1977 coup and led a one-party socialist state before overseeing the return to multiparty democracy in 1993; he remained president until 2004.
