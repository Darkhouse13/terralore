# The Chronicle of Cambodia: Heir to Angkor and the Khmer Empire, scarred by genocide, reborn as a constitutional kingdom under the shadow of one family's long rule.

Founding: Independence of the Kingdom of Cambodia from France — 9 November 1953. On 9 November 1953 King Norodom Sihanouk formally declared the full independence of Cambodia, completing the transfer of sovereignty from France after his self-styled Royal Crusade for Independence. Cambodia had been a French protectorate since 1863 and part of French Indochina from 1887; over 1953 the French handed back control of the police and judiciary in August and command of the army in October, and on 9 November General de Langlade ceremonially surrendered authority before the Royal Palace in Phnom Penh. The date is celebrated as Cambodia's Independence Day. This modern founding moment stands at the end of a far longer arc of Khmer statehood reaching back through the French protectorate, the long decline after Angkor, and the Khmer Empire founded by Jayavarman II in 802.

Cambodia is the modern heir of one of Southeast Asia's most brilliant civilizations. From the Hinduized kingdoms of Funan and Chenla rose the Khmer Empire (802-1431), whose god-kings raised Angkor Wat and the Bayon and ruled much of mainland Southeast Asia before Thai and Vietnamese pressure forced the abandonment of Angkor. Reduced to a buffer state, Cambodia became a French protectorate in 1863 and part of French Indochina, regaining full independence under King Norodom Sihanouk on 9 November 1953. The country was then swept into the Vietnam War, endured the catastrophic Khmer Rouge genocide of 1975-79, a Vietnamese occupation, and a United Nations transition that restored the monarchy in 1993; it remains a kingdom dominated since the 1980s by Hun Sen and, since 2023, his son Hun Manet.

## Funan and Chenla: The Indianized Kingdoms — 1st century - 802 CE

From the trading ports of the Mekong delta rose Funan, the first great Hinduized kingdom of Southeast Asia, succeeded by Chenla, whose princes laid the foundations of Khmer statehood.

- 1st century CE: **Rise of Funan** — Funan emerges around the Mekong delta as the first major Hinduized kingdom of Southeast Asia, prospering on maritime trade between India and China. [funan]
- 6th century CE: **Chenla supplants Funan** — Chenla, ruled by princes descended from the kings of Funan, absorbs Funan's territory and becomes the dominant power in the region. [chenla, funan]
- late 8th century CE: **Fragmentation of Chenla** — Chenla splits into a Land Chenla and a Water Chenla and loses cohesion, leaving the Khmer lands divided on the eve of the Angkorian unification. [chenla, cambodia-place]

## The Khmer Empire and Angkor — 802 - 1431 CE

From 802 the god-kings of Angkor ruled much of mainland Southeast Asia, raising Angkor Wat and the Bayon in one of the most spectacular flowerings of premodern civilization.

- 802 CE: **Jayavarman II founds the Khmer Empire** — Jayavarman II is consecrated as devaraja and universal ruler on Mahendraparvata, asserting Khmer independence and inaugurating the empire of Angkor. [jayavarman-ii, khmer-empire]
- early 12th century: **Suryavarman II and Angkor Wat** — Suryavarman II reunites the empire and begins Angkor Wat, dedicated to Vishnu and the largest religious structure in the world, completed after his death. [suryavarman-ii, angkor-wat]
- 1181 - c. 1220: **Jayavarman VII rebuilds Angkor Thom and the Bayon** — After repelling a Cham invasion, Jayavarman VII embraces Mahayana Buddhism, expands the empire to its greatest extent, and builds Angkor Thom and the Bayon. [jayavarman-vii, the-bayon]
- 1431 CE: **Thai capture of Angkor** — After repeated Tai attacks, Angkor is captured in 1431 and subsequently abandoned as a capital, marking the end of the imperial era. [khmer-empire, cambodia-khmer-state]

Figures:

- Jayavarman II (reigned from c. 790; consecrated 802 CE) — Founder of the Khmer Empire. Returning from exile, he united the Chenla principalities and in 802 had himself consecrated god-king on Mahendraparvata, founding the empire of Angkor and the cult of the devaraja that would sustain it for centuries.
- Suryavarman II (reigned c. 1113 - c. 1150) — Builder of Angkor Wat. A warlike and ambitious king who reunited the empire after decades of unrest, expanded its frontiers into present-day Thailand, and began Angkor Wat, the largest religious structure in the world.
- Jayavarman VII (c. 1120/25 - c. 1220 CE) — Buddhist god-king of Angkor. One of the most forceful kings of Angkor, he restored the empire after a Cham invasion, adopted Mahayana Buddhism, and built Angkor Thom, the Bayon, and a network of hospitals and highways across the realm.

## Decline, Foreign Pressure, and the French Protectorate — 1431 - 1953 CE

Squeezed for centuries between expanding Siam and Vietnam, a shrunken Cambodia sought protection from France in 1863, becoming a colony within French Indochina until independence in 1953.

- 1863 CE: **French protectorate established** — King Norodom signs a treaty placing Cambodia under French protection to fend off Siamese and Vietnamese pressure; Siam acknowledges the protectorate by 1867. [norodom, cambodia-independence-ebsco]
- 1887 CE: **Cambodia joined to French Indochina** — Cambodia is incorporated into the colonial federation of French Indochina, its monarchy preserved as a figurehead while the French administer the country. [cambodia-independence-ebsco, cambodia-place]
- 9 March 1945: **Japan ends French rule; Sihanouk proclaims independence** — Japan removes the French administration in the closing months of the war, and King Sihanouk briefly proclaims Cambodian independence before France returns. [cambodia-independence-ebsco, cambodia-wwii]
- 9 November 1953: **Full independence declared** — After Sihanouk's Royal Crusade for Independence, France transfers sovereignty and King Norodom Sihanouk declares Cambodia's full independence in Phnom Penh. [cambodia-independence, cambodia-independence-ebsco]

Figures:

- Norodom (reigned 1860-1904) — King who accepted French protection. Facing Siamese and Vietnamese pressure, he signed the 1863 treaty that placed Cambodia under French protection, a step that preserved the kingdom's existence even as it surrendered its sovereignty.

## War, Revolution, and Genocide — 1953 - 1979 CE

Drawn into the Vietnam War despite Sihanouk's neutrality, Cambodia fell to the Khmer Rouge in 1975, whose radical revolution killed perhaps a quarter of the population before Vietnam intervened.

- March 1970: **Lon Nol coup and the Khmer Republic** — While Sihanouk is abroad, the National Assembly removes him and Lon Nol takes power, abolishing the monarchy, proclaiming the Khmer Republic, and joining the war on the U.S. side. [lon-nol, cambodia-civil-war]
- 17 April 1975: **Khmer Rouge capture Phnom Penh** — Pol Pot's Khmer Rouge seize the capital, empty the cities, rename the country Democratic Kampuchea, and abolish money, markets, religion, and private life. [khmer-rouge, cambodian-genocide]
- 1975-1979: **The Cambodian genocide** — Under the Khmer Rouge an estimated 1.5 to 3 million people, about a quarter of the population, die from execution, forced labour, starvation, and disease. [cambodian-genocide, khmer-rouge]
- December 1978 - January 1979: **Vietnamese intervention ends the regime** — Vietnam invades Cambodia, captures Phnom Penh in January 1979, ousts the Khmer Rouge, and ends the genocide, beginning a decade-long occupation. [cambodia-vietnamese-intervention, cambodian-genocide]

Figures:

- Pol Pot (1925-1998) — Leader of the Khmer Rouge. Born Saloth Sar, he led the Khmer Rouge to power in 1975 and presided over Democratic Kampuchea, whose radical agrarian revolution caused the deaths of perhaps a quarter of Cambodia's people before Vietnam ousted the regime in 1979.
- Lon Nol (1913-1985) — President of the Khmer Republic. The general who led the 1970 coup against Sihanouk, abolished the monarchy, and ran the U.S.-aligned Khmer Republic until its collapse and the Khmer Rouge victory in April 1975.

## Occupation, the UN Transition, and the Restored Kingdom — 1979 - 1998 CE

A decade of Vietnamese-backed rule and continued civil war gave way to the 1991 Paris Peace Agreements and a vast United Nations mission that supervised elections and restored the constitutional monarchy in 1993.

- 23 October 1991: **Paris Peace Agreements signed** — The Cambodian factions sign the Paris Agreements on a Comprehensive Political Settlement, ending the war and delegating transitional authority to the United Nations. [paris-agreement, untac]
- 1992-1993: **UNTAC mission** — The UN Transitional Authority in Cambodia repatriates over 300,000 refugees and organizes national elections, one of the largest peacekeeping operations of its era. [untac, cambodia-vietnamese-intervention]
- 1993 CE: **Constitutional monarchy restored** — UN-supervised elections produce a new constitution restoring the kingdom, and Norodom Sihanouk returns to the throne as constitutional monarch. [untac, cambodia-place]
- 1998 CE: **Death of Pol Pot and collapse of the Khmer Rouge** — The Khmer Rouge insurgency disintegrates and Pol Pot dies, ending the movement responsible for the genocide of the 1970s. [pol-pot, khmer-rouge]

Figures:

- Norodom Sihanouk (1922-2012) — King and statesman. Twice king of Cambodia (1941-55 and 1993-2004), he won the country's independence in 1953, dominated its politics for decades, survived the Khmer Rouge years in exile and captivity, and returned to the throne when the monarchy was restored in 1993.

## Modern Cambodia: Hun Sen and Hun Manet — 1998 - present

Stability and growth under the long, increasingly authoritarian rule of Hun Sen gave way in 2023 to a dynastic succession, as his son Hun Manet took the premiership of a one-party-dominated kingdom.

- 1999 CE: **Cambodia joins ASEAN** — Cambodia becomes a member of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, marking its reintegration into regional and international affairs after decades of war and isolation. [cambodia-place]
- 2018 CE: **Opposition dissolved, CPP entrenched** — With the main opposition party dissolved by the courts, Hun Sen's Cambodian People's Party wins every National Assembly seat, consolidating one-party dominance. [hun-sen, cambodia-since-2000]
- 2018-2022: **Khmer Rouge tribunal concludes** — The UN-backed Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia convicts senior Khmer Rouge leaders of crimes against humanity and genocide before concluding its work. [cambodian-genocide]
- 22 August 2023: **Hun Manet succeeds his father** — After 38 years in power, Hun Sen hands the premiership to his son Hun Manet, who is sworn in on 22 August 2023 in a dynastic succession. [hun-manet, hun-sen]

Figures:

- Hun Sen (born 1952) — Long-serving prime minister. Prime minister of Cambodia from 1985 to 2023, a former Khmer Rouge cadre who defected, he built the Cambodian People's Party into a dominant force, presiding over growth and stability alongside deepening authoritarianism before handing power to his son.
- Hun Manet (born 1977) — Prime minister since 2023. Eldest son of Hun Sen and a West Point graduate and army commander, he succeeded his father as prime minister in August 2023, continuing the family's and the Cambodian People's Party's hold on power.
