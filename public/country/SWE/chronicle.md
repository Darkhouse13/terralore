# The Chronicle of Sweden: From Viking traders on the eastern rivers to a Baltic empire, a long neutrality, and at last into NATO.

Founding: Gustav Vasa elected King of Sweden — 6 June 1523. The modern Swedish state is conventionally dated to 6 June 1523, when the Estates meeting at Strängnäs elected Gustav Eriksson Vasa king of Sweden. His election was the culmination of a war of liberation against King Christian II of Denmark, whose 1520 massacre of the Swedish elite, the Stockholm Bloodbath, had killed Gustav's own father and turned the country against Danish rule. With his accession Sweden left the Kalmar Union for good and embarked on the building of a unified, hereditary monarchy: Gustav Vasa nationalised the Catholic Church, broke with Rome, founded a dynasty and laid the administrative foundations of the state that would soon rise to dominate the Baltic. The date is still treated as the birth of the modern kingdom, and its quincentenary was marked across Sweden in 2023.

Sweden's history runs from the seafaring Norsemen who carved trade routes east toward Byzantium and the Caliphate, through a medieval Christian kingdom drawn into and then out of a Scandinavian union, to a brief but dazzling career as a great power straddling the Baltic. The modern Swedish state is conventionally dated to 1523, when Gustav Vasa was elected king after a war of liberation that shattered the Danish-led Kalmar Union, and went on to break with Rome and build a centralised Lutheran monarchy. In the seventeenth century, under Gustavus Adolphus and his successors, Sweden intervened decisively in the Thirty Years' War, won territory at the Peace of Westphalia and seized Scania from Denmark, becoming the dominant power of the north until the catastrophe of Poltava in 1709 brought the empire down. The nineteenth century reshaped Sweden again: it lost Finland to Russia in 1809, gained and then peacefully relinquished Norway, founded the Bernadotte dynasty, and from 1814 embraced a long tradition of neutrality. That tradition outlasted two world wars and the Cold War, until Russia's invasion of Ukraine led Sweden to abandon two centuries of non-alignment and join NATO in March 2024.

## Vikings, Birka and the Coming of Christianity — c. 750 – 1150

Seafaring Norsemen of the Mälaren valley turned east, founding trading towns and river routes toward Byzantium before missionaries brought the cross to a pagan land.

- c. 750–970: **The trading town of Birka** — Birka on the island of Björkö in Lake Mälaren flourished as a key Baltic trading settlement, connecting Scandinavia by river route to the Byzantine Empire and the Islamic world, before its abandonment in the late tenth century. [birka-unesco, sweden-history-britannica]
- c. 829–831: **Ansgar's mission and the first Christian congregation** — The Frankish missionary Ansgar founded at Birka the earliest known Christian congregation in Sweden, the first organised Christian presence among a still largely pagan people. [birka-unesco, sweden-history-britannica]
- 9th–11th c.: **The Rus and the eastern river routes** — Swedish Norsemen, called Varangians or Rus, opened and dominated the river trade routes east through Ladoga and Novgorod toward Constantinople and Baghdad, exchanging furs, silver and slaves. [birka-unesco, sweden-history-britannica]
- c. 1008: **Christianization advances under royal patronage** — The conversion of the Swedes proceeded through the eleventh century by way of baptised kings and the founding of churches, even as paganism endured at the temple of Uppsala until the faith finally prevailed. [sweden-history-britannica]

Figures:

- Ansgar (801 – 865) — Missionary, 'Apostle of the North'. A Frankish monk dispatched to Scandinavia, Ansgar established at Birka around 829–831 the first known Christian congregation in Sweden. Though paganism long endured, he is remembered as the apostle who first brought the cross to the Swedes.
- The Rus traders (9th–11th centuries) — Varangian merchants and travellers. Swedish Norsemen who followed the eastern rivers as far as Byzantium and the Caspian, the Rus built a far-reaching trade network in silver and furs and lent their name to the lands they crossed, a defining mark of Sweden's Viking Age.

## The Kalmar Union, the Bloodbath and the War of Liberation — 1397 – 1523

Three Nordic crowns were joined under one monarch, but Danish dominance bred Swedish revolt — until a massacre in Stockholm lit the rebellion that made Gustav Vasa king.

- Jun 1397: **Formation of the Kalmar Union** — Queen Margaret united Denmark, Norway and Sweden under one monarch, her grandnephew Erik of Pomerania, creating a Scandinavian union intended to resist German power but soon dominated by Denmark. [kalmar-britannica, historiska-kalmar]
- 15th c.: **Swedish revolts and the Sture regents** — Resentment of Danish dominance and of taxes and offices given to royal favourites fuelled recurring Swedish risings, with regents of the Sture family ruling Sweden in defiance of the union crown. [historiska-kalmar, sweden-history-britannica]
- 8–10 Nov 1520: **The Stockholm Bloodbath** — After capturing Stockholm and breaking his promise of amnesty, King Christian II of Denmark had some eighty-two Swedish nobles, clergy and citizens condemned as heretics and executed, including Gustav Vasa's father. [bloodbath-britannica, historiska-bloodbath]
- 1521–1523: **The Swedish War of Liberation** — Gustav Vasa raised Dalarna in revolt against Christian II, leading a peasant army that expelled the Danes and was declared regent of Sweden, ending Danish control of the realm. [vasa-britannica, sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]
- 6 Jun 1523: **Gustav Vasa elected king** — The Estates meeting at Strängnäs elected Gustav Vasa king of Sweden, dissolving the Kalmar Union and founding the hereditary Vasa monarchy that is regarded as the start of the modern Swedish state. [vasa-britannica, riksdagen-decisions, sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]

Figures:

- Margaret I (1353 – 1412) — Queen and founder of the Kalmar Union. Ruler of Denmark, Norway and Sweden, Margaret united the three crowns at Kalmar in 1397 under her grandnephew Erik of Pomerania, governing the Nordic realms as a single power until her death in 1412.
- Christian II (1481 – 1559) — King of Denmark and the Kalmar Union. The last union king to rule Sweden, Christian II provoked the country's break with the union through the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520, in which he had the Swedish elite executed, triggering the revolt that cost him the Swedish crown.

## The Vasa Monarchy and the Swedish Reformation — 1523 – 1611

Gustav Vasa forged a centralised hereditary kingdom, broke with Rome and turned the Church to the service of the crown, laying the foundations of a Lutheran state.

- 1523–1560: **Reign of Gustav I Vasa** — Gustav Vasa built a centralised national monarchy, restored royal finances, suppressed revolts and founded the Vasa dynasty, ruling for nearly four decades as the architect of the modern Swedish kingdom. [vasa-britannica, sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]
- 1527: **The Reformation at Västerås** — At the Riksdag of Västerås, Gustav Vasa broke with Rome, transferred the Catholic Church's property to the crown and took control of church administration, beginning Sweden's transformation into a Lutheran state. [historiska-reformation, riksdagen-decisions, sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]
- 1541: **The complete Swedish Bible** — The first full Bible in Swedish, the work of the reformers Olaus and Laurentius Petri, was printed, advancing the Lutheran cause and shaping the Swedish written language and popular literacy. [historiska-reformation]
- 1544: **The crown made hereditary** — The Estates declared the Swedish crown hereditary in the Vasa line, ending the older elective principle and entrenching the dynasty's hold on the throne. [sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]

Figures:

- Gustav I Vasa (1496 – 1560) — Founder of the modern Swedish state. Elected king in 1523 after leading the war of liberation, Gustav Vasa broke with Rome, built a centralised hereditary monarchy and founded the dynasty that would raise Sweden to greatness. He is regarded as the father of the modern nation.
- Olaus Petri (1493 – 1552) — Reformer and theologian. The leading preacher of the Swedish Reformation, Olaus Petri spread Lutheran teaching, helped translate the scriptures into Swedish and shaped the liturgy and language of the new state church under Gustav Vasa.

## Stormaktstiden: The Swedish Empire and Its Fall — 1611 – 1721

Under Gustavus Adolphus and his heirs Sweden became the master of the Baltic, winning empire on the battlefields of Germany — only to lose it all on the field of Poltava.

- Jul 1630: **Sweden enters the Thirty Years' War** — Gustavus Adolphus landed in Pomerania and intervened on the Protestant side, turning the tide of the Thirty Years' War with a series of victories before his death at Lützen in 1632. [westphalia-britannica, sweden-vasa-kings-britannica]
- 1648: **Peace of Westphalia** — The settlement ending the Thirty Years' War granted Sweden Western Pomerania, Bremen and Verden and a vote in the imperial diet, confirming it as a dominant power on the Baltic and in northern Germany. [westphalia-britannica]
- 26 Feb 1658: **Treaty of Roskilde** — After Charles X Gustav's invasion of Denmark, the Treaty of Roskilde forced Denmark to cede Scania, Blekinge, Halland and Bohuslän to Sweden, fixing its southern border and marking the empire's territorial peak. [roskilde-britannica]
- 27 Jun 1709: **Battle of Poltava** — Peter the Great of Russia routed Charles XII's invading army at Poltava in Ukraine, a decisive defeat that broke Swedish military power and began Russian supremacy in eastern Europe. [poltava-britannica]
- 30 Aug 1721: **Treaty of Nystad ends the empire** — The peace ending the Great Northern War ceded Sweden's Baltic provinces — Estonia, Livonia, Ingria and part of Karelia — to Russia, ending the age of greatness, though Finland was restored to Sweden. [nystad-reference, poltava-britannica]

Figures:

- Gustavus Adolphus (1594 – 1632) — King and founder of the Swedish Empire. Ascending in 1611, Gustavus Adolphus reformed the army and state and led Sweden into the Thirty Years' War in 1630, winning victories that made his kingdom a great power before he fell at Lützen in 1632.
- Charles XII (1682 – 1718) — Warrior king of the falling empire. A gifted and relentless commander, Charles XII defended the empire against a hostile coalition in the Great Northern War, but his catastrophic defeat at Poltava in 1709 doomed Sweden's great-power era. He died in 1718.

## Loss of Finland, Union with Norway and the Birth of Neutrality — 1721 – 1905

A diminished Sweden lost Finland to Russia, gained Norway under a new French-born dynasty, and turned to the neutrality and emigration that would mark its modern age.

- 17 Sep 1809: **Treaty of Fredrikshamn: the loss of Finland** — Ending the Finnish War, the Treaty of Fredrikshamn (Hamina) ceded the whole of Finland and the Åland Islands to Russia, stripping Sweden of about a third of its territory and people. [hamina-britannica, sweden-history-britannica]
- 1810: **Bernadotte chosen as heir** — The French marshal Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte was elected Swedish crown prince, founding the Bernadotte dynasty; he ruled from 1818 as Charles XIV John, king of Sweden and Norway. [kiel-britannica, sweden-history-britannica]
- 14 Jan 1814: **Treaty of Kiel and the union with Norway** — Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden by the Treaty of Kiel; Norwegian resistance produced not annexation but a personal union of the two kingdoms under one crown, lasting until 1905. [kiel-britannica, norway-union-britannica]
- 1840s–1920s: **The great emigration to America** — Driven by rural poverty and population growth, roughly 1.3 million Swedes emigrated, chiefly to the United States, in one of the largest population movements of the age, reshaping Swedish society at home. [loc-swedes]
- 1905: **Peaceful dissolution of the union with Norway** — After Norway's parliament declared the union dissolved, Sweden agreed at Karlstad to a peaceful separation, recognising Norwegian independence without war in a settlement held up as a diplomatic model. [norway-union-britannica]

Figures:

- Charles XIV John (Bernadotte) (1763 – 1844) — King and founder of the present dynasty. Born Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte, a marshal of Napoleon, he was elected Swedish heir in 1810 and became king in 1818. He secured Norway, founded the reigning Bernadotte dynasty and set Sweden on its long course of neutrality.
- Gustav III (1746 – 1792) — Enlightened and absolutist king. Through the coup of 1772 Gustav III ended the Age of Liberty and restored royal power, presiding over a brilliant cultural age before being assassinated at a masked ball in 1792.

## Neutrality, the People's Home and the Road to NATO — 1905 – present

Sweden stayed out of two world wars, built a celebrated welfare state, joined the European Union, and at last in 2024 abandoned two centuries of non-alignment for NATO.

- 1918–1921: **Universal suffrage and democracy** — Sweden completed its democratic transformation, introducing universal and equal suffrage for men and women, as the Riksdag rather than the crown became the centre of political power. [riksdagen-decisions, welfare-britannica]
- 1928: **The 'people's home' (folkhemmet)** — Per Albin Hansson set out the vision of Sweden as a folkhemmet, a good home built on equality and mutual care, which the Social Democrats made the foundation of the modern welfare state. [welfare-britannica]
- 1939–1945: **Neutrality in the Second World War** — Sweden preserved its neutrality through the Second World War, remaining unoccupied while continuing controversial trade with Germany and offering refuge to those fleeing Nazi persecution. [welfare-britannica, sweden-history-britannica]
- 1 Jan 1995: **Accession to the European Union** — After the end of the Cold War made strict neutrality obsolete, Sweden joined the European Union, while choosing to keep its own currency rather than adopt the euro. [welfare-britannica, sweden-history-britannica]
- 7 Mar 2024: **Sweden joins NATO** — Prompted by Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Sweden abandoned over two centuries of non-alignment and became NATO's 32nd member on 7 March 2024, gaining the protection of Article 5. [nato-sweden, nato-flag]

Figures:

- Per Albin Hansson (1885 – 1946) — Prime Minister and architect of the welfare state. Social Democratic prime minister for most of 1932–1946, Hansson gave Sweden the idea of the folkhemmet, the 'people's home', and steered the country through the Second World War as a neutral, laying the foundations of its welfare model.
- Dag Hammarskjöld (1905 – 1961) — Diplomat and UN Secretary-General. A Swedish economist and statesman, Hammarskjöld served as the second Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1953 until his death in 1961, embodying Sweden's postwar identity as a neutral champion of international order.
