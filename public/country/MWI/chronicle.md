Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 6 July 1964. On 6 July 1964 the British protectorate of Nyasaland became the independent state of Malawi, a member of the Commonwealth, with Hastings Kamuzu Banda as its first prime minister. The name 'Malawi' was deliberately chosen to evoke the Maravi (Malawi) Confederacy, the Chewa-led state that had flourished on the shores of Lake Nyasa from roughly the late fifteenth century, and the lake itself was renamed Lake Malawi. The United States recognised the new nation the same day by elevating its consulate at Blantyre to embassy status. Independence was the culmination of a long struggle: the Chilembwe rising of 1915, the founding of the Nyasaland African Congress in 1944, and above all the fierce African opposition to the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland, dissolved at the end of 1963. Two years later, in 1966, Malawi became a republic with Banda as president, and in 1971 he had himself declared president for life.

Malawi takes both its name and its heart from Lake Malawi (Lake Nyasa), the great body of water along which the Maravi (Malawi) Confederacy of the Chewa-speaking peoples rose around the late fifteenth century. Bantu-speaking farmers had settled the Malawi plateau over the preceding centuries, and the women of the Chewa left a living tradition of rock painting at Chongoni, now a UNESCO World Heritage site. In the nineteenth century the Yao, Tumbuka and the Ngoni — refugees of the southern African Mfecane — reshaped the region, and a brutal Swahili-Arab and Yao slave trade scarred the lake. The explorer David Livingstone reached Lake Nyasa in 1859, and the Scottish Presbyterian missions he inspired at Livingstonia and Blantyre campaigned against the trade and trained an African elite. Britain proclaimed the British Central Africa Protectorate in 1891, renamed Nyasaland in 1907. John Chilembwe's short-lived 1915 rising against colonial rule and forced labour made him an early nationalist martyr, and African opposition to the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland (1953–1963) fuelled the independence movement led by Hastings Kamuzu Banda. Malawi became independent of the United Kingdom on 6 July 1964; Banda ruled as autocratic life-president until a 1993 referendum and the 1994 multiparty elections opened a democratic era of contested, sometimes turbulent, but repeatedly peaceful transfers of power.

## Bantu farmers and the rock-painters of Chongoni — c. 1st – 15th centuries CE

Over the first centuries of the Common Era Bantu-speaking farmers settled the Malawi plateau around the great lake, where earlier hunter-gatherers and later Chewa women left an enduring tradition of rock painting at Chongoni.

- c. 1st–4th centuries CE: **Bantu-speaking farmers settle the plateau** — Bantu-speaking peoples moved into the Lake Malawi region during the early centuries of the Common Era, introducing ironworking and settled agriculture and absorbing or displacing earlier hunter-gatherer communities. [wiki-history-malawi, britannica-malawi]
- Late Iron Age: **The Chewa rock-painting tradition at Chongoni** — Chewa farming communities, especially women, developed a distinctive rock-painting tradition on the granite hills of the central plateau, linked to initiation, funerals and rainmaking, building on far older paintings by BaTwa hunter-gatherers. [unesco-chongoni, wiki-chongoni]

## The Maravi Confederacy — c. 1480 – 1700

From the late fifteenth century the Chewa-led Maravi (Malawi) Confederacy, ruled by the Kalonga, spread out from the southwestern shores of Lake Nyasa — the state from which the modern country takes its name.

- c. 1480: **Founding of the Maravi Confederacy** — The Amaravi, ancestors of the Chewa, established a confederacy under the Kalonga on the southwestern shores of Lake Nyasa, ruling from Mankhamba — the state that gave Malawi its name. [britannica-maravi, wiki-maravi, wiki-history-malawi]
- 1616: **Gaspar Bocarro crosses the region** — The Portuguese traveller Gaspar Bocarro journeyed through the territory, leaving what is generally regarded as the first European account of the land and peoples of the future Malawi. [wiki-history-malawi]
- c. 17th century: **The Maravi Empire at its peak** — In the seventeenth century the confederacy administered a broad domain reaching across parts of modern Malawi, Mozambique and Zambia, trading ivory and iron with Portuguese and Swahili merchants on the coast. [britannica-maravi, wiki-maravi, adf-maravi]
- early 18th century: **Decline of the confederacy** — Fighting among the sub-chiefs and the spreading slave trade eroded the Kalonga's authority, and the Maravi state fragmented into smaller Chewa chiefdoms. [britannica-maravi, wiki-maravi]

## Migrations, missionaries and the slave trade — c. 1800 – 1890

The nineteenth century brought the Yao, the Ngoni and the Tumbuka, a devastating Swahili-Arab and Yao slave trade on the lake, and the explorer David Livingstone, whose example planted the Scottish Presbyterian missions that fought the trade.

- c. 1820s–1850s: **Ngoni and Yao migrations reshape the region** — Ngoni warrior bands fleeing the Mfecane settled around Lake Nyasa, while the Yao migrated from northern Mozambique and the Tumbuka occupied the northern highlands, overlaying new peoples on the older Chewa chiefdoms. [britannica-malawi, wiki-history-malawi]
- c. 1840: **The Jumbe and the slave trade at Nkhotakota** — The Swahili-Arab trader Salim bin Abdallah, the Jumbe, established himself at Nkhotakota, which became a major slaving entrepôt on the western lakeshore; the Yao became leading participants in the trade across the lake. [britannica-malawi, wiki-history-malawi]
- September 1859: **Livingstone reaches Lake Nyasa** — David Livingstone, on the Zambezi expedition, reached Lake Nyasa and reported it as a corridor of the slave caravans, calling for commerce and Christianity to end the trade. [britannica-livingstone, wiki-umca]
- 1875–1876: **Scottish missions founded at Livingstonia and Blantyre** — Inspired by Livingstone, the Free Church of Scotland established the Livingstonia mission in 1875 and the Church of Scotland the Blantyre mission in 1876; both campaigned against the slave trade and trained an African Christian elite. [wiki-livingstonia, britannica-livingstone, wiki-christianity-malawi]

Figures:

- David Livingstone (1813–1873) — Scottish explorer, missionary and abolitionist. Reached Lake Nyasa in 1859 and exposed the Central African slave trade to British opinion; his call for 'Commerce, Christianity and Civilization' to end the trade inspired the Scottish missions at Livingstonia and Blantyre.

## Protectorate, Nyasaland and the Chilembwe rising — 1891 – 1944

Britain proclaimed the British Central Africa Protectorate in 1891, renamed Nyasaland in 1907; in 1915 the minister John Chilembwe led a short-lived rising against colonial rule and forced labour, becoming an early nationalist martyr.

- 1891: **British Central Africa Protectorate proclaimed** — Britain proclaimed a protectorate over the lake region and the Shire highlands, with Harry Johnston as first commissioner; he suppressed the slave trade by force and parcelled out land to European settlers through certificates of claim. [wiki-bca-protectorate, britannica-johnston]
- 1907: **The protectorate renamed Nyasaland** — The British Central Africa Protectorate was renamed the Nyasaland Protectorate, the name by which the territory would be known until independence. [wiki-bca-protectorate, britannica-malawi]
- 23 January 1915: **The Chilembwe rising** — Reverend John Chilembwe led an uprising against colonial rule, forced labour and wartime conscription; his followers attacked the A. L. Bruce estate at Magomero and killed William Jervis Livingstone before British forces swiftly suppressed the revolt. [britannica-chilembwe, blackpast-chilembwe, wiki-chilembwe]
- February 1915: **Chilembwe killed** — John Chilembwe was shot dead as he fled toward Mozambique; though the rising failed, he became an enduring symbol of Malawian nationalism and is honoured as a national martyr. [britannica-chilembwe, blackpast-chilembwe]
- 1944: **Founding of the Nyasaland African Congress** — African political associations coalesced into the Nyasaland African Congress, the first territory-wide nationalist body, which would later spearhead opposition to federation. [britannica-malawi, wiki-history-malawi]

Figures:

- John Chilembwe (c. 1871–1915) — Baptist minister and nationalist leader. American-educated founder of the Providence Industrial Mission who led the 1915 rising against colonial rule, forced labour and wartime conscription; killed within weeks, he is revered as an early martyr of Malawian nationalism.

## Federation, Banda and independence — 1953 – 1971

African opposition to the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland (1953–1963) galvanised the independence movement under Hastings Kamuzu Banda; Malawi became independent on 6 July 1964, and by 1971 Banda had made himself president for life.

- 1953: **Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland created** — Britain bound Nyasaland into a settler-dominated federation with Southern and Northern Rhodesia, provoking deep and sustained African opposition across Nyasaland. [britannica-federation, ebsco-federation]
- March 1959: **State of emergency and Operation Sunrise** — Amid mounting agitation against federation, the governor declared a state of emergency and detained Banda and over a thousand Congress members; the Devlin Commission later condemned the use of force and likened Nyasaland to a police state. [wiki-nyasaland-emergency, wiki-devlin]
- 1961: **Malawi Congress Party wins the elections** — Banda's Malawi Congress Party won the 1961 legislative elections decisively, securing African control of the colonial legislature and accelerating the path to self-government. [britannica-malawi, britannica-banda]
- 31 December 1963: **The Federation dissolved** — Faced with African opposition and irreconcilable tensions, the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland was dissolved at the end of 1963, clearing the way for the separate independence of Malawi, Zambia and Rhodesia. [britannica-federation, ebsco-federation]
- 6 July 1964: **Malawi independent** — Nyasaland became the independent state of Malawi within the Commonwealth, with Hastings Kamuzu Banda as prime minister; the United States recognised the new nation the same day. [statedept-malawi, britannica-malawi, britannica-banda]
- 1971: **Banda declared President for Life** — After Malawi became a republic in 1966 with the Malawi Congress Party as sole legal party, the National Assembly in 1971 proclaimed Banda President for Life, completing the one-party state. [britannica-banda, blackpast-banda]

## The Banda regime and the democratic transition — 1971 – present

Hastings Banda ruled Malawi as an austere life-president for three decades; a 1993 referendum and the 1994 multiparty elections ended his regime and opened a democratic era of contested but repeatedly peaceful transfers of power.

- 1971–1993: **Three decades of one-party rule** — Banda ruled as life-president through the Malawi Congress Party, detaining, exiling or killing opponents and maintaining a tightly controlled, conservative and pro-Western state. [britannica-banda, blackpast-banda]
- 14 June 1993: **Referendum ends the one-party state** — Malawians voted by roughly two to one to abandon single-party rule and adopt multiparty democracy, opening the way to free elections. [wiki-1993-referendum, britannica-malawi]
- 17 May 1994: **First multiparty elections; Banda defeated** — In the first multiparty general election since independence, Bakili Muluzi of the United Democratic Front defeated Hastings Banda, who peacefully gave up power after three decades. [wiki-1994-election, britannica-banda]
- 2012: **Joyce Banda becomes head of state** — After the death in office of President Bingu wa Mutharika, Vice-President Joyce Banda succeeded him, becoming Malawi's first woman president. [wiki-joyce-banda, wiki-bingu]
- 2020: **Court-ordered election re-run** — After the Constitutional Court annulled the 2019 presidential election for irregularities, Lazarus Chakwera won the court-ordered re-run, a rare judicial reversal of a presidential result on the continent. [wiki-2020-election, wiki-chakwera]
- September 2025: **Peaceful alternation of power** — Peter Mutharika won the 2025 general election, defeating the incumbent Lazarus Chakwera, who conceded — marking another peaceful transfer of power in multiparty Malawi. [wiki-2025-election]

Figures:

- Hastings Kamuzu Banda (c. 1898–1997) — First President of Malawi. British-trained physician who led Malawi to independence in 1964 and ruled as autocratic life-president from 1971 until his defeat in the 1994 multiparty election; he relinquished power peacefully and died in 1997.
- Bakili Muluzi (born 1943) — President of Malawi, 1994–2004. United Democratic Front leader who defeated Banda in the 1994 election to become Malawi's first democratically elected president of the multiparty era, serving two terms.
