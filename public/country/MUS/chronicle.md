# The Chronicle of Mauritius: An empty island remade by every ocean that touched it

History of Mauritius: An empty island remade by every ocean that touched it. 6 eras and 21 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/MUS/chronicle
Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 12 March 1968. Mauritius became an independent sovereign state within the Commonwealth on 12 March 1968, ending British colonial rule. Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam, leader of the Labour Party, became its first prime minister. The island became a republic on 12 March 1992.

Mauritius had no native human population: it was known to Arab and Malay sailors and visited by the Portuguese before the Dutch settled it in 1598, naming it for Maurice of Nassau and hunting its flightless dodo to extinction. France built a sugar-and-slavery colony, the Isle de France, around the port it founded at Port Louis; Britain seized the island in 1810 and kept it as 'Mauritius'. After slavery was abolished in 1835, the British imported nearly half a million Indian indentured labourers, reshaping the island's people. Independent since 12 March 1968 and a republic since 1992, Mauritius became a diversified, multi-ethnic democracy frequently cited as an African success story, while pressing a long sovereignty claim over the Chagos Archipelago.

## The Empty Island & Its First Visitors — Antiquity – 1598

Mauritius is one of the few inhabited places on Earth with no indigenous population: a volcanic island left empty until ocean-going sailors began to call.

- medieval period: **Known to Arab and Malay sailors** — Mariners crossing the Indian Ocean are thought to have known the uninhabited island, reputedly recorded on Arab maps as 'Dina Arobi'. [eb-mauritius, wiki-mauritius, govmu-explore]
- c. 1507–1512: **Portuguese chart the Mascarenes** — Portuguese navigators sight and map Mauritius and the neighbouring islands as a provisioning stop, but found no colony. [wiki-mauritius, eb-mauritius]

## The Dutch & the Dodo — 1598 – 1710

The Dutch gave the island its name and the world a byword for extinction, but never made the colony pay, and abandoned it after little more than a century.

- 1598: **The Dutch name the island Mauritius** — Admiral Wybrand van Warwijck claims the island and names it for Maurice of Nassau, stadtholder of the Dutch Republic. [eb-mauritius, govmu-explore, wiki-mauritius]
- 1638: **First permanent Dutch settlement** — The Dutch East India Company establishes a settlement, exploiting ebony forests and introducing sugar cane and livestock. [wiki-mauritius, eb-mauritius]
- 1660s: **Extinction of the dodo** — Hunting, deforestation and introduced animals wipe out the flightless dodo within decades of human arrival; last accepted sightings fall in the 1660s. [nhm-dodo, wiki-dodo]
- 1710: **The Dutch abandon Mauritius** — After unprofitable settlements plagued by storms, pests and disease, the Dutch East India Company leaves the island. [eb-mauritius, wiki-mauritius]

## Isle de France: Sugar & Slavery — 1715 – 1810

France claimed the empty island as the Isle de France, founded Port Louis, and built a plantation economy on the forced labour of enslaved Africans and Malagasy.

- 1715: **France claims the Isle de France** — France takes formal possession of the uninhabited island, renaming it Isle de France; colonisation begins under the French East India Company from 1721. [eb-mauritius, wiki-isledefrance]
- 1735–1736: **La Bourdonnais founds Port Louis** — Governor Mahé de La Bourdonnais develops Port Louis as a fortified naval base, dockyard and capital, anchoring French power in the Indian Ocean. [eb-mauritius, wiki-isledefrance]
- 18th century: **A slave-worked plantation economy** — Enslaved Africans, Malagasy and others are imported in large numbers to work sugar and other estates; they become the majority of the population. [wiki-isledefrance, unesco-lemorne]
- 18th – early 19th century: **Maroons of Le Morne Brabant** — Escaped slaves take refuge on the isolated cliffs of Le Morne, later inscribed by UNESCO as a symbol of the struggle for freedom. [unesco-lemorne, wiki-lemorne]

Figures:

- Mahé de La Bourdonnais (1699–1753) — Governor of the Isle de France. French governor from 1735 who built Port Louis into a naval base and developed the island's economy and infrastructure.

## British Mauritius: Abolition & Indenture — 1810 – 1910

Britain captured the island in 1810; after abolishing slavery in 1835 it imported nearly half a million indentured Indians, remaking the island's people for good.

- August–December 1810: **British capture of the Isle de France** — After the French naval victory at Grand Port, a large British force lands and compels the surrender of the island in December 1810. [eb-grandport, eb-mauritius]
- 1814: **Treaty of Paris confirms British rule** — Britain retains the island and renames it Mauritius, while French settlers keep their language, laws and religion. [eb-mauritius, wiki-mauritius]
- 1 February 1835: **Abolition of slavery** — Slavery is formally ended in Mauritius, freeing tens of thousands of enslaved people, followed by a transitional 'apprenticeship'. [eb-mauritius, unesco-lemorne]
- 1834–c. 1910: **Indian indenture and the Aapravasi Ghat** — Around half a million indentured labourers from India arrive through the Port Louis depot, the 'Great Experiment', reshaping the island's population. [eb-aapravasi, unesco-aapravasi]

## The Road to Independence — 1910 – 1968

A sugar-island society of descendants of the enslaved and the indentured organised, won the vote, and steered a divided colony to independence in 1968.

- 1936: **Founding of the Labour Party** — The Mauritius Labour Party is founded, channelling demands of workers and the indentured-descended majority for rights and representation. [blackpast-ramgoolam, eb-mauritius]
- 1965: **Chagos detached as the BIOT** — Before independence, Britain separates the Chagos Archipelago to create the British Indian Ocean Territory, leasing Diego Garcia for a US base. [eb-chagos, wiki-expulsion]
- 12 March 1968: **Independence from the United Kingdom** — Mauritius becomes an independent state within the Commonwealth, with Seewoosagur Ramgoolam as first prime minister. [eb-independence, blackpast-ramgoolam]

Figures:

- Seewoosagur Ramgoolam (1900–1985) — First Prime Minister of Mauritius. Labour leader and physician who led Mauritius to independence in 1968 and governed the young state through its first decades.

## Republic & African Success Story — 1968 – present

From a one-crop colony, Mauritius built a diversified economy and a durable multi-ethnic democracy, while pursuing its claim to the Chagos Archipelago.

- 1970s onward: **Diversification beyond sugar** — An export-processing zone, tourism and financial services transform a one-crop colony into a diversified, growing economy. [eb-independence, worldbank-mauritius]
- 12 March 1992: **Mauritius becomes a republic** — The island replaces the British monarch with a Mauritian president as head of state, retaining its parliamentary democracy. [eb-independence, wiki-mauritius]
- 2019: **ICJ advisory opinion on Chagos** — The International Court of Justice finds Britain's continued administration of the Chagos Archipelago unlawful; the UN General Assembly concurs. [eb-chagos, commons-chagos]
- 22 May 2025: **UK–Mauritius Chagos agreement** — The two states sign a treaty recognising Mauritian sovereignty over the archipelago while leasing the Diego Garcia base to the UK for an initial 99 years. [commons-chagos, fcdo-chagos]

Figures:

- Sir Anerood Jugnauth (1930–2021) — Prime Minister and later President. Dominant figure of post-1982 politics who oversaw much of the island's economic diversification and the transition to a republic in 1992.
