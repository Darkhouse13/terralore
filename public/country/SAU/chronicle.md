# The Chronicle of Saudi Arabia: From the incense roads of ancient Arabia and the cradle of Islam to the oil-fueled kingdom of the Al Saud.

Founding: Proclamation of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia — 23 September 1932. On 23 September 1932 the regions of Najd and the Hejaz, conquered and consolidated over three decades by Abdulaziz ibn Saud (Ibn Saud), were merged and renamed the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia by royal decree. The kingdom's deeper origins lie in the birth of Islam in the Hejaz in the 7th century, when the Prophet Muhammad preached in Mecca and Medina, and in the First Saudi State founded in 1744 at Diriyah through the alliance between the Al Saud ruler Muhammad ibn Saud and the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.

Saudi Arabia occupies the heart of the Arabian Peninsula, a land of deserts and oases that gave rise to ancient caravan kingdoms, became the birthplace of Islam in the 7th century, and was unified into a single modern state in 1932. The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was proclaimed by Abdulaziz ibn Saud after a thirty-year campaign that began with his capture of Riyadh in 1902. Its roots reach back to the religious and political alliance forged in 1744 at Diriyah between the Al Saud and the reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab. The discovery of oil in 1938 transformed a poor pilgrimage economy into one of the world's largest energy exporters, and the kingdom remains custodian of Islam's holiest cities, Mecca and Medina.

## Ancient Arabia and the Caravan Kingdoms — c. 3000 BCE - 6th century CE

Long before Islam, the Arabian Peninsula was crossed by incense roads that sustained wealthy oasis kingdoms and linked southern Arabia to the Mediterranean and Mesopotamia.

- c. 3000 BCE: **The Dilmun trading culture flourishes** — Around the eastern shores of the Arabian Peninsula, the Dilmun culture became a hub linking Mesopotamia with the Indus valley, remembered in Sumerian literature as a land of prosperity and long life. [majalla-kingdoms]
- c. 5th century BCE: **The kingdom of Lihyan rises at Dadan** — The Lihyanite kingdom, centered on Dadan in the AlUla region, grew wealthy from the incense trade and tribute, developing its own language, script, and irrigated agriculture. [wiki-lihyan, majalla-kingdoms]
- c. 1st century BCE: **The Nabataeans make Hegra their southern capital** — The Nabataean kingdom extended south and developed Hegra (al-Hijr) as a major caravan city, carving monumental rock-cut tombs that survive as the second-largest Nabataean site after Petra. [unesco-hegra, saudipedia-hegra]
- 106 CE: **Rome annexes the Nabataean kingdom** — The Roman Empire absorbed the Nabataean realm, after which Hegra and the northern caravan cities gradually declined as trade routes shifted. [unesco-hegra, majalla-kingdoms]

Figures:

- Aretas IV (reigned 9 BCE - 40 CE) — Nabataean king. The most prominent Nabataean monarch, under whom Hegra reached its peak as a caravan city and the great rock-cut tombs were carved.

## The Birth of Islam: Muhammad, Mecca and Medina — c. 570 - 632 CE

In the early 7th century the Prophet Muhammad began preaching a strict monotheism in Mecca; his migration to Medina in 622 founded the first Muslim community and reshaped world history.

- c. 570 CE: **Birth of Muhammad in Mecca** — Muhammad was born in Mecca into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh, the tribe that controlled the pilgrimage sanctuary of the Kaaba. [britannica-hijrah, wiki-muhammad]
- c. 610 CE: **The first revelations** — According to Islamic tradition Muhammad, at about forty, began receiving revelations through the angel Gabriel, proclaiming a strict monotheism that would form the Quran. [wiki-muhammad, britannica-hijrah]
- 622 CE: **The Hijra to Medina** — Facing persecution, Muhammad and his followers migrated from Mecca to Yathrib (Medina), founding the first Muslim community; the event marks year one of the Islamic calendar. [britannica-hijrah, wiki-muhammad]
- c. 630 CE: **The submission of Mecca** — Muhammad returned to Mecca largely without bloodshed, cleansed the Kaaba of idols, and established Islam across much of the Hejaz. [wiki-muhammad]
- 632 CE: **Death of Muhammad** — By the Prophet's death much of the Arabian Peninsula had embraced Islam, leaving Mecca and Medina as the enduring holy cities of the faith. [wiki-muhammad, britannica-arabia]

Figures:

- Muhammad (c. 570 - 632 CE) — Prophet of Islam. Founder of Islam, born in Mecca, whose preaching of monotheism and migration to Medina in 622 created the first Muslim community and reshaped the history of Arabia and the wider world.

## The Caliphates and the Hejaz under Outside Rule — 632 - 1744 CE

After Muhammad's death the centers of Islamic power moved away from Arabia, and for more than a thousand years the Hejaz and its holy cities were governed by distant caliphs, local sharifs, and finally the Ottomans.

- 632 - 661 CE: **Medina as capital of the early caliphate** — Under the first (Rashidun) caliphs, Medina served as the capital of an expanding Muslim empire before power shifted away from Arabia. [britannica-arabia, wiki-sharifate]
- 661 - 750 CE: **Umayyad and Abbasid rule over the Hejaz** — The Umayyads shifted the empire's capital to Damascus and the Abbasids later ruled from Baghdad, leaving Arabia politically peripheral while Mecca and Medina remained its religious centre. [britannica-arabia]
- 10th century CE: **The Sharifate of Mecca takes shape** — A line of sharifs descended from the Prophet came to administer Mecca and the Hejaz, holding local authority for nearly a thousand years under shifting overlords. [wiki-sharifate]
- 1517 CE: **Ottoman suzerainty over the Hejaz** — After defeating the Mamluks, the Ottomans gained sovereignty over the holy cities; the Sharif of Mecca recognized the Ottoman sultan as caliph while retaining local power. [wiki-sharifate]

## The First and Second Saudi States and the Al Saud-Wahhab Alliance — 1744 - 1891

In 1744 a pact between the ruler of Diriyah and a puritan reformer fused religious revival with political ambition, creating the first state of the House of Saud and the religious-political partnership at the kingdom's foundation.

- 1744: **The Diriyah pact founds the First Saudi State** — Muhammad ibn Saud, ruler of Diriyah, and the reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab formed an alliance joining political power and religious revival, the foundation of the first Saudi state. [wiki-first-state, saudipedia-first-state, unesco-diriyah]
- 1811 - 1818: **The Ottoman-Egyptian campaign against the Saudis** — On behalf of the Ottoman sultan, the Egyptian forces of Muhammad Ali Pasha waged war on the expanding First Saudi State, driving it from the holy cities and into the Najdi heartland. [wiki-siege-diriyah, saudipedia-first-state]
- 1818: **The fall and destruction of Diriyah** — After a months-long siege Ibrahim Pasha took Diriyah in September 1818 and razed it, ending the First Saudi State; the last imam was executed in Constantinople. [wiki-siege-diriyah, unesco-diriyah]
- 1824: **Turki ibn Abdullah founds the Second Saudi State** — Turki ibn Abdullah Al Saud expelled the occupying garrisons, re-established Saudi rule, and made Riyadh the capital of a revived but smaller state. [wiki-second-state, saudipedia-second-state]
- 1891: **The Al Rashid end the Second Saudi State** — Weakened by internal feuds, the second state fell to the rival Al Rashid of Hail, who drove the Al Saud into exile in Kuwait. [wiki-second-state, britannica-saudi-kingdom]

Figures:

- Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab (1703 - 1792) — Religious reformer. Najdi theologian whose call for a return to strict monotheism, allied with the Al Saud in 1744, became the religious foundation of the Saudi states.
- Turki ibn Abdullah Al Saud (c. 1755 - 1834) — Founder of the Second Saudi State. Expelled the occupying forces in 1824 and re-established Saudi rule with Riyadh as its capital, restoring the dynasty after the destruction of Diriyah.

## Unification and the Founding of the Kingdom (1902-1932) — 1902 - 1932

A daring raid on Riyadh in 1902 launched Abdulaziz ibn Saud on a thirty-year campaign that reunited Najd and the Hejaz into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

- January 1902: **Abdulaziz captures Riyadh** — Returning from exile, Abdulaziz ibn Saud and a small force seized the Masmak fortress in Riyadh from the Al Rashid, launching the campaign to rebuild the Saudi state. [wiki-battle-riyadh, britannica-ibn-saud]
- 1925: **Conquest of the Hejaz and the holy cities** — Abdulaziz's forces took Mecca and Medina, ending Hashemite rule in the Hejaz and bringing the holy cities under Saudi control. [wiki-unification, britannica-saudi-kingdom]
- 20 May 1927: **The Treaty of Jeddah** — Britain formally recognized the independence and sovereignty of Ibn Saud's Kingdom of the Hejaz and Nejd, securing its international standing. [britannica-jeddah, wiki-jeddah-treaty]
- 23 September 1932: **Proclamation of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia** — By royal decree the regions of Najd and the Hejaz were unified and renamed the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, with Abdulaziz ibn Saud as its first king. [wiki-proclamation, britannica-saudi-kingdom, saudipedia-unification]

Figures:

- Abdulaziz ibn Saud (Ibn Saud) (c. 1875 - 1953) — Founder and first King of Saudi Arabia. Recaptured Riyadh in 1902 and, over three decades of conquest and diplomacy, unified the Arabian Peninsula, proclaiming the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932 and reigning until his death.

## Oil, Modernization and Contemporary Saudi Arabia — 1933 - present

The discovery of oil in 1938 turned a poor desert kingdom into a global energy power, financing rapid modernization while the Al Saud navigated regional crises and, more recently, an ambitious drive to diversify beyond oil.

- 1933: **The oil concession is granted** — Abdulaziz granted an oil exploration concession to the Standard Oil Company of California, the agreement that would become the basis of Aramco. [saudipedia-oil, wiki-oil-history]
- 1938: **Oil struck at Dammam Well No. 7** — Commercial quantities of oil were discovered at Dammam Well No. 7 in March 1938, the find that transformed Saudi Arabia's economy and the world energy market. [wiki-dammam7, saudipedia-oil]
- 1973: **The Arab oil embargo** — Under King Faisal, Saudi Arabia led Arab OPEC states in an embargo against supporters of Israel, sharply raising oil prices and demonstrating the kingdom's economic power. [britannica-saudi-kingdom, baker-embargo]
- 2008: **Hegra becomes a UNESCO World Heritage Site** — The Nabataean site of Hegra (al-Hijr) was inscribed as Saudi Arabia's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognizing the kingdom's deep pre-Islamic past. [unesco-hegra]
- 2016: **Vision 2030 is launched** — Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman unveiled Vision 2030, a program to diversify the economy beyond oil, grow the private sector and tourism, and reshape Saudi society. [vision2030-gov, pif-vision2030]

Figures:

- King Faisal (1906 - 1975) — King of Saudi Arabia, 1964-1975. Modernizing monarch who led the 1973 oil embargo and raised Saudi Arabia to the status of a world power before his assassination in 1975.
- Mohammed bin Salman (born 1985) — Crown Prince and Prime Minister. Architect of Vision 2030, the 2016 program to diversify the economy beyond oil and transform Saudi society, and the dominant figure in 21st-century Saudi politics.
