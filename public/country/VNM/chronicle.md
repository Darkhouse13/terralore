# The Chronicle of Vietnam: A thousand years under Chinese rule, a thousand more of fiercely defended independence, and a modern nation forged in war and reform.

Founding: Declaration of Independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam — 2 September 1945. On 2 September 1945, in Hanoi's Ba Dinh Square, Ho Chi Minh read the Declaration of Independence proclaiming the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, echoing the American and French declarations of rights. The act came after the long arc of Vietnamese statehood: the legendary kingdoms of Van Lang and Au Lac ruled by the Hung kings and An Duong Vuong, roughly a thousand years of Chinese rule from 111 BCE until Ngo Quyen's victory at Bach Dang in 938 CE, and centuries of independent dynasties. Independence in 1945 was not the end of the struggle; war with France and then the division of the country gave way only after 1975 to the reunified Socialist Republic of Vietnam, formally proclaimed on 2 July 1976.

Vietnamese history stretches from the legendary kingdoms of the Red River delta to one of Asia's most dynamic modern economies. Tradition traces the nation to Van Lang and the Hung kings and to the kingdom of Au Lac, but the verifiable story begins with more than a millennium of Chinese domination, from the Han conquest in 111 BCE until Ngo Quyen's victory on the Bach Dang River in 938 CE. The independent dynasties that followed, the Ly, Tran, and Le, built a Confucian state, expanded steadily southward at the expense of Champa, and repeatedly threw back invaders, including three Mongol assaults under Kublai Khan. The Nguyen dynasty unified the long country under Gia Long in 1802, only to fall under French colonial rule within the French Indochina established in 1887. On 2 September 1945 Ho Chi Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, opening three decades of war: against France, then in a divided nation through the Vietnam War, until reunification in 1975 and 1976. Since the Doi Moi reforms of 1986, Vietnam has transformed itself into a fast-growing, globally integrated state while remaining a one-party socialist republic.

## Legendary Origins and a Thousand Years of Chinese Rule — c. 2879 BCE - 938 CE

From the rice paddies of the Red River delta emerged the legendary kingdoms of the Hung kings, but the verifiable nation was forged in more than a thousand years of resistance to Chinese imperial rule.

- c. 2879 BCE (traditional): **Legendary founding of Van Lang** — Vietnamese tradition dates the kingdom of Van Lang and its line of Hung kings to 2879 BCE; the historical Bronze Age Dong Son culture of the Red River delta flourished in the first millennium BCE. [hung-vuong, vietnam-place]
- c. 3rd century BCE: **Kingdom of Au Lac and Co Loa** — An Duong Vuong overthrows the last Hung king and unites the land as Au Lac, building the fortified capital of Co Loa north of modern Hanoi. [vietnam-place]
- 111 BCE: **Han conquest begins Chinese rule** — The Chinese Han dynasty conquers the region, opening more than a thousand years of direct Chinese administration, taxation, and cultural influence. [vietnam-place]
- 40-43 CE: **Revolt of the Trung sisters** — Trung Trac and Trung Nhi lead a rebellion that briefly drives out the Chinese before being defeated in 43 CE; they become lasting symbols of Vietnamese resistance. [trung-sisters, vietnam-place]
- 938 CE: **Battle of Bach Dang and independence** — Ngo Quyen destroys a Southern Han fleet at the Bach Dang River using hidden iron-tipped stakes, ending Chinese rule; he proclaims himself king in 939. [ngo-quyen, vietnam-place]

Figures:

- Trung Sisters (Hai Ba Trung) (d. 43 CE) — Leaders of anti-Chinese revolt. Sisters Trung Trac and Trung Nhi raised a revolt in 40 CE that briefly liberated dozens of districts from Chinese rule and established a short-lived independent state; defeated in 43 CE, they became the most celebrated heroines of Vietnamese national memory.
- Ngo Quyen (898-944 CE) — Founder of independent Vietnam. His decisive victory over the Southern Han at the Bach Dang River in 938 ended roughly a millennium of Chinese domination; proclaiming himself king the following year, he is regarded as the founder of Vietnamese independence.

## Independent Dynasties: Ly, Tran, and Le — 1009 - 1788 CE

Under the Ly, Tran, and Le dynasties, Vietnam built an enduring Confucian state, repelled the Mongols three times, and expelled Ming China, while pushing its frontier steadily southward.

- 1009 CE: **Founding of the Ly dynasty** — Ly Cong Uan takes the throne and founds the Ly dynasty, moving the capital to Thang Long (Hanoi) in 1010 and establishing the enduring institutions of independent Dai Viet. [vietnam-place]
- 1258, 1285 and 1288 CE: **Tran defeat of the Mongol invasions** — Under Tran Hung Dao the Tran dynasty repels three invasions by Kublai Khan's Mongols, climaxing in the 1288 victory at the Bach Dang River where the Mongol fleet is destroyed on hidden stakes. [tran-dynasty, tran-hung-dao]
- 1407 CE: **Ming China occupies Dai Viet** — The Chinese Ming dynasty conquers Dai Viet and imposes two decades of harsh direct rule, provoking widespread resistance. [le-loi, vietnam-place]
- 1428 CE: **Le Loi expels the Ming and founds the Le dynasty** — After a ten-year war of liberation, Le Loi drives out the Ming, restores independence, and establishes the Le dynasty, which would rule, nominally, for centuries. [le-loi]
- 1471 CE: **Conquest of Champa's capital** — Emperor Le Thanh Tong's forces capture Vijaya, the capital of Champa, accelerating the southward expansion (Nam tien) of Vietnamese settlement. [vietnam-place]

Figures:

- Tran Hung Dao (1228-1300 CE) — General and national hero. The supreme commander who defeated the Mongol invasions of the 13th century, most famously by destroying Kublai Khan's fleet at the Bach Dang River in 1288; revered ever since as one of Vietnam's greatest military strategists.
- Le Loi (Le Thai To) (1385-1433 CE) — Founder of the Le dynasty. A landowner from Thanh Hoa who led a decade-long insurgency against Ming occupation and, victorious in 1428, founded the Le dynasty and restored the independence of Dai Viet.

## The Nguyen Dynasty and Expansion South — 1788 - 1858 CE

Emerging from the chaos of the Tay Son rebellion, Nguyen Anh reunified a country stretched from the Red River to the Mekong delta, founding in 1802 the last imperial dynasty of Vietnam.

- 1771-1789 CE: **Tay Son rebellion** — A peasant-led uprising overthrows the Nguyen and Trinh lords and the Le dynasty, and in 1789 routs an invading Chinese Qing army near Thang Long. [gia-long, vietnam-place]
- 1802 CE: **Gia Long founds the Nguyen dynasty** — Nguyen Anh defeats the Tay Son, reunifies the country, and is crowned emperor as Gia Long at Hue, founding Vietnam's last imperial dynasty. [gia-long, nguyen-dynasty]
- 1804 CE: **The name Vietnam adopted** — The unified realm is recognized under the name Vietnam, with its capital at Hue and a centralized administration modeled on Qing China. [nguyen-dynasty, vietnam-place]
- 1820-1841 CE: **Reign of Minh Mang** — Emperor Minh Mang consolidates the Confucian state, completes administrative reforms, and intensifies suspicion of European traders and Catholic missionaries. [nguyen-dynasty]
- 1858 CE: **French attack on Da Nang** — A French and Spanish naval force assaults Da Nang, beginning the military conquest that would bring Vietnam under colonial rule. [french-indochina, vietnam-place]

Figures:

- Gia Long (Nguyen Anh) (1762-1820 CE) — Founding emperor of the Nguyen dynasty. After decades of war against the Tay Son, he reunified Vietnam in 1802 and ruled as its first Nguyen emperor from Hue, his reign name fusing the names of Saigon and Hanoi to proclaim a country whole from south to north.

## French Indochina — 1858 - 1945 CE

Conquered piece by piece, Vietnam was divided into the colony of Cochinchina and the protectorates of Annam and Tonkin, bound into French Indochina, where colonial exploitation bred a rising tide of nationalism.

- 1862 CE: **France annexes Cochinchina** — Following the capture of Saigon, France secures the southern provinces of Cochinchina as a colony, its first permanent foothold in Vietnam. [french-indochina, vietnam-place]
- 17 October 1887: **French Indochina established** — France unites Cochinchina, Annam, Tonkin, and Cambodia into the Indochinese Union; Laos is added in 1893, binding Vietnam into a colonial federation centered on Hanoi. [french-indochina]
- 1930 CE: **Founding of the Indochinese Communist Party** — Ho Chi Minh helps unite rival communist groups into the Indochinese Communist Party, which becomes the organizational core of the independence struggle. [ho-chi-minh, vietnam-place]
- 1944-1945 CE: **Great Vietnamese Famine** — Under Japanese occupation and wartime mismanagement, famine strikes northern Vietnam, killing an estimated one to two million people and deepening revolutionary discontent. [vietnam-place]
- 2 September 1945: **Declaration of independence** — As Japan surrenders, the Viet Minh seize power in the August Revolution and Ho Chi Minh proclaims the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi's Ba Dinh Square. [declaration-1945, ho-chi-minh]

Figures:

- Ho Chi Minh (1890-1969) — Revolutionary leader and founding president. Born Nguyen Sinh Cung, he spent decades abroad as a communist organizer before leading the Viet Minh, proclaiming Vietnamese independence in 1945, and serving as president of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam through the wars against France and the United States.

## The Wars: Independence, Division, and Reunification — 1945 - 1976 CE

Three decades of war, against France, between a divided North and South, and against the United States, cost millions of Vietnamese lives before the country was reunified in 1975 and 1976.

- 1946-1954 CE: **First Indochina War** — War between France and the Viet Minh ends with the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu on 7 May 1954, terminating French colonial rule in Vietnam. [first-indochina-war, dien-bien-phu]
- 1954 CE: **Geneva Accords divide Vietnam** — The Geneva Conference partitions Vietnam at the 17th parallel into a communist North and an anti-communist South pending reunification elections that are never held. [geneva-accords, dien-bien-phu]
- 1964 CE: **Gulf of Tonkin and U.S. escalation** — The Gulf of Tonkin incident leads Congress to pass a resolution authorizing the vast escalation of direct U.S. military involvement in the war. [gulf-of-tonkin, vietnam-war]
- 1968 CE: **Tet Offensive** — A coordinated communist surprise offensive across South Vietnam, though costly militarily, undermines American public support and shifts the war toward U.S. withdrawal. [vietnam-war]
- 30 April 1975: **Fall of Saigon and reunification** — North Vietnamese forces capture Saigon, ending the war; the country is formally reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on 2 July 1976. [vietnam-war, vietnam-place]

Figures:

- Vo Nguyen Giap (1911-2013) — Commanding general. The self-taught military strategist who built the People's Army of Vietnam, masterminded the victory at Dien Bien Phu in 1954, and led communist forces through the Vietnam War to the fall of Saigon in 1975.
- Ngo Dinh Diem (1901-1963) — First president of South Vietnam. The Catholic anti-communist nationalist who led the U.S.-backed Republic of Vietnam from 1955, his authoritarian and divisive rule ended in his assassination during a 1963 coup.

## Doi Moi Reforms and Modern Vietnam — 1976 - present

After years of postwar hardship and isolation, the Doi Moi reforms of 1986 opened Vietnam's economy and launched its transformation into one of the world's fastest-growing nations.

- 1979 CE: **Sino-Vietnamese War** — China invades northern Vietnam in response to Vietnam's ousting of the Khmer Rouge in Cambodia; the brief but bloody border war deepens Vietnam's isolation. [vietnam-place]
- 1986 CE: **Doi Moi reforms launched** — The Communist Party's Sixth Congress adopts Doi Moi, replacing central planning with a socialist-oriented market economy and opening Vietnam to private enterprise and foreign investment. [doi-moi, vietnam-place]
- 1995 CE: **Normalization with the U.S. and ASEAN membership** — Vietnam restores diplomatic relations with the United States and joins the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, ending decades of isolation. [vietnam-place]
- 2007 CE: **Accession to the World Trade Organization** — Vietnam joins the WTO, cementing its integration into the global economy and accelerating an export-driven manufacturing boom. [doi-moi, vietnam-place]

Figures:

- Nguyen Van Linh (1915-1998) — Reform-era party leader. As general secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam from 1986, he became the leading political champion of the Doi Moi reforms that opened the country's economy.
