# The Chronicle of Pakistan: From the planned cities of the Indus to a nuclear-armed republic.

Founding: Independence from Britain — 14 Aug 1947. Pakistan came into being at midnight on 14 August 1947 under the Indian Independence Act, which partitioned British India into two dominions. Muhammad Ali Jinnah, revered as Quaid-e-Azam, took office as the country's first Governor-General, and Liaquat Ali Khan became its first prime minister. The new state — then comprising West Pakistan and a distant East Pakistan separated by some 1,600 km of Indian territory — was born amid one of the largest forced migrations in history.

Pakistan occupies one of the oldest cradles of civilisation on Earth, where the Bronze Age cities of Mohenjo-daro and Harappa flourished more than four thousand years ago along the Indus. Over the millennia its valleys and passes carried Persians, Greeks, Buddhists of Gandhara, Muslim sultans and the Mughal emperors who made Lahore a jewel of their realm. In the twentieth century the demand of British India's Muslims for a homeland — crystallised in the 1940 Lahore Resolution under Muhammad Ali Jinnah — produced the modern state, which won independence on 14 August 1947 amid the trauma of Partition. Since then Pakistan has lived through the loss of its eastern wing in 1971, recurring cycles of civilian and military rule, and its emergence as a nuclear power and the world's fifth most populous nation.

## The Indus Valley Civilisation — c. 2600 – c. 1900 BCE

Along the Indus and its tributaries arose one of the world's earliest urban civilisations, its grid-planned cities a wonder of the Bronze Age.

- c. 2600 BCE: **Mature phase of the Indus cities** — Mohenjo-daro, Harappa and hundreds of other settlements reach their peak, with grid-planned streets, advanced drainage and standardised weights across a vast trading network. [britannica-indus, britannica-mohenjodaro]
- c. 2600 BCE: **Mohenjo-daro flourishes** — On the right bank of the Indus in Sindh, Mohenjo-daro becomes one of the civilisation's largest cities, renowned for its citadel and Great Bath; it is today a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [unesco-mohenjodaro, britannica-mohenjodaro]
- c. 1900 BCE: **Decline of the Harappan cities** — The major cities are gradually abandoned as weakening monsoons and shifting rivers undermine the agricultural base, ending the civilisation's urban phase. [britannica-indus]

## Gandhara and the Ancient Empires — c. 518 BCE – c. 450 CE

Persians, Greeks, Mauryans and Kushans swept through the Indus passes, and at Taxila and Peshawar the Gandhara school fused Greece and Buddhism in stone.

- c. 518 BCE: **Achaemenid Persia annexes the Indus lands** — The Persian empire incorporates the Indus region as among its wealthiest provinces, bringing centralised administration and Aramaic writing to the area. [loc-pakistan, britannica-taxila]
- 326 BCE: **Alexander defeats Porus at the Hydaspes** — Alexander the Great crosses the Indus passes and defeats King Porus on the Jhelum, marking the easternmost reach of his empire before his army turns homeward. [britannica-taxila, loc-pakistan]
- c. 127 CE: **Kanishka and the Gandhara golden age** — Under the Kushan emperor Kanishka, ruling from Peshawar, Mahayana Buddhism and the Greco-Buddhist Gandhara school of art flourish, depicting the Buddha in human form. [britannica-kanishka, britannica-taxila]

Figures:

- Kanishka (reigned c. 127–150 CE) — Kushan emperor. The greatest Kushan ruler, who governed Gandhara from Peshawar, championed Mahayana Buddhism and convened a great Buddhist council. His reign saw the flowering of the Gandhara school of art.

## Islam, the Sultans and the Mughals — 711 – 1707

Islam arrived with Arab armies in Sindh, spread under the Delhi sultans, and reached its height under the Mughals, who made Lahore a city of imperial splendour.

- 711 CE: **Muhammad ibn Qasim conquers Sindh** — An Umayyad army under Muhammad ibn Qasim conquers Sindh and southern Punjab, bringing Islam to the Indus lands for the first time. [loc-pakistan]
- 1206: **Rise of the Delhi Sultanate** — Muslim dynasties ruling from Delhi extend their dominion over the Indus plains, deepening the region's Islamic culture over three centuries. [loc-pakistan]
- 1526: **Babur founds the Mughal Empire** — Babur's victory ends the Delhi Sultanate and establishes the Mughal Empire, which makes Lahore one of its great capitals. [britannica-mughal, loc-pakistan]
- 1671–1673: **Badshahi Mosque built at Lahore** — Emperor Aurangzeb commissions the monumental Badshahi Mosque opposite the Lahore Fort, for centuries the largest mosque in the world and a landmark of Mughal Lahore. [unesco-lahore, britannica-mughal]

Figures:

- Babur (1483–1530) — Founder of the Mughal Empire. A Central Asian prince descended from Timur and Genghis Khan who founded the Mughal Empire in 1526, beginning a dynasty that would make Lahore one of its most splendid cities.

## The British Raj and the Pakistan Movement — 1849 – 1947

British annexation of Punjab brought the Indus lands under the Raj; a century later, the demand for a Muslim homeland gathered behind Jinnah and the Lahore Resolution.

- 1849: **Britain annexes the Punjab** — After defeating the Sikh kingdom, the British annex Punjab, completing colonial control over the lands that would become Pakistan. [loc-pakistan]
- 1906: **Founding of the All-India Muslim League** — The All-India Muslim League is founded to represent the political interests of India's Muslims; it would become the engine of the Pakistan Movement. [loc-pakistan, britannica-jinnah]
- 23 Mar 1940: **The Lahore Resolution** — Meeting in Lahore, the Muslim League under Jinnah adopts the resolution demanding independent states for Muslims, the foundational charter of the Pakistan Movement. [britannica-jinnah, loc-pakistan]
- 18 Jul 1947: **Indian Independence Act passed** — The British Parliament passes the Indian Independence Act, partitioning British India and providing for the creation of the dominions of India and Pakistan. [britannica-independenceact]

Figures:

- Muhammad Ali Jinnah (1876–1948) — Founder of Pakistan, Quaid-e-Azam. A Karachi-born barrister and leader of the All-India Muslim League who championed the two-nation theory and led the movement that created Pakistan, becoming its first Governor-General. He is revered as Quaid-e-Azam, the 'Great Leader'.

## Partition and Independence — 1947 – 1971

Freedom on 14 August 1947 came with the agony of Partition; a fractured early democracy gave way to military rule and, in 1971, the loss of the eastern wing.

- 14 Aug 1947: **Independence of Pakistan** — Pakistan becomes independent under the Indian Independence Act, with Jinnah as Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan as prime minister, comprising West and East Pakistan. [britannica-jinnah, britannica-independenceact]
- 1947–1948: **Partition migration and violence** — The Radcliffe Line divides Punjab and Bengal, and millions are uprooted across the new borders amid massacres and mass migration, with deaths estimated from the hundreds of thousands into the millions. [britannica-radcliffe, britannica-independenceact]
- 16 Oct 1951: **Assassination of Liaquat Ali Khan** — Pakistan's first prime minister is assassinated at a rally in Rawalpindi, deepening the political instability of the new state's early years. [britannica-liaquat]
- 1958: **Ayub Khan's military coup** — General Ayub Khan seizes power in Pakistan's first military takeover, beginning a decade of presidential rule and a recurring pattern of army intervention in politics. [loc-pakistan, britannica-zia]

Figures:

- Liaquat Ali Khan (1895–1951) — First Prime Minister of Pakistan. A close associate of Jinnah and a leading figure of the Muslim League, Liaquat Ali Khan became Pakistan's first prime minister in 1947 and guided the fledgling state until his assassination in 1951.

## Bangladesh, Military Rule and the Modern Republic — 1971 – present

The loss of East Pakistan in 1971 remade the country, which has since cycled between civilian and military rule, become a nuclear power, and grown into a vast, youthful nation.

- 16 Dec 1971: **Loss of East Pakistan and birth of Bangladesh** — After a brutal war and Indian intervention, Pakistani forces surrender at Dhaka and East Pakistan becomes the independent state of Bangladesh, dismembering the country. [britannica-bangladesh, britannica-1971war]
- 4 Apr 1979: **Execution of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto** — Deposed in General Zia-ul-Haq's 1977 coup, former prime minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto is tried and hanged, ushering in a decade of military rule and Islamisation. [britannica-bhutto, britannica-zia]
- 28 May 1998: **Pakistan conducts nuclear tests** — Following India's tests, Pakistan detonates nuclear devices in Balochistan, openly joining the ranks of nuclear-armed states. [loc-pakistan]
- 27 Dec 2007: **Assassination of Benazir Bhutto** — Former prime minister Benazir Bhutto is assassinated at a rally weeks before national elections, after which civilian democratic rule is restored in 2008. [britannica-bhutto]

Figures:

- Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (1928–1979) — President and Prime Minister of Pakistan. A charismatic statesman who took power after the 1971 defeat, gave Pakistan its 1973 constitution and launched its nuclear programme, before being overthrown by Zia-ul-Haq and executed in 1979.
- Mohammad Zia-ul-Haq (1924–1988) — Military ruler of Pakistan. The army chief who seized power in 1977, had Bhutto hanged, and ruled through martial law and a programme of Islamisation until his death in a 1988 plane crash.
