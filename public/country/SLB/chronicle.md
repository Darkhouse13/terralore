# The Chronicle of Solomon Islands: An archipelago misnamed for a king's gold, forged by settlement, war and sovereignty

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 7 July 1978. The British Solomon Islands Protectorate became the independent state of Solomon Islands on 7 July 1978, remaining within the Commonwealth with the British monarch as head of state. Sir Peter Kenilorea, who had led the independence negotiations with Britain, became the country's first prime minister.

The Solomon Islands, a Melanesian archipelago east of New Guinea, were peopled first by Papuan settlers tens of thousands of years ago and then by Austronesian-speaking Lapita voyagers about 3,000 years ago, producing one of the most linguistically diverse populations on Earth. The Spanish navigator Álvaro de Mendaña reached them in 1568 and named them for the biblical King Solomon in the mistaken belief that he had found the source of that king's gold; centuries of isolation followed. The nineteenth century brought the coercive 'blackbirding' labour trade, Christian missionaries and, in 1893, a British protectorate, before the islands became one of the pivotal battlegrounds of the Pacific War at Guadalcanal in 1942–43. Independent since 7 July 1978, the country has navigated the ethnic conflict known as 'the Tensions', an Australian-led regional intervention, and the great-power attention that followed its 2019 switch of recognition from Taiwan to the People's Republic of China.

## Settlement of a Thousand Tongues — Antiquity – 1568

Two great waves of migration — Papuan and then Austronesian — filled the archipelago, leaving one of the most linguistically diverse populations on Earth.

- c. 30,000+ years ago: **Papuan settlers reach the islands** — Papuan-speaking peoples occupy the main Solomon chain, part of Near Oceania, in some of the earliest recorded sea crossings by humans. [eb-history-slb]
- c. 1000 BCE: **Arrival of the Lapita** — Austronesian-speaking Lapita voyagers, ancestors of much of the later Pacific, settle the islands and mix with existing populations. [eb-history-slb, iao-lapita]

## Mendaña and the King's Gold — 1568 – 1850

A Spanish expedition gave the islands a famous and misleading name, then left them in near-total isolation from Europe for nearly three centuries.

- 1568: **Mendaña reaches and names the islands** — Álvaro de Mendaña becomes the first European to reach the archipelago; rumoured gold leads to the name 'Solomon Islands' after the biblical king. [eb-history-slb, eb-mendana]
- 1595: **Mendaña's failed colony** — A second Spanish expedition attempts a settlement in the Santa Cruz islands but collapses amid disease and conflict; Mendaña dies there. [eb-mendana, eb-history-slb]

Figures:

- Álvaro de Mendaña de Neira (1542–1595) — Spanish navigator. Sailing from Peru, he reached the islands in 1568 and, on rumours of gold, gave them the name of King Solomon; his 1595 colonising voyage ended in his death.

## Blackbirding, Missions and the Protectorate — 1850 – 1941

The nineteenth century broke the islands' isolation through a coercive labour trade, Christian missions, and the imposition of a British protectorate in 1893.

- 1860s–1900s: **The blackbirding labour trade** — Recruiters carry tens of thousands of Solomon Islanders, many coerced, to plantations in Queensland and Fiji, bringing disease and depopulation. [eb-blackbirding, eb-history-slb]
- 1871: **Killing of Bishop Patteson** — John Coleridge Patteson, first Anglican Bishop of Melanesia, is killed at Nukapu, reportedly in reprisal for blackbirders' raids on islanders. [anglican-patteson, anu-melanesianmission]
- 1893: **British protectorate proclaimed** — Britain declares a protectorate over the southern Solomons to curb labour-trade abuses and forestall French and German expansion. [uq-protectorate, eb-history-slb]
- 1899: **Germany cedes the northern islands** — Germany transfers most of the northern Solomons to Britain in exchange for recognition of its claims elsewhere, enlarging the protectorate. [uq-protectorate, eb-history-slb]

Figures:

- John Coleridge Patteson (1827–1871) — First Anglican Bishop of Melanesia. A gifted linguist who led the Melanesian Mission across the islands; his killing on Nukapu drew British attention to the abuses of the labour trade.

## Guadalcanal and the Turning of the Pacific War — 1942 – 1952

The islands became one of the decisive battlegrounds of the Second World War, and the war in turn lit the first sparks of an independence movement.

- 7 August 1942: **US landings on Guadalcanal** — US Marines land on Guadalcanal and Tulagi in the first major American amphibious offensive of the war, seizing the future Henderson Field. [awm-guadalcanal, ww2museum-guadalcanal]
- February 1943: **Japanese evacuate Guadalcanal** — After a six-month seesaw campaign, Japan withdraws its forces; Guadalcanal becomes a turning point of the Pacific War. [eb-guadalcanal, ww2museum-guadalcanal]
- 1944–1952: **The Maasina Rule movement** — A post-war Malaitan movement for self-government and custom, born of the wartime labour corps, is credited as an early step toward independence. [uq-protectorate, eb-history-slb]

## The Road to Independence — 1952 – 1978

Through the post-war decades the protectorate built representative institutions, leading to full independence under Peter Kenilorea in 1978.

- 1976: **Internal self-government** — Solomon Islands attains internal self-government, the final constitutional step before independence, with Peter Kenilorea as chief minister. [eb-history-slb, eb-kenilorea]
- 7 July 1978: **Independence from the United Kingdom** — The protectorate becomes the independent state of Solomon Islands within the Commonwealth, with Sir Peter Kenilorea as first prime minister. [eb-kenilorea, statedept-slb, eb-history-slb]
- 7 July 1978: **The United States recognises the new state** — Senator John Glenn delivers President Carter's letter of recognition to Prime Minister Kenilorea on independence day itself; diplomatic relations follow on 9 October. [statedept-solomon]
- 1988: **The Melanesian Spearhead Group** — Solomon Islands joins Vanuatu and Papua New Guinea in a bloc formed to preserve Melanesian cultural traditions — small Pacific states organising around what they share. [bbc-solomon]

Figures:

- Sir Peter Kenilorea (1943–2016) — First Prime Minister of Solomon Islands. A Malaitan teacher and civil servant who led the independence negotiations and became the nation's first prime minister at independence in 1978.

## Tensions, Recovery and Great-Power Attention — 1978 – present

Independent Solomon Islands has weathered ethnic conflict, a regional intervention and the geopolitical pull of competing great powers in the Pacific.

- 1998–2003: **The Tensions** — Ethnic conflict between Guadalcanal and Malaitan groups around Honiara displaces thousands and, in 2000, topples Prime Minister Ulufa'alu. [dva-ramsi, eb-history-slb]
- 1998: **East Rennell inscribed, and later imperilled** — The southern third of Rennell Island — the largest raised coral atoll in the world — joins the World Heritage List; it is moved to the List in Danger in 2013. [unesco-rennell]
- June 2000: **The Malaita Eagle Force coup** — Militants take Prime Minister Ulufa'alu hostage and force his resignation; an Australian-brokered treaty that October begins a weapons handover that does not hold. [bbc-solomon]
- December 2002: **Cyclone Zoe strikes Tikopia** — A cyclone devastates the remote islands of Tikopia and Anuta; a relief vessel sails expecting mass casualties and finds nobody killed. [bbc-solomon]
- 2003–2017: **RAMSI intervention** — At the government's request, Australia leads a multinational mission that restores order, disarms militias and rebuilds institutions until 2017. [dva-ramsi]
- August 2003: **Harold Keke surrenders** — The Isatabu Freedom Movement leader gives himself up to Australian forces and is charged with the murder of the priest and MP Augustine Geve. [bbc-solomon]
- September 2019: **Switch from Taiwan to China** — Manasseh Sogavare's government ends recognition of Taiwan and establishes diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China. [voa-chinaswitch]
- November 2021: **Honiara riots** — Protests over the China switch and provincial grievances turn into deadly riots in Honiara; Australia and Pacific partners send security forces. [csis-crisis, voa-chinaswitch]
- 2022: **The security agreement with China** — A security pact signed with Beijing sharpens Western concern about the Pacific, three years after the switch in recognition. [csis-crisis, asiasociety-2024]
- May 2024: **Jeremiah Manele becomes prime minister** — After general elections, parliament selects Jeremiah Manele as prime minister, signalling continuity in relations with China and Australia. [voa-manele, asiasociety-2024]

Figures:

- Manasseh Sogavare — Prime Minister (multiple terms). A dominant figure of recent Solomon Islands politics whose government switched recognition to Beijing in 2019 and signed a security pact with China in 2022.
