Founding: The Self-Government Act recognises Greenlanders' right to independence — 21 June 2009. Greenland's defining modern political moment is the entry into force, on 21 June 2009, of the Act on Greenland Self-Government (Selvstyre; Danish Act no. 473 of 12 June 2009). Approved by a Greenlandic referendum in November 2008 in which about 75 per cent voted in favour, the Act replaced the Home Rule arrangement of 1979 and was concluded between the Greenland and Danish governments as 'equal partners'. Its preamble recognises 'that the people of Greenland is a people pursuant to international law with the right of self-determination', and it sets out an explicit, lawful path to full independence: if Greenlanders so decide, negotiations begin between the two governments, and any agreement must be endorsed by Greenland's parliament (Inatsisartut), approved by a referendum in Greenland and ratified by the Danish Folketing. The Act came into force on 21 June, Greenland's National Day, marking the thirtieth anniversary of Home Rule and the further transfer of competence over fields of responsibility from Copenhagen to Nuuk.

Greenland (Kalaallit Nunaat, 'land of the Kalaallit') is the world's largest island and an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark, not an independent state. Its human story is one of repeated Arctic migrations: successive Paleo-Inuit peoples, the Saqqaq and the Dorset, followed by the Thule whaling culture from whom today's Greenlandic Inuit descend, and a Norse colony founded by Erik the Red around 985 that flourished for four centuries before vanishing by the 15th. Recolonised from 1721 by the Dano-Norwegian missionary Hans Egede, Greenland was a Danish colony until 1953, when it became an integrated county of Denmark, and it acquired Home Rule in 1979. On 21 June 2009 the Act on Greenland Self-Government came into force, recognising the people of Greenland as a people under international law with the right to self-determination and setting out a path to full independence, even as resources and renewed great-power interest in the Arctic place the island's future at the centre of world attention.

## The First Peoples: Saqqaq, Dorset and Thule — c. 2500 BCE – c. 1300 CE

In a series of migrations from Arctic North America, the Saqqaq, the Dorset and finally the Thule whalers settled Greenland, the last being the ancestors of today's Kalaallit.

- c. 2500 BCE: **The Saqqaq settle Greenland** — The Saqqaq, the earliest known people in Greenland, migrated from Arctic North America and spread along the coasts as caribou and seal hunters, beginning more than four millennia of continuous Arctic settlement. [aasivissuit-unesco, greenland-history-britannica]
- c. 800 BCE: **The Dorset (Tuniit) culture** — The Dorset Paleo-Inuit, expert sea-mammal hunters known for their carved art, followed the Saqqaq and occupied Greenland's coasts through the long pre-Thule centuries. [aasivissuit-unesco, greenland-history-britannica]
- c. 1200–1300: **Arrival of the Thule** — The Thule people migrated eastward from Alaska with dog sleds, kayaks and whale-hunting technology, settling Greenland's coasts and becoming the direct ancestors of the modern Kalaallit (Greenlandic Inuit). [greenland-history-britannica, aasivissuit-unesco]

## Erik the Red and the Norse Colony — c. 985 – c. 1450

Banished from Iceland, Erik the Red led Norse settlers to found the Eastern and Western Settlements around 985; for four centuries they farmed at the edge of the ice before vanishing.

- c. 985: **Erik the Red founds the Norse colony** — Erik the Red, exiled from Iceland, led settlers to south-west Greenland and established the Eastern and Western Settlements, the first European colony on the island. [erik-britannica, kujataa-unesco]
- c. 1000: **Leif Erikson sails to Vinland** — Erik's son Leif Erikson voyaged west from Greenland to North America, reaching the land the sagas called Vinland and becoming the first European known to have set foot on the American continent. [erik-britannica]
- 1261: **Submission to the Norwegian crown** — The Greenlanders accepted the sovereignty of the king of Norway, tying the colony to the Scandinavian crowns and, through later unions, ultimately to Denmark. [greenland-history-britannica]
- 1408: **The Norse settlements vanish** — A wedding at Hvalsey Church in 1408 is the last dated record of Norse Greenland; the Eastern Settlement was abandoned within decades, the colony's disappearance still debated and linked to the Little Ice Age. [greenland-history-britannica, kujataa-unesco]

Figures:

- Erik the Red (c. 950 – c. 1003) — Founder of the Norse colony in Greenland. Outlawed from Iceland for manslaughter, Erik Thorvaldsson explored the south-western coast, gave the island its alluring name, and led the settlers who founded the Eastern and Western Settlements around 985, establishing the first European presence in Greenland.

## Hans Egede and Two Centuries of Danish Rule — 1721 – 1940

The missionary Hans Egede reopened Greenland to Europe in 1721, beginning a Dano-Norwegian recolonisation, a royal trade monopoly and a long era of Danish administration.

- 1721: **Hans Egede reaches Greenland** — The Dano-Norwegian missionary Hans Egede landed in Greenland seeking the lost Norse colonists, found only Inuit, and founded a mission and trading post at Godthåb (Nuuk), beginning the Danish recolonisation. [egede-britannica, greenland-history-britannica]
- 1776: **The royal trade monopoly** — Denmark established the Royal Greenland Trading Department, granting the crown a strict monopoly over all trade with Greenland and sealing the colony off from foreign contact for nearly two centuries. [greenland-history-britannica]
- 5 Apr 1933: **Danish sovereignty confirmed** — The Permanent Court of International Justice ruled in the Eastern Greenland case that Denmark held sovereignty over the whole island, rejecting Norway's occupation of the eastern coast. [eastern-greenland-pcij, denmark-history-state]

Figures:

- Hans Egede (1686 – 1758) — Dano-Norwegian missionary, 'Apostle of Greenland'. Sailing from Bergen in 1721 to seek the lost Norse colonists, Egede instead settled among the Inuit, founded Godthåb (Nuuk), learned the Greenlandic language and began the colony's Christianisation. He is regarded as the founder of modern Greenland's links with Denmark.

## War, the Cold War and the End of Colony — 1940 – 1979

Cut off from occupied Denmark, Greenland fell under American protection in the Second World War, became a Cold War frontier with Thule Air Base, and in 1953 ceased to be a colony.

- 9 Apr 1941: **Greenland under U.S. protection** — With Denmark occupied, the Danish minister Henrik Kauffmann signed the Agreement Relating to the Defense of Greenland with the United States, placing the island under American protection and opening it to U.S. bases. [denmark-history-state, diis-us-greenland]
- 1951–1953: **Thule Air Base built** — Under a 1951 NATO-era defence agreement, the United States constructed Thule Air Base in north-west Greenland, a major Cold War installation for bombers and early-warning radar, now the Pituffik Space Base. [diis-us-greenland, denmark-history-state]
- 5 Jun 1953: **From colony to Danish county** — The revised Danish constitution abolished Greenland's colonial status and made it an integral county of the Kingdom of Denmark, a change the United Nations accepted as decolonisation. [greenland-history-britannica, naalakkersuisut-about, loc-greenland-selfgov]

## Home Rule and Self-Government — 1979 – 2009

A rising Greenlandic national movement won Home Rule in 1979, took Greenland out of the European Community, and in 2009 secured Self-Government and a recognised right to independence.

- 1 May 1979: **Home Rule established** — After a January 1979 referendum, the Home Rule Act came into force, giving Greenland its own parliament and government with Jonathan Motzfeldt as first premier and responsibility for many domestic affairs. [loc-greenland-selfgov, naalakkersuisut-about]
- 1 Feb 1985: **Greenland leaves the European Community** — Following a 1982 referendum, Greenland withdrew from the European Community in 1985, mainly to control its fishing waters, becoming the only territory ever to leave the bloc. [naalakkersuisut-about, greenland-history-britannica]
- 25 Nov 2008: **Self-Government referendum** — In a referendum some 75 per cent of Greenlandic voters approved the Self-Government arrangement, paving the way for greater autonomy and recognition of the right to self-determination. [naalakkersuisut-about, loc-greenland-selfgov]
- 21 Jun 2009: **Act on Greenland Self-Government in force** — The Self-Government Act came into force on Greenland's National Day, recognising the people of Greenland as a people under international law with the right to self-determination and a legal path to independence. [selfgov-act-stm, stm-greenland, loc-greenland-selfgov]

Figures:

- Jonathan Motzfeldt (1938 – 2010) — First Premier of Greenland under Home Rule. A Lutheran pastor and founder of the Siumut party, Motzfeldt was a leading architect of Greenlandic Home Rule and became the territory's first premier when it took effect on 1 May 1979, dominating Greenlandic politics for two decades.

## Self-Government, Resources and the Question of Independence — 2009 – present

Since 2009 a self-governing Greenland has debated independence and the development of its resources, even as renewed great-power interest in the Arctic has thrust the island onto the world stage.

- 23 Apr 2021: **Múte B. Egede becomes premier** — After elections in 2021, Múte Bourup Egede of Inuit Ataqatigiit became premier, the youngest in Greenland's history, advocating independence as a long-term aim grounded in economic self-reliance. [egede-wilson, naalakkersuisut-about]
- 11 Mar 2025: **The 2025 election under world attention** — Amid intense U.S. interest in the island, Greenlanders voted on 11 March 2025; the centre-right, gradualist Demokraatit party won the largest share, and most Greenlanders rejected becoming part of the United States. [greenland-commons-library, naalakkersuisut-about]
- 7 Apr 2025: **Jens-Frederik Nielsen becomes premier** — Jens-Frederik Nielsen of Demokraatit took office as premier on 7 April 2025, supporting independence pursued pragmatically and insisting Greenland's future is for Greenlanders to decide. [greenland-commons-library, naalakkersuisut-about]

Figures:

- Múte Bourup Egede (born 1987) — Premier of Greenland, 2021–2025. Leader of the left-green Inuit Ataqatigiit, Egede became Greenland's youngest premier in 2021. He pressed for eventual independence while prioritising economic development and resisting external pressure to alter the island's status.
- Jens-Frederik Nielsen (born 1986) — Premier of Greenland from 2025. Leader of the centre-right Demokraatit, Nielsen led his party to a surprise win in the March 2025 election and became premier on 7 April 2025, backing a gradual, pragmatic road to independence amid intense international interest in the island.
