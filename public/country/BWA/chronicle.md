# The Chronicle of Botswana: From the rock art of the Kalahari to a diamond democracy

Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 30 September 1966. The Bechuanaland Protectorate became the independent Republic of Botswana on 30 September 1966, with Seretse Khama as its first president. It entered nationhood as one of the world's poorest countries, with only a few kilometres of paved road and a handful of university graduates.

Botswana's story begins with the San (Basarwa), among the world's oldest continuous hunter-gatherer societies, whose ancestors left tens of thousands of paintings at the Tsodilo Hills. Cattle-keeping Bantu-speaking Tswana chiefdoms later organised the eastern hardveld into powerful merafe (nations) ruled by dikgosi. Hemmed in by the upheavals of the difaqane, by Boer expansion from the Transvaal and by the Ndebele, the Tswana drew on missionary contact and, in 1895, on a celebrated appeal by three kings to keep their land out of Cecil Rhodes's company. Britain declared the Bechuanaland Protectorate in 1885 but governed it from Mafeking, outside its borders, and largely neglected it. At independence on 30 September 1966 the country was one of the poorest on earth; the 1967 discovery of diamonds at Orapa, prudently managed under a stable multiparty democracy, transformed it into one of Africa's notable development successes.

## Hunters, Herders and the Painted Hills — c. 70,000 BCE – c. 500 CE

The San leave one of humanity's oldest artistic records in the Tsodilo Hills before herders and the first farmers arrive.

- c. 24,000 BP: **Rock art at Tsodilo Hills** — San hunter-gatherers paint the Tsodilo Hills; some images are dated to around 24,000 years before present, part of a record of over 4,500 paintings. [unesco-tsodilo, wiki-tsodilo, britmuseum-tsodilo]
- c. 2,000 years ago: **Herders and farmers arrive** — Peoples bringing cattle, sheep and pottery move into present-day Botswana, beginning the long shift from hunter-gathering toward herding and farming societies. [wiki-history-bw, britannica-botswana]
- c. 200 CE: **Early Bantu settlement** — Bantu-speaking settlers, among them the ancestors of the Kalanga, establish farming and cattle-keeping communities in the eastern part of the region. [wiki-history-bw]

## The Tswana Nations and the Difaqane — c. 1700 – 1840

Cattle-keeping Tswana merafe consolidate on the eastern hardveld, then are convulsed by the difaqane and Ndebele raids.

- mid-18th century: **Rise of the Tswana merafe** — The Bangwaketse form the first major Tswana state in the region, followed by the Bakwena, Bangwato and Batawana, cattle-keeping chiefdoms ruled by dikgosi. [wiki-history-bw, britannica-botswana]
- early 19th century: **The difaqane convulses the region** — Wars and forced migrations linked to the wider mfecane/difaqane disrupt Tswana society, bringing raiding, displacement and famine. [wiki-history-bw, sahistory-matabele]
- 1826: **Sebego expels the Kololo** — Sebego, regent of the Bangwaketse, raises some 4,000 men and permanently drives the invading Kololo out of the region. [wiki-history-bw]
- 1830s: **Ndebele raids under Mzilikazi** — Mzilikazi's Ndebele raid Tswana communities through the 1830s, capturing cattle, women and children before migrating north across the Limpopo. [sahistory-matabele, worldhistory-matabele]

## Missionaries, Boers and Mounting Pressure — 1816 – 1885

Christianity spreads through the chiefdoms as the Tswana are caught between Boer expansion, the Ndebele and the gun trade.

- 1816: **London Missionary Society arrives** — LMS missionaries reach the Tswana lands; Robert Moffat later founds Kuruman and translates the Bible into Setswana. [wiki-history-bw, britannica-botswana]
- 1845: **Livingstone founds Kolobeng** — David Livingstone establishes the Kolobeng mission among the Bakwena near Kgosi Sechele, his only church and the source of his single convert. [wiki-history-bw]
- 1852: **Boer attack on the Bakwena** — Transvaal Boers attack Sechele's Bakwena and sack Livingstone's Kolobeng mission, part of mounting pressure from the Transvaal on Tswana land. [wiki-history-bw]
- 1875: **Khama III becomes kgosi of the Bangwato** — The Christian reformer Khama III takes the Bangwato chieftainship, banning liquor and aligning his powerful morafe with the missionaries. [encyaf-khama, wiki-history-bw]

Figures:

- David Livingstone (1813–1873) — LMS missionary and explorer. Settled among the Bakwena and founded the Kolobeng mission in 1845; his travels from this base opened the interior to European attention.

## The Bechuanaland Protectorate — 1885 – 1965

Britain declares a protectorate it then neglects; three kings sail to London to keep their land out of Rhodes's company.

- 31 March 1885: **Bechuanaland Protectorate declared** — After Sir Charles Warren's expedition, Britain proclaims a protectorate over the territory north of the Molopo to check Boer and German expansion. [wiki-bechuanaland, britannica-botswana]
- 1895: **The three kings' mission to Britain** — Khama III, Sebele I and Bathoen I travel to Britain and persuade the government to keep the protectorate out of Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company. [wiki-bechuanaland, encyaf-khama, wiki-bathoen, makingafrican-willoughby]
- 1895: **Capital moved to Mafeking** — The protectorate's administration is seated at Mafeking, a town outside the territory's own borders, where it remains until the 1960s. [wiki-bechuanaland, britannica-botswana]
- 1948: **Seretse Khama marries Ruth Williams** — Bangwato heir Seretse Khama marries Englishwoman Ruth Williams in London, igniting a crisis with apartheid South Africa and Britain. [britannica-seretse, wiki-seretse]
- 1950–1956: **Exile and return of Seretse Khama** — Britain exiles Seretse and Ruth to appease South Africa; he is allowed home in 1956 after renouncing the Bangwato throne, fuelling nationalism. [britannica-seretse, wiki-seretse]

Figures:

- Khama III (Khama the Good) (c. 1837–1923) — Kgosi of the Bangwato. Christian reformer who banned alcohol and led the 1895 mission to Britain; his statecraft helped keep the protectorate out of Rhodes's company.
- Bathoen I (c. 1845–1910) — Kgosi of the Bangwaketse. Mission-educated chief who, with Khama III and Sebele I, won British public and royal support in 1895 to preserve Tswana self-rule.

## Independence, Diamonds and Democracy — 1966 – present

A penniless protectorate becomes a stable democracy and, on the strength of Orapa's diamonds, a development success.

- 1965: **BDP wins the first general election** — Seretse Khama's Bechuanaland Democratic Party wins self-government elections; Gaborone is built as the new capital. [wiki-history-bw, britannica-seretse]
- 30 September 1966: **Independence of Botswana** — The protectorate becomes the Republic of Botswana with Seretse Khama as first president, entering nationhood as one of the world's poorest states. [wiki-history-bw, britannica-seretse, wiki-bechuanaland]
- 1967: **Diamonds discovered at Orapa** — De Beers geologists locate a major diamond kimberlite pipe at Orapa, the find that would transform Botswana's economy. [wiki-orapa, theboar-botswana]
- 1969: **Debswana partnership formed** — Botswana and De Beers form a 50-50 joint venture to mine diamonds, securing for the state a direct share of mineral wealth. [wiki-orapa, theboar-botswana]
- 1980: **Death of Seretse Khama** — Botswana's founding president dies in office; power passes peacefully to Vice-President Quett Masire, confirming the country's democratic stability. [britannica-seretse, wiki-seretse]
