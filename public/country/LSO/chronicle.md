# The Chronicle of Lesotho: From Thaba Bosiu to the Mountain Kingdom

History of Lesotho: From Thaba Bosiu to the Mountain Kingdom. 5 eras and 21 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/LSO/chronicle
Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 4 October 1966. Basutoland became the independent Kingdom of Lesotho on 4 October 1966 under King Moshoeshoe II as constitutional monarch and Leabua Jonathan of the Basotho National Party as prime minister, ending nearly a century of British rule.

Lesotho is a mountainous kingdom in southern Africa, entirely surrounded by the territory of South Africa. Its San hunter-gatherer inhabitants left some of the world's richest rock art before Sotho-Tswana farmers settled the region. The Basotho nation was forged in the 1820s by King Moshoeshoe I, who gathered refugees of the difaqane at his stronghold of Thaba Bosiu, fought the Boers of the Orange Free State, and secured British protection in 1868 to keep his land from absorption into South Africa. As the British protectorate of Basutoland it was kept out of the Union of South Africa, and on 4 October 1966 it became the independent Kingdom of Lesotho.

## Hunters, Painters and Farmers — Prehistory – c. 1800 CE

San hunter-gatherers and their rock art, then Sotho-Tswana farmers, share the highlands of the Maloti-Drakensberg.

- for millennia: **San rock art of the Maloti-Drakensberg** — San hunter-gatherers occupy the mountain rock shelters, leaving one of the world's richest bodies of rock art depicting eland, dance and trance. [britmuseum-rockart, wiki-history-lesotho]
- from c. 2nd millennium CE: **Sotho-Tswana settlement** — Bantu-speaking Sotho-Tswana farmers and herders settle the region, bringing cattle, cultivation and iron-working alongside earlier San and Khoekhoe peoples. [wiki-history-lesotho, britmuseum-rockart]
- 18th century: **Dispersed Sotho chiefdoms** — Numerous small, rivalrous Sotho-speaking chiefdoms occupy the highveld and mountains, with no single overarching authority. [wiki-history-lesotho, eb-lesotho]

## Moshoeshoe and the Birth of the Basotho — c. 1820 – 1868

Amid the difaqane, Moshoeshoe I gathers scattered refugees at Thaba Bosiu and welds them into a nation.

- c. 1824: **Moshoeshoe occupies Thaba Bosiu** — Moshoeshoe I settles the defensible mountain of Thaba Bosiu, the stronghold from which he gathers difaqane refugees into the Basotho nation. [eb-lesotho, eb-moshoeshoe, sahistory-moshoeshoe]
- 1833: **Paris missionaries arrive** — Moshoeshoe invites the Paris Evangelical Missionary Society into his kingdom; the missionaries help create a written Sesotho and advise him on dealings with Europeans. [eb-lesotho, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1858: **Senekal's War with the Orange Free State** — Conflict erupts with the Boers of the Orange Free State over land in the western lowlands, the first of the Free State–Basotho Wars. [eb-lesotho, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1865: **Seqiti War and loss of the lowlands** — A devastating war with the Orange Free State sees Boer commandos overrun much of the fertile western lowlands and besiege Thaba Bosiu. [eb-lesotho, wiki-history-lesotho]

Figures:

- Moshoeshoe I (c. 1786–1870) — Founder and first paramount chief of the Basotho. Gathered the scattered survivors of the difaqane at Thaba Bosiu and, through diplomacy more than war, forged the Basotho nation, securing its survival by appealing for British protection.

## Basutoland: Protectorate and the Gun War — 1868 – 1909

British protection saves Basotho land from the Boers, but annexation to the Cape sparks the Gun War.

- 12 March 1868: **Basutoland declared a British protectorate** — Britain takes Moshoeshoe's kingdom under its protection, halting Boer absorption and fixing the borders that broadly survive as modern Lesotho. [eb-lesotho, sahistory-becomes-british, wiki-history-lesotho]
- February 1869: **Convention of Aliwal North** — The boundary settlement with the Orange Free State confirms the loss of the fertile western lowlands, the "Conquered Territory", to the Boers. [eb-lesotho, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1871: **Annexation to the Cape Colony** — Basutoland is annexed to the Cape Colony without Basotho consent, beginning a deeply unpopular period of colonial rule. [eb-gun-war, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1880: **The Gun War** — The Basotho rebel against Cape disarmament and taxation; they retain their guns and effectively repudiate Cape authority. [eb-gun-war, wiki-gun-war, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1884: **Return to direct British rule** — Unable to control the territory, the Cape gives it up; Basutoland reverts to direct rule from Britain under a resident commissioner. [eb-gun-war, wiki-history-lesotho]

## High Commission Territory to Independence — 1910 – 1966

Held apart from South Africa, Basutoland moves through decades of indirect rule to nationhood.

- 1910: **Kept out of the Union of South Africa** — On the formation of the Union, Basutoland is excluded as a British High Commission Territory rather than incorporated into South Africa. [britannica-southern-africa, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 20th century: **Migrant labour to South Africa** — With little arable land, large numbers of Basotho men become migrant workers on South African farms and gold mines, and remittances become central to the economy. [samp-migration, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1965: **Basotho National Party wins the pre-independence election** — Leabua Jonathan's BNP narrowly defeats the Basutoland Congress Party, positioning him to lead the territory to independence. [wiki-leabua-jonathan, wiki-history-lesotho]
- 4 October 1966: **Independence as the Kingdom of Lesotho** — Basutoland becomes the independent Kingdom of Lesotho under King Moshoeshoe II and Prime Minister Leabua Jonathan, ending British rule. [wiki-history-lesotho, wiki-leabua-jonathan, historycentral-indep]

Figures:

- Leabua Jonathan (1914–1987) — First Prime Minister of Lesotho. Leader of the Basotho National Party who led Lesotho to independence in 1966, then suspended the constitution in 1970 after losing the first post-independence election, ruling until his overthrow in 1986.

## The Kingdom in the Sky — 1966 – present

Coups, a contested monarchy and a great water scheme define independent Lesotho, the mountain enclave.

- 1970: **Constitution suspended after lost election** — Having apparently lost the first post-independence election, Leabua Jonathan annuls the result, suspends the constitution and rules by decree. [wiki-leabua-jonathan, wiki-history-lesotho]
- January 1986: **Military coup and the water treaty** — A military coup overthrows Jonathan; in the same year the new government signs the treaty launching the Lesotho Highlands Water Project with South Africa. [wiki-history-lesotho, lhwp-conversation]
- 15 January 1996: **Death of Moshoeshoe II** — After exile and a brief restoration, King Moshoeshoe II dies in a car accident; his son Letsie III returns to the throne. [wiki-history-lesotho, eb-moshoeshoe-ii]
- 1998: **Disputed election and SADC intervention** — A contested election sparks unrest and a South African-led SADC military intervention; much of Maseru is left in ruins. [wiki-history-lesotho]
- 1998–2002: **Katse and Mohale dams completed** — The Katse Dam (Phase 1A) and then the Mohale Dam are completed, sending Lesotho's mountain water to South Africa's Vaal system in exchange for royalties. [lhwp-conversation, wiki-lesotho, afdb-lhwp]
