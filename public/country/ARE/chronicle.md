# The Chronicle of United Arab Emirates: From the Bronze Age oases and pearling dhows of the lower Gulf to the oil-fueled federation of seven emirates.

Founding: Formation of the United Arab Emirates federation — 2 December 1971. On 2 December 1971 the rulers of Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm al-Quwain and Fujairah proclaimed the United Arab Emirates, a federation born as Britain ended the treaty system that had governed the Trucial Coast since the 19th century. Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan of Abu Dhabi, the principal architect of the union, became its first president, with Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum of Dubai as vice president. The seventh emirate, Ras al-Khaimah, acceded on 10 February 1972, completing the federation of seven.

The United Arab Emirates occupies the southeastern corner of the Arabian Peninsula, a coast of desert, oasis and sea whose people built Bronze Age settlements at Hili and Umm an-Nar, dived for pearls, and traded across the Indian Ocean for millennia. In the 19th century a series of treaties with Britain bound the coastal sheikhdoms into the Trucial States, and the collapse of the pearl trade in the 1930s gave way to the discovery of oil in Abu Dhabi from 1958. When Britain announced its withdrawal from the Gulf, the rulers of the emirates joined to proclaim the United Arab Emirates on 2 December 1971 under Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan. Oil wealth and the meteoric rise of Dubai have since transformed the federation into one of the world's most globalized states.

## Bronze Age Oases and the Pearling Coast — c. 3000 BCE - 600 CE

Long before the federation, the lower Gulf nourished sophisticated Bronze Age cultures at Hili and Umm an-Nar, oasis farmers tapping groundwater and seafarers trading copper, dates and pearls across the Arabian Sea.

- c. 3rd millennium BCE: **Hafit-period tombs near Jebel Hafit** — Beehive-shaped stone tombs of the Hafit period appear on the slopes of Jebel Hafit near Al Ain, the earliest monumental architecture of the region and a marker of settled life at the desert's edge. [unesco-alain, unesco-news774]
- c. 2500 BCE: **The Umm an-Nar culture flourishes** — Named for an island off Abu Dhabi, the Umm an-Nar culture built circular communal tombs, fortified towers and agricultural settlements at Hili, trading copper and goods with Mesopotamia and the Indus valley. [abudhabi-hili, unesco-alain]
- Iron Age: **The aflaj irrigation system at Hili** — Hili preserves one of the oldest known examples of the aflaj, underground channels that carried mountain groundwater to oasis date gardens, sustaining agriculture in an arid land. [unesco-alain, unesco-news774]

## Islam, Julfar and the Maritime Trade — 630 - 1700 CE

Islam reached the coast in the lifetime of the Prophet, and over the following centuries the ports of the lower Gulf, above all Julfar, grew into busy entrepots in the trade between Arabia, Persia, India and the East.

- c. 630 CE: **Islam reaches the lower Gulf coast** — Islam spread to southeastern Arabia during the lifetime of the Prophet Muhammad, drawing the coastal communities into the new faith and the trade networks of the Muslim world. [uae-embassy-history, uae-mofa-history]
- c. 632 CE: **The Battle of Dibba in the Ridda Wars** — After the Prophet's death, a campaign by the first caliph Abu Bakr against apostate tribes culminated in a battle at Dibba on the east coast, securing the region within the Islamic order. [uae-embassy-history]
- 16th century: **Portuguese seize control of the coast** — Portuguese naval expansion fortified Gulf ports to dominate the spice and pearl trade, beginning two centuries of European and Omani contest over the region's sea lanes. [uae-mofa-history, loc-gulf-study]

## The Qawasim and the Making of the Trucial States — 1700 - 1892

The Qasimi sea power dominated the lower Gulf until British campaigns broke it; a chain of treaties from 1820 onward then bound the coastal sheikhdoms to Britain and gave them the name by which they were long known, the Trucial States.

- 1819: **Britain breaks the Qasimi sea power** — After repeated clashes over Gulf shipping, British forces subdued the Qasimi dynasty and razed Ras al-Khaimah, ending the maritime dominance of the Qawasim in the lower Gulf. [britannica-qasimi, britannica-uae-history]
- 1820: **The General Treaty of Peace** — The coastal sheikhs signed the General Treaty of Peace with Britain, limiting maritime aggression and initiating the series of agreements that established British paramountcy over the future emirates. [britannica-uae-history, britannica-qasimi]
- 1853: **The Perpetual Maritime Truce** — The Treaty of Maritime Peace in Perpetuity committed the rulers to a permanent truce at sea, giving the coast the name Trucial Coast and its statelets the name Trucial States. [britannica-perpetual-truce, britannica-uae-history]
- 1892: **The Exclusive Agreement with Britain** — The Trucial rulers agreed to cede no territory and form no foreign relations except through Britain, in return for British protection, making the coast in effect a British protected zone. [britannica-exclusive, britannica-uae-history]

## The Fall of Pearls, the Rise of Oil, and Federation — 1900 - 1972

The collapse of the pearl trade plunged the coast into hardship until oil was struck in Abu Dhabi; when Britain announced its departure, the rulers united, founding the United Arab Emirates on 2 December 1971.

- late 1920s-1930s: **Collapse of the pearl trade** — The Great Depression and the Japanese cultured pearl destroyed the natural-pearl economy that had sustained the Trucial Coast, bringing years of poverty and hunger. [uae-embassy-history, uae-mofa-history]
- 1962: **Abu Dhabi exports its first oil** — After commercial oil was struck in Abu Dhabi from 1958-1960, the first cargo of crude was exported from Das Island in 1962, beginning the transformation of the emirates' fortunes. [uae-embassy-history, britannica-uae-history]
- 1966: **Sheikh Zayed becomes ruler of Abu Dhabi** — Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan succeeded his brother Shakhbut as ruler of Abu Dhabi and began directing oil revenue into the building of a modern state. [britannica-zayed, uae-embassy-history]
- January 1968: **Britain announces withdrawal from the Gulf** — The British government declared it would withdraw its forces and end its treaty role in the Gulf by the end of 1971, prompting the Trucial rulers to plan a federation. [britannica-uae-history, britannica-rashid]
- 2 December 1971: **The United Arab Emirates is founded** — The rulers of Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm al-Quwain and Fujairah proclaimed the United Arab Emirates, with Sheikh Zayed as first president and Sheikh Rashid as vice president. [uae-portal-history, britannica-uae-history, britannica-zayed]
- 10 February 1972: **Ras al-Khaimah joins the federation** — The seventh emirate, Ras al-Khaimah, acceded to the union in February 1972, completing the United Arab Emirates as a federation of seven emirates. [uae-mofa-history, britannica-rasalkhaimah]

Figures:

- Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (c. 1918 - 2004) — Ruler of Abu Dhabi and first President of the UAE. Ruler of Abu Dhabi from 1966 and principal architect of the federation, Zayed used oil wealth to build a modern state and led the emirates into union, serving as the UAE's first president from 1971 until his death.
- Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum (c. 1912 - 1990) — Ruler of Dubai and Vice President of the UAE. Sheikh of Dubai from 1958 and chief builder of the modern emirate, Rashid dredged Dubai Creek and developed its port and trade; co-founder of the UAE, he served as vice president and later prime minister.

## Oil Wealth and the Global Federation — 1972 - present

Petroleum revenue and the spectacular rise of Dubai turned a cluster of poor sheikhdoms into one of the world's most globalized economies, while the federation deepened under Zayed's successors.

- 1973: **A common currency and federal integration** — The federation introduced the UAE dirham and advanced reforms to unify infrastructure and government, knitting the seven emirates into a single modern state. [britannica-zayed, britannica-uae-history]
- January 2010: **The Burj Khalifa opens in Dubai** — Dubai inaugurated the Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world, a symbol of the emirate's wager on trade, real estate and tourism rather than oil. [britannica-uae-history, uae-portal-history]
- February 2021: **The Hope probe reaches Mars** — The UAE's Hope spacecraft entered orbit around Mars, making the country the first Arab nation and the fifth worldwide to reach the planet. [uae-portal-history, britannica-mbz]
- May 2022: **Mohamed bin Zayed elected president** — Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan was elected president of the United Arab Emirates, succeeding his late brother Khalifa and continuing the Al Nahyan leadership of the federation. [britannica-mbz]

Figures:

- Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan (born 1961) — President of the UAE and ruler of Abu Dhabi. A son of the founding president Zayed, he shaped Abu Dhabi's foreign and economic policy for decades before being elected president of the United Arab Emirates in May 2022.
