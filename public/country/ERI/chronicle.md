# The Chronicle of Eritrea: From the Red Sea ports of Aksum to Africa's longest war of independence and one of its most closed states.

Founding: Independence from Ethiopia — 24 May 1993 (de facto 1991). Eritrea's modern statehood was won by armed struggle and confirmed by ballot. After three decades of war, Eritrean People's Liberation Front fighters entered Asmara on 24 May 1991, ending Ethiopian rule de facto as the Marxist Derg collapsed in Addis Ababa. The EPLF formed a provisional government but deferred a final decision to the Eritrean people. In a UN-monitored referendum held over 23-25 April 1993, an overwhelming majority voted for independence, and on 24 May 1993 Eritrea formally proclaimed itself a sovereign state, joining the United Nations days later. Isaias Afwerki, the EPLF's wartime leader, became its first and, decades on, still only president.

Eritrea occupies the southwestern shore of the Red Sea, a corridor where Africa, Arabia and the Mediterranean world have met for three thousand years. Its highlands and coast formed part of the heartland of the Kingdom of Aksum, whose port of Adulis carried ivory, gold and incense to Rome, India and Arabia, while the Dahlak Islands and Massawa became centres of Red Sea and Indian Ocean trade. After Aksum's decline the coast passed through Beja, Muslim and then Ottoman hands, and in the nineteenth century through Egyptian control, before Italy proclaimed Eritrea its 'first-born colony' in 1890 and built Asmara into a modernist colonial capital. Italy used Eritrea as the springboard for its invasions of Ethiopia, including the army shattered at Adwa in 1896 and the conquest of 1935-36. British forces ended Italian rule in 1941; in 1952 the United Nations federated Eritrea with Ethiopia, only for Emperor Haile Selassie to annex it outright in 1962. That act opened a thirty-year war of independence, waged first by the Eritrean Liberation Front and then by the Eritrean People's Liberation Front, which captured Asmara in 1991 as the Derg fell in Ethiopia. A referendum in 1993 brought formal independence under Isaias Afwerki, but a ruinous 1998-2000 border war with Ethiopia and decades of one-party rule have made Eritrea one of the most isolated countries on earth.

## Aksum, Adulis and the Red Sea Trade — c. 1st century CE - c. 7th century CE

The Eritrean coast and highlands lay at the heart of the Kingdom of Aksum, whose port of Adulis linked Africa to Rome, Arabia and India.

- mid-1st century CE: **Adulis named in the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** — The Greek navigational handbook describes Adulis as the principal, legally regulated port serving the Aksumite hinterland, trading ivory, rhinoceros horn, tortoise shell and other goods. [jstor-adulis, newworld-aksum]
- c. 1st-2nd century CE: **Aksum emerges as a Red Sea trading empire** — Aksum consolidates control of the northern highlands and the port of Adulis, growing into a major commercial power linking Rome, Arabia and India. [newworld-aksum, wiki-eritrea-history]
- c. 3rd century CE: **Aksumite coinage** — Aksumite kings begin minting gold, silver and bronze coins, advertising the kingdom's wealth and its place among the great powers of the age. [newworld-aksum]
- c. 7th century CE: **Decline of Aksum and Adulis** — Shifting trade routes and the rise of Islam on the Arabian shore erode Red Sea commerce; Adulis fades and Aksum's power contracts inland. [newworld-aksum, britannica-massawa]

## The Beja, the Dahlak Sultanate and the Muslim Coast — c. 8th century - 1557

After Aksum, the coast became a Muslim-dominated trading world of the Beja, the Dahlak Islands and the rising port of Massawa, set against the Christian highlands.

- c. 8th century: **Beja ascendancy on the coast** — After the fall of Aksum, Beja confederacies dominate the caravan routes and coastal trade of what is now Eritrea, holding sway in the Massawa region for centuries. [wiki-massawa, britannica-massawa]
- 11th century: **Rise of the Sultanate of Dahlak** — The Dahlak Islands become a wealthy Muslim sultanate controlling pearl fisheries and the trade between the Red Sea and the Christian highlands. [wiki-massawa, britannica-massawa]
- 14th-15th century: **Massawa grows as a leading port** — Massawa develops into the principal harbour of the coast, mediating trade between the highland interior and the Arabian Peninsula. [wiki-massawa]
- 1557: **Ottomans capture Massawa** — Ottoman forces under Ozdemir Pasha take Massawa from the highland kingdom, bringing the Eritrean coast under the Ottoman Empire and ending local dynastic control. [wiki-massawa, britannica-massawa]

## Ottomans, Egyptians and the Contested Coast — 1557 - 1885

For three centuries the Red Sea coast was ruled at arm's length from Constantinople and Cairo, while the highlands stayed beyond imperial reach.

- 1557: **Ottoman Habesh province established** — After taking Massawa, the Ottomans organise their Red Sea holdings as the province of Habesh, ruling the coast through local agents. [wiki-massawa, britannica-massawa]
- 1865: **Egyptian administration of Massawa** — Egypt, with Ottoman consent, takes over the administration of Massawa and the coast, extending its influence toward the highlands. [britannica-massawa, wiki-massawa]
- 1869: **Italy gains a foothold at Assab** — An Italian shipping company acquires the bay of Assab on the southern Red Sea coast, Italy's first toehold on the future Eritrean shore. [wiki-italian-eritrea, worldhistory-italian-eritrea]
- 1885: **Italy occupies Massawa** — As Egypt withdraws amid the Mahdist crisis, Italian troops occupy Massawa, the key port of the coast, beginning Italy's drive to build a colony. [wiki-italian-eritrea, britannica-massawa]

## Italy's First-Born Colony — 1890 - 1941

Italy proclaimed Eritrea its colonia primogenita in 1890, built Asmara into a modernist capital, and used the territory as the base for its wars on Ethiopia.

- 1 January 1890: **Colony of Eritrea proclaimed** — Italy unites its Red Sea holdings into the colony of Eritrea, promoted as its colonia primogenita or 'first-born colony', with the capital initially at Massawa. [worldhistory-italian-eritrea, wiki-italian-eritrea]
- 1 March 1896: **Battle of Adwa** — Advancing from Eritrea, an Italian army is destroyed by Emperor Menelik II at Adwa, the worst defeat of a European army in Africa, securing Ethiopian sovereignty and fixing Eritrea's southern frontier. [britannica-adwa, blackpast-adwa]
- 1897: **Asmara becomes the colonial capital** — The colonial seat is moved from Massawa to the highland town of Asmara, later rebuilt as a planned modernist city. [unesco-asmara, wiki-italian-eritrea]
- October 1935 - May 1936: **Conquest of Ethiopia launched from Eritrea** — Italian forces invade Ethiopia from Eritrea, taking Addis Ababa in May 1936; Eritrea is absorbed into Italian East Africa. [wiki-second-italo, wiki-italian-eritrea]
- February-April 1941: **British defeat Italy at Keren** — Allied forces overcome Italian troops at the Battle of Keren; Asmara and Massawa surrender, ending Italian rule and beginning British military administration. [wiki-keren, wiki-bma-eritrea]

Figures:

- Hamid Idris Awate (c. 1910-1962) — Eritrean nationalist; fired the first shots of the independence war. A lowland fighter who, on 1 September 1961, opened fire on Ethiopian forces at Mount Adal, an act remembered as the beginning of the Eritrean armed struggle.

## Federation, Annexation and the Thirty-Year War — 1941 - 1991

British administration gave way to a UN federation with Ethiopia in 1952; its abolition in 1962 ignited a thirty-year war of independence.

- 1941: **British military administration begins** — Following the Allied victory at Keren, Britain administers Eritrea for the next decade while its post-war status is debated. [wiki-bma-eritrea]
- 11 September 1952: **UN federation with Ethiopia** — Under UN Resolution 390 A (V) of 1950, Eritrea becomes an autonomous unit federated with Ethiopia, keeping its own parliament, flag and control of internal affairs. [wiki-federation, history-state-eritrea]
- 1 September 1961: **Armed struggle begins at Mount Adal** — Hamid Idris Awate and a small ELF band fire on Ethiopian forces, the conventional start of the thirty-year Eritrean War of Independence. [wiki-eritrean-war, wiki-eritrea-history]
- November 1962: **Haile Selassie annexes Eritrea** — After steadily eroding Eritrean autonomy, the emperor dissolves the federation and annexes Eritrea as a province, hardening the independence war. [wiki-federation, wiki-eritrea-history]
- early-mid 1970s: **Rise of the EPLF** — The Eritrean People's Liberation Front splits from the ELF and becomes the dominant, Marxist-led nationalist movement waging the war. [wiki-eplf, csis-eritrea]
- 24 May 1991: **EPLF captures Asmara** — As the Derg regime collapses in Ethiopia, EPLF forces enter Asmara, winning de facto independence and forming a provisional government. [wiki-eplf, wiki-eritrean-war]

Figures:

- Haile Selassie (1892-1975) — Emperor of Ethiopia. The Ethiopian emperor whose dismantling of Eritrean autonomy and 1962 annexation of the territory turned a federation into a province and helped ignite the long war of independence.

## Independence, Border War and the Closed State — 1991 - present

A 1993 referendum brought formal independence; a devastating border war with Ethiopia and decades of one-party rule have left Eritrea among the world's most closed states.

- 23-25 April 1993: **Independence referendum** — Eritreans vote overwhelmingly for independence in a UN-monitored referendum, with near-unanimous approval on a high turnout. [wiki-referendum, history-state-eritrea]
- 24 May 1993: **Formal independence** — Eritrea proclaims itself a sovereign state and is admitted to the United Nations days later; Isaias Afwerki becomes its first president. [wiki-referendum, history-state-eritrea]
- May 1998 - June 2000: **Border war with Ethiopia** — A dispute over Badme escalates into a full-scale war that kills tens of thousands and displaces more than a million before a ceasefire. [wiki-1998-invasion, wiki-algiers]
- 12 December 2000: **Algiers Agreement** — Eritrea and Ethiopia sign the Algiers peace agreement; a boundary commission later awards Badme to Eritrea, but Ethiopia refuses to withdraw, leaving a frozen stand-off. [wiki-algiers]
- since 2001: **Entrenched one-party rule** — Promised elections and the ratified constitution are shelved; indefinite national service and bans on independent press, parties and religion make Eritrea one of the world's most closed states. [hrw-peace-deal, factbook-eritrea]
- 9 July 2018: **Peace with Ethiopia** — Isaias Afwerki and Abiy Ahmed sign a declaration formally ending the border war, though internal repression in Eritrea continues largely unchanged. [hrw-peace-deal]

Figures:

- Isaias Afwerki (born 1946) — EPLF leader and first president of Eritrea. Wartime commander of the EPLF who became Eritrea's first president on independence in 1993 and has ruled the one-party state ever since, without national elections.
