Founding: Independence from the United Kingdom — 21 September 1981. On 21 September 1981 the British colony of British Honduras — renamed Belize in June 1973 — became the independent nation of Belize, the last British territory on the American mainland to win statehood. Independence came after three decades of nationalist politics led by George Cadle Price and the People's United Party, which had carried the colony through universal adult suffrage in 1954, internal self-government in 1964, and the renaming of 1973. It was achieved within the Commonwealth, with the British monarch as head of state represented by a governor-general, and Price as the new country's first prime minister. The path had been complicated by Guatemala's territorial claim: Guatemala refused to recognise the new state, and a British garrison remained in Belize for years after independence to deter any incursion. The United Nations General Assembly had repeatedly backed Belize's right to self-determination, and dozens of nations recognised it immediately; the United States did so on 29 October 1981. Belize thus entered the world as a sovereign state with its borders intact but its sovereignty still formally contested by its western neighbour.

Belize is a small, English-speaking nation on the Caribbean coast of Central America, the only country in the region with a British colonial past rather than a Spanish one. Its lowland forests were once dense with Maya settlement — Caracol, Xunantunich, Lamanai and Altun Ha among them — before the Classic Maya world unravelled in the ninth and tenth centuries. From the seventeenth century British buccaneers and woodcutters, the "Baymen", settled the coast to cut logwood and then mahogany, importing enslaved Africans for the work and repelling a final Spanish assault at the Battle of St. George's Caye in 1798; the colony of British Honduras was formally constituted in 1862. A distinctive plural society of Creoles, Garifuna, Maya and Mestizos took shape, and under George Cadle Price and the People's United Party the colony moved through self-government to independence as Belize on 21 September 1981 — though Guatemala's long-standing claim to the territory, neutrally before the International Court of Justice since 2019, remains unresolved.

## The land of the Maya — c. 1500 BCE – c. 1000 CE

Long before any European arrived, the lowlands of Belize were dense with Maya cities — Caracol, Xunantunich, Lamanai and Altun Ha — before the Classic Maya world came apart in the ninth and tenth centuries.

- c. 1500 BCE: **Maya settlement of the lowlands** — Maya-speaking peoples settled the lowland forests and river valleys of present-day Belize, founding farming villages that over centuries grew into towns and cities across the heart of the Maya world. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize, mfa-belize-history]
- 562 CE: **Caracol defeats Tikal** — Caracol, allied with Calakmul, won a decisive "Star War" victory over Tikal in 562 CE; the defeated city carved no monuments for over a century, marking Caracol's rise as a great power of the southern lowlands. [caracol-project, britannica-history-belize]
- c. 600–800 CE: **Height of the Classic Maya cities** — Caracol, Xunantunich, Lamanai, Altun Ha and others flourished as ceremonial and political centres, with monumental architecture, hieroglyphic inscriptions and populations in the tens of thousands sustained by intensive agriculture. [caracol-project, britannica-history-belize, mfa-belize-history]
- 9th–10th centuries: **The Classic Maya collapse** — From the late eighth century the southern lowland cities ceased raising monuments and were abandoned, in a prolonged collapse attributed to drought, environmental stress and warfare — though Lamanai and scattered Maya communities endured. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize]

## The Baymen, the forest and the slaves — c. 1638 – 1798

From the seventeenth century British buccaneers and woodcutters — the Baymen — settled the coast to cut logwood and mahogany, importing enslaved Africans for the work and beating off a final Spanish assault at St. George's Caye in 1798.

- c. 1638: **British woodcutters settle the coast** — English and Scottish buccaneers and logwood cutters began settling the coast of present-day Belize, beyond effective Spanish control; calling themselves the Baymen, they cut logwood for the European dye trade. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize, officialhistorian-belize]
- early 18th century: **Enslaved Africans imported for the timber trade** — From the early 1700s the Baymen imported enslaved Africans, many via Jamaica and Bermuda, to cut and haul logwood and then mahogany — the labour on which the settlement's forest economy rested. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize]
- 1763 and 1783: **Spain concedes cutting rights** — By the treaties of Paris (1763) and Versailles (1783) Spain granted British subjects the right to cut logwood — and after 1786 the more valuable mahogany — within defined limits, while reserving sovereignty over the territory. [britannica-history-belize, mfa-belize-history, loc-guyana-belize]
- 10 September 1798: **Battle of St. George's Caye** — In engagements off St. George's Caye, the outnumbered Baymen, a small British force and enslaved Africans repelled a Spanish fleet from the Yucatán; the Spanish withdrew on 10 September, ending the last attempt to expel the settlers. [britannica-history-belize, mfa-belize-history, officialhistorian-belize]

## British Honduras and a plural society — 1798 – 1950

The settlement was formally constituted the colony of British Honduras in 1862; emancipation, the arrival of the Garifuna and successive refugees forged a plural Creole, Garifuna, Maya and Mestizo society — and a frontier Guatemala would claim.

- 1802: **The Garifuna reach the southern coast** — The Garifuna (Garinagu), deported by the British from St. Vincent, settled the southern coast around Stann Creek and Punta Gorda; their arrival on 19 November is marked as Garifuna Settlement Day, a national holiday from 1941. [loc-guyana-belize, britannica-history-belize]
- 1 August 1838: **Emancipation completed** — Following abolition across the British Empire from 1834, the apprenticeship system ended in British Honduras on 1 August 1838, freeing the enslaved; the freed African population formed the core of the colony's Creole society. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize]
- from 1847: **Caste War refugees swell the population** — The Caste War of Yucatán drove thousands of Maya and Spanish-speaking Mestizo refugees into the colony's north and west, adding to the Creole and Garifuna populations and deepening Belize's plural society. [britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize]
- 1859: **The Anglo-Guatemalan boundary treaty** — Britain and Guatemala signed a treaty fixing the settlement's limits from the Río Hondo to the Sarstoon; the unfulfilled Article 7, promising a means of communication never built, became the root of Guatemala's later claim. [oas-belize-guatemala, icj-belize-guatemala, mfa-belize-history]
- 12 May 1862: **The Colony of British Honduras** — The settlement was formally declared the Colony of British Honduras under a lieutenant-governor subordinate to Jamaica; it became a separate Crown Colony in 1871 and was made independent of Jamaica under its own governor in 1884. [britannica-history-belize, officialhistorian-belize, loc-guyana-belize]

## Price, the PUP and independence — 1950 – 1981

George Cadle Price and the People's United Party led British Honduras from universal suffrage through self-government and the renaming to Belize, to independence within the Commonwealth on 21 September 1981.

- 29 September 1950: **The People's United Party founded** — Amid anger over the 1949 currency devaluation, the People's United Party was founded and soon led by George Cadle Price; it championed democratic rights and self-rule and would dominate Belizean politics for decades. [britannica-history-belize, britannica-price, mfa-belize-history]
- 1954: **Universal adult suffrage** — Britain granted universal adult suffrage to British Honduras in 1954; the PUP won the ensuing elections, beginning a long run of electoral dominance that carried Price's party toward self-government and independence. [britannica-history-belize, mfa-belize-history]
- 1964: **Internal self-government** — Under a new constitution Britain granted internal self-government, retaining only defence and foreign affairs; George Price became premier, with Belizeans now running their own domestic affairs. [britannica-history-belize, britannica-price, mfa-belize-history]
- June 1973: **British Honduras renamed Belize** — In June 1973 the colony's name was officially changed from British Honduras to Belize, an assertion of national identity adopted eight years before formal independence. [officialhistorian-belize, britannica-history-belize, loc-guyana-belize]
- 21 September 1981: **Independence as Belize** — Belize became an independent state within the Commonwealth on 21 September 1981 with George Price as first prime minister — the last British colony on the Central American mainland to win independence, its frontier still claimed by Guatemala. [officialhistorian-belize, britannica-history-belize, mfa-belize-history]
- 29 Oct 1981: **Recognition and an embassy** — Five weeks after independence the United States raises its consulate general to embassy status, recognising Belize and establishing diplomatic relations on the same day. [officialhistorian-belize]

Figures:

- George Cadle Price (1919–2011) — Father of the Nation; first Prime Minister of Belize. Belize City man of Creole and Mestizo descent and a Roman Catholic intellectual once bound for the priesthood, Price led the People's United Party from the 1950s, served as First Minister and premier under British rule, and became independent Belize's first prime minister in 1981. Honoured as "Father of the Nation", he steered the country through suffrage, self-government and independence while defending its territory against Guatemala's claim.

## Independent Belize and the Guatemalan question — 1981 – present

Since independence Belize has been a stable Commonwealth democracy of two alternating parties, a reef-and-rainforest tourism economy and a famed barrier reef — while the unresolved Guatemalan claim has been carried, neutrally, before the International Court of Justice.

- 1996: **Barrier Reef made a World Heritage site** — UNESCO inscribed the Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System — seven protected areas including the Blue Hole and Half Moon Caye — as a World Heritage site, recognising the largest reef in the Atlantic-Caribbean and second-largest on Earth. [unesco-barrier-reef, britannica-belize]
- 8 December 2008: **Special Agreement on the dispute** — Belize and Guatemala signed a Special Agreement committing to submit Guatemala's territorial, insular and maritime claim to the International Court of Justice, subject to approval by referendum in each country. [oas-belize-guatemala, icj-belize-guatemala]
- 8 May 2019: **Belize votes to go to the ICJ** — After Guatemalans approved referral on 15 April 2018, Belizeans voted on 8 May 2019 to submit the dispute to the International Court of Justice; the Court was seised of the case on 7 June 2019. [icj-belize-guatemala, oas-belize-guatemala, belize-referendum]
- 2020 onward: **Pleadings before the World Court** — Guatemala and Belize filed written pleadings before the International Court of Justice in the case "Guatemala's Territorial, Insular and Maritime Claim (Guatemala/Belize)", moving toward a final, binding judgment on the century-old dispute. [icj-belize-guatemala]
- July 2026: **A nation of 419,000** — The Statistical Institute of Belize puts the mid-year population at 418,982 — a country the size of Wales with fewer people than a mid-sized city, holding the Atlantic's largest reef and some of the Maya world's greatest cities. [sib-belize]

Figures:

- Dean Barrow (born 1951) — Prime Minister of Belize (2008–2020). Belize's first prime minister of African Belizean descent and its longest-serving, leading the United Democratic Party through three terms; under his government the Special Agreement with Guatemala advanced toward referenda and the International Court of Justice.
- John Briceño (born 1960) — Prime Minister of Belize (since 2020). Leader of the People's United Party who became prime minister in 2020, governing as Belize pressed its case at the International Court of Justice and managed the reef-and-tourism economy that anchors the modern nation.
