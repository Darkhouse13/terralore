# The Chronicle of Tunisia: From Carthage to the cradle of the Arab Spring

History of Tunisia: From Carthage to the cradle of the Arab Spring. 6 eras and 22 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/TUN/chronicle
Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from France — 20 March 1956. On 20 March 1956 France recognised Tunisian sovereignty, ending seventy-five years of protectorate rule. Habib Bourguiba, leader of the Neo-Destour, became prime minister; in July 1957 the monarchy of the Husaynid beys was abolished and a republic proclaimed, with Bourguiba as its first president.

Tunisia occupies the fertile hinge of the Mediterranean where the Phoenician city of Carthage once rivalled Rome. Across three thousand years the land was Punic, Roman, Christian, Vandal and Byzantine before the Arab conquest planted Islam and founded the holy city of Kairouan in 670. A succession of dynasties — Aghlabid, Fatimid, Zirid and Hafsid — made Ifriqiya a centre of learning before Ottoman governors and the Husaynid beys ruled from Tunis. France imposed a protectorate in 1881; Habib Bourguiba's Neo-Destour won independence in 1956; and in 2010–2011 the Jasmine Revolution overthrew Zine El Abidine Ben Ali and lit the fuse of the Arab Spring.

## Carthage & the Punic Wars — c. 814 BCE – 146 BCE

Phoenician settlers built a maritime empire on the African coast — and fought Rome to the death for the Mediterranean.

- c. 814 BCE: **Foundation of Carthage** — Phoenician settlers from Tyre establish Carthage, which grows into a dominant Mediterranean trading power. [eb-carthage146, wiki-tunisia-history]
- 218 BCE: **Hannibal's war on Rome** — In the Second Punic War Hannibal invades Italy with elephants and wins at Cannae, before defeat at Zama in 202 BCE. [eb-punicwars, whe-punicwars]
- 146 BCE: **Rome destroys Carthage** — Roman armies raze Carthage in the Third Punic War; its lands become the province of Africa. [eb-carthage146, africaproconsularis]

Figures:

- Hannibal Barca (247–183 BCE) — Carthaginian general. Led Carthage in the Second Punic War, marching war elephants over the Alps to threaten Rome itself before his defeat at Zama.

## Roman Africa, Christianity & the Vandals — 146 BCE – 647 CE

Rebuilt by Rome, the province of Africa Proconsularis became a granary of the empire and a cradle of Latin Christianity, then passed to Vandals and Byzantines.

- c. 29 BCE: **Augustus refounds Carthage** — Carthage is rebuilt as the capital of Africa Proconsularis, a prosperous grain-exporting Roman province. [africaproconsularis, wiki-tunisia-history]
- c. 238 CE: **Amphitheatre of El Djem** — The colossal amphitheatre of Thysdrus is built, the largest Roman amphitheatre in Africa and a later UNESCO World Heritage Site. [unesco-eljem]
- 439 CE: **Vandals take Carthage** — Gaiseric's Vandals seize Carthage, making it the capital of a kingdom in North Africa. [wiki-tunisia-history, africaproconsularis]
- 533–534 CE: **Byzantine reconquest** — Belisarius restores Carthage to the Eastern Roman Empire, ending Vandal rule. [wiki-tunisia-history]

## Islam, Kairouan & the Medieval Dynasties — 670 – 1574

The Arab conquest brought Islam and founded Kairouan; under Aghlabids, Fatimids, Zirids and Hafsids, Ifriqiya became a centre of faith, learning and trade.

- 670 CE: **Founding of Kairouan** — Uqba ibn Nafi establishes Kairouan with its Great Mosque, the first Arab-Muslim capital of the Maghreb. [unesco-kairouan, smarthistory-kairouan]
- 800–909 CE: **Aghlabid dynasty** — The Aghlabids rule Ifriqiya from Kairouan, rebuild the Great Mosque in stone, and conquer Sicily. [smarthistory-kairouan, wiki-tunisia-history]
- 909 CE: **Fatimid Caliphate** — The Shia Fatimids overthrow the Aghlabids; after moving to Cairo in 972 they leave the Zirids to govern locally. [smarthistory-kairouan, wiki-tunisia-history]
- 1229–1574: **Hafsid dynasty rules from Tunis** — The Hafsids make Tunis a prosperous capital of trade and learning until the Ottoman conquest. [wiki-tunisia-history, smarthistory-kairouan]

Figures:

- Uqba ibn Nafi (d. 683) — Arab general, founder of Kairouan. Led the Arab advance into the Maghreb and founded Kairouan in 670, establishing Islam's first capital in the region.

## Ottoman Rule & the Husaynid Beys — 1574 – 1881

Tunis became an autonomous Ottoman province governed, from 1705, by the Husaynid dynasty of beys ruling from Le Bardo.

- 1574: **Ottoman conquest of Tunis** — The Ottomans take Tunis from Spain, making Tunisia an autonomous province of the empire. [wiki-ottoman-tunisia, eb-husaynid]
- 1705: **Husaynid dynasty founded** — Al-Husayn ibn Ali is proclaimed bey, founding the dynasty that rules Tunisia until 1957. [eb-husaynid, wiki-husaynid]
- 1861: **First written constitution** — Tunisia adopts the Arab world's first written constitution amid attempts at modernising reform. [wiki-tunisia-history]

Figures:

- Al-Husayn I ibn Ali (c. 1669–1740) — Founder of the Husaynid dynasty. An Ottoman officer proclaimed bey of Tunis in 1705; his line ruled Tunisia for two and a half centuries with growing autonomy from Istanbul.

## French Protectorate & Independence — 1881 – 1957

France imposed a protectorate in 1881; a nationalist movement led by Habib Bourguiba's Neo-Destour won independence in 1956.

- 12 May 1881: **Treaty of Bardo** — The bey signs the Treaty of Bardo, placing Tunisia under a French protectorate. [wiki-bardo, wiki-french-protectorate]
- 1934: **Neo-Destour founded** — Habib Bourguiba and colleagues break from the Destour to form the Neo-Destour, the engine of the independence movement. [wiki-neodestour, eb-bourguiba]
- 20 March 1956: **Tunisian independence** — France recognises Tunisian sovereignty; Bourguiba becomes prime minister. [wiki-independence, eb-bourguiba]
- 25 July 1957: **Republic proclaimed** — The monarchy is abolished and Tunisia becomes a republic with Bourguiba as first president. [eb-bourguiba]

Figures:

- Habib Bourguiba (1903–2000) — Independence leader, first president. Founder of the Neo-Destour and architect of independence; as president he built a secular, modernising state and championed women's rights.

## The Republic & the Jasmine Revolution — 1957 – present

Bourguiba's secular republic gave way to Ben Ali's long rule, until the 2010–2011 Jasmine Revolution ignited the Arab Spring.

- 1956: **Code of Personal Status** — Bourguiba's government bans polygamy and expands women's rights, a landmark reform in the Arab world. [wiki-personal-status, eb-bourguiba]
- 1987: **Ben Ali takes power** — Zine El Abidine Ben Ali removes Bourguiba in a bloodless coup, beginning twenty-three years of authoritarian rule. [eb-jasmine, wiki-jasmine]
- 17 Dec 2010: **Mohamed Bouazizi self-immolation** — A street vendor sets himself alight in Sidi Bouzid, triggering nationwide protests. [eb-jasmine, wiki-jasmine]
- 14 Jan 2011: **Jasmine Revolution ousts Ben Ali** — Ben Ali flees the country; the uprising sparks the wider Arab Spring and a Tunisian democratic transition. [eb-jasmine, wiki-jasmine]

Figures:

- Mohamed Bouazizi (1984–2011) — Street vendor whose protest sparked the revolution. His self-immolation in Sidi Bouzid in December 2010 ignited the Jasmine Revolution and, beyond Tunisia, the Arab Spring.
