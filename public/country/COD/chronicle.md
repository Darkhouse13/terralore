# The Chronicle of DR Congo: From the savanna kingdoms to Africa's heart

History of DR Congo: From the savanna kingdoms to Africa's heart. 6 eras and 27 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/COD/chronicle
Updated: 2026-06-13

Founding: Independence from Belgium — 30 June 1960. The Belgian Congo became the independent Republic of the Congo on 30 June 1960, with Patrice Lumumba as prime minister and Joseph Kasa-Vubu as president. Within weeks the new state was plunged into the Congo Crisis.

The Democratic Republic of the Congo occupies the vast forested basin of the Congo River, a land that gave rise to the Christian kingdom of Kongo and the savanna empires of Luba, Lunda and Kuba. Drawn into the Atlantic and eastern slave trades, the region was seized in the 1880s by King Leopold II of Belgium as a private possession whose rubber regime killed on a catastrophic scale. Independence in 1960 opened onto crisis, the assassination of Patrice Lumumba, the long dictatorship of Mobutu, and two devastating wars whose aftershocks still rack the eastern provinces.

## Kongo & the Savanna Kingdoms — c. 1390 – c. 1700

Before any colony there were sophisticated African states — the kingdom of Kongo near the Atlantic, and the Luba, Lunda and Kuba kingdoms of the southern savanna.

- c. 1390: **Kingdom of Kongo founded** — Lukeni lua Nimi establishes the kingdom of Kongo on the lower Congo River, ruling from Mbanza Kongo. [eb-kongo, wiki-kongo]
- 1491: **Kongo's king baptised as João I** — After Diogo Cão's 1483 landfall, King Nzinga a Nkuwu accepts Catholic baptism, taking the name João I and opening close ties with Portugal. [wiki-kongo, eb-afonso]
- 1526: **Afonso I protests the slave trade** — King Afonso I (Nzinga Mbemba) writes to João III of Portugal complaining that traders are illegally enslaving his subjects, even as the Atlantic trade expands. [eb-afonso, wiki-kongo, metmuseum-savanna]
- c. 1625: **Kuba kingdom unified by Shyaam** — Shyaam a-Mbul a Ngoong founds the Bushong dynasty, uniting chiefdoms between the Sankuru and Kasai into the Kuba kingdom famed for its art. [wiki-kuba, metmuseum-savanna]
- 29 Oct 1665: **Battle of Mbwila breaks Kongo** — Portuguese forces defeat Kongo and kill King António I at Mbwila (Ambuila); the kingdom splinters into rival factions and a long civil war. [wiki-kongo, eb-kongo]

Figures:

- Afonso I (Nzinga Mbemba) (c. 1460–1542) — Manikongo (King of Kongo). Christian king of Kongo who built ties with Portugal, fostered literacy and the Church, yet protested the illegal enslavement of his subjects.

## The Slave Trades & the Scramble — c. 1700 – 1885

Two slaving systems — Atlantic to the west, Swahili-Arab to the east — bled the Congo basin, even as European explorers mapped the river for empire.

- 18th century: **Atlantic slave trade at its height** — Central African captives are funnelled in vast numbers into the transatlantic trade, hollowing out communities across the lower Congo. [wiki-kongo, blackpast-cfs]
- c. 1860s–1880s: **Tippu Tip's eastern slaving state** — Swahili-Arab trader Tippu Tip builds a domain around Kasongo in Maniema, raiding the eastern Congo for ivory and slaves. [eb-tippu, wiki-tippu]
- 1877: **Stanley descends the Congo River** — Henry Morton Stanley completes the first European traverse of the Congo from interior to the Atlantic, mapping the basin for would-be colonisers. [blackpast-cfs, wiki-cfs]
- 1879–1884: **Leopold hires Stanley for the Congo** — King Leopold II commissions Stanley to build stations and obtain treaties from local chiefs, laying the basis for a private colonial claim. [wiki-cfs, blackpast-cfs]

Figures:

- Tippu Tip (Hamad bin Muhammad) (c. 1832–1905) — Swahili-Arab trader and warlord. Zanzibar-born ivory and slave trader of Omani and African descent who carved out a domain in the eastern Congo basin, centred on Kasongo.

## The Congo Free State — 1885 – 1908

Recognised at the Berlin Conference as King Leopold II's personal possession, the Congo Free State became one of history's most lethal colonial regimes.

- 1885: **Congo Free State proclaimed** — Following the Berlin Conference, Leopold II establishes the Congo Free State as his personal possession, recognised by the European powers. [wiki-cfs, blackpast-cfs, eb-leopold]
- 1890s: **The rubber regime and the Force Publique** — Monopoly decrees and rubber quotas, enforced by the Force Publique through hostage-taking and mutilation, drive a system of forced labour and mass death. [wiki-cfs, blackpast-cfs]
- 1904: **The Casement Report** — British consul Roger Casement publishes a report documenting atrocities; E. D. Morel's Congo Reform Association amplifies the outcry. [wiki-casement, wiki-cfs]
- 15 Nov 1908: **Belgium annexes the Belgian Congo** — Under international pressure, Belgium takes the territory from Leopold II, ending the Free State and creating the Belgian Congo colony. [wiki-cfs, blackpast-cfs]

Figures:

- Leopold II of Belgium (1835–1909) — King of the Belgians; ruler of the Congo Free State. Held the Congo basin as his private possession from 1885; his rubber regime caused catastrophic loss of life before Belgium annexed the colony in 1908.

## Belgian Congo & Independence — 1908 – 1965

Half a century of Belgian rule ended abruptly in 1960 — and independence opened onto secession, mutiny and the murder of the first prime minister.

- 1908: **Belgian Congo established** — The colony is administered by Belgium for half a century, its economy built on Katangan copper and other minerals. [wiki-cfs, factbook-drc]
- 30 June 1960: **Independence from Belgium** — The Congo becomes independent with Lumumba as prime minister and Kasa-Vubu as president. [wiki-lumumba, factbook-drc]
- 11 July 1960: **Katanga secedes; the Congo Crisis** — An army mutiny and Moïse Tshombe's Belgian-backed secession of Katanga plunge the new state into crisis and UN intervention. [wiki-lumumba]
- 17 Jan 1961: **Assassination of Lumumba** — Captured and transferred to Katanga, Patrice Lumumba is killed; Belgian officers were present and foreign involvement is widely documented. [wiki-lumumba]
- 1965: **Mobutu seizes power** — Colonel Joseph-Désiré Mobutu takes full control in a military coup, beginning a three-decade dictatorship. [eb-mobutu, wiki-mobutu]

Figures:

- Patrice Lumumba (1925–1961) — First Prime Minister of independent Congo. Nationalist leader and first prime minister; dismissed amid the Congo Crisis and assassinated in January 1961, he became a symbol of African independence.

## Mobutu's Zaire — 1965 – 1997

For more than three decades Mobutu Sese Seko ruled a country he renamed Zaire — a single-party state of pageantry, plunder and Cold War patronage.

- 1971: **Congo renamed Zaire** — Under his 'authenticité' campaign, Mobutu renames the country Zaire and adopts the name Mobutu Sese Seko. [eb-mobutu, wiki-mobutu]
- 1994: **Rwandan refugee crisis in the Kivus** — The Rwandan genocide drives over a million refugees into eastern Zaire, destabilising the Kivu provinces. [wiki-second-congo, eb-mobutu]
- 1996–1997: **First Congo War** — A Rwanda- and Uganda-backed rebellion under Laurent-Désiré Kabila advances across the country against the collapsing Mobutu regime. [blackpast-kabila, wiki-mobutu]
- May 1997: **Mobutu falls; Kabila takes power** — Mobutu flees into exile and dies months later; Kabila seizes Kinshasa and restores the name Democratic Republic of the Congo. [blackpast-kabila, eb-mobutu, wiki-mobutu]

Figures:

- Mobutu Sese Seko (1930–1997) — President of Zaire (1965–1997). Seized power in 1965 and ruled for over three decades, renaming the country Zaire; his rule became a byword for kleptocracy before his fall in 1997.

## Africa's World War & After — 1998 – present

A second, far deadlier war drew in nine nations, and despite peace agreements the eastern Kivus have never known lasting calm.

- Aug 1998: **Second Congo War begins** — Kabila breaks with Rwanda and Uganda; nine nations and many militias are drawn into 'Africa's World War,' the deadliest conflict since 1945. [wiki-second-congo]
- 16 Jan 2001: **Laurent Kabila assassinated** — President Laurent-Désiré Kabila is shot dead in Kinshasa; his son Joseph Kabila succeeds him and seeks peace. [wiki-second-congo, blackpast-kabila]
- 2003: **Peace agreements end the war** — The 2002 Sun City and Pretoria accords and a transitional government formally end the Second Congo War in 2003. [wiki-second-congo]
- 2012: **M23 rebellion seizes Goma** — The M23 rebel movement briefly captures Goma in North Kivu before being pushed back; conflict festers across the east. [crisisgroup-m23, wiki-second-congo]
- 2025: **M23 retakes Goma** — After re-emerging in 2021, M23 — with Rwandan backing — captures Goma again in early 2025, deepening the humanitarian crisis. [crisisgroup-m23]

Figures:

- Laurent-Désiré Kabila (1939–2001) — President (1997–2001). Veteran guerrilla who ousted Mobutu in 1997, then fell out with his foreign backers, triggering the Second Congo War; assassinated in 2001.
