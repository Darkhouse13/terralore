# The Chronicle of Honduras: From the Maya kingdom of Copán and the Lenca resistance to a Central American republic shaped by federation, fruit companies, and democratic struggle.

Founding: Independence from Spain — 15 Sept 1821. On 15 September 1821 the provinces of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, Honduras among them, declared their independence from Spain. Honduras was briefly drawn into the short-lived Mexican Empire, then in 1823 joined the United Provinces (later Federal Republic) of Central America. The federation fractured amid conflict between Liberals and Conservatives, and Honduras separated to become a fully sovereign state in 1838. The United States recognised Honduras as a distinct independent nation on 19 April 1853.

Honduras occupies a heartland of ancient Mesoamerica, where the Maya city of Copán raised the longest hieroglyphic inscription in the Americas before its abandonment around 1200. After the Spanish arrived in 1502 and overcame the resistance led by the Lenca chief Lempira, the territory became a silver-mining province of the Captaincy General of Guatemala. Honduras declared independence from Spain on 15 September 1821, passed briefly through Mexican annexation and the Federal Republic of Central America championed by Francisco Morazán, then emerged as a sovereign state in 1838. Its modern history has been marked by the banana economy and U.S. fruit companies, long stretches of military rule, the devastation of Hurricane Mitch in 1998, the political crisis of 2009, and a twenty-first century shaped by migration and a contested democratic life.

## Copán and the First Peoples — c. 1000 BCE - 1525 CE

In western Honduras the Maya raised the kingdom of Copán, with its dynasty of sixteen kings and the longest hieroglyphic stairway of the ancient Americas, while the Lenca and other peoples held the rest of the land.

- c. 1000 BCE: **Copán settled** — A small farming village on the Copán River grows over centuries into a major Maya centre in what is now western Honduras. [britannica-copan]
- c. 426-822 CE: **Dynasty of sixteen kings** — A line of at least sixteen rulers governs Copán, raising temples, stelae, and the Hieroglyphic Stairway during the city's Classic-period height. [britannica-copan, britannica-hieroglyphic-stairway]
- c. 755 CE: **Hieroglyphic Stairway completed** — The fifteenth ruler finishes the great stairway, whose roughly 1,200 carved glyphs form the longest known Maya inscription. [britannica-hieroglyphic-stairway, unesco-copan]
- c. 1200 CE: **Copán abandoned** — After centuries of decline the Maya completely abandon Copán, leaving its monuments to the forest. [britannica-copan]

## Conquest, Lempira, and Colonial Silver — 1502 - 1821

Spanish forces overcame the resistance of the Lenca chief Lempira and bound Honduras into the Captaincy General of Guatemala, where the silver strikes around Tegucigalpa shaped a poor and thinly settled colony.

- 1502 CE: **Columbus reaches Honduras** — On his fourth voyage Christopher Columbus makes landfall on the Caribbean coast of Honduras. [britannica-honduras-history]
- 1537 CE: **Lempira's resistance** — The Lenca chief Lempira unites more than two hundred communities against the Spanish before his revolt is crushed. [britannica-honduras-history, britannica-lenca]
- 1570s: **Silver strike and rise of Tegucigalpa** — A highland silver discovery draws prospectors and gives rise to Tegucigalpa, long a rival to Comayagua. [loc-honduras-study, britannica-honduras-history]

Figures:

- Lempira (died c. 1537) — Lenca chief and resistance leader. Warrior chief of the Lenca who in 1537 united more than two hundred indigenous communities in organised resistance to the Spanish conquest of the western highlands. Defeated and killed, he became Honduras's foremost national hero, and the country's currency bears his name.

## Independence, Federation, and Morazán — 1821 - 1838

Independence from Spain in 1821 led through a brief Mexican annexation into the Federal Republic of Central America, whose foremost figure, the Honduran Francisco Morazán, fought to hold the union together before it dissolved.

- 15 Sept 1821: **Independence from Spain** — The provinces of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, Honduras among them, declare their independence from Spain. [history-state-honduras, britannica-honduras-history]
- 1823 CE: **Federal Republic of Central America** — After a brief Mexican annexation, Honduras joins the United Provinces of Central America under a federal constitution adopted in 1824. [history-state-honduras, britannica-united-provinces]
- 1830-1840: **Morazán leads the federation** — The Honduran Liberal Francisco Morazán serves as president of the United Provinces, fighting to preserve the union against Conservative revolts. [britannica-morazan]
- 1838 CE: **Honduras becomes sovereign** — Amid the federation's collapse, Honduras separates to become a fully independent state. [history-state-honduras, britannica-united-provinces]

Figures:

- Francisco Morazán (1792-1842) — President of the Federal Republic of Central America. Honduran-born soldier and statesman, the outstanding political and military figure of Central America in his era. As Liberal president of the United Provinces he sought to build a unified, secular republic; defeated by Conservatives, he was executed in 1842 and is honoured as the champion of Central American union.

## The Banana Economy and the Original Banana Republic — 1876 - 1932

From the late nineteenth century, U.S. fruit companies turned Honduras's north coast into vast banana plantations, binding the nation's economy and politics so tightly to foreign capital that the term banana republic was coined for it.

- c. 1899: **Rise of the fruit companies** — U.S. firms, foremost United Fruit and Standard Fruit, develop the north coast, making bananas Honduras's dominant export. [britannica-banana-republic, britannica-honduras-history]
- 1910-1911: **Cuyamel and the Zemurray coup** — Samuel Zemurray's Cuyamel Fruit Company buys plantation land and, in 1911, backs an armed coup to secure a favourable government. [britannica-banana-republic]
- 1929 CE: **United Fruit absorbs Cuyamel** — Zemurray sells Cuyamel to the United Fruit Company, concentrating the Honduran banana industry as the Depression begins. [britannica-banana-republic]

## Military Rule and the Cold-War Frontier — 1932 - 1998

Through much of the twentieth century Honduras was governed by the military or by leaders it sustained; in the 1980s it became a U.S. staging ground in the Central American conflicts, before Hurricane Mitch closed the century in catastrophe.

- 1933-1949: **Carías dictatorship** — Tiburcio Carías Andino governs as authoritarian president, opening an era of military influence over Honduran politics. [britannica-honduras-history, loc-honduras-study]
- 1969 CE: **War with El Salvador** — A brief border war with El Salvador, rooted in migration disputes, erupts amid a football tournament and hardens the military's role. [britannica-honduras-history]
- 1980s: **Contra war staging ground** — Honduras hosts an expanded U.S. military presence at Soto Cano and serves as a base for the Nicaraguan Contras against the Sandinistas. [britannica-honduras-history]
- Oct-Nov 1998: **Hurricane Mitch** — One of the deadliest Atlantic hurricanes devastates Honduras, killing some 7,000 people and ruining infrastructure and crops. [britannica-hurricane-mitch, britannica-honduras-history]

## Crisis, Migration, and a New Century — 1998 - present

The new century brought the political crisis of 2009, surging emigration and gang violence, the first woman president in 2022, and a contested election that handed power to a new president in 2026.

- 28 June 2009: **Removal of President Zelaya** — The military removes President Manuel Zelaya and Congress installs Roberto Micheletti; a later OAS commission deems the removal an illegal coup while finding Zelaya had defied the courts. [britannica-zelaya, britannica-honduras-21st]
- 27 Jan 2022: **Xiomara Castro inaugurated** — Xiomara Castro takes office as Honduras's first woman president, ending years of National Party rule. [britannica-castro, britannica-honduras-21st]
- 2022 CE: **Extradition of Juan Orlando Hernández** — Former president Juan Orlando Hernández is extradited to the United States to face drug-trafficking charges, later resulting in conviction. [britannica-hernandez]
- 27 Jan 2026: **Asfura succeeds Castro** — After a narrow, contested 2025 election, Nasry Asfura of the National Party is inaugurated president, succeeding Xiomara Castro. [britannica-castro, atlantic-council-asfura]

Figures:

- Manuel Zelaya (born 1952) — President of Honduras, 2006-2009. Liberal-turned-leftist president whose push for constitutional change ended in his removal by the military and Congress on 28 June 2009, an episode later judged an illegal coup by an OAS truth commission.
- Xiomara Castro (born 1959) — President of Honduras, 2022-2026. Candidate of the Libre party and wife of Manuel Zelaya, who in January 2022 became the first woman president of Honduras and the first left-wing leader to complete a full term in office.
