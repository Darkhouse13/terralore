Founding: Founding of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea — 9 September 1948. The Democratic People's Republic of Korea was formally proclaimed in the northern half of the Korean peninsula on 9 September 1948, with Kim Il Sung as its first premier, weeks after the Republic of Korea was established in the south. The state arose from the division of Korea after the defeat of Japan in 1945, when the peninsula was split along the 38th parallel into a Soviet occupation zone in the north and an American zone in the south. The Korean nation itself is far older: tradition traces it to the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon, and statehood took historical form in the Three Kingdoms of Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla and the later Goryeo and Joseon dynasties, a shared heritage that belongs to both Koreas. The DPRK's founding institutionalized the Cold War partition into two rival Korean governments, each claiming to represent the whole peninsula.

North Korea shares the deep history of the Korean peninsula, from the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon and the Three Kingdoms to the long Goryeo and Joseon dynasties, a past common to both Koreas. After thirty-five years of Japanese colonial rule, Japan's defeat in 1945 left the peninsula divided along the 38th parallel into Soviet and American occupation zones. In the north the Democratic People's Republic of Korea was proclaimed on 9 September 1948 under Kim Il Sung, and in 1950 the Korean War broke out, ending in 1953 in an armistice rather than a peace treaty. The state Kim built around the ideology of Juche and a command economy passed dynastically to his son Kim Jong Il and grandson Kim Jong Un, endured a devastating famine in the 1990s, and developed nuclear weapons while remaining one of the world's most closed countries into the present.

## Shared Korean Heritage — c. 2333 BCE - 1910 CE

Long before partition, the whole peninsula shared a single history: the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon, the Three Kingdoms, and the long dynastic ages of Goryeo and Joseon, the common inheritance of both Koreas.

- 2333 BCE (legendary): **Legendary founding of Gojoseon** — According to the Samguk yusa, the mythical Dangun establishes Gojoseon, traditionally regarded as the first Korean kingdom and a shared origin point for both Koreas. [korea-place, gojoseon-whe]
- 668 CE: **Silla unifies the peninsula** — Allied with Tang China, Silla conquers Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668, then expels the Tang, achieving the first unification of Korea under native rule. [korea-place, three-kingdoms]
- 918 CE: **Goryeo dynasty founded** — Wang Geon establishes the Goryeo dynasty, which reunifies the peninsula and gives rise to the Western name Korea. [korea-place]
- 1392 CE: **Joseon dynasty founded** — Yi Seong-gye founds the long-lived Joseon dynasty, which adopts Neo-Confucianism and under King Sejong creates the hangul alphabet. [joseon-dynasty, korea-place]

## Colonial Rule and Division — 1910 - 1948 CE

Japan annexed Korea in 1910 and ruled it for thirty-five years; Japan's defeat in 1945 brought liberation alongside a division of the peninsula that hardened into two rival states by 1948.

- 1910 CE: **Japanese annexation of Korea** — Japan formally annexes Korea, ending the Joseon dynasty and beginning thirty-five years of colonial rule over the entire peninsula. [korea-japanese-rule, korea-place]
- 15 August 1945: **Liberation and division at the 38th parallel** — Japan's surrender frees Korea but the peninsula is split along the 38th parallel into Soviet and American occupation zones, setting the stage for two rival states. [state-korean-war, archives-korean-conflict]
- 9 September 1948: **Founding of the DPRK** — The Democratic People's Republic of Korea is proclaimed under Kim Il Sung, weeks after the Republic of Korea is established in the south, institutionalizing the division. [history-north-korea, kim-il-sung]

Figures:

- Kim Il Sung (1912-1994) — Founding leader of North Korea. An anti-Japanese guerrilla who fought in Manchuria and served with Soviet forces, Kim Il Sung was installed with Soviet backing and became the first premier of the DPRK in 1948. He ruled until his death in 1994, building an extensive cult of personality as the Great Leader.

## The Korean War — 1950 - 1953 CE

The first major armed conflict of the Cold War devastated the peninsula and ended not in victory for either side but in an armistice that froze the division still in place today.

- 25 June 1950: **North Korea invades the South** — North Korean forces cross the 38th parallel, opening the Korean War; the North nearly overruns the peninsula before a US-led UN coalition intervenes. [state-korean-war, history-state-korean-war-short]
- Late 1950: **China enters the war** — After UN forces push deep into the north, the People's Republic of China intervenes and drives the front back toward the 38th parallel, producing a stalemate. [state-korean-war, history-state-korean-war-short]
- 27 July 1953: **Korean War armistice** — An armistice halts the fighting and creates the Demilitarized Zone; no peace treaty is signed, freezing the division of the peninsula to the present day. [state-korean-war, archives-korean-conflict]

## The Juche State — 1953 - 1994 CE

From the ruins of war Kim Il Sung built a centralized command economy and a cult of personality around the doctrine of Juche, ruling for four decades and arranging a dynastic succession to his son.

- December 1972: **Socialist Constitution adopted** — A new constitution creates the presidency for Kim Il Sung, enshrines Juche as the Workers' Party's guiding idea, and confirms Pyongyang as the capital. [juche, history-north-korea]
- October 1980: **Kim Jong Il designated successor** — Kim Il Sung formally designates his son Kim Jong Il as his successor, beginning an unprecedented hereditary transfer of power in a communist state. [kim-jong-il, history-north-korea]
- 17 September 1991: **North and South Korea join the UN** — Both Korean states are admitted to the United Nations with separate seats, an acknowledgment of the durability of the two-state division. [un-membership, history-north-korea]
- 8 July 1994: **Death of Kim Il Sung** — Kim Il Sung dies after ruling for nearly five decades; he is declared Eternal President and power passes to his son Kim Jong Il. [kim-il-sung, kim-jong-il]

Figures:

- Kim Il Sung (1912-1994) — Eternal President; founding leader. North Korea's founder ruled from 1948 until his death in 1994, building a command economy and the Juche ideology and an extensive cult of personality. He launched the 1950 invasion of the South and arranged a hereditary succession to his son.

## Famine and the Second Succession — 1994 - 2011 CE

Under Kim Jong Il the country suffered a catastrophic famine in the 1990s, pursued nuclear weapons through years of crisis and negotiation, and prepared a third generation of dynastic rule.

- 1994-1999: **The Arduous March famine** — A famine driven by the loss of Soviet support, reduced Chinese aid and flooding kills an estimated several hundred thousand or more North Koreans, a defining national trauma. [history-north-korea, loc-country-study]
- June 2000: **First inter-Korean summit** — Kim Jong Il hosts South Korean president Kim Dae-jung in Pyongyang in the first summit between the two Koreas, briefly easing tensions. [history-north-korea, kim-jong-il]
- 9 October 2006: **First nuclear test** — North Korea conducts its first underground nuclear test, having withdrawn from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty in 2003 and cycled through failed negotiations. [acatimeline, crs-nuclear]
- 17 December 2011: **Death of Kim Jong Il** — Kim Jong Il dies and is succeeded by his youngest son Kim Jong Un, completing a third generation of hereditary rule. [kim-jong-il, kim-jong-un]

Figures:

- Kim Jong Il (1941-2011) — Leader of North Korea, 1994-2011. Designated his father's successor in 1980, Kim Jong Il became de facto leader in 1994 and ruled through the 1990s famine and the development of nuclear weapons under a military-first policy. He died in December 2011 after arranging the succession of his son.

## The Kim Jong Un Era — 2011 - present

The third Kim consolidated power, expanded the nuclear and missile arsenal, conducted unprecedented summit diplomacy that ultimately stalled, and redefined the state's relationship with the South.

- 2017: **Largest nuclear test and ICBM launches** — North Korea conducts its most powerful nuclear test and tests intercontinental ballistic missiles, prompting expanded UN Security Council sanctions. [kim-jong-un, crs-nuclear]
- 12 June 2018: **Singapore summit with the United States** — Kim Jong Un becomes the first North Korean leader to meet a sitting U.S. president, signing a joint statement with Donald Trump on working toward denuclearization. [acatimeline, kim-jong-un]
- 27-28 February 2019: **Hanoi summit collapses** — A second Trump-Kim summit in Hanoi ends without agreement over sanctions and denuclearization, after which diplomacy stalls and weapons testing resumes. [acatimeline, kim-jong-un]
- January 2024: **South Korea declared principal enemy** — Kim Jong Un renounces the goal of peaceful reunification, declaring South Korea a hostile separate state and the North's principal enemy and calling for constitutional revision. [crs-nuclear, kim-jong-un]

Figures:

- Kim Jong Un (born 1984) — Leader of North Korea since 2011. The grandson of Kim Il Sung, Kim Jong Un took power in 2011 and consolidated control while expanding the nuclear and missile arsenal. He held unprecedented summits with U.S. and South Korean leaders in 2018-2019 and later declared South Korea the North's principal enemy.
