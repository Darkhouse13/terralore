Founding: Unification of Upper and Lower Egypt — c. 3100 BCE. Egyptian tradition and the archaeological record place the founding of the Egyptian state in the union of the Two Lands—Upper and Lower Egypt—under a single ruler around 3100 BCE, identified with King Narmer (often equated with the legendary Menes) at the opening of the First Dynasty. The Narmer Palette depicts the king wearing both the White Crown of the south and the Red Crown of the north, symbolising a dominion that endured, in changing forms, for three millennia. The modern state took its present form on 18 June 1953, when the Free Officers who had seized power the previous year abolished the monarchy of the Muhammad Ali dynasty and declared the Republic of Egypt, with Muhammad Naguib as its first president.

Egypt is among the world's oldest continuous civilisations, born when the kingdoms of Upper and Lower Egypt were welded into a single state around 3100 BCE. For three thousand years pharaonic dynasties raised the pyramids, temples and a sophisticated bureaucracy along the Nile, before the land passed in turn to Persian, Macedonian, Roman and Byzantine rule. The Arab conquest of 639-642 made Egypt a centre of Islamic learning and power under the Fatimids, Ayyubids and Mamluks; Ottoman suzerainty after 1517 gave way to the reforming dynasty of Muhammad Ali and, from 1882, to British occupation. Formal independence in 1922 and the Free Officers' revolution of 1952 culminated in the proclamation of the Republic in 1953, since when Egypt has been a pivotal power in the Arab world.

## Predynastic Egypt and the Unification — c. 5000–c. 2686 BCE

Farming communities along the Nile coalesced into rival kingdoms, then into the first unified state under a single king.

- c. 3500 BCE: **Naqada cultures flourish in Upper Egypt** — Predynastic settlements in Upper Egypt develop social hierarchy, craft specialisation and long-distance trade, laying the groundwork for state formation. [worldhistory-narmer]
- c. 3100 BCE: **Unification under Narmer (Menes)** — Upper and Lower Egypt are united into a single kingdom under King Narmer, traditionally identified with Menes, inaugurating the First Dynasty and the dynastic state. [worldhistory-narmer]
- c. 3100 BCE: **Memphis founded as royal capital** — A new capital is established at Memphis near the meeting point of the Two Lands, becoming the administrative heart of early pharaonic Egypt. [worldhistory-narmer]

Figures:

- Narmer (fl. c. 3100 BCE) — First king of a unified Egypt. Naqada III ruler of Upper Egypt commonly identified with the legendary Menes; the Narmer Palette commemorates his dominion over both Upper and Lower Egypt at the dawn of the First Dynasty.

## The Pharaonic Kingdoms — c. 2686–332 BCE

Across the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms, Egypt built the pyramids, an empire, and one of antiquity's most enduring cultures.

- c. 2630 BCE: **Djoser's Step Pyramid at Saqqara** — Imhotep designs the Step Pyramid for King Djoser, the first monumental stone structure in Egypt and a prototype for the pyramids to come. [britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom, unesco-memphis]
- c. 2560 BCE: **Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza** — King Khufu raises the Great Pyramid at Giza, the largest of the pyramids and the sole surviving wonder of the ancient world. [unesco-memphis, britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom]
- c. 2055 BCE: **Mentuhotep II reunifies Egypt** — Mentuhotep II of Thebes defeats the rival Heracleopolitan kings and reunites Egypt, inaugurating the Middle Kingdom. [britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom]
- c. 1274 BCE: **Battle of Kadesh** — Ramses II confronts the Hittites at Kadesh in Syria; the ensuing accord is among the earliest documented peace treaties. [britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom]
- c. 1336 BCE: **Tutankhamun restores the old gods** — The young Tutankhamun reverses Akhenaten's Aten cult and restores traditional Egyptian religion; his intact tomb, found in 1922, made him world-famous. [britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom]

Figures:

- Hatshepsut (r. c. 1479–c. 1458 BCE) — Female pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty. One of the few women to rule as pharaoh in her own right, she presided over a prosperous reign of building and trade, including the mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahri.
- Ramses II (r. c. 1279–c. 1213 BCE) — Pharaoh of the Nineteenth Dynasty. Known as Ramses the Great, his long reign saw extensive monument-building, the temples of Abu Simbel and the campaign against the Hittites at Kadesh.

## Greco-Roman and Byzantine Egypt — 332 BCE–639 CE

Conquered by Alexander, ruled by the Ptolemies and then Rome, Egypt became a Hellenistic metropolis and the breadbasket of empire.

- 332 BCE: **Alexander the Great conquers Egypt** — Alexander expels Persian rule, is hailed as pharaoh and founds Alexandria, opening the Hellenistic age in Egypt. [worldhistory-ptolemaic, britannica-ancient-egypt-old-kingdom]
- 305 BCE: **Ptolemy I founds the Ptolemaic dynasty** — Alexander's general Ptolemy takes the royal title in Egypt, founding a Macedonian dynasty that rules from Alexandria for nearly three centuries. [worldhistory-ptolemaic]
- 30 BCE: **Rome annexes Egypt** — After Cleopatra VII and Mark Antony are defeated at Actium, Octavian annexes Egypt as a Roman province, ending the Ptolemaic dynasty. [worldhistory-ptolemaic]
- 451 CE: **Council of Chalcedon and the Coptic schism** — Doctrinal disputes over the nature of Christ separate the Egyptian (Coptic) Church from the imperial church of Constantinople. [worldhistory-ptolemaic]

Figures:

- Cleopatra VII (69–30 BCE; r. 51–30 BCE) — Last pharaoh of the Ptolemaic dynasty. The last independent ruler of ancient Egypt, she allied with Julius Caesar and Mark Antony; her death after the defeat at Actium ended three thousand years of pharaonic Egypt.

## Islamic Egypt: Caliphs, Fatimids and Mamluks — 639–1517 CE

The Arab conquest made Egypt a Muslim land and, under the Fatimids, Ayyubids and Mamluks, a centre of power and learning with Cairo at its heart.

- 639–642 CE: **Arab conquest of Egypt** — Amr ibn al-As leads the Muslim conquest of Byzantine Egypt and founds Fustat, beginning the country's transformation into a Muslim land. [wikipedia-arab-conquest]
- 969 CE: **Fatimids conquer Egypt and found Cairo** — The Fatimid general Jawhar conquers Egypt and founds al-Qahira (Cairo); the al-Azhar mosque, begun in 970, becomes a great seat of learning. [britannica-ayyubid]
- 1171 CE: **Saladin founds the Ayyubid dynasty** — Saladin abolishes the Fatimid caliphate, restores Sunni rule in Egypt and establishes the Ayyubid dynasty, later retaking Jerusalem in 1187. [britannica-saladin, britannica-ayyubid]
- 1250 CE: **Mamluks seize power** — The slave-soldier Mamluks overthrow the last Ayyubid ruler and establish a sultanate that will govern Egypt and Syria for over two and a half centuries. [britannica-ayyubid]
- 1260 CE: **Battle of Ain Jalut** — Mamluk forces defeat the Mongols at Ain Jalut in Palestine, halting their westward advance and securing Egypt as the bastion of the Islamic world. [britannica-ayyubid]

Figures:

- Saladin (Salah al-Din) (1137/38–1193) — Founder of the Ayyubid dynasty. Kurdish general who ended Fatimid rule in Egypt, united Egypt and Syria, and recaptured Jerusalem from the Crusaders in 1187, becoming a celebrated figure in both Muslim and European tradition.

## Ottoman Rule, Muhammad Ali and British Occupation — 1517–1952

An Ottoman province reshaped by Muhammad Ali's reforms, Egypt fell under British control before nationalist agitation won nominal independence.

- 1517: **Ottoman conquest of Egypt** — Selim I defeats the Mamluks and incorporates Egypt into the Ottoman Empire as a province governed from Istanbul. [britannica-ottoman-egypt]
- 1805: **Muhammad Ali takes power** — Recognised as Ottoman governor, Muhammad Ali founds a reforming dynasty and modernises Egypt's army, economy and administration. [britannica-ottoman-egypt]
- 1869: **Opening of the Suez Canal** — The Suez Canal links the Mediterranean and Red seas, transforming global trade but deepening Egypt's debts to European powers. [britannica-suez-crisis]
- 1882: **British occupation begins** — Britain defeats the Urabi revolt and occupies Egypt, taking effective control of the country while leaving the khedive nominally in place. [britannica-ottoman-egypt]
- 28 February 1922: **Declaration of Egyptian independence** — After the 1919 revolution led by Saad Zaghloul and the Wafd, Britain unilaterally declares Egypt independent; Fuad I becomes king of the new Kingdom of Egypt. [wikipedia-1919-revolution]

Figures:

- Muhammad Ali (c. 1769–1849; r. 1805–1848) — Ottoman governor and founder of the modern dynasty. Often called the founder of modern Egypt, he created a centralised state, a conscript army and new industries, establishing a dynasty that ruled until the 1952 revolution.
- Saad Zaghloul (1859–1927) — Nationalist leader of the Wafd. Figurehead of the 1919 revolution and founder of the Wafd party, whose campaign for self-rule forced Britain's 1922 declaration of Egyptian independence.

## The Modern Republic — 1952–present

From the Free Officers' revolution to Nasser, Sadat and beyond, Egypt became a republic and a leading power in the Arab world.

- 23 July 1952: **Free Officers' revolution** — A coup by the Free Officers overthrows King Farouk, ending the Muhammad Ali dynasty's effective rule and beginning the republican era. [britannica-nasser, wikipedia-declaration-republic]
- 18 June 1953: **Republic of Egypt proclaimed** — The monarchy is formally abolished and Egypt is declared a republic, with Muhammad Naguib as its first president. [wikipedia-declaration-republic]
- 1956: **Suez Crisis** — Nasser nationalises the Suez Canal; an invasion by Britain, France and Israel is forced into withdrawal, confirming Egypt's sovereignty and Nasser's standing. [britannica-suez-crisis, britannica-nasser]
- 1979: **Egypt–Israel peace treaty** — Following the 1978 Camp David Accords, Sadat signs a peace treaty with Israel, the first between Israel and an Arab state, returning the Sinai to Egypt. [britannica-sadat]
- 2011: **Egyptian revolution and Mubarak's fall** — Mass protests from 25 January force President Hosni Mubarak to resign on 11 February after nearly thirty years in power. [britannica-nasser]

Figures:

- Gamal Abdel Nasser (1918–1970; president 1954–1970) — President of Egypt. Leader of the 1952 revolution and architect of Arab nationalism, he nationalised the Suez Canal, championed non-alignment and pursued state-led modernisation including the Aswan High Dam.
- Anwar Sadat (1918–1981; president 1970–1981) — President of Egypt. He launched the October War of 1973 and then made peace with Israel through the Camp David Accords and the 1979 treaty, winning the Nobel Peace Prize before his assassination in 1981.
