# The Chronicle of Ukraine: From the cradle of Kievan Rus' to a sovereign nation defending its borders against invasion.

Founding: Independence from the Soviet Union — 24 August 1991. Modern Ukraine traces its statehood to 24 August 1991, when the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine's parliament, adopted the Act of Independence amid the collapse of the Soviet Union following the failed August coup in Moscow. The declaration was overwhelmingly confirmed by a nationwide referendum on 1 December 1991, in which more than 90 percent of voters across every region endorsed independence; the Soviet Union was formally dissolved later that month. The deeper roots of Ukrainian statehood reach back over a thousand years to Kievan Rus', the medieval realm centred on Kyiv whose ruler Volodymyr the Great adopted Christianity in 988, and to the Cossack Hetmanate of the seventeenth century. The independence of 1991 restored a sovereign Ukrainian state after centuries in which Ukrainian lands had been divided among the Russian, Austrian and Polish-Lithuanian states and, in the twentieth century, ruled from Moscow.

Ukraine's history begins with Kievan Rus', the medieval federation centred on Kyiv that, under Volodymyr the Great, adopted Christianity from Byzantium in 988 and reached its height under Yaroslav the Wise. After the Mongol sack of Kyiv in 1240 the Rus' lands fragmented and were drawn into the orbits of Galicia-Volhynia, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and Poland. From the steppe frontier rose the Cossacks and the Zaporozhian Sich, whose great uprising under Bohdan Khmelnytsky in 1648 created a Cossack Hetmanate that, by the 1654 Council of Pereiaslav, fell under Muscovite protection, dividing the Ukrainian lands between Russia and Poland and, after the partitions, between the Russian and Austrian empires. A modern national movement, voiced by the poet Taras Shevchenko, produced the short-lived Ukrainian People's Republic of 1917-1921, which was defeated and absorbed into Soviet Ukraine, where the man-made famine of 1932-33, the Holodomor, killed millions. After the devastation of the Second World War and the Holocaust on Ukrainian soil, Ukraine declared independence on 24 August 1991, confirmed by referendum on 1 December 1991, and gave up the world's third-largest nuclear arsenal under the 1994 Budapest Memorandum. Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and its full-scale invasion of 2022, together with the purported annexation of four oblasts, have been condemned by the United Nations General Assembly as without legal validity.

## Kievan Rus' and the Baptism of 988 — c. 880 – 1240

A federation of Slavic and Varangian principalities centred on Kyiv adopted Christianity from Byzantium, flourished under Yaroslav the Wise, and was shattered by the Mongol invasion.

- 988: **The Baptism of Rus'** — Grand Prince Volodymyr the Great adopted Christianity in the Byzantine rite and had the people of Kyiv baptized, bringing Rus' into Eastern Christendom and shaping the culture of the East Slavs. [vladimir-britannica, kyivanrus-britannica, kyiv-unesco]
- 1019–1054: **Reign of Yaroslav the Wise** — Yaroslav consolidated Kievan Rus', codified the Russkaya Pravda, promoted Christianity and learning, and built the Saint-Sophia Cathedral, marking the height of the realm. [yaroslav-britannica, kyiv-unesco]
- c. 1037: **Saint-Sophia Cathedral begun in Kyiv** — Built with Byzantine masters under Yaroslav the Wise as the main church of the Rus' capital, Saint-Sophia symbolised Kyiv as a 'new Constantinople'; it is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [kyiv-unesco, yaroslav-britannica]
- 1199: **Principality of Galicia-Volhynia formed** — Roman Mstyslavych united Galicia and Volhynia into a single powerful principality in the south-west, one of the major successor states to emerge as Kievan Rus' fragmented. [history-ukraine-britannica, kyivanrus-britannica]
- 1240: **Mongol sack of Kyiv** — The Mongols of the Golden Horde stormed and destroyed Kyiv, ending its dominance and breaking the political unity of the Rus' lands. [kyivanrus-britannica, history-ukraine-britannica]

Figures:

- Volodymyr the Great (c. 956 – 1015) — Grand Prince of Kyiv. Grand prince of Kyiv who consolidated the provinces of Kyiv and Novgorod into a single state and in 988 adopted Byzantine Christianity, baptizing Rus' and anchoring it to Eastern Christendom. He was later canonized as 'equal to the Apostles'.
- Yaroslav the Wise (c. 978 – 1054) — Grand Prince of Kievan Rus'. Son of Volodymyr, Yaroslav brought Kievan Rus' to its cultural and political height, issuing the Russkaya Pravda law code, founding churches and monasteries, gathering books, and building the Saint-Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv.

## Between Lithuania, Poland and the Steppe — 1240 – 1648

After the fall of Kyiv the Rus' lands passed under Lithuanian and Polish rule, while on the southern frontier the Cossacks and the Zaporozhian Sich took shape.

- 14th century: **Rus' lands pass to Lithuania and Poland** — Following the collapse of Galicia-Volhynia, most of the former Rus' lands were absorbed by the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, while Galicia in the west passed to the Kingdom of Poland. [history-ukraine-britannica]
- late 15th–16th c.: **Rise of the Cossacks** — On the southern steppe frontier free men known as Cossacks emerged, evolving by the mid-sixteenth century a democratic military organisation with an assembly, the rada, and elected hetmans. [cossacks-britannica, zaporozhian-britannica]
- 16th century: **The Zaporozhian Sich** — The Cossacks established the Zaporozhian Sich, a fortified camp on the lower Dnieper that became the political and military heart of Cossack Ukraine and a bastion of Orthodox resistance. [zaporozhian-britannica, cossacks-britannica]
- 1 Jul 1569: **Union of Lublin brings Ukraine into the Commonwealth** — The union of Poland and Lithuania placed the bulk of the Ukrainian lands within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, intensifying pressures of serfdom and Catholicism on the Orthodox population. [history-ukraine-britannica]

Figures:

- Dmytro Vyshnevetsky (died 1563) — Cossack leader, traditional founder of the Sich. A frontier prince associated in tradition with organising the early Zaporozhian Cossacks into a fortified host on the lower Dnieper, defending the steppe frontier against Tatar and Ottoman raids.
- Roman Mstyslavych (died 1205) — Prince of Galicia-Volhynia. Prince who in 1199 united Galicia and Volhynia into a single principality, the strongest of the south-western Rus' successor states before the Mongol invasions.

## The Cossack Hetmanate and the Partition of Ukrainian Lands — 1648 – 1795

Khmelnytsky's uprising created a Cossack state that fell under Muscovite protection in 1654, dividing the Ukrainian lands between Russia and Poland, while the west passed to Austria.

- 1648: **Khmelnytsky's uprising** — Bohdan Khmelnytsky led the Zaporozhian Cossacks, allied with the Crimean Tatars, in a great rebellion against Polish rule, founding a self-governing Cossack Hetmanate in the Ukrainian lands. [khmelnytsky-britannica, cossacks-britannica]
- 1654: **Council of Pereiaslav** — Khmelnytsky and the Cossack assembly swore allegiance to the tsar, placing the Hetmanate under Muscovite protection while seeking to retain its autonomy, and drawing Muscovy into war with Poland. [khmelnytsky-britannica, history-ukraine-britannica]
- 1667: **Partition along the Dnieper** — The Truce of Andrusovo and later settlements divided the Ukrainian lands between Russia and Poland, with the left bank and Kyiv under Russian control and the right bank remaining Polish. [history-ukraine-britannica, khmelnytsky-britannica]
- 1775: **Destruction of the Zaporozhian Sich** — Russian troops destroyed the Zaporozhian Sich, the bastion of Cossack self-government, completing the suppression of Cossack autonomy under the Russian Empire. [zaporozhian-britannica, cossacks-britannica]
- 1772–1795: **Galicia to Austria, right bank to Russia** — The partitions of Poland transferred Galicia to the Habsburg Empire in 1772 and most of the right-bank Ukrainian lands to the Russian Empire by 1795, dividing the Ukrainian people between two empires. [history-ukraine-britannica]

Figures:

- Bohdan Khmelnytsky (c. 1595 – 1657) — Hetman of the Zaporozhian Cossacks. Leader of the 1648 uprising against Polish rule, Khmelnytsky founded the Cossack Hetmanate and, by the 1654 Council of Pereiaslav, placed it under Muscovite protection, a turning point that drew the eastern Ukrainian lands into the Russian orbit.
- Ivan Mazepa (1639 – 1709) — Hetman of Ukraine. Hetman of the Cossack state who, seeking to escape growing Russian domination, allied with Sweden against Tsar Peter I; his cause was lost at the Battle of Poltava in 1709, after which Cossack autonomy was sharply curtailed.

## National Awakening and the Ukrainian People's Republic — 1795 – 1921

Divided between the Russian and Austrian empires, Ukrainians built a modern national identity, voiced by Taras Shevchenko, that erupted into a short-lived independent republic during the First World War.

- 1840: **Shevchenko publishes 'Kobzar'** — The poet Taras Shevchenko, born a serf, published his collection Kobzar, giving voice to a distinct Ukrainian nation and laying the foundations of the modern Ukrainian literary language. [shevchenko-reference, history-ukraine-britannica]
- Nov 1917: **Ukrainian People's Republic proclaimed** — Amid the Russian Revolution, the Central Rada in Kyiv under Mykhailo Hrushevsky proclaimed the Ukrainian People's Republic, the first modern Ukrainian state. [wwi-ukraine-britannica, history-ukraine-britannica]
- 22 Jan 1918: **Declaration of independence** — The Central Rada's Fourth Universal proclaimed the full independence of the Ukrainian People's Republic from Russia, as German, Bolshevik and other forces contested the country. [wwi-ukraine-britannica]
- 1921: **Defeat and Soviet incorporation** — After years of war between Bolshevik, Polish, White and Ukrainian forces, the People's Republic was defeated; most Ukrainian lands were incorporated into the Soviet state, while Galicia passed to Poland. [wwi-ukraine-britannica, history-ukraine-britannica]

Figures:

- Taras Shevchenko (1814 – 1861) — Poet and national figure. Born a serf, Shevchenko became Ukraine's national poet. His collection Kobzar (1840) imagined Ukraine as a distinct nation with its own culture and history, and he is regarded as the founder of the modern Ukrainian written language.
- Mykhailo Hrushevsky (1866 – 1934) — Historian and head of the Central Rada. Ukraine's foremost historian and chairman of the Central Rada, Hrushevsky presided over the proclamation of the Ukrainian People's Republic in 1917 and became a symbol of the modern national movement.

## Soviet Ukraine, the Holodomor and the Second World War — 1921 – 1991

Soviet rule brought the man-made famine of the Holodomor, the devastation and Holocaust of the Second World War, the 1954 transfer of Crimea, and finally independence in 1991.

- 1932–33: **The Holodomor** — Soviet collectivisation and grain seizures caused a man-made famine that killed millions of Ukrainians; many states and, in 2022, the European Parliament have recognised it as a genocide. [holodomor-britannica, holodomor-europarl]
- 29–30 Sep 1941: **Babyn Yar massacre** — German SS and police units shot 33,771 Jews at the Babyn Yar ravine outside Kyiv over two days; in all some 100,000 people were murdered there during the occupation. [babynyar-ushmm, babiyar-britannica]
- 19 Feb 1954: **Crimea transferred to the Ukrainian SSR** — The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet transferred the Crimean Peninsula from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR, citing economic and geographic ties, an administrative change within the USSR. [crimea-transfer-reference, history-ukraine-britannica]
- 26 Apr 1986: **Chernobyl disaster** — An explosion at the Chernobyl nuclear plant in northern Ukraine caused the worst nuclear accident in history, exposing Soviet failings and fuelling Ukrainian discontent in the final Soviet years. [history-ukraine-britannica]
- 24 Aug 1991: **Declaration of independence** — Amid the collapse of the Soviet Union, Ukraine's parliament declared independence on 24 August 1991, confirmed by referendum on 1 December with over 90 percent voting in favour. [history-ukraine-britannica]

Figures:

- Leonid Kravchuk (1934 – 2022) — First President of independent Ukraine. Former Communist official who led Ukraine to independence, was elected its first president in December 1991, and co-signed the Belavezha Accords dissolving the Soviet Union.
- Vyacheslav Chornovil (1937 – 1999) — Dissident and national leader. A leading Soviet-era dissident and founder of the Rukh popular movement, Chornovil campaigned for Ukrainian independence and democracy in the final years of Soviet rule.

## Independent Ukraine, Revolutions and the Defence of Sovereignty — 1991 – present

A sovereign Ukraine surrendered its nuclear arsenal for security assurances, turned toward Europe through two revolutions, and faced annexation and invasion that the United Nations has declared without legal validity.

- 5 Dec 1994: **Budapest Memorandum** — Ukraine agreed to surrender its nuclear arsenal and join the Non-Proliferation Treaty; Russia, the US and the UK pledged to respect its sovereignty and existing borders and to refrain from the use of force against it. [budapest-untreaty]
- 2004: **The Orange Revolution** — Mass protests against a fraudulent presidential election forced a court-ordered re-run that brought the pro-Western Viktor Yushchenko to the presidency over Viktor Yanukovych. [orange-britannica]
- 2013–14: **Revolution of Dignity (Euromaidan)** — Protests sparked by the rejection of an EU association agreement grew into the Revolution of Dignity; after deadly clashes President Yanukovych fled the country in February 2014. [ukraine-crisis-britannica]
- Mar 2014: **Russia annexes Crimea; UN Resolution 68/262** — Russia seized Crimea and declared its annexation after a referendum deemed illegal; the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 68/262, affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity and declaring the referendum without legal validity. [ukraine-crisis-britannica, unga-68-262, unga-68-262-lawmonitor]
- 24 Feb 2022: **Full-scale invasion; UN Resolution ES-11/1** — Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine; the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution ES-11/1 deploring the aggression and demanding the immediate, complete and unconditional withdrawal of all Russian forces. [invasion-2022-britannica, unga-es11-1]
- Sep–Oct 2022: **Purported annexation of four oblasts; UN Resolution ES-11/4** — Russia proclaimed the annexation of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson; the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution ES-11/4, condemning the attempted annexation as invalid and calling for non-recognition. [unga-es11-4, invasion-2022-britannica]

Figures:

- Viktor Yushchenko (born 1954) — President of Ukraine. Pro-Western reformer whose disputed defeat in the 2004 election sparked the Orange Revolution; a court-ordered re-run made him president, marking Ukraine's turn toward Europe.
- Volodymyr Zelensky (born 1978) — President of Ukraine. Elected president in 2019, Zelensky became the wartime leader of Ukraine after Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, rallying national and international support for the country's defence.
