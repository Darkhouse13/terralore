# The Chronicle of Belarus: A borderland of Europe, from the Principality of Polotsk and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania to a sovereign republic in Russia's orbit.

Founding: Independence of the Republic of Belarus — 25 August 1991. The modern Republic of Belarus dates its sovereignty to 1990-1991. On 27 July 1990 the Supreme Soviet of the Byelorussian SSR adopted a Declaration of State Sovereignty, and on 25 August 1991, in the aftermath of the failed coup in Moscow, that declaration was given the force of constitutional law, effectively proclaiming independence and renaming the republic the Republic of Belarus. Belarusian statehood, however, reaches far deeper in time: to the medieval Principality of Polotsk, the first form of statehood on Belarusian soil; to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, in which the Belarusian lands and their Ruthenian chancery language were central; and to the Belarusian People's Republic proclaimed on 25 March 1918. It was on Belarusian soil, in the Belavezha forest on 8 December 1991, that the leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus declared that the USSR had ceased to exist.

Belarus is a land that has stood for more than a millennium at the meeting point of the East Slavic, Baltic and Polish worlds. Its first state, the Principality of Polotsk, emerged among the Krivichi as an early centre of Kievan Rus before being absorbed, after the Mongol storm, into the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, where the Ruthenian, or Old Belarusian, chancery language became the language of law and where Francysk Skaryna printed the first book in an East Slavic language in 1517. Joined to Poland in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Belarusian lands passed to the Russian Empire in the partitions of 1772 to 1795 and endured a long century of Russification before a fragile national awakening produced the short-lived Belarusian People's Republic of 1918 and then the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic. The twentieth century fell on Belarus with extraordinary violence: Stalinist terror at Kurapaty, partition with Poland under the Treaty of Riga, and a Nazi occupation in which the republic lost roughly a quarter to a third of its people. A founding member of the United Nations in 1945 and the place where the Soviet Union was dissolved at Belavezha in 1991, independent Belarus has since 1994 been governed by Alyaksandr Lukashenka in ever-closer union with Russia.

## Polotsk and the Early East Slavs — c. 800 – 1240

Among the Krivichi on the Western Dvina rose the Principality of Polotsk, the first state on Belarusian soil and an early, often defiant, centre of Kievan Rus.

- 8th-9th c.: **Rise of the Principality of Polotsk** — The Krivichi formed a principality around Polotsk on the Western Dvina, the oldest centre of statehood on Belarusian soil and an early constituent of Kievan Rus with a strong tradition of autonomy. [belarus-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]
- c. 988: **Christianization of the Rus lands** — The conversion of Kievan Rus to Orthodox Christianity in the late tenth century bound Polotsk and the Belarusian lands to Byzantine Christendom and its written, ecclesiastical culture. [history-belarus-britannica]
- 1068: **Vseslav of Polotsk seizes Kiev** — Prince Vseslav of Polotsk, the principality's most celebrated ruler, briefly held the throne of Kiev itself, a measure of Polotsk's ambition and its rivalry with the senior princes of Rus. [history-belarus-britannica]
- 1237-1240: **The Mongol invasion of Rus** — The Mongol onslaught destroyed Kievan Rus and shattered the old federation of principalities; the western Belarusian lands, comparatively sheltered, were soon drawn into the orbit of rising Lithuania. [history-belarus-britannica, gdl-britannica]

Figures:

- Vseslav of Polotsk (c. 1039 – 1101) — Prince of Polotsk. The most renowned ruler of medieval Polotsk, Vseslav defended and expanded the principality's autonomy within Kievan Rus and in 1068 briefly seized the throne of Kiev, becoming a near-legendary figure of early Belarusian and Rus history.
- Euphrosyne of Polotsk (c. 1101 – 1167) — Princess, abbess and patron. A princess of the Polotsk line who became a nun, founded convents and promoted learning and manuscript copying, she is venerated as a patron saint of the Belarusian lands and a symbol of Polotsk's medieval cultural flowering.

## Heart of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania — 1240 – 1569

Absorbed into a vast Baltic-Slavic state, the Belarusian lands gave the Grand Duchy of Lithuania its chancery language and, in Francysk Skaryna, the first printer of an East Slavic book.

- 14th c.: **Belarusian lands within the Grand Duchy** — Over the 150 years after the Mongol invasion the Grand Duchy of Lithuania expanded to absorb the Belarusian lands, whose Ruthenian (Old Belarusian) tongue became the chancery language of the state. [gdl-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]
- 6 Aug 1517: **Skaryna prints the first East Slavic book** — Francysk Skaryna, born in Polotsk, issued his Psalter from his press in Prague, the first book printed in an East Slavic language, followed by further biblical books in 1517-1519. [skaryna-ldk, history-belarus-britannica]
- c. 1520-1522: **A printing press in Vilnius** — Moving his workshop to Vilnius, capital of the Grand Duchy, Skaryna produced the Little Travel Book and later The Apostle, the first books printed within the Grand Duchy of Lithuania itself. [skaryna-ldk]
- from the 1520s: **Mir Castle and the Radziwiłł magnates** — The Gothic castle at Mir, begun in the 1520s and rebuilt in Renaissance style under the Radziwiłł family, became one of the great fortified residences of the Grand Duchy and is now a UNESCO World Heritage site. [mir-unesco, nesvizh-unesco]

Figures:

- Francysk Skaryna (before 1490 – c. 1551) — Printer, physician and humanist. Born in Polotsk and educated at Kraków and Padua, Skaryna printed the Psalter in Prague in 1517, the first book in an East Slavic language, and later founded the first press in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania at Vilnius. He is celebrated as the father of Belarusian printing.
- The Radziwiłł family (16th century onward) — Magnate dynasty of the Grand Duchy. Among the most powerful houses of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the Radziwiłłs built and held the residential complex at Nesvizh and the castle at Mir, making their domains centres of European architecture, learning and the arts.

## The Commonwealth, Partition and the Russian Empire — 1569 – 1917

Joined to Poland in a great commonwealth, then annexed by Russia in the partitions, the Belarusian lands endured a century of Russification before a national awakening took shape.

- 1569: **Union of Lublin and the Commonwealth** — The Union of Lublin merged Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania into the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, placing the Belarusian lands at the heart of a vast federal state and accelerating the Polonisation of their elites. [commonwealth-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]
- 1772-1795: **Partitions of Poland and Russian annexation** — In three partitions Russia, Prussia and Austria divided the Commonwealth; Russia took most of the Belarusian lands, and after 1795 all of Belarus was ruled by the Russian Empire. [history-belarus-britannica, belarus-britannica]
- 1839: **Suppression of the Uniate Church and Russification** — The Uniate (Greek Catholic) Church was abolished and absorbed into Russian Orthodoxy, part of a sustained imperial policy of Russification that discouraged the Belarusian language and name. [history-belarus-britannica]
- 1816-1855: **The Struve Geodetic Arc** — Five of the surviving station points of the Struve Geodetic Arc, the first accurate measurement of a long meridian segment carried out across the empire between 1816 and 1855, lie in Belarus and are inscribed by UNESCO. [struve-unesco]
- 1906-1916: **Nasha Niva and the national awakening** — The newspaper Nasha Niva, published in Vilnius from 1906, became the platform of the Belarusian national revival, carrying the early work of the poets Yanka Kupala and Yakub Kolas. [kupala-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]

Figures:

- Yanka Kupala (1882 – 1942) — National poet of Belarus. The foremost poet of the Belarusian national revival, Kupala wrote in a Belarusian language that had been discouraged under the Russian Empire, giving the emerging nation a literature and a near-anthem in his verse. He remains the central figure of modern Belarusian letters.
- Yakub Kolas (1882 – 1956) — Poet and writer. Together with Kupala the co-founder of modern Belarusian literature, Kolas published his early verse in Nasha Niva and gave epic and lyric voice to the life and aspirations of the Belarusian peasantry during the national awakening.

## People's Republic, Soviet Republic and Terror — 1917 – 1939

A fleeting Belarusian People's Republic gave way to a Soviet republic; the Treaty of Riga split the nation in two, and Stalinist terror filled the pits of Kurapaty.

- 25 Mar 1918: **Belarusian People's Republic proclaimed** — Under German occupation, the Rada in Minsk proclaimed the independence of the Belarusian People's Republic, the first modern declaration of Belarusian statehood, though it never controlled the territory and was soon driven into exile. [bssr-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]
- 1 Jan 1919: **Founding of the Byelorussian SSR** — As the Germans withdrew, the Bolsheviks announced the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic; in 1922 it became one of the four founding republics of the Soviet Union. [bssr-britannica]
- 18 Mar 1921: **Treaty of Riga partitions Belarus** — The treaty ending the Polish-Soviet War divided Belarus between Poland and Soviet Russia along the line of the first partition; western Belarus went to Poland and the east became the Byelorussian SSR. [bssr-britannica]
- 1937-1941: **Stalinist terror and Kurapaty** — Amid forced collectivisation, the NKVD carried out mass executions of Belarusians at Kurapaty near Minsk between 1937 and 1941; the mass graves, revealed in 1988, became the emblem of Soviet repression in Belarus. [kurapaty-sciencespo]

Figures:

- Yazep Losik (1883 – 1940) — Belarusian national activist and linguist. A leading figure of the Belarusian national movement and a scholar of the Belarusian language, Losik was among the intelligentsia who built the modern nation only to perish in the Stalinist terror, dying in Soviet detention.
- Zianon Pazniak (born 1944) — Archaeologist and discoverer of Kurapaty. The archaeologist whose investigations brought the Kurapaty mass graves to public knowledge in 1988, Pazniak helped force a reckoning with Stalinist crimes and went on to lead the Belarusian national revival movement of the late Soviet years.

## Reunification, the Nazi Catastrophe and Soviet Belarus — 1939 – 1986

Reunited in 1939, then devastated by a Nazi occupation that killed a quarter to a third of its people, Belarus rebuilt as a Soviet republic and a founding member of the United Nations.

- 17 Sep 1939: **Reunification of western Belarus** — Following the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact and the German invasion of Poland, Soviet forces entered eastern Poland and western Belarus was annexed to the Byelorussian SSR, reuniting the Belarusian lands. [bssr-britannica, history-belarus-britannica]
- 1941-1944: **Nazi occupation and mass death** — The German occupation killed a quarter to a third of the population; the people of Belarus fell from about 9.2 million before the war to roughly 6.3 million by late 1944, with cities like Minsk left in ruins. [archives-war, history-belarus-britannica]
- 22 Mar 1943: **The Khatyn massacre** — German forces and auxiliaries burned the village of Khatyn with its inhabitants, one of hundreds of Belarusian villages annihilated in anti-partisan reprisals and a lasting symbol of the occupation's brutality. [archives-war]
- 1945: **Founding member of the United Nations** — In recognition of the Belarusian people's role in defeating fascism, the Byelorussian SSR signed the UN Charter at San Francisco in 1945, becoming a founding member of the United Nations despite not being an independent state. [un-belarus, bssr-britannica]

Figures:

- Kuzma Kiselyov (1903 – 1977) — Foreign minister of the Byelorussian SSR. As People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs of the Byelorussian SSR, Kiselyov led the Belarusian delegation to the 1945 San Francisco Conference and signed the United Nations Charter, securing Belarus a founding seat in the world organisation.
- Vasil Bykau (1924 – 2003) — Writer of the war generation. A frontline veteran and Belarus's most internationally renowned writer, Bykau drew on the partisan war and Nazi occupation to produce unflinching novels of moral choice under terror, becoming a conscience of the nation and later a critic of authoritarian rule.

## Chernobyl, Independence and the Lukashenka Era — 1986 – present

Scarred by Chernobyl and born into independence at the dissolution of the USSR on its own soil, Belarus has since 1994 been ruled by Alyaksandr Lukashenka in deepening union with Russia.

- 26 Apr 1986: **The Chernobyl disaster** — The reactor explosion at Chernobyl in Ukraine sent about 70 percent of its radioactive fallout onto Belarus, contaminating nearly a quarter of the country's territory and affecting some 2.2 million people. [chernobyl-un, belarus-britannica]
- 25 Aug 1991: **Independence of Belarus** — After declaring sovereignty on 27 July 1990, the Supreme Soviet gave the declaration constitutional force on 25 August 1991 in the wake of the Moscow coup, proclaiming the independent Republic of Belarus. [sovereignty-icl, bssr-britannica]
- 8 Dec 1991: **Belavezha Accords dissolve the USSR** — In the Belavezha forest in Belarus, part of the UNESCO-listed Belovezhskaya Pushcha, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus, including Stanislau Shushkevich, signed accords declaring that the USSR had ceased to exist and founding the Commonwealth of Independent States. [belavezha-eu, bialowieza-unesco, bssr-britannica]
- 1994: **Lukashenka elected president** — Alyaksandr Lukashenka won Belarus's first presidential election on an anti-corruption platform and went on to consolidate authoritarian rule and forge ever-deeper ties with Russia. [lukashenko-britannica]
- 8 Dec 1999: **Treaty on the Union State with Russia** — Lukashenka and Yeltsin signed a Treaty on the Creation of a Union State, a blueprint for deep Russian-Belarusian integration that has bound the two countries ever closer. [union-state-britannica, lukashenko-britannica]
- Aug-Sep 2020: **Disputed election and mass protests** — Lukashenka's claimed landslide re-election sparked the largest protests in Belarusian history; the EU declared the vote neither free nor fair and refused to recognise his mandate, while he held on with Russian support. [eu-belarus-2020, lukashenko-britannica]

Figures:

- Stanislau Shushkevich (1934 – 2022) — First head of independent Belarus. A physicist who chaired the Supreme Soviet of newly independent Belarus, Shushkevich signed the Belavezha Accords in 1991 that dissolved the Soviet Union. A reformer, he was forced from office in 1994 as Lukashenka rose to power.
- Alyaksandr Lukashenka (born 1954) — President of Belarus since 1994. Elected in 1994 as Belarus's first president, the former collective-farm manager built a durable authoritarian state, anchored Belarus to Russia through the Union State, and clung to power after the disputed 2020 election that the EU refused to recognise.
