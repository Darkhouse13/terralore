# The Chronicle of Qatar: From pearling shores and Bedouin tribes to a gas-rich peninsula state and global stage.

Founding: Independence from Britain — 3 September 1971. Qatar declared its independence from Great Britain on 3 September 1971, ending the protectorate established by the 1916 Anglo-Qatari Treaty. The United States recognised the new State of Qatar two days later, on 5 September 1971, and the two established diplomatic relations in March 1972. Qatar's deeper roots as a polity are traced to the British agreement signed with Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani in 1868, which set the course for both Qatari autonomy and the rule of the Al Thani dynasty.

Qatar is a small peninsula thrusting north into the Persian Gulf, long inhabited by pearl divers, fishermen and Bedouin tribes whose fortunes rose and fell with the pearl trade. The walled town of Al Zubarah flourished as a pearling and trading port in the late 18th century before its destruction in 1811. The Al Thani family rose to leadership over the peninsula in the 19th century, and a separate British agreement signed with Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani in 1868 is taken to mark Qatar's emergence as a distinct entity; a 1916 treaty made it a British protectorate. Oil discovered in 1939 and, later, the vast offshore North Field gas reserves transformed Qatar after independence on 3 September 1971 into one of the world's wealthiest states and a prominent diplomatic and media actor.

## Pearls, Tribes and the Town of Al Zubarah — Antiquity - early 19th century

For millennia the peninsula's people lived from the sea and the desert, and in the 18th century the walled town of Al Zubarah grew into a flourishing pearling and trading port.

- from antiquity: **Pearling and fishing villages on the Gulf coast** — The peninsula's people lived from pearl diving, fishing and seasonal Bedouin herding, with pearls the great export carried across the Indian Ocean and Arabia from the early Islamic period and earlier. [unesco-zubarah, loc-gulf-studies]
- c. 1760s: **Founding of Al Zubarah** — Families migrating from Kuwait, including the Al Khalifa, settled the northwest coast and built Al Zubarah, which grew into a flourishing walled pearling and trading town. [qm-zubarah, loc-gulf-studies]
- 1811: **Destruction of Al Zubarah** — After attacks by rival Gulf powers, the thriving town of Al Zubarah was burned to the ground; it fell into decline and was abandoned by the early 20th century. [qm-zubarah, unesco-zubarah]

## The Rise of the Al Thani and the 1868 Agreement — 1850s - 1871

Amid disputes with Bahrain and Wahhabi pressure, Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani emerged as leader of the peninsula's tribes, and a separate British agreement in 1868 is taken to mark Qatar's emergence as a distinct entity.

- 1867: **Bahraini attack and the Qatari-Bahraini conflict** — Forces from Bahrain and its allies attacked Al Wakrah and Al Bidda, an assault the British viewed as a breach of Gulf agreements and that sharpened Qatar's sense of separate identity. [eb-history-qatar, diwan-mohammed]
- 12 September 1868: **British agreement with Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani** — Colonel Lewis Pelly concluded a separate agreement with Mohammed bin Thani, widely taken to mark Qatar's emergence as a distinct entity and the rise of Al Thani rule. [eb-qatar-history, diwan-mohammed]
- 1871: **Ottoman garrison established** — The Ottoman Empire extended its presence into eastern Arabia and placed a garrison on the Qatari peninsula, beginning several decades of nominal Ottoman authority. [eb-qatar-history, qatar-gov-wajba]

Figures:

- Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani (c. 1788 - 1878) — First Al Thani ruler of the Qatari peninsula. Recognised by Britain in the 1868 agreement, he is regarded as the first of the Al Thani to lead the whole peninsula, founding the dynasty that still rules Qatar.

## Between the Ottomans and Britain: The 1916 Protectorate — 1871 - 1935

Sheikh Jassim bin Mohammed, founder of modern Qatar, balanced Ottoman and British power and defeated an Ottoman force in 1893; in 1916 his son made Qatar a British protectorate.

- 18 December 1878: **Sheikh Jassim succeeds as ruler** — Jassim bin Mohammed Al Thani formally succeeded his father, an event commemorated as Qatar's National Day and marking the consolidation of the tribes under Al Thani leadership. [qatar-gov-wajba, diwan-mohammed]
- March 1893: **Battle of Al Wajba** — Sheikh Jassim's tribal forces defeated an Ottoman expedition at Al Wajba, forcing the release of Qatari captives in a victory remembered as a key assertion of Qatari autonomy. [qatar-gov-wajba, eb-history-qatar]
- 3 November 1916: **Anglo-Qatari Treaty: British protectorate** — Sheikh Abdullah bin Jassim signed a treaty (ratified 1918) placing Qatar under British protection in exchange for foregoing relations and concessions with other powers without British consent. [qdl-treaty-1916, eb-qatar-history]
- 1935: **First oil concession signed** — Sheikh Abdullah bin Jassim granted an oil concession, opening Qatar to the exploration that would lead to the discovery of oil within a few years. [eb-qatar-history]

Figures:

- Sheikh Jassim bin Mohammed Al Thani (ruled c. 1878 - 1913) — Founder of modern Qatar. The founding figure of the Qatari state, he unified the tribes, admitted then resisted the Ottomans, and won the Battle of Al Wajba in 1893; his 1878 succession is marked as National Day.

## Oil, the End of Pearls, and Independence — 1939 - 1971

The discovery of oil in 1939 and exports from 1949 began to transform a pearling economy in decline, and in 1971 Qatar became an independent state.

- 1939: **Oil discovered at Dukhan** — Following the 1935 concession, oil was discovered at the Dukhan field, though development stalled during the Second World War. [eb-qatar-history]
- 1949: **First oil exports begin** — Commercial export of Dukhan crude began in 1949, starting the transformation of an economy battered by the collapse of the pearl trade in the 1930s. [eb-qatar-history, loc-gulf-studies]
- 3 September 1971: **Independence from Britain** — Qatar declared independence from Great Britain, ending the protectorate established in 1916; the United States recognised the new State of Qatar on 5 September 1971. [soh-qatar, eb-qatar-history]

## The Gas Boom: Hamad, Al Jazeera and New Wealth — 1971 - 2013

Under Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, who took power in 1995, Qatar developed the vast North Field gas reserves and launched Al Jazeera, becoming one of the world's wealthiest and most visible small states.

- 27 June 1995: **Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa takes power** — Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani became emir in 1995, launching reforms and the development of Qatar's natural gas wealth that would make it one of the richest states in the world. [eb-hamad, eb-qatar-history]
- 1 November 1996: **Al Jazeera launched** — Qatar founded Al Jazeera, the first independent Arabic-language satellite news channel, broadcasting from Doha and giving the small state global reach and influence. [eb-aljazeera, eb-hamad]
- mid-1990s: **North Field LNG development** — Qatar invested heavily in liquefied natural gas from its offshore North Field, becoming the world's leading LNG exporter and reaching among the highest per-capita incomes globally. [eb-qatar-history]
- June 2013: **Hamad abdicates in favour of Tamim** — In a transfer of power unusual for the Gulf, Sheikh Hamad abdicated in favour of his son Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, then 33, to make way for a new generation. [eb-hamad, eb-tamim]

Figures:

- Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (born 1952) — Emir of Qatar (1995 - 2013). Architect of modern Qatar's wealth and influence, he developed North Field gas, founded Al Jazeera, eased censorship and held the first municipal elections before abdicating in favour of his son in 2013.

## Qatar Under Tamim: Blockade and World Stage — 2013 - 2026

Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani led Qatar through a four-year blockade by neighbouring states, ended in 2021, and hosted the first World Cup in the Arab world in 2022.

- June 2013: **Sheikh Tamim becomes emir** — Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani succeeded his father as emir of Qatar, having earlier overseen the country's successful bid to host the 2022 World Cup. [eb-tamim]
- 5 June 2017: **Blockade by neighbouring states** — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Egypt cut ties and imposed a land, sea and air blockade, accusing Qatar of supporting terrorism; Qatar rejected their thirteen demands as a breach of its sovereignty. [eb-tamim, eb-qatar-history]
- 5 January 2021: **Al Ula declaration ends the blockade** — At the GCC summit in Al Ula, the parties signed a solidarity and stability agreement restoring relations and reopening routes, ending the nearly four-year dispute. [eb-tamim]
- 20 November - 18 December 2022: **Qatar hosts the FIFA World Cup** — Qatar staged the FIFA World Cup, the first in an Arab and Muslim country, after a decade of major infrastructure investment, drawing both global celebration and scrutiny. [eb-worldcup-2022, eb-tamim]

Figures:

- Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani (born 1980) — Emir of Qatar (since 2013). Emir since 2013, he steered Qatar through the 2017-2021 blockade and the 2022 World Cup, sustaining its gas wealth and prominent role as a regional diplomatic mediator.
