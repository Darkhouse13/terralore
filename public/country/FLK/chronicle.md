Founding: Re-establishment of continuous British administration — 2 January 1833. The defining political-status moment of the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas) is the British re-establishment of control on 2 January 1833, the start of the continuous administration that has governed the islands ever since. After a brief Argentine presence on East Falkland in the wake of Buenos Aires's independence from Spain — itself disrupted by a U.S. naval raid in 1831 — a British force arrived and required the small remaining Argentine garrison to leave, by Britain's and the islanders' own accounts without loss of life. A permanent British administration followed within about a year, and a civil lieutenant governor was appointed in 1841/1842. The United Kingdom dates its modern sovereignty and settlement from this point and bases its position on the wishes of the inhabitants and continuous administration since 1833. Argentina rejects this account, treating 1833 not as a founding but as the illegitimate occupation of territory it had inherited from Spain, and it has maintained a sovereignty claim ever since — a dispute the United Nations formally recognises and asks the two governments to settle through negotiation. This entry presents both positions without endorsing either.

The Falkland Islands (Spanish: Islas Malvinas) are a British Overseas Territory of some 3,500 people in the South Atlantic, about 480 km east of southern Argentina, which maintains a long-standing claim to the archipelago it calls the Islas Malvinas. Uninhabited at the time of their first recorded sighting, the islands saw competing French, British and Spanish settlements in the 1760s, and after Spanish administration from Buenos Aires and a post-independence Argentine presence, Britain re-established control in 1833 — the start of the continuous administration that defines the islands' present status. A pastoral colony built on sheep and wool grew up around a British-descended population, while the United Nations listed the territory among its Non-Self-Governing Territories and recognised a sovereignty dispute between Argentina and the United Kingdom. Argentina's 1982 invasion triggered a 74-day war that Britain won at the cost of roughly a thousand lives on both sides; in a 2013 referendum islanders voted almost unanimously to remain a British Overseas Territory, a result the United Kingdom upholds and Argentina rejects.

## Empty Shores and Competing Claims — 1592 – 1811

Uninhabited when Europeans first recorded them, the islands drew rival 18th-century settlements — French, British and Spanish — whose overlapping claims planted the seeds of a dispute that endures.

- 1592: **First recorded sighting** — The English navigator John Davis is credited with the first recorded European sighting of the uninhabited islands; the territory marks Falklands Day on 14 August in commemoration. [govfk-history, britannica-falklands]
- 1764: **French Port Louis founded** — Louis-Antoine de Bougainville established the first settlement, Port Louis, on East Falkland, and named the islands the Malouines — origin of the Spanish Malvinas. [britannica-falklands, britannica-bougainville]
- 1765–1766: **British Port Egmont established** — A British expedition under Commodore John Byron claimed West Falkland in 1765, and Britain founded Port Egmont on Saunders Island the following year. [britannica-falklands, govfk-history]
- 1767: **Spain buys out the French and administers from Buenos Aires** — Spain purchased the French settlement around 1767 and governed the islands from Buenos Aires; in 1770 it expelled the British from Port Egmont, restored after a 1771 settlement. [britannica-falklands]

## Buenos Aires, Vernet, and 1833 — 1816 – 1840

After Buenos Aires declared independence it asserted sovereignty and planted a settlement at Port Louis; a U.S. raid and then a British return in 1833 reset control — the founding of the islands' continuous administration, and the start of Argentina's enduring claim.

- 1820: **Buenos Aires proclaims sovereignty** — Having declared independence from Spain in 1816, the Buenos Aires government proclaimed its sovereignty over the islands, the basis of Argentina's later claim. [britannica-falklands, statedept-southatlantic]
- 1831: **USS Lexington raid on Port Louis** — The U.S. warship Lexington destroyed the Argentine settlement on East Falkland in reprisal for the seizure of American sealing ships, ending Vernet's colony. [britannica-falklands]
- 2 January 1833: **Britain re-establishes control (founding of continuous administration)** — A British force required the small Argentine garrison to leave Port Louis and re-asserted British control — the start of continuous British administration, which Argentina contests as illegitimate occupation. [britannica-falklands, govfk-history, statedept-southatlantic]
- 1841/1842: **First British lieutenant governor** — Britain appointed a civilian lieutenant governor for the Falklands, formalising the colonial administration begun in 1833. [britannica-falklands]

Figures:

- Luis Vernet (1791–1871) — Commandant at Puerto Soledad. Merchant appointed by Buenos Aires to govern its settlement on East Falkland; his seizure of American sealers brought the USS Lexington raid of 1831 and, two years later, the British return.

## The Pastoral Colony of Sheep and Wool — 1840 – 1945

Around the new capital at Stanley, a sheep-farming colony took root: wool became the islands' livelihood, large estates spread across the camp, and a settled British-descended community formed.

- 1845: **Stanley becomes the capital** — The seat of government moved to Stanley on East Falkland, which became the islands' capital, port and main settlement. [britannica-falklands, govfk-history]
- 1851: **Falkland Islands Company chartered** — The Falkland Islands Company received a royal charter in 1851 and grew into the colony's largest landowner and sheep rancher, central to its wool economy. [britannica-falklands]
- 1885: **A self-supporting wool colony** — By 1885 a British community of some 1,800 people on the islands was self-supporting, sustained mainly by sheep farming and wool exports. [britannica-falklands]
- December 1914: **Battle of the Falkland Islands** — A British naval squadron destroyed a German cruiser force off the islands in an early First World War engagement, underscoring their strategic position near Cape Horn. [britannica-battle-1914]

## Decolonisation and the Sovereignty Dispute — 1946 – 1981

As decolonisation reshaped the post-war world, the United Nations listed the islands as a Non-Self-Governing Territory and recognised a sovereignty dispute, calling on Britain and Argentina to negotiate.

- 1946: **Listed as a Non-Self-Governing Territory** — The Falkland Islands (Malvinas) were placed on the UN list of Non-Self-Governing Territories after the UK transmitted information under Article 73(e) of the Charter. [un-decolonization]
- 1964: **Decolonisation Committee takes up the question** — From 1964 the UN Special Committee on Decolonisation began considering the question of the Falkland Islands (Malvinas) and the dispute between the two governments. [un-decolonization]
- 1965: **General Assembly Resolution 2065 (XX)** — The UN General Assembly recognised a sovereignty dispute between Argentina and the United Kingdom and invited both to negotiate a peaceful settlement, taking account of the islanders' interests. [un-decolonization, un-press-2025]

## The 1982 War — 1982

On 2 April 1982 Argentine forces invaded; Britain dispatched a task force across 13,000 km of ocean, and after a 74-day campaign the islands were retaken, at a cost of roughly a thousand lives on both sides.

- 2 April 1982: **Argentine invasion** — Argentine forces landed and seized the islands, overwhelming the small British garrison; Argentina described it as recovering the Malvinas, Britain as an invasion of its territory. [statedept-southatlantic, britannica-war]
- 2–4 May 1982: **Belgrano and Sheffield sunk** — A British submarine sank the Argentine cruiser General Belgrano on 2 May; on 4 May an Argentine Exocet missile sank the destroyer HMS Sheffield. [britannica-war]
- 21 May 1982: **British landings at San Carlos** — British forces landed at San Carlos on East Falkland and advanced overland, capturing Darwin and Goose Green before closing on Stanley. [britannica-war]
- 14 June 1982: **Argentine surrender at Stanley** — Argentine forces surrendered at Stanley after a 74-day campaign; about 255 British and 649 Argentine personnel and three islanders died in the war. [statedept-southatlantic, britannica-war]
- 3 April 1982: **Security Council Resolution 502** — The Council determines that a breach of the peace exists, calls for an immediate cessation of hostilities, demands the prompt withdrawal of all Argentine forces, and urges both governments to seek a diplomatic settlement under the Charter. Adopted 10 in favour, 1 against, 4 abstentions. [unscr-502]
- 3 April 1982: **The Commons sits on a Saturday** — Parliament meets on a Saturday for the first time in a generation. Margaret Thatcher opens by telling the House that, for the first time in many years, British sovereign territory has been invaded by a foreign power. [hansard-1982]

Figures:

- Margaret Thatcher (1925–2013) — Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. Told a Saturday sitting of the Commons on 3 April 1982 that British sovereign territory had been invaded, and despatched the task force that retook the islands in ten weeks.
- Leopoldo Galtieri (1926–2003) — President of Argentina and head of the military junta. Ordered the operation Argentina describes as the recovery of the Malvinas; the defeat that followed ended the junta and opened the way to Argentina's return to civilian rule in 1983.

## Referendum, Fisheries and Oil to the Present — 1983 – 2026

Since the war the islands have prospered on fisheries and looked to offshore oil, voted overwhelmingly in 2013 to remain British, and remained the subject of an unresolved dispute the UN still asks the two governments to negotiate.

- 1986: **Fisheries licensing zone established** — The Falkland Islands government created a fisheries conservation and licensing zone; squid licensing revenue became the mainstay of the post-war economy. [britannica-falklands]
- 10–11 March 2013: **Sovereignty referendum** — About 99.8% of islanders voting (turnout c. 92%) chose to remain a British Overseas Territory; the UK upheld the result while Argentina rejected its validity. [congress-hres170, govfk-history]
- 2025: **UN reiterates call for negotiations** — The UN Special Committee on Decolonisation again adopted a resolution asking Argentina and the United Kingdom to resume talks toward resolving the sovereignty dispute. [un-press-2025, un-decolonization]
- present: **Contested status and an oil prospect** — The islands remain a British Overseas Territory claimed by Argentina, with an economy led by fisheries and the prospect of Sea Lion offshore oil development. [un-decolonization, sealion-rockhopper]
