# The Chronicle of South Korea: A peninsula of ancient kingdoms, half a century of colonization and fratricidal war, and a startling rise from ruin to global cultural powerhouse.

Founding: Founding of the Republic of Korea — 15 August 1948. The Republic of Korea was formally proclaimed in the southern half of the Korean peninsula on 15 August 1948, three years to the day after liberation from Japanese colonial rule, with Syngman Rhee as its first president. Korean tradition reaches back far earlier, to the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon, said to have been founded in 2333 BCE by the mythical Dangun. Statehood took historical form in the Three Kingdoms of Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla, and continued through the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties. The 1948 republic was born of a wrenching division: after Japan's defeat in 1945 the peninsula was split along the 38th parallel between Soviet and American occupation zones, producing two rival Korean states whose separation endures.

Korean history reaches back to the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon and the early states of the peninsula, through the Three Kingdoms of Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla, the unification under Silla in 668, and the long dynastic ages of Goryeo and Joseon. The Joseon dynasty, founded in 1392, ruled for more than five centuries and gave Korea its alphabet, hangul, before falling to Japanese imperial pressure. Japan annexed Korea in 1910 and ruled it with harsh repression until 1945, when liberation came hand in hand with national division along the 38th parallel. The Republic of Korea was proclaimed in the south on 15 August 1948; within two years it was nearly destroyed by the Korean War of 1950 to 1953, which ended in armistice rather than peace and left the peninsula permanently split. From poverty and authoritarian rule, South Korea built one of the fastest industrial transformations in modern history, the Miracle on the Han River, then won its democracy through mass struggle in 1987. Today it is a prosperous democracy and a worldwide exporter of technology and culture.

## Gojoseon and the Three Kingdoms — c. 2333 BCE - 668 CE

From the legendary kingdom of Gojoseon to the rival realms of Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla, the early peninsula forged distinct states, adopted Buddhism, and built the foundations of Korean civilization.

- 2333 BCE (legendary): **Legendary founding of Gojoseon** — According to the Samguk yusa, the mythical Dangun establishes Gojoseon, traditionally regarded as the first Korean kingdom; the date is commemorated as National Foundation Day. [korea-place, gojoseon-whe]
- 108 BCE: **Fall of Gojoseon to Han China** — The Han dynasty conquers Gojoseon and establishes commanderies in the north, fragmenting the peninsula into smaller competing states. [korea-place, gojoseon-whe]
- c. 57 BCE - 1st century CE: **Emergence of the Three Kingdoms** — Silla, Goguryeo, and Baekje take shape as the three dominant kingdoms of the peninsula, each later adopting Buddhism and centralized rule. [three-kingdoms, korea-place]
- c. 372 CE: **Introduction of Buddhism** — Buddhism is officially adopted in Goguryeo and spreads through the kingdoms, becoming a major force in art, statecraft, and religious life. [three-kingdoms, korea-place]
- 668 CE: **Silla unifies the peninsula** — Allied with Tang China, Silla conquers Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668, then expels the Tang, achieving the first unification of Korea under native rule. [three-kingdoms, korea-unified-silla]

Figures:

- Dangun (mythical, traditionally from 2333 BCE) — Legendary founder of Gojoseon. The mythical progenitor of the Korean nation, said in the Samguk yusa to have founded Gojoseon in 2333 BCE. No historical evidence supports his existence, but he remains a powerful symbol of Korean origins, honored each year on National Foundation Day.

## Unified Silla and Goryeo — 668 - 1392 CE

Under Silla and then Goryeo, a unified Korea flourished as a Buddhist civilization, produced masterworks of art and printing, and weathered devastating Mongol invasions.

- c. 751 CE: **Bulguksa and Seokguram built** — Unified Silla constructs the Bulguksa temple and the Seokguram grotto near Gyeongju, masterpieces of Buddhist art later inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage. [korea-unified-silla, seokguram-unesco]
- 918 CE: **Wang Geon founds Goryeo** — The general Wang Geon establishes the Goryeo dynasty at Kaesong; by 936 he reunifies the peninsula, giving rise to the Western name Korea. [korea-goryeo, korea-place]
- 1231 CE: **Mongol invasions begin** — Mongol armies invade Goryeo; after prolonged resistance from Ganghwa Island, Korea submits to Yuan overlordship for roughly a century. [korea-goryeo, korea-place]
- 1236-1251 CE: **Tripitaka Koreana completed** — Goryeo Buddhists carve the entire Buddhist canon onto more than 80,000 woodblocks as a prayer for deliverance from the Mongols, a printing landmark preserved at Haeinsa. [korea-goryeo, tripitaka-unesco]
- 1392 CE: **Fall of Goryeo** — Weakened by factionalism and Mongol decline, Goryeo is overthrown by the general Yi Seong-gye, who founds the Joseon dynasty. [joseon-dynasty, korea-place]

Figures:

- Wang Geon (Taejo of Goryeo) (877-943 CE) — Founder of the Goryeo dynasty. A regional general who founded Goryeo in 918 and reunified the peninsula by 936, accepting the surrender of the last Silla king. His dynasty's name, by way of Goryeo, gave the world the word Korea.

## The Joseon Dynasty — 1392 - 1910 CE

For more than five centuries the Confucian Joseon kingdom shaped Korean society, language, and statecraft, giving Korea its alphabet before foreign pressure and Japanese ambition brought the dynasty to its end.

- 1392 CE: **Joseon dynasty founded** — Yi Seong-gye establishes the Joseon dynasty, adopts Neo-Confucianism as state ideology, and soon moves the capital to Hanyang (Seoul). [joseon-dynasty, korea-place]
- 1443-1446 CE: **Creation of hangul** — King Sejong the Great sponsors the Korean alphabet, completed in 1443 and promulgated in 1446 to make literacy accessible to ordinary people. [joseon-dynasty, hunminjeongeum-unesco]
- 1592-1598 CE: **Japanese (Imjin) invasions repelled** — Toyotomi Hideyoshi's forces invade Korea; resistance led by Admiral Yi Sun-sin and his turtle ships, with Ming Chinese aid, ultimately drives them out. [joseon-dynasty, korea-place]
- 1905 CE: **Korea made a Japanese protectorate** — After defeating Russia, Japan imposes a protectorate treaty stripping Korea of its diplomatic sovereignty, a prelude to full annexation. [korea-japanese-rule, korea-place]
- 1910 CE: **Japanese annexation of Korea** — Japan formally annexes Korea, ending the Joseon dynasty and more than five centuries of Korean monarchy and beginning 35 years of colonial rule. [korea-japanese-rule, korea-place]

Figures:

- King Sejong the Great (1397-1450 CE) — Fourth king of Joseon. Reigning from 1418 to 1450, Sejong sponsored the creation of the hangul alphabet and presided over advances in science, music, and governance, making his reign a cultural golden age still revered in Korea.
- Yi Sun-sin (1545-1598 CE) — Joseon admiral. The naval commander who defeated far larger Japanese fleets during the Imjin invasions of the 1590s, most famously at Myeongnyang, using innovative tactics and armored turtle ships. He remains a Korean national hero.

## Japanese Colonial Rule — 1910 - 1945 CE

For thirty-five years Korea was a colony of imperial Japan, subjected to political repression, economic exploitation, forced labor, and a campaign to erase Korean identity, even as a determined independence movement endured.

- 1910 CE: **Annexation and colonial government** — Japan installs a Government-General in Seoul, ruling Korea through military and police power and beginning systematic economic exploitation. [korea-japanese-rule]
- 1 March 1919: **March First Movement** — A Declaration of Independence sparks nationwide demonstrations by some two million Koreans; the peaceful protests are violently suppressed with thousands killed. [march-first-movement, korea-japanese-rule]
- April 1919: **Korean Provisional Government formed** — Korean nationalists establish a Provisional Government in exile in Shanghai to coordinate the independence struggle against Japanese rule. [korea-japanese-rule, korea-place]
- c. 1939-1945 CE: **Forced labor and wartime mobilization** — As the Pacific War expands, Japan conscripts hundreds of thousands of Koreans for forced labor and military service and coerces women into sexual slavery. [korea-japanese-rule]
- Night of 10-11 August 1945: **Rusk and Bonesteel propose the 38th parallel** — During an overnight meeting in John McCloy's Pentagon office, US Army officers Dean Rusk and Charles Bonesteel are asked to retire to an adjoining room and propose where US forces should accept Japan's surrender in Korea. They recommend the 38th parallel to include Seoul in the American area, though it lay further north than US forces could reach if the Soviet Union disagreed (Rusk's own later account, 1950). [frus-1945-korea-rusk, frus-1945-general-order-1]
- 15 August 1945: **Liberation from Japan** — Japan's surrender ends 35 years of colonial rule; Koreans celebrate Gwangbokjeol, the Restoration of Light, even as foreign occupation and division loom. [korea-japanese-rule, state-korean-war]
- 16 August 1945: **Stalin does not object to General Order No. 1** — General Order No. 1, which assigns the Japanese surrender in Korea north of 38 degrees north latitude to Soviet forces and south of it to US forces, is approved by President Truman on 17 August. In his 16 August reply Stalin writes that he has no objection in principle to the order, asking only for changes concerning the Kuril Islands and northern Hokkaido; Rusk later recalled being surprised that the Soviet Union accepted the parallel. [frus-1945-stalin-reply, frus-1945-general-order-1, frus-1945-korea-rusk]

Figures:

- Yu Gwan-sun (1902-1920) — Independence activist. A schoolgirl who became a symbol of the March First Movement, she organized demonstrations, was imprisoned and tortured by Japanese authorities, and died in prison at the age of seventeen, becoming an enduring martyr of the independence cause.

## Division, the Korean War, and Authoritarian Rebuilding — 1945 - 1979 CE

Liberation gave way to national division, the devastation of the Korean War, and decades of authoritarian rule under which a shattered South was forced into rapid industrialization.

- 1945 CE: **Division at the 38th parallel** — The peninsula is split into Soviet and American occupation zones along the 38th parallel, setting the stage for two rival Korean states. [state-korean-war, korea-place]
- 7-8 September 1945: **American forces land at Incheon** — MacArthur's Proclamation No. 1 (7 September) assumes military government over Korea south of 38 degrees north latitude. A small advance party of the US XXIV Corps had landed at Kimpo airfield near Seoul on 4 September; the bulk of the corps lands at Incheon and enters Seoul on 8 September. [frus-1945-macarthur-proclamation, schnabel-ch02]
- 10 May 1948: **Election in the south** — Elections for a National Assembly are held in South Korea, with an estimated 80 percent of eligible voters taking part; the North does not take part or recognize the result, and the UN commission is barred from the North. The UN Temporary Commission on Korea reports the results valid. [schnabel-ch02]
- 15 August 1948: **Republic of Korea proclaimed** — South Korea is established under President Syngman Rhee after a constitution is promulgated and UN-observed elections held; the DPRK is founded in the north weeks later. [first-republic, korea-place]
- 25 June 1950: **The Korean War** — North Korea invades the South; a US-led UN force and Chinese intervention turn the war into a grinding stalemate that devastates the peninsula and kills millions. [state-korean-war, korea-place]
- 1-4 August 1950: **The Pusan Perimeter** — US and ROK units withdraw behind the Naktong River into a rectangle nearly 100 miles from north to south and about 50 miles from east to west around Pusan, where the Eighth Army commander orders that there will be no further retreat. [schnabel-ch07]
- 15 September 1950: **Inchon landing** — US Marines, backed by naval and air bombardment, assault Inchon and quickly defeat its North Korean defenders. On 27 September MacArthur is authorized to conduct operations north of the 38th parallel. [schnabel-ch10]
- 21 November 1950: **US troops reach the Yalu** — Troops of the US 7th Division's 17th Infantry reach the Yalu River at Hyesanjin, as Chinese armies mass along the river in Manchuria and an unknown number have already entered Korea. [schnabel-ch14]
- 27 July 1953: **Korean War armistice** — An armistice halts the fighting near the 38th parallel and creates the Demilitarized Zone; no peace treaty is signed, freezing the division to the present day. [state-korean-war, korea-place]
- 27 July 1953: **The Demilitarized Zone is defined** — The armistice, signed at Panmunjom by Lt. Gen. William K. Harrison Jr. for the UN Command and Gen. Nam Il for the Korean People's Army and the Chinese People's Volunteers, fixes a military demarcation line and has both sides withdraw two kilometres from it, making a buffer zone about four kilometres wide. The line follows the positions held at the end of the fighting, not the 38th parallel; the coast-to-coast length is given as 238 kilometres by National Geographic. [nara-armistice, natgeo-dmz]
- 16 May 1961: **Park Chung-hee's military coup** — General Park Chung-hee seizes power and launches state-led, export-driven industrialization, the Miracle on the Han River, alongside authoritarian repression. [park-chung-hee, korea-place]

Figures:

- Syngman Rhee (1875-1965) — First president of South Korea. A longtime independence activist who became the Republic of Korea's first president in 1948 and led it through the Korean War. His rule grew autocratic and corrupt, ending when the April Revolution of 1960 forced him into exile.
- Park Chung-hee (1917-1979) — Military leader and president. Seizing power in 1961, he ruled for eighteen years, driving South Korea's rapid industrialization through state planning and the chaebol while suppressing dissent. He was assassinated by his own intelligence chief in 1979.

## Democratization and Modern South Korea — 1980 - present

Through mass struggle South Korea won its democracy, survived financial crisis, and transformed an economic miracle into a global cultural force that now reaches every corner of the world.

- 18-27 May 1980: **Gwangju Uprising** — A pro-democracy uprising in Gwangju is crushed by the military with heavy loss of life, becoming a catalyst and symbol for South Korea's democracy movement. [gwangju-uprising, gwangju-unesco]
- June 1987: **June Democratic Struggle** — Millions protest nationwide, forcing the regime to grant direct presidential elections and democratic reform, founding the present Sixth Republic. [june-struggle, korea-place]
- 1988 CE: **Seoul Summer Olympics** — Seoul hosts the Summer Olympics, showcasing South Korea's recovery and helping cement its turn toward democracy and global engagement. [seoul-1988, korea-place]
- 1997-1998 CE: **Asian financial crisis and IMF rescue** — A regional financial crisis forces South Korea to seek a major IMF bailout; sweeping reforms follow under President Kim Dae-jung. [kim-dae-jung, korea-place]
- June 2000: **First inter-Korean summit** — President Kim Dae-jung meets North Korea's Kim Jong-il in Pyongyang under the Sunshine Policy; Kim Dae-jung receives the Nobel Peace Prize that year. [kim-dae-jung]

Figures:

- Kim Dae-jung (1924-2009) — President and democracy advocate. A dissident once sentenced to death under military rule, he became president in 1998, led South Korea out of financial crisis, and pursued reconciliation with the North through the Sunshine Policy, winning the 2000 Nobel Peace Prize.
