# The Chronicle of Djibouti: A small Horn of Africa republic at the Bab-el-Mandeb gateway, from ancient trade and early Islam through French Somaliland to a coveted hub of world shipping and foreign military bases.

Founding: Independence from France as the Republic of Djibouti — 27 June 1977. After more than a century of French presence, the people of the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas voted overwhelmingly for independence in a referendum on 8 May 1977. The Republic of Djibouti formally came into being on 27 June 1977, the last French territory on the African mainland to gain independence. Hassan Gouled Aptidon, leader of the independence movement, became the country's first president. The new state inherited the strategic port and the railway to Ethiopia that had defined the colony, as well as a delicate balance between its Afar and Somali Issa populations that would shape its politics for decades.

Djibouti occupies a slip of arid coast where the Red Sea narrows into the Gulf of Aden at the strait of Bab-el-Mandeb, one of the world's busiest maritime chokepoints. The region has been bound to long-distance trade since antiquity, when its shores formed part of the commercial world of the Horn linked to ancient Egypt's Land of Punt, and Islam reached the nearby Red Sea coast within the first century of the faith; the port of Zeila, just to the south, became an early Muslim centre. The land is home to two principal peoples, the Afar and the Somali Issa, whose territories were drawn into the orbit of the medieval Muslim sultanates of Ifat and Adal. French interest began in 1862 with the purchase of the anchorage of Obock; treaties with local Afar and Somali rulers in the 1880s extended French control, and in 1888 Léonce Lagarde founded the port-city of Djibouti, which became the capital of the colony of French Somaliland (Côte française des Somalis) in 1896. The Franco-Ethiopian Railway, built between 1897 and 1917, tied the port to Addis Ababa and gave landlocked Ethiopia its principal outlet to the sea. After referendums in 1958 and 1967—the latter renaming the territory the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas—a 1977 vote led to independence on 27 June 1977 under President Hassan Gouled Aptidon. A civil war between the government and the Afar-based FRUD rebellion ran from 1991 to 1994, with a final accord in 2001. Since 1999 the country has been led by Ismail Omar Guelleh. Modern Djibouti thrives on its location, hosting foreign military bases—French, the US base Camp Lemonnier, Chinese, Japanese and others—and serving as a vital trans-shipment port and gateway for Ethiopian trade.

## Ancient Trade and the Coming of Islam — Antiquity – c. 12th century CE

Bound to the maritime trade of the Horn since antiquity, the coast around the Bab-el-Mandeb received Islam very early, and the nearby port of Zeila grew into a centre of the faith.

- ancient times: **Coast woven into Horn of Africa trade** — The shores of the Bab-el-Mandeb form part of the ancient commercial world of the Horn, exchanging aromatics, ivory and other goods with Egypt and southern Arabia; the wider region is linked to the fabled Land of Punt. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 7th century CE: **Islam reaches the Red Sea coast** — Islam spreads to the coast of the Horn within the first century of the faith, carried by merchants and migrants from Arabia across the narrow strait. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- c. 9th–10th century: **Zeila a centre of Islam and trade** — The port of Zeila, just south of present-day Djibouti, grows into one of the oldest Muslim towns of the Horn and a hub of Red Sea commerce serving the interior. [wikipedia-history-djibouti, newworld-djibouti]

## The Afar, the Issa and the Muslim Sultanates — c. 1150 – 1862

The medieval Muslim sultanates of Ifat and Adal reached into the region, drawing the Afar and Somali peoples into the long struggle between the coastal Muslim states and the Christian Ethiopian highlands.

- late 13th century: **Sultanate of Ifat dominant on the routes** — The Walashma-ruled Sultanate of Ifat, with Zeila as its principal port, commands the trade routes between the Ethiopian highlands and the Red Sea, spreading Muslim political power into the region. [wikipedia-history-djibouti, britannica-djibouti]
- c. 1332: **Ifat broken by Ethiopia; rise of Adal** — The Ethiopian emperor Amda Seyon defeats Ifat; the Sultanate of Adal succeeds it as the leading Muslim power contesting the Christian highlands. [wikipedia-history-djibouti, britannica-djibouti]
- 1529: **Adal's invasion of the highlands** — Adal forces under Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi ('Gran') invade Christian Ethiopia, the high point of the coastal Muslim states' power before Adal's later decline. [britannica-djibouti, newworld-djibouti]
- c. 17th–18th century: **Local Afar and Issa polities** — After Adal's decline the region fragments into Afar sultanates such as Tadjoura and Raheita and Issa Somali clan territories, the local powers France would later engage. [wikipedia-history-djibouti, britannica-djibouti]

## The French Foothold and the Founding of Djibouti — 1862 – 1896

France bought the anchorage of Obock in 1862, signed treaties with local sultans in the 1880s, and in 1888 founded the port-city of Djibouti, which became the capital of French Somaliland in 1896.

- 1862: **France buys the anchorage of Obock** — France purchases the anchorage of Obock on the Gulf of Tadjoura from local Afar sultans, its first foothold on the coast. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti, france-diplomatie-djibouti]
- 1884: **Lagarde extends French control** — Léonce Lagarde is sent to oversee French interests; treaties with Afar and Somali rulers over the following years extend a French protectorate around the Gulf of Tadjoura. [wikipedia-lagarde, britannica-djibouti]
- 1888: **Founding of Djibouti city** — Lagarde founds the port-city of Djibouti on the southern shore of the gulf, with a superior harbour, and transfers the administration there from Obock. [wikipedia-lagarde, wikipedia-history-djibouti, britannica-djibouti]
- 20 May 1896: **Colony of French Somaliland created** — A French decree constitutes the holdings as the colony of the Côte française des Somalis (French Somaliland), with Djibouti as its capital, replacing Obock as the seat of government. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]

Figures:

- Léonce Lagarde (1860–1936) — First French governor of the colony. Diplomat and administrator who consolidated French control on the coast from 1884, founded the city of Djibouti in 1888 and governed the new colony of French Somaliland until 1899.

## The Railway and the Colony of French Somaliland — 1897 – 1957

The Franco-Ethiopian Railway, built between 1897 and 1917, made Djibouti the principal outlet to the sea for landlocked Ethiopia and the economic heart of the colony.

- 1897: **Franco-Ethiopian Railway begun** — Construction starts on the railway from the port of Djibouti toward the Ethiopian interior, the project that will define the colony's economy. [wikipedia-railway, britannica-djibouti]
- 1917: **Railway reaches Addis Ababa** — The line is completed to Addis Ababa, giving landlocked Ethiopia its main outlet to the sea and confirming Djibouti as the entrepôt for Ethiopian trade. [wikipedia-railway, africanews-railway, britannica-djibouti]
- 1942: **Djibouti rallies to Free France** — After a period under Vichy control and blockade during the Second World War, the colony joins the Free French forces. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 1946: **Reorganised as an overseas territory** — French Somaliland becomes an overseas territory of the French Union, gaining representation in Paris and limited local institutions amid the wider decolonisation of Africa. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]

## Referendums and the Road to Independence — 1958 – 1977

Two referendums kept the territory within France and renamed it for its Afar and Issa peoples; a third, in 1977, brought independence on 27 June under Hassan Gouled Aptidon.

- 1958: **First referendum keeps ties to France** — Voters in French Somaliland choose to remain within the French Community rather than join independent Somalia. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 19 March 1967: **Renamed Territory of the Afars and the Issas** — A second, disputed referendum again favours association with France; the territory is renamed the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 8 May 1977: **Independence referendum passes** — A final referendum returns an overwhelming majority for independence from France. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 27 June 1977: **Republic of Djibouti proclaimed** — Djibouti becomes independent, the last French territory on mainland Africa to do so; Hassan Gouled Aptidon becomes the first president. [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti, newworld-djibouti]

Figures:

- Hassan Gouled Aptidon (1916–2006) — First President of Djibouti (1977–1999). Issa politician who led the independence campaign and became Djibouti's founding president, ruling for over two decades and establishing a single-party state before handing power to his nephew in 1999.

## Civil War, Guelleh and the Republic of Bases — 1977 – present

An Afar rebellion brought civil war from 1991 to 1994, ended by accords culminating in 2001; under Ismail Omar Guelleh since 1999, Djibouti has become a coveted host of foreign military bases and a vital port.

- 1981: **One-party state established** — President Gouled makes Djibouti a one-party state under the People's Rally for Progress (RPP). [britannica-djibouti, wikipedia-history-djibouti]
- 1991–1994: **Civil war with the FRUD rebellion** — The Afar-based Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy launches an insurgency in 1991; the civil war runs until a 1994 accord with the movement's moderate wing largely ends it. [wikipedia-civilwar, wikipedia-frud, britannica-djibouti]
- 1999: **Ismail Omar Guelleh becomes president** — Gouled steps down and his nephew and chief of staff Ismail Omar Guelleh wins the presidency, beginning a long tenure that continues to the present. [wikipedia-guelleh, britannica-djibouti]
- 12 May 2001: **Final FRUD peace accord** — President Guelleh presides over a final peace agreement with the hardline FRUD faction led by Ahmed Dini, formally ending the decade-long conflict. [wikipedia-civilwar, newhumanitarian-frud]
- 1 August 2017: **China opens its first overseas base** — China inaugurates its first overseas military base in Djibouti, joining French, US (Camp Lemonnier), Japanese and other forces and confirming the country's role as a hub of foreign military presence. [wikipedia-pla-base, crs-china-djibouti, wikipedia-foreign-bases]

Figures:

- Ismail Omar Guelleh (born 1947) — President of Djibouti (since 1999). Nephew and long-time chief of staff of Hassan Gouled Aptidon, he succeeded him in 1999 and, after term limits were removed in 2010, has remained in power, presiding over the country's rise as a host of foreign military bases.
- Ahmed Dini Ahmed (1932–2004) — Afar leader and head of the armed FRUD. Veteran Afar politician who proclaimed the 1977 independence declaration and later led the hardline faction of the FRUD rebellion until the final peace accord of 2001.
