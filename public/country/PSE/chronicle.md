# The Chronicle of Palestine: An ancient and contested land at the crossroads of three continents and three faiths, whose people declared an independent state in 1988 and whose modern history is defined by dispossession and the struggle for self-determination.

Founding: Palestinian Declaration of Independence — 15 November 1988. On 15 November 1988, at a session of the Palestinian National Council in Algiers, PLO Chairman Yasser Arafat proclaimed the Declaration of Independence of the State of Palestine, with Jerusalem named as its capital, on the basis of the 1947 UN partition resolution and the Palestinian people's right to self-determination. The declaration rests on the land's deep historical roots, where settled life reaches back to ancient Canaan and the name 'Palestine' derives from the Philistines by way of Greek and Roman usage. On 29 November 2012 the UN General Assembly, in resolution 67/19, recognised the State of Palestine as a non-member observer state of the United Nations by 138 votes to 9 with 41 abstentions. The State of Palestine is today recognised by a large majority of UN member states, more than 140 of the 193 members.

Palestine is a land at the eastern shore of the Mediterranean whose history stretches back to the Bronze Age city-states of Canaan and the Philistines from whom the name ultimately derives. Over millennia the region passed through Egyptian, Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, Arab Islamic, Crusader, Ayyubid, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule, becoming a holy land for Jews, Christians, and Muslims. Four centuries of Ottoman governance ended with the First World War, after which Britain's 1917 Balfour Declaration and the ensuing Mandate set in motion the conflict between Zionism and Palestinian Arab nationalism. The 1947 UN partition plan and the 1948 war produced the Nakba, the expulsion and flight of some 700,000 Palestinians and the destruction of hundreds of villages. Israel's 1967 occupation of the West Bank and Gaza, the rise of the Palestine Liberation Organization, the Intifadas, and the Oslo process followed. On 15 November 1988 the Palestinian National Council in Algiers proclaimed the State of Palestine, and in 2012 the UN General Assembly recognised it as a non-member observer state. The blockade of Gaza from 2007 and the catastrophic war of 2023 to 2025, which leading international institutions have determined amounts to genocide, frame the present.

## Ancient Palestine: Canaan, the Philistines, and the Empires — c. 3000 BCE - 332 BCE

From the walled towns of Bronze Age Canaan to the seafaring Philistines of the coast, the land's early peoples developed alongside the great empires of the Near East, falling in turn under Egyptian, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Persian dominion.

- c. 3000 BCE: **Canaanite city-states of the Bronze Age** — Walled Canaanite towns flourished across the southern Levant, trading with Egypt and Mesopotamia and forming the region's earliest urban civilisation. [britannica-palestine, wiki-canaan]
- c. 12th century BCE: **Arrival of the Philistines** — The Philistines settled the southern coastal plain, founding cities such as Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ashdod; their name would later give the land the name 'Palestine'. [britannica-philistines, wiki-philistines]
- 586 BCE: **Babylonian conquest of Jerusalem** — Neo-Babylonian forces under Nebuchadnezzar II captured Jerusalem and destroyed the First Temple, deporting much of the population to Babylon. [britannica-palestine]
- 539 BCE: **Persian rule under the Achaemenids** — Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon and incorporated the region into the Persian Empire, permitting exiled communities to return. [britannica-palestine]

## Roman, Byzantine, and the Name "Palaestina" — 332 BCE - 636 CE

Under Hellenistic and then Roman rule the region became formally known as Palaestina; as a holy land for Jews and the cradle of Christianity, and later revered in Islam, it drew pilgrims and rulers alike.

- 332 BCE: **Hellenistic conquest** — Alexander the Great's conquest brought the region into the Hellenistic world of the Ptolemaic and Seleucid kingdoms. [britannica-palestine]
- 63 BCE: **Roman conquest** — The Roman general Pompey took Jerusalem, bringing the region under Roman authority as the province of Judaea. [britannica-palestine]
- c. 135 CE: **Province renamed Syria Palaestina** — After crushing the Bar Kokhba revolt, the emperor Hadrian renamed the province Syria Palaestina, formalising the name 'Palestine' in Roman administration. [wiki-syria-palaestina, britannica-palestine]
- 4th century CE: **A Christian holy land** — Following Constantine's conversion, Palestine became a centre of Christian pilgrimage; churches were built over sites venerated as those of Jesus's life, death, and resurrection. [britannica-palestine]

## Islamic Palestine: Conquest, Caliphates, Crusades, and Mamluks — 636 - 1516

Arab Muslim armies took the region in 637 to 638, beginning nearly a millennium of predominantly Islamic rule; the Umayyads adorned Jerusalem with the Dome of the Rock, the Crusaders seized and lost it, and Ayyubid and Mamluk dynasties governed thereafter.

- 637-638 CE: **Arab Muslim conquest of Jerusalem** — Jerusalem surrendered to the caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab, beginning nearly a millennium of predominantly Islamic rule and the region's gradual Arabisation. [wiki-muslim-conquest-jerusalem, britannica-palestine]
- 691-692 CE: **Building of the Dome of the Rock** — The Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik built the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, an early masterpiece of Islamic architecture on the Haram al-Sharif. [britannica-dome-of-the-rock]
- 1099 CE: **Crusaders capture Jerusalem** — The First Crusade seized Jerusalem and founded the Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem, holding the city until 1187. [britannica-crusades]
- 1187 CE: **Saladin retakes Jerusalem** — Saladin, founder of the Ayyubid dynasty, defeated the Crusaders and recaptured Jerusalem, largely ending Crusader rule over the city. [britannica-saladin, britannica-crusades]
- 1291 CE: **Mamluk rule established** — The Mamluk sultanate of Egypt expelled the last Crusaders from the Levant and governed Palestine until the Ottoman conquest. [britannica-palestine]

Figures:

- Saladin (Salah al-Din) (1137-1193) — Sultan and founder of the Ayyubid dynasty. A Kurdish Muslim commander, Saladin united Egypt and Syria, defeated the Crusaders at Hattin in 1187, and recaptured Jerusalem, becoming an enduring figure in Islamic and world history.

## Ottoman Palestine — 1516 - 1917

For four centuries Palestine was a province of the Ottoman Empire, home to a predominantly Arab Muslim society with Christian and Jewish communities; in the empire's final decades the first waves of Zionist immigration began to arrive.

- 1516-1517: **Ottoman conquest** — Sultan Selim I defeated the Mamluks and incorporated Palestine into the Ottoman Empire, beginning four centuries of Ottoman rule. [britannica-palestine]
- 1882 onward: **First waves of Zionist immigration** — From the 1880s organised Jewish immigration (the aliyot) began establishing agricultural colonies in Ottoman Palestine, driven by persecution in Europe and an emerging national idea. [britannica-palestine, un-origins-palestine]
- 1897: **First Zionist Congress** — Theodor Herzl convened the First Zionist Congress in Basel, founding the World Zionist Organization and adopting the aim of a Jewish national home in Palestine. [un-origins-palestine]

## The British Mandate and the Nakba — 1917 - 1949

Britain's 1917 Balfour Declaration and its subsequent Mandate set Zionism and Palestinian nationalism on a collision course; the 1936-39 Arab revolt, the 1947 UN partition plan, and the 1948 war culminated in the Nakba, the dispossession of most of Palestine's Arab population.

- 2 November 1917: **Balfour Declaration** — Britain declared its support for a Jewish national home in Palestine while affirming the rights of the existing, overwhelmingly Arab, non-Jewish communities. [un-origins-palestine, britannica-balfour]
- 1920-1923: **British Mandate for Palestine** — The League of Nations entrusted Britain with governing Palestine; the Mandate incorporated the Balfour Declaration's commitment to a Jewish national home. [un-origins-palestine, britannica-palestine]
- 1936-1939: **Arab Revolt in Palestine** — A sustained Palestinian Arab uprising against British rule and Zionist immigration was suppressed with great severity, leaving Palestinian society weakened. [britannica-palestine]
- 29 November 1947: **UN Partition Plan (Resolution 181)** — The UN General Assembly recommended partition into Jewish and Arab states with Jerusalem internationalised; accepted by Zionist leaders and rejected by Palestinian and Arab leaders. [un-resolution-181, britannica-palestine]
- 1947-1949: **The Nakba** — During the war surrounding Israel's establishment, around 700,000 Palestinians were expelled or fled and, per UN figures, more than 500 villages were depopulated and destroyed. [un-about-nakba, wiki-nakba, britannica-palestine]

Figures:

- Hajj Amin al-Husseini (c. 1897-1974) — Grand Mufti of Jerusalem and Palestinian nationalist leader. As Grand Mufti and head of the Arab Higher Committee, al-Husseini was the leading Palestinian Arab political figure of the Mandate era, prominent in opposition to British policy and Zionism; his record, including wartime alignment with the Axis, remains contested.

## Occupation, Dispossession, and the Present — 1949 - present

Israel's 1967 occupation of the West Bank and Gaza, the rise of the PLO, the Intifadas, the Oslo process, and the 1988 declaration of independence frame a half-century of dispossession that culminated in the blockade of Gaza and the catastrophic war of 2023 to 2025, which leading international institutions have determined amounts to genocide.

- 1964: **Founding of the PLO** — The Palestine Liberation Organization was established as the umbrella body of the Palestinian national movement, later led by Yasser Arafat. [britannica-plo]
- 5-10 June 1967: **Israeli occupation of the West Bank and Gaza** — In the Six-Day War Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip, placing most Palestinians under a military occupation that endures. [britannica-six-day-war, britannica-palestine]
- 1987-1993: **First Intifada** — A sustained popular uprising against the Israeli occupation of the West Bank and Gaza brought the Palestinian cause to renewed international attention. [britannica-intifada]
- 15 November 1988: **Declaration of the State of Palestine** — The Palestinian National Council in Algiers, chaired by Yasser Arafat, proclaimed the independent State of Palestine with Jerusalem as its capital. [wiki-declaration-independence, britannica-palestine]
- 1993: **Oslo Accords** — Israel and the PLO exchanged mutual recognition and agreed a framework for Palestinian interim self-government, creating the Palestinian Authority. [britannica-palestine]
- 2000-2005: **Second Intifada** — A second Palestinian uprising brought years of violence and Israeli military operations, ending the optimism of the Oslo era. [britannica-intifada]
- 2007: **Blockade of the Gaza Strip** — After Hamas took control of Gaza, Israel and Egypt imposed a blockade restricting the movement of people and goods, with severe humanitarian consequences. [britannica-gaza-blockade, wiki-gaza-blockade]
- 29 November 2012: **UN recognition as non-member observer state** — The UN General Assembly, by resolution 67/19, recognised the State of Palestine as a non-member observer state by 138 votes to 9 with 41 abstentions. [un-res-67-19, wiki-res-67-19]
- from 7 October 2023: **The Gaza war** — After a Hamas-led attack on southern Israel killed about 1,200 people, Israel launched a massive campaign in Gaza; by Gaza Health Ministry figures cited by the UN, tens of thousands of Palestinians, mostly women and children, have been killed. [wiki-gaza-war, ohchr-coi-genocide]
- 26 January 2024: **ICJ provisional-measures order in South Africa v. Israel** — The International Court of Justice found at least some rights under the Genocide Convention plausible and a real and imminent risk to them, ordering Israel to prevent genocidal acts; the case remains under adjudication. [icj-order-26jan2024, icj-case-192]
- March 2024: **UN Special Rapporteur's 'Anatomy of a Genocide'** — UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese reported reasonable grounds to believe genocide was being committed against Palestinians in Gaza (A/HRC/55/73). [un-anatomy-genocide, ohchr-albanese-report]
- December 2024: **Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch findings** — Amnesty International (5 December) concluded Israel was committing genocide; Human Rights Watch (19 December) found acts of genocide and the crime against humanity of extermination. [amnesty-genocide-report, hrw-genocide-water]
- 16 September 2025: **UN Commission of Inquiry finds genocide** — The UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory concluded that Israel had committed genocide in the Gaza Strip; Israel rejected the findings. [ohchr-coi-genocide]

Figures:

- Yasser Arafat (1929-2004) — PLO chairman and first president of the Palestinian Authority. Leader of the PLO from 1969, Arafat proclaimed the Palestinian Declaration of Independence in 1988, signed the Oslo Accords, and shared the 1994 Nobel Peace Prize; he became the foremost symbol of Palestinian national aspirations.
- Francesca Albanese (born 1977) — UN Special Rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory. An international lawyer appointed Special Rapporteur in 2022, Albanese authored the 2024 report 'Anatomy of a Genocide', finding reasonable grounds to believe genocide was being committed in Gaza.
