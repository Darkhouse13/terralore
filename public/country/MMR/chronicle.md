Founding: Independence of the Union of Burma — 4 January 1948. On 4 January 1948 Burma became an independent sovereign state, leaving the British Empire entirely rather than remaining within the Commonwealth. The architect of independence, the nationalist leader Aung San, had negotiated the transfer of power with British Prime Minister Clement Attlee in January 1947 but was assassinated with most of his cabinet in July 1947; the new nation was led to independence by U Nu. The date crowned a long arc of statehood reaching back through the Konbaung, Toungoo and Pagan kingdoms to the Pyu city-states, and through more than six decades of British colonial rule that had ended the Burmese monarchy in 1885. Independence proved fragile: ethnic insurgency and political instability gave way in 1962 to military rule that would shape the country for generations.

Myanmar's recorded history begins with the Pyu city-states of the Irrawaddy basin, where Buddhism gained its first lasting foothold in Southeast Asia, and rises to the kingdom of Pagan (Bagan), whose king Anawrahta made Theravada Buddhism the foundation of Burmese civilization. The Toungoo and Konbaung dynasties built the largest Burmese empires before the Konbaung kings lost three Anglo-Burmese Wars, ending in the British annexation of the whole country in 1885 and its rule first as a province of British India and, from 1937, as a separate colony. After Japanese occupation in the Second World War and the rise of the nationalist Aung San, Burma won independence on 4 January 1948. General Ne Win's 1962 coup opened decades of military rule punctuated by the 1988 uprising and the long pro-democracy struggle of Aung San Suu Kyi; a fragile opening after 2011 was reversed by the military's coup of 1 February 2021, and the country remains gripped by conflict in 2026.

## The Pyu City-States and the Coming of Buddhism — c. 200 BCE - 900 CE

In the dry plains of the Irrawaddy, the Tibeto-Burman Pyu built walled, moated cities that gave Buddhism its earliest lasting home in Southeast Asia.

- c. 200 BCE: **Rise of the Pyu city-states** — Tibeto-Burman Pyu peoples establish walled, moated cities such as Beikthano, Halin and Sri Ksetra in the Irrawaddy basin, founding the region's first urban civilization. [unesco-pyu, history-of-myanmar]
- early centuries CE: **Buddhism takes root** — The Pyu cities provide the earliest evidence of Buddhism's introduction into Southeast Asia, embedding the religion in the region's culture and statecraft. [unesco-pyu]
- 9th century CE: **Decline of the Pyu** — Raids from the Nanzhao kingdom and the southward migration of the Burman people erode Pyu power, clearing the way for the rise of Bagan. [history-of-myanmar, pagan-kingdom]

## The Kingdom of Pagan (Bagan) — 849 - 1297 CE

On a bend of the Irrawaddy, King Anawrahta forged the first unified Burmese kingdom and made Theravada Buddhism its soul, leaving behind the temple-covered plain of Bagan.

- 849 CE (traditional): **Founding of Bagan** — The city of Bagan is traditionally established on a bend of the Irrawaddy, later to become the seat of the first Burmese kingdom. [pagan-kingdom, unesco-bagan]
- 1044-1077 CE: **Reign of Anawrahta** — Anawrahta unifies the Burman north and the Mon south into the first Burmese kingdom and adopts Theravada Buddhism as the state religion. [anawrahta, pagan-kingdom]
- 1057 CE (traditional): **Conquest of Thaton** — Anawrahta captures the Mon city of Thaton, by tradition bringing Buddhist scriptures, monks and artisans that enrich Pagan's culture. [anawrahta, history-of-myanmar]
- 1287 CE: **Mongol invasion and collapse** — Armies of Kublai Khan's Mongol empire invade and shatter the kingdom; a further incursion in 1297 ends Bagan as a unifying power. [pagan-kingdom, history-of-myanmar]

Figures:

- Anawrahta (reigned 1044-1077) — Founder-king of Pagan. The first king of a unified Myanmar, he joined the Burman north and Mon south and, converted by the monk Shin Arahan, made Theravada Buddhism the dominant religion and lasting cultural foundation of the country.

## Empires Reborn: Toungoo and Konbaung — 1510 - 1824 CE

Twice after Bagan's fall the Burmese kings rebuilt great empires, under Bayinnaung of Toungoo and Alaungpaya of Konbaung, the second the largest in the country's history.

- 1551-1581 CE: **Reign of Bayinnaung** — Bayinnaung of the Toungoo dynasty reunifies Myanmar and conquers the Shan states and Siam, building the most powerful empire in mainland Southeast Asia. [bayinnaung, toungoo-dynasty]
- 1752 CE: **Alaungpaya founds the Konbaung dynasty** — The village chief Alaungpaya leads a revolt against Mon overlords and establishes the Konbaung dynasty, the last royal house of Burma. [konbaung-dynasty, history-of-myanmar]
- 1757 CE: **Reconquest of Lower Burma** — Alaungpaya's forces capture Pegu and subdue the Mon south, reuniting the country under Konbaung rule. [konbaung-dynasty]
- 1784-1785 CE: **Annexation of Arakan** — Konbaung expansion absorbs the kingdom of Arakan on the western coast, bringing the Burmese frontier up against British-held Bengal. [history-of-myanmar, anglo-burmese-wars]

Figures:

- Bayinnaung (reigned 1551-1581) — King of the Toungoo dynasty. The conqueror who reunified Myanmar and subdued the Shan states and Siam, making the Toungoo realm the most powerful kingdom in mainland Southeast Asia and a great patron of Buddhism.
- Alaungpaya (1714-1760) — Founder of the Konbaung dynasty. A village chief who rose against Mon rule to found the Konbaung dynasty in 1752 and reunify the country, founding the last and largest of the Burmese empires.

## The Anglo-Burmese Wars and British Rule — 1824 - 1948 CE

Three wars over six decades stripped the Konbaung kings of their kingdom; Britain abolished the monarchy in 1885 and ruled Burma as part of India and then as a separate colony until independence.

- 1824-1826 CE: **First Anglo-Burmese War** — War over the Arakan and Assam frontiers ends with the Treaty of Yandabo (1826), forcing Burma to cede Arakan and Tenasserim to Britain. [anglo-burmese-wars, treaty-of-yandabo]
- 1852 CE: **Second Anglo-Burmese War** — British forces annex Lower Burma, including the Irrawaddy delta and the province renamed Pegu, tightening control of the coastline. [anglo-burmese-wars]
- November 1885: **Third Anglo-Burmese War and the end of the monarchy** — Britain takes Mandalay, exiles King Thibaw, and annexes Upper Burma on 1 January 1886, abolishing the Burmese monarchy and completing the conquest. [anglo-burmese-wars, british-burma]
- 1937 CE: **Burma separated from British India** — Burma is made a distinct crown colony with limited self-government, administered separately from the Indian Empire. [british-burma, history-of-myanmar]
- 19 July 1947: **Assassination of Aung San** — Aung San, who had negotiated independence with Britain, is assassinated with most of his cabinet months before the transfer of power. [aung-san, officehistorian-burma]

Figures:

- Aung San (1915-1947) — Nationalist leader and architect of independence. The founding father of independent Burma, he led the wartime nationalist movement, switching from Japanese to Allied alliance, and negotiated independence with Britain in 1947 before his assassination that July.

## Independence, Ne Win, and Decades of Military Rule — 1948 - 2011 CE

A democratic republic gave way in 1962 to General Ne Win's army, whose isolating socialism and the suppression of the 1988 uprising made Aung San Suu Kyi the face of a long struggle for democracy.

- 4 January 1948: **Independence of the Union of Burma** — Burma becomes a fully independent republic outside the Commonwealth under Prime Minister U Nu, soon facing communist and ethnic insurgencies. [history-of-myanmar, officehistorian-burma]
- 2 March 1962: **Ne Win's coup** — General Ne Win overthrows the elected government, imprisons U Nu, and inaugurates decades of military rule under the Burmese Way to Socialism. [ne-win, history-of-myanmar]
- 1988 CE: **The 8888 uprising** — A nationwide pro-democracy uprising is crushed by the military, which forms a new junta; Aung San Suu Kyi emerges as the democracy movement's leader. [myanmar-since-1988, aung-san-suu-kyi]
- 1990 CE: **Annulled NLD election victory** — Aung San Suu Kyi's National League for Democracy wins elections in a landslide, but the junta ignores the result and detains her for years. [aung-san-suu-kyi, myanmar-since-1988]
- 2007 CE: **The Saffron Revolution** — Buddhist monks lead mass protests against the regime that are violently suppressed, drawing renewed international condemnation. [myanmar-since-1988]

Figures:

- Ne Win (1911-2002) — Military ruler of Burma. The general who seized power in 1962 and ruled until 1988, combining a repressive military dictatorship with the isolating socialist program known as the Burmese Way to Socialism.
- Aung San Suu Kyi (born 1945) — Pro-democracy leader and Nobel laureate. Daughter of the independence hero Aung San, she led a decades-long nonviolent struggle for democracy, won the 1991 Nobel Peace Prize, and spent years under house arrest before later entering government.

## Opening, the Rohingya Crisis, and the 2021 Coup — 2011 - present

A decade of cautious opening brought Aung San Suu Kyi to power and the Rohingya catastrophe to the world's attention, before the army seized control again in 2021 and plunged Myanmar into renewed war.

- 2011 CE: **Reforms under Thein Sein** — A quasi-civilian government begins political and economic liberalization, releasing prisoners, easing censorship, and restoring Aung San Suu Kyi's freedoms. [thein-sein, myanmar-since-1988]
- November 2015: **NLD wins free elections** — Aung San Suu Kyi's National League for Democracy wins Myanmar's first fairly contested elections; she becomes state counsellor in a government sharing power with the army. [aung-san-suu-kyi, myanmar-since-1988]
- August 2017: **Rohingya crisis in Rakhine State** — Military 'clearance operations' drive more than 700,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh; a UN fact-finding mission later finds evidence of genocidal violence. [ohchr-rohingya]
- 1 February 2021: **Military coup** — The army seizes power, detains Aung San Suu Kyi and the elected leadership, and installs a junta under Min Aung Hlaing, triggering mass protests and civil war. [coup-2021, myanmar-civil-war]

Figures:

- Aung San Suu Kyi (born 1945) — State counsellor, 2016-2021. After years of house arrest she led the NLD to power and governed from 2016, but was detained and imprisoned in the 2021 coup; her tenure was clouded internationally by the Rohingya crisis.
- Min Aung Hlaing (born 1956) — Military commander-in-chief and junta leader. Commander-in-chief of Myanmar's armed forces who led the 2021 coup and chairs the ruling State Administration Council; named by UN investigators over the 2017 Rohingya atrocities.
