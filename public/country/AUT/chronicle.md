Founding: Ostarrîchi: the first mention of Austria — 996. The conventional origin of Austria is dated to 1 November 996, when a charter of Emperor Otto III used the word 'Ostarrîchi', the 'eastern realm', the earliest known form of the German name Österreich. It referred to the marchia orientalis, the Eastern March of the Holy Roman Empire entrusted in 976 to the Bavarian house of Babenberg. Under the Babenbergs the march grew into a coherent principality; in 1156 the Privilegium Minus of Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa elevated Austria to a duchy and detached it from Bavaria, and Henry II 'Jasomirgott' made Vienna his residence. When the male Babenberg line ended in 1246, the duchy passed, after a Bohemian interlude, to the Habsburgs in 1278, opening the dynasty that would shape Austria and much of Europe until 1918.

Austria's history is the history of a frontier that became the centre of Europe. The name first appears in 996 as 'Ostarrîchi', the 'eastern realm', a march of the Holy Roman Empire ruled by the Babenberg dynasty, who in 1156 were raised to dukes and made Vienna their seat. When the Babenberg line died out, the Habsburgs seized the duchy after Rudolf I's victory at the Marchfeld in 1278, beginning a dynasty that would rule for more than six centuries and turn Vienna into an imperial capital. The Habsburgs held the line against two great Ottoman sieges, in 1529 and 1683, ruled the Holy Roman Empire for generations, and under Maria Theresa reformed their sprawling possessions. In 1804 they proclaimed the Empire of Austria, and after 1867 governed it jointly with Hungary as a multi-ethnic Dual Monarchy. The assassination of the heir Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo in 1914 helped ignite the First World War, and defeat dissolved Austria-Hungary in 1918. A small German-speaking republic was left behind, its borders fixed by the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919), which forbade union with Germany and ceded South Tyrol to Italy. Hitler annexed Austria in the Anschluss of 1938; after the Second World War and a decade of four-power occupation, the Austrian State Treaty of 1955 restored full sovereignty, and Austria declared its permanent neutrality. It joined the European Union in 1995.

## Ostarrîchi: The Babenberg March and Duchy — 976 – 1246

A frontier march on the Danube, granted to the Babenbergs, acquired the name of Austria, grew into a duchy and chose Vienna as its seat.

- 976: **The Eastern March granted to the Babenbergs** — The marchia orientalis, a frontier march of the Holy Roman Empire on the Danube, was entrusted to Count Liutpold of the Bavarian house of Babenberg, beginning their long rule over the future Austria. [habsburg-ostarrichi]
- 1 Nov 996: **First mention of 'Ostarrîchi'** — A charter of Emperor Otto III used the word 'Ostarrîchi', the 'eastern realm', the earliest recorded form of the name Österreich (Austria), referring to the Babenberg march. [habsburg-ostarrichi]
- 1156: **The Privilegium Minus raises Austria to a duchy** — Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa's Privilegium Minus elevated Austria from a march to a duchy and detached it from Bavaria, granting the Babenbergs wide hereditary rights; Vienna became their seat. [habsburg-ostarrichi]
- 1246: **End of the Babenberg line** — Duke Frederick II 'the Quarrelsome' was killed fighting the Hungarians, leaving no male heir; the extinction of the Babenbergs left the duchy of Austria contested among neighbouring powers. [habsburg-ostarrichi]

Figures:

- Leopold III (1073 – 1136) — Margrave of Austria, patron saint. The most revered of the Babenberg margraves, Leopold III consolidated the dynasty and founded religious houses such as Klosterneuburg. He was later canonised and became the patron saint of Austria.
- Henry II 'Jasomirgott' (c. 1107 – 1177) — First Duke of Austria. Under the Privilegium Minus of 1156 Henry II became the first duke of an Austria raised above the rank of a march. He moved his residence to Vienna, fixing the city as the centre of Austrian power.

## The Rise of the Habsburgs and the Ottoman Wars — 1278 – 1740

A new dynasty seized the duchy at the Marchfeld, made Vienna an imperial capital, and held the line against two great Ottoman sieges.

- 26 Aug 1278: **Battle of the Marchfeld** — Rudolf I of Habsburg defeated and killed the Bohemian king Ottokar II near Dürnkrut on the Marchfeld, seizing the Austrian lands; in 1282 he enfeoffed his sons with them, founding Habsburg rule in Austria. [habsburg-takeover]
- 1529: **First Ottoman siege of Vienna** — The army of Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent besieged Vienna for three weeks in the autumn of 1529 before withdrawing, the first great Ottoman assault on the Habsburg capital. [siege-1529]
- 12 Sep 1683: **Second Ottoman siege and the relief of Vienna** — An Ottoman army under Grand Vizier Kara Mustafa besieged Vienna until a relief force led by Charles V of Lorraine and King Jan III Sobieski of Poland routed it on 12 September 1683, a turning point against Ottoman expansion. [siege-1683]
- 1697–1718: **Prince Eugene and the conquest of Hungary** — After Vienna's deliverance, Prince Eugene of Savoy won victories such as Zenta (1697) and the capture of Belgrade (1717); the Peace of Karlowitz (1699) secured Habsburg control of Hungary and Transylvania. [prince-eugene]

Figures:

- Rudolf I of Habsburg (1218 – 1291) — Roman-German king, founder of Habsburg Austria. Elected king in 1273, Rudolf defeated Ottokar II at the Marchfeld in 1278 and granted the Austrian lands to his sons, beginning more than six centuries of Habsburg rule over Austria.
- Prince Eugene of Savoy (1663 – 1736) — Habsburg general. The foremost commander of the Habsburg armies, Eugene crushed the Ottomans at Zenta in 1697 and took Belgrade in 1717, driving the Turks from Hungary and earning a place at the heart of Austrian national memory.

## Maria Theresa, the Empire of Austria and the Holy Roman Empire — 1740 – 1848

A reforming empress held the dynasty together, and her descendants exchanged the Holy Roman crown for a new imperial title of their own.

- 1740–1780: **Reign of Maria Theresa** — Inheriting the Habsburg lands under the Pragmatic Sanction, Maria Theresa survived the War of the Austrian Succession and reformed the administration, army and education across her dominions. [maria-theresa]
- 11 Aug 1804: **The Empire of Austria proclaimed** — In response to Napoleon's imperial coronation, Franz II issued a patent assuming the hereditary title of Emperor of Austria as Franz I, formally constituting the Habsburg lands as an empire. [empire-1804]
- 6 Aug 1806: **Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire** — Franz II laid down the crown of the Holy Roman Empire, which was dissolved after some thousand years, leaving the Habsburg ruler as Emperor of Austria alone. [empire-1804]
- 1848: **Revolution and the accession of Franz Joseph** — The revolutions of 1848 shook the Austrian Empire, drove the chancellor Metternich into exile and brought the eighteen-year-old Franz Joseph to the throne, where he would remain until 1916. [franz-joseph]

Figures:

- Maria Theresa (1717 – 1780) — Ruler of the Habsburg dominions. The only female sovereign of the Habsburg lands, Maria Theresa ruled from 1740 to 1780 as archduchess of Austria and queen of Hungary and Bohemia, modernising the state and founding the house of Habsburg-Lorraine.
- Franz II/I (1768 – 1835) — Last Holy Roman Emperor, first Emperor of Austria. Franz proclaimed the Empire of Austria in 1804 and in 1806 dissolved the Holy Roman Empire, ending a millennial institution and transforming the Habsburg realm into a single hereditary empire.

## The Dual Monarchy and the Catastrophe of 1918 — 1848 – 1918

A multi-ethnic empire reorganised itself as Austria-Hungary, until a shot at Sarajevo helped unleash a war that destroyed it.

- 1867: **The Austro-Hungarian Compromise** — The Ausgleich of 1867 reorganised the empire into the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary, two states sharing a common monarch, foreign policy, finance and army; Franz Joseph was crowned king of Hungary on 8 June. [compromise-1867, franz-joseph]
- 28 Jun 1914: **Assassination at Sarajevo** — Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the throne, and his wife Sophie were assassinated in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip, the spark that led Austria-Hungary to war. [sarajevo]
- 28 Jul 1914: **Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia** — After the July Crisis, Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, triggering a chain of alliances that plunged Europe into the First World War. [sarajevo]
- 12 Nov 1918: **Collapse of Austria-Hungary and the First Republic** — As the war was lost the empire disintegrated; Emperor Karl I renounced participation in government on 11 November and the Republic of German-Austria was proclaimed on 12 November 1918, ending Habsburg rule. [end-monarchy-1918]

Figures:

- Franz Joseph I (1830 – 1916) — Emperor of Austria, King of Hungary. Ruling from 1848 to 1916, Franz Joseph presided over the creation of Austria-Hungary in 1867 and over a multi-ethnic empire whose tensions and the war that began in 1914 he did not live to see resolved.
- Franz Ferdinand (1863 – 1914) — Archduke and heir to the throne. Heir to Franz Joseph, Franz Ferdinand was assassinated with his wife Sophie at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip, the event that precipitated the outbreak of the First World War.

## The First Republic, Saint-Germain and the Anschluss — 1918 – 1945

A small republic, its borders dictated and union with Germany forbidden, was nonetheless annexed by Hitler and dragged into the Second World War.

- 10 Sep 1919: **Treaty of Saint-Germain** — Signed for Austria by Karl Renner, the treaty confirmed the breakup of Austria-Hungary, forbade union with Germany without League of Nations approval, limited Austria's army and ceded South Tyrol to Italy. [saint-germain]
- 1919: **South Tyrol ceded to Italy** — Under Saint-Germain the German-speaking southern part of historic Tyrol was awarded to Italy, placing a large German-speaking population under Italian rule and creating a lasting territorial grievance. [saint-germain, degasperi-gruber]
- 1934: **Dollfuss and the corporate state** — Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss suspended parliament and established an authoritarian 'corporate state'; he was assassinated by Austrian Nazis in July 1934, deepening the First Republic's crisis. [anschluss-ushmm]
- 12–13 Mar 1938: **The Anschluss** — German troops entered Austria on 12 March 1938 and on 13 March a law proclaimed reunification with the Reich; Austrian independence ended and a manipulated April plebiscite recorded near-total approval. [anschluss-ushmm]

Figures:

- Karl Renner (1870 – 1950) — First Chancellor of the Republic. A Social Democratic statesman, Renner headed the first government of the republic and signed the Treaty of Saint-Germain in 1919; he returned to lead Austria's provisional government in 1945 and became its first post-war president.
- Engelbert Dollfuss (1892 – 1934) — Chancellor of Austria. Dollfuss dismantled parliamentary democracy and built an authoritarian Catholic 'corporate state' in 1933-1934, banning rival parties. He was assassinated by Austrian Nazis in a failed coup in July 1934.

## The Second Republic: Neutrality, South Tyrol and Europe — 1945 – present

Occupied by four powers for a decade, Austria regained sovereignty in 1955, declared permanent neutrality, settled the South Tyrol question and joined the European Union.

- 15 May 1955: **The Austrian State Treaty** — Signed at the Belvedere in Vienna by the four occupying powers and foreign minister Leopold Figl, the treaty restored Austria's full sovereignty and ended the four-power occupation; it came into force on 27 July 1955. [state-treaty-ebsco]
- 26 Oct 1955: **Declaration of permanent neutrality** — After the last occupation troops left, the Austrian parliament passed a constitutional law declaring the republic's permanent neutrality, the condition on which the Soviet Union had agreed to withdraw. [state-treaty-ebsco]
- 5 Sep 1946: **Gruber-De Gasperi Agreement on South Tyrol** — Austrian foreign minister Karl Gruber and Italian premier Alcide De Gasperi signed an accord committing Italy to grant autonomy and language and education rights to South Tyrol's German-speaking population. [degasperi-gruber]
- 20 Jan 1972: **South Tyrol's Second Autonomy Statute** — After the dispute reached the UN (Resolutions 1497 of 1960 and 1661 of 1961) and the 'Package' of 1969, the Second Autonomy Statute took effect in 1972, granting real autonomy to the province of Bolzano. [southtyrol-package]
- 1 Jan 1995: **Accession to the European Union** — Following a referendum on 12 June 1994 in which about two-thirds voted in favour, Austria joined the European Union on 1 January 1995, integrating the neutral republic into the European mainstream. [austria-eu]

Figures:

- Leopold Figl (1902 – 1965) — Foreign Minister and Chancellor. A survivor of Nazi concentration camps and a leading figure of the Second Republic, Figl signed the Austrian State Treaty for Austria in 1955 and is remembered for proclaiming 'Austria is free!' on its conclusion.
- Karl Gruber (1909 – 1995) — Foreign Minister of Austria. As foreign minister of the new republic, Gruber negotiated the 1946 agreement with the Italian premier De Gasperi that committed Italy to autonomy for German-speaking South Tyrol, the basis of the later settlement.
