Founding: Independence of the Republic of the Sudan — 1 January 1956. Sudan became a sovereign, independent republic on 1 January 1956, ending the Anglo-Egyptian Condominium under which Britain and Egypt had jointly governed the country since 1899. A self-government statute and elected parliament preceded the declaration, and the Sudanese parliament voted unanimously for independence rather than union with Egypt. Civil war between the largely Muslim and Arabic-speaking north and the African, Christian and animist south had already broken out months before independence, in 1955.

Sudan straddles the middle Nile, where one of Africa's oldest civilisations took shape. From the Bronze-Age city of Kerma grew the Kingdom of Kush, whose rulers conquered Egypt as the 25th Dynasty of "Black Pharaohs" and later raised the pyramids of Meroe. Christian Nubian kingdoms held the river for centuries before Islamisation and the rise of the Funj Sultanate of Sennar and the Sultanate of Darfur. A Turco-Egyptian conquest from 1820 founded Khartoum and provoked the Mahdist revolt, crushed by Kitchener at Omdurman in 1898. Under Anglo-Egyptian rule until independence on 1 January 1956, the new state was riven almost at once by north-south civil war. Two long wars, the Darfur conflict, the secession of South Sudan in 2011, a revolution in 2019 and a devastating war between rival generals from 2023 have shaped a nation still searching for stable peace.

## Nubia, Kerma and the Kingdom of Kush — c. 2500 BCE – c. 350 CE

Along the Nile south of Egypt rose Kerma and the Kingdom of Kush, whose rulers conquered Egypt itself and later built the pyramids of Meroe.

- c. 2500–1500 BCE: **The Kerma culture** — The city-state of Kerma dominates the middle Nile, one of the earliest urban civilisations in sub-Saharan Africa and a rival of pharaonic Egypt. [wiki-kush, wh-kush]
- c. 1070 BCE: **Kush independent at Napata** — As Egypt's New Kingdom collapses, Kush re-emerges as an independent kingdom centred on Napata, near Gebel Barkal. [wiki-kush, eb-sudan-kush]
- c. 727 BCE: **Piye conquers Egypt — the 25th Dynasty** — The Kushite king Piye (Piankhi) brings Egypt under Nubian rule, founding the 25th Dynasty of "Black Pharaohs"; Taharqa later reigns at its height. [wiki-kush, wh-kush]
- 671 BCE: **Assyrians expel the Kushites from Egypt** — Esarhaddon and then Ashurbanipal invade Egypt, driving the Kushite pharaohs back to Nubia by the 660s BCE. [wiki-kush]
- c. 593–590 BCE: **Capital moves to Meroe** — After Psamtik II of Egypt sacks Napata, the Kushite court shifts south to Meroe, beginning the Meroitic age of iron, script and pyramids. [wiki-kush, eb-sudan-kush]

Figures:

- Piye (Piankhi) (reigned c. 747–714 BCE) — King of Kush, founder of the 25th Dynasty. Kushite king who conquered Egypt around 727 BCE, uniting Kush and Egypt under Nubian rule and inaugurating the dynasty of "Black Pharaohs".
- Taharqa (reigned c. 690–664 BCE) — Pharaoh of the 25th Dynasty. The most renowned Kushite pharaoh, who ruled over both Egypt and Kush at the dynasty's height before Assyrian invasions forced the Kushites south.

## The Christian Kingdoms of Nubia — c. 350 – c. 1500 CE

After Kush, three Christian kingdoms held the Nubian Nile for nearly a millennium, shielded from Muslim Egypt by a unique treaty of coexistence.

- 6th century CE: **Nubia converts to Christianity** — Nobatia, Makuria and Alodia adopt Christianity, partly through missions linked to the Byzantine court; the Nubian Nile becomes a Christian realm. [wiki-christian-nubia, ebsco-baqt]
- c. 652 CE: **The Baqt treaty** — After repelling an Arab invasion, Makuria and Muslim Egypt agree to the Baqt, a durable pact of non-aggression and regulated trade. [wiki-baqt, ebsco-baqt]
- 14th century: **Decline of Makuria** — Under Mamluk pressure and Arab settlement, the Christian kingdom of Makuria fragments and its capital at Dongola is lost. [wiki-christian-nubia]
- c. 1500–1504: **Fall of Alodia** — The last Christian Nubian kingdom, Alodia, collapses as new Muslim powers rise on the central Nile, ending Nubian Christianity's long ascendancy. [wiki-funj, wiki-christian-nubia]

## Islam, Sennar and Darfur — 1504 – 1820

Two Muslim sultanates — the Funj of Sennar and the Keira of Darfur — dominated the lands between the Nile and the deserts as Islam took root.

- 1504: **Funj Sultanate founded at Sennar** — Amara Dunqas establishes the Funj Sultanate, a Muslim confederation centred on Sennar on the Blue Nile. [wiki-funj, eb-sudan-funj]
- mid-17th century: **Sultanate of Darfur founded** — Sulayman Solong founds the Keira dynasty in the west; under Ahmad Bukr Islam becomes the state religion. [wiki-darfur]
- late 17th century: **Sennar at its height** — The Funj capital flourishes as one of the major trading cities of the African interior, dealing in gold, ivory and enslaved people. [wiki-funj]
- 1792: **Darfur's capital fixed at al-Fashir** — The Keira sultans establish al-Fashir as their capital; Darfur controls caravan routes across the Sahara. [wiki-darfur]

Figures:

- Amara Dunqas (reigned c. 1504 – c. 1533/4) — Founder of the Funj Sultanate. First ruler of the Funj Sultanate of Sennar, under whom the dynasty established itself on the Blue Nile and adopted Islam.

## Turco-Egyptian Rule and the Mahdist Revolt — 1820 – 1899

An Egyptian conquest founded Khartoum and bound the Sudan to Cairo — until a messianic revolt destroyed it, only to be crushed in turn at Omdurman.

- 1820–1821: **Turco-Egyptian conquest; Khartoum founded** — Muhammad Ali's armies conquer the Sudan; Khartoum is founded c. 1821 at the junction of the Blue and White Niles as the seat of Egyptian rule. [eb-sudan-turco, blackpast-khartoum]
- 1881: **Muhammad Ahmad proclaims himself the Mahdi** — A religious teacher declares himself the awaited redeemer and launches a holy war against Turco-Egyptian rule. [wiki-mahdist, eb-omdurman]
- 26 January 1885: **Fall of Khartoum; death of Gordon** — Mahdist forces storm Khartoum after a long siege and kill General Charles Gordon; the Mahdi dies months later. [wiki-mahdist, eb-siege-khartoum]
- 2 September 1898: **Battle of Omdurman** — Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian army destroys the Khalifa's forces with overwhelming firepower, ending the Mahdist state. [eb-omdurman, wiki-mahdist]
- 1899: **Anglo-Egyptian Condominium established** — Sudan is placed under joint British and Egyptian rule by the Condominium agreements of January and July 1899. [eb-condominium, wiki-anglo-egyptian]

Figures:

- Muhammad Ahmad (the Mahdi) (1844–1885) — Religious leader, founder of the Mahdist state. Sufi teacher who in 1881 declared himself the Mahdi and led the revolt that overthrew Turco-Egyptian rule and captured Khartoum in 1885.
- Charles George Gordon (1833–1885) — British general, governor-general of the Sudan. Sent to evacuate Khartoum, he was besieged and killed when the city fell to the Mahdists on 26 January 1885.

## Independence and the Long Civil Wars — 1899 – 2005

Independence in 1956 inaugurated a state at war with itself, as two generation-long conflicts between north and south consumed millions of lives.

- 1 January 1956: **Independence of the Sudan** — Sudan becomes a sovereign republic, ending Anglo-Egyptian rule; its parliament chooses independence over union with Egypt. [eb-condominium, wiki-sudan]
- 1955–1972: **First civil war** — Rebellion in the south, begun in 1955, grows into a 17-year war ended by the Addis Ababa Agreement of 27 February 1972, which grants southern autonomy. [wiki-first-civil-war, wiki-addis-ababa]
- 1983–2005: **Second civil war begins** — Nimeiri scraps southern autonomy and imposes sharia; the SPLM/A under John Garang launches a war that kills an estimated two million people. [encyclopedia-civil-wars, wiki-sudan]
- 1989: **Bashir seizes power** — Omar al-Bashir takes power in an Islamist-backed military coup, beginning a three-decade rule. [wiki-sudan]
- 2005: **Comprehensive Peace Agreement** — The 2005 accord ends the second civil war, grants the south autonomy, and promises a referendum on independence. [encyclopedia-civil-wars, wiki-sudan]

Figures:

- John Garang (1945–2005) — Founder of the SPLM/A; Sudanese vice-president. Led the southern Sudan People's Liberation Movement/Army through the second civil war and signed the 2005 peace deal, becoming national vice-president shortly before his death.

## Darfur, Secession and the War of the Generals — 2003 – present

The Darfur catastrophe, the loss of the south and a popular revolution gave way to a ruinous war between the army and a former militia.

- February 2003: **War in Darfur begins** — Rebel attacks meet a government-backed Janjaweed campaign; UN estimates put the toll at about 300,000 dead and some three million displaced. [wiki-darfur-war]
- 2009–2010: **ICC indicts Bashir** — The International Criminal Court issues warrants for President al-Bashir for war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide — the first against a sitting head of state. [icc-bashir, ushmm-bashir]
- 9 July 2011: **South Sudan secedes** — After a referendum, South Sudan becomes independent, taking most of the former Sudan's oil with it. [wiki-sudan, usip-south-sudan]
- 11 April 2019: **Revolution ousts Bashir** — Months of mass protests prompt the military to remove Omar al-Bashir after three decades in power. [wiki-2019-coup, hrw-bashir]
- 15 April 2023: **War between SAF and RSF** — Fighting erupts between the army under al-Burhan and the RSF under "Hemedti", spreading nationwide and causing mass death, displacement and famine. [wiki-sudan, wiki-darfur-war]

Figures:

- Omar al-Bashir (born 1944) — President of Sudan, 1989–2019. Seized power in 1989 and ruled until his overthrow in 2019; indicted by the ICC over the Darfur conflict, the first sitting head of state to be so charged.
