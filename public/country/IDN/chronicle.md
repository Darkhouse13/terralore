Founding: Proclamation of Independence — 17 Aug 1945. On the morning of 17 August 1945, days after Japan's surrender ended the Second World War, Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta proclaimed Indonesia an independent republic in Jakarta; the next day they were named president and vice-president. The Dutch sought to restore colonial rule, and only after the Indonesian National Revolution of 1945-1949 did the Netherlands formally recognise Indonesian sovereignty, transferring it on 27 December 1949.

Indonesia is a vast archipelago of thousands of islands whose history stretches from early Austronesian seafarers through the great Hindu-Buddhist maritime realms of Srivijaya and Majapahit, the coming of Islam and the spice-trade sultanates, and more than three centuries of Portuguese and Dutch colonial intrusion centred on the Dutch East India Company. A national awakening in the early twentieth century, accelerated by the Japanese occupation of 1942-45, culminated in the Proclamation of Independence on 17 August 1945 by Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta. After a four-year revolution the Netherlands recognised Indonesian sovereignty in December 1949. The republic passed through Sukarno's Guided Democracy, the violent birth of Suharto's New Order amid the mass killings of 1965-66, and since 1998 has built one of the world's largest democracies.

## Early Peoples and the Hindu-Buddhist Kingdoms: Srivijaya and Majapahit — c. 7th – 15th century CE

Indianised maritime kingdoms rose across Sumatra and Java, from the Buddhist trading thalassocracy of Srivijaya to the imperial reach of Majapahit, leaving monuments such as Borobudur and Prambanan.

- c. 671 CE: **Rise of the Srivijaya thalassocracy** — From its base at Palembang in Sumatra, the Buddhist kingdom of Srivijaya comes to dominate maritime trade through the Straits of Malacca and Sunda and becomes a renowned centre of Buddhist scholarship. [britannica-srivijaya]
- c. 780–850 CE: **Construction of Borobudur** — Under the Sailendra dynasty in central Java the great Buddhist monument of Borobudur is built; the world's largest Buddhist temple, it is today a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [worldhistory-borobudur, unesco-borobudur]
- 1293: **Founding of the Majapahit empire** — Raden Wijaya establishes Majapahit in eastern Java, which grows into the most powerful Hindu-Buddhist empire of the archipelago. [britannica-majapahit, wikipedia-majapahit]
- 1350–1389: **Majapahit's golden age under Hayam Wuruk** — Under King Hayam Wuruk and his prime minister Gajah Mada, Majapahit reaches its height, projecting power and trade across much of the archipelago. [britannica-majapahit]

Figures:

- Gajah Mada (died c. 1364) — Prime minister of Majapahit. The powerful mahapatih (chief minister) under whom Majapahit reached its greatest extent. Tradition holds that he vowed not to rest until he had unified the archipelago, and he became a lasting symbol of Indonesian unity.

## The Spread of Islam and the Spice-Trade Sultanates — 13th – 16th century

Carried largely by traders along the sea lanes, Islam spread through the archipelago, and as Majapahit faded a constellation of Muslim sultanates grew rich on the spice trade.

- late 13th c.: **Islam takes root in northern Sumatra** — The early Muslim sultanate of Samudera Pasai in northern Sumatra marks the establishment of Islam in the archipelago, spread by traders along the maritime routes. [britannica-history-indonesia, wikipedia-islam-spread]
- c. 1475: **Rise of the Demak Sultanate on Java** — Demak emerges as the first major Islamic state on Java and a base from which Islam spreads across the island as Majapahit's power collapses. [britannica-history-indonesia]
- 16th century: **Spice sultanates of Ternate, Tidore and Aceh flourish** — Muslim sultanates controlling the clove, nutmeg and pepper trade grow rich and powerful, making the archipelago the hub of a global spice economy. [britannica-history-indonesia, wikipedia-islam-spread]

## Portuguese and Dutch Colonialism and the VOC — 1511 – 1799

Lured by spices, Europeans seized the trade by force. After the Portuguese took Malacca, the Dutch East India Company built a commercial empire on monopoly, violence and the near-extermination of the Banda islanders.

- 1511: **Portuguese capture of Malacca** — Afonso de Albuquerque seizes the trading city of Malacca, beginning sustained European involvement in the archipelago's spice trade. [wikipedia-malacca-1511]
- 20 Mar 1602: **Founding of the Dutch East India Company (VOC)** — The Dutch Republic charters the VOC with sweeping powers to trade, wage war and govern, launching nearly two centuries of company rule in the Indies. [wikipedia-voc]
- 1619: **Founding of Batavia** — Governor-General Jan Pieterszoon Coen storms Jayakarta and builds Batavia (modern Jakarta) as the VOC's Asian headquarters. [wikipedia-voc]
- 1621: **The Banda massacre** — To enforce the nutmeg monopoly, Coen's forces kill, starve, enslave or deport almost the entire population of the Banda Islands, an atrocity that destroys Bandanese society. [wikipedia-banda]
- 31 Dec 1799: **Dissolution of the VOC** — Bankrupt after decades of war, debt and corruption, the VOC is dissolved and its possessions pass to the Dutch state, ushering in direct colonial rule. [wikipedia-voc]

Figures:

- Jan Pieterszoon Coen (1587–1629) — Governor-General of the VOC. Architect of Dutch power in the Indies who founded Batavia and directed the 1621 destruction of the Banda Islands' population to secure the nutmeg monopoly. He remains a deeply controversial figure remembered for ruthless violence in the service of commerce.

## The Dutch East Indies and the National Awakening — 1800 – 1942

Under direct Dutch rule the islands became the Netherlands East Indies, exploited through forced cultivation; from this colonial order rose, in the early twentieth century, the first stirrings of Indonesian nationalism.

- 1830: **The Cultivation System** — The Dutch impose the Cultuurstelsel, forcing Javanese peasants to grow export crops for the colonial state; it enriches the Netherlands while causing widespread hardship and local famines. [britannica-history-indonesia, wikipedia-national-awakening]
- 1908: **Founding of Budi Utomo** — The cultural society Budi Utomo is founded, widely regarded as the start of organised Indonesian nationalism and the National Awakening. [wikipedia-national-awakening, britannica-indonesia]
- 1927: **Sukarno founds the Indonesian National Party** — Sukarno establishes the PNI, demanding full independence and giving the nationalist movement a charismatic mass leader. [britannica-indonesia]
- 28 Oct 1928: **The Youth Pledge (Sumpah Pemuda)** — Young nationalists vow to recognise one Indonesian motherland, nation and language, a foundational moment in forging a unified national identity. [wikipedia-national-awakening, britannica-indonesia]

Figures:

- Sukarno (1901–1970) — Nationalist leader and future first president. Founder of the Indonesian National Party and the movement's pre-eminent orator. Repeatedly imprisoned and exiled by the Dutch, he articulated the ideology of a unified, independent Indonesia and would proclaim its independence in 1945.

## Japanese Occupation, Independence and the Revolution — 1942 – 1949

The Japanese occupation shattered Dutch prestige and armed a generation of nationalists; the Proclamation of 17 August 1945 launched a four-year revolution that ended in recognised independence.

- 1942: **Japanese occupation of the Indies** — Japan conquers the Dutch East Indies, ending Dutch rule; its occupation brings forced labour and hardship but also mobilises and arms Indonesian nationalists. [britannica-indonesia, wikipedia-japanese-occupation]
- 17 Aug 1945: **Proclamation of Independence** — Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta proclaim Indonesia an independent republic in Jakarta, days after Japan's surrender; they become president and vice-president the next day. [wikipedia-proclamation, ebsco-independence]
- 1945–1949: **The Indonesian National Revolution** — Indonesians wage a four-year armed and diplomatic struggle against Dutch attempts to reimpose colonial rule, enduring two major Dutch offensives and heavy losses. [ebsco-independence]
- 27 Dec 1949: **Recognition of sovereignty** — Following the Round Table Conference in The Hague, the Netherlands formally transfers sovereignty and recognises the independence of Indonesia. [ebsco-independence, wikipedia-proclamation]

Figures:

- Mohammad Hatta (1902–1980) — Co-proclaimer and first vice-president. A Dutch-educated economist and nationalist who co-signed the 1945 Proclamation alongside Sukarno and served as the republic's first vice-president. He was a leading advocate of cooperative economics and constitutional government.

## Sukarno, the New Order and Democratic Indonesia — 1949 – present

The young republic moved from Sukarno's Guided Democracy into the violent birth of Suharto's New Order amid the mass killings of 1965-66, and after three decades of authoritarian rule became, from 1998, a vigorous democracy.

- 1965–1966: **The anti-communist mass killings** — Following the 30 September coup attempt blamed on the PKI, an army-led purge kills an estimated 500,000 to over a million suspected communists and others, a vast atrocity that paves Suharto's path to power. [hrw-1965, wikipedia-killings-1965]
- 1968: **Suharto's New Order** — Suharto formally becomes president and rules for over three decades, delivering growth and stability through authoritarian control, military dominance and pervasive corruption. [britannica-indonesia, wikipedia-killings-1965]
- 21 May 1998: **Fall of Suharto and Reformasi** — Amid economic collapse and mass protests, Suharto resigns after 32 years in power, launching the Reformasi era of democratic reform and decentralisation. [wikipedia-fall-suharto, britannica-indonesia]
- 2004: **First direct presidential election** — Indonesians directly elect their president for the first time, consolidating one of the world's largest democracies after the authoritarian New Order. [britannica-indonesia]

Figures:

- Suharto (1921–2008) — Second president; leader of the New Order. The general who oversaw the 1965-66 mass killings and supplanted Sukarno, ruling Indonesia from 1967-68 until 1998. His New Order brought economic development but also authoritarianism, military repression and large-scale corruption.
