Founding: Declaration of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus — 15 November 1983. On 15 November 1983 the Turkish-Cypriot legislative assembly, under the leadership of Rauf Denktaş, issued a unilateral declaration of independence proclaiming the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus in the area held by Turkish forces since 1974. Turkish Cypriots presented the move as the assertion of their right to self-determination and security after years of intercommunal conflict and the failure of negotiations to restore a workable partnership. The United Nations Security Council responded three days later with Resolution 541 (1983), which considered the declaration legally invalid, called for its withdrawal, and called upon all states not to recognise any Cypriot state other than the Republic of Cyprus; Resolution 550 (1984) reaffirmed this position. To date only Turkey has recognised the TRNC, while the United Nations, the European Union, and the international community continue to regard the territory as part of the Republic of Cyprus that is, in their characterisation, under Turkish occupation.

Northern Cyprus, formally the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC), is a self-declared state occupying roughly the northern third of the island of Cyprus. Its emergence is rooted in the island's Ottoman period, which from 1571 established a Turkish-Cypriot Muslim community alongside the Greek-Cypriot majority, in British rule from 1878, and above all in the breakdown of the bicommunal Republic of Cyprus founded in 1960. After intercommunal violence from 1963, a 1974 coup by Greek-Cypriot nationalists backed by the military junta in Athens, and the Turkish military intervention that followed and partitioned the island, Turkish Cypriots formed a separate administration that on 15 November 1983 unilaterally declared independence. The United Nations Security Council, in Resolutions 541 (1983) and 550 (1984), declared that declaration legally invalid and called on states not to recognise it; only Turkey has done so, and the international community continues to regard the area as part of the Republic of Cyprus. The island remains divided by the UN-patrolled Green Line through Nicosia, the failed 2004 Annan Plan referendum the last major reunification attempt, with the dispute unresolved to the present.

## Ancient Cyprus and the Ottoman Inheritance — c. 1500 BCE - 1878 CE

A strategically placed eastern-Mediterranean island ruled in turn by many empires, Cyprus passed to the Ottomans in 1571; their three centuries of rule settled Anatolian Muslims and created the Turkish-Cypriot community that the island's modern history would centre upon.

- 1570-1571: **Ottoman conquest of Cyprus** — An Ottoman army took Cyprus from Venice, capturing Nicosia and Famagusta and beginning more than three centuries of Ottoman rule that settled a Muslim Turkish-Cypriot community on the island. [britannica-cyprus-ottoman, loc-cyprus-study]
- 16th-17th centuries: **Emergence of the Turkish-Cypriot community** — Muslim settlers, many from Anatolia, established a distinct Turkish-Cypriot community alongside the Greek-Cypriot majority, while the Orthodox Church regained authority over Greek Cypriots under the millet system. [britannica-cyprus-ottoman, loc-cyprus-study]

## British Rule and the Roots of Conflict — 1878 - 1960

Britain administered Cyprus from 1878 and annexed it in the First World War; under colonial rule Greek-Cypriot demands for enosis, union with Greece, and Turkish-Cypriot opposition to it hardened the communal divide that would define the island.

- 1878: **British administration of Cyprus begins** — Under the Cyprus Convention the Ottoman Empire transferred the administration of Cyprus to Britain, which annexed the island in 1914 and made it a Crown colony in 1925. [britannica-cyprus-history, loc-cyprus-study]
- 1955-1959: **EOKA campaign for enosis** — The guerrilla organisation EOKA, backed by Archbishop Makarios, fought British rule in pursuit of enosis (union with Greece); Turkish Cypriots opposed enosis and called for taksim (partition). [britannica-cyprus-history, britannica-makarios]
- February 1959: **Zürich and London agreements** — Britain, Greece, and Turkey, with Greek-Cypriot and Turkish-Cypriot leaders, agreed terms for an independent bicommunal republic, barring both enosis and partition and naming the three powers as guarantors. [britannica-cyprus-history, loc-cyprus-study]
- 1914: **Britain annexes Cyprus** — After more than three centuries of Ottoman rule and thirty-six years of British administration, Britain annexes the island outright on entering the First World War against the Ottoman Empire. [bbc-cyprus, britannica-cyprus-history]

## The Bicommunal Republic and Its Breakdown — 1960 - 1974

The Republic of Cyprus, born in 1960 with an intricate power-sharing constitution, soon foundered; intercommunal violence from 1963 brought UN peacekeepers in 1964 and drove Turkish Cypriots into armed enclaves, dividing the island in practice a decade before its formal partition.

- 16 August 1960: **Independence of the Republic of Cyprus** — Cyprus became an independent bicommunal republic under a power-sharing constitution, with Makarios III as president and Fazıl Küçük as Turkish-Cypriot vice-president, and Britain retaining sovereign base areas. [britannica-cyprus-history, loc-cyprus-study]
- December 1963: **Breakdown of power-sharing and intercommunal violence** — After President Makarios proposed constitutional amendments curtailing Turkish-Cypriot safeguards, the partnership collapsed and intercommunal violence broke out; Turkish Cypriots withdrew from the bicommunal state into enclaves. [britannica-cyprus-history, loc-cyprus-breakdown]
- 4 March 1964: **UN peacekeeping force (UNFICYP) established** — By Resolution 186 the UN Security Council created UNFICYP to prevent renewed fighting and restore normal conditions; the force has remained deployed in Cyprus ever since. [un-unficyp-mandate, unscr-186]

## The 1974 Coup and the Partition of the Island — 1974 - 1975

A coup by Greek-Cypriot nationalists backed by the junta in Athens overthrew Makarios in July 1974 and gave Turkey the occasion for a military intervention that the Turkish side calls a peace operation and the Republic of Cyprus calls an invasion; the island was divided, and a separate Turkish-Cypriot administration took shape.

- 15 July 1974: **Coup against Makarios** — Greek-Cypriot nationalists in the National Guard, backed by the military junta in Athens, overthrew President Makarios in pursuit of enosis and installed Nikos Sampson. [britannica-cyprus-history, usoh-cyprus-coup-1974]
- 20 July 1974: **Turkish military intervention** — Invoking its guarantor role under the 1960 treaty, Turkey landed forces in Cyprus; described by Turkey as a peace operation and by the Republic of Cyprus as an invasion, it ended with Turkish control of the northern third of the island. [britannica-cyprus-history, usoh-cyprus-coup-1974]
- 13 February 1975: **Turkish Federated State of Cyprus proclaimed** — The Turkish-Cypriot leadership under Rauf Denktaş proclaimed a 'Turkish Federated State of Cyprus' in the north, presented as a building block for a future federation rather than a separate sovereign state. [britannica-cyprus-history, loc-cyprus-study]
- 20 July 1974: **Security Council Resolution 353** — The Council calls on all states to respect the sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity of Cyprus, demands an immediate ceasefire and an end to foreign military intervention, and demands the withdrawal of foreign military personnel not authorised by international agreements. [unscr-353]

Figures:

- Makarios III (1913-1977) — Archbishop and first president of the Republic of Cyprus. Head of the Orthodox Church of Cyprus and a leader of the enosis movement, Makarios became the first president of the independent republic in 1960; he was overthrown in the 1974 coup but returned to office in the south before his death in 1977.

## The Declaration of Independence and Its Rejection — 1983 - 1990

On 15 November 1983 the Turkish-Cypriot leadership unilaterally declared the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus; the UN Security Council at once declared the act legally invalid, and only Turkey extended recognition, fixing the island's contested division in its present form.

- 15 November 1983: **Declaration of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus** — The Turkish-Cypriot assembly under Rauf Denktaş unilaterally declared the independent Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus in the island's north, presenting it as an act of self-determination. [britannica-cyprus-history, unscr-541]
- 18 November 1983: **UN Security Council Resolution 541** — The Security Council deplored the declaration, considered it legally invalid, called for its withdrawal, and called on all states not to recognise any Cypriot state other than the Republic of Cyprus. [unscr-541, un-digital-541]
- 11 May 1984: **UN Security Council Resolution 550** — The Security Council reaffirmed Resolution 541, condemned secessionist actions including the exchange of ambassadors with Turkey, and addressed the status of the fenced town of Varosha. [unscr-550]
- 1981: **The Committee on Missing Persons is established** — The leaders of the two communities set up a bicommunal committee, with United Nations participation, to recover and identify the remains of the missing — a joint list of 2,002 people, 492 Turkish Cypriots and 1,510 Greek Cypriots. [cmp-cyprus]

Figures:

- Rauf Denktaş (1924-2012) — Founding president of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus. A lawyer and the dominant Turkish-Cypriot political figure for half a century, Denktaş led the community through the conflicts of the 1960s and 1970s, headed the Turkish Federated State from 1975, proclaimed the TRNC in 1983, and served as its president until 2005, championing a two-state vision of the island.

## A Divided Island to the Present — 1990 - present

Repeated UN-led efforts to reunite the island, above all the Annan Plan put to referendums in 2004, have failed; Cyprus entered the European Union still divided, and the question of Northern Cyprus remains unresolved into the 2020s.

- April 2003: **Crossing points open along the Green Line** — For the first time in decades, crossing points in the UN buffer zone were opened, allowing movement between north and south and easing the communities' separation without settling the dispute. [britannica-cyprus-history, un-unficyp-mandate]
- 24 April 2004: **Annan Plan referendums fail** — The UN reunification plan was approved by a majority of Turkish Cypriots but rejected by a large majority of Greek Cypriots in simultaneous referendums, so it was not implemented. [britannica-cyprus-history]
- 1 May 2004: **Republic of Cyprus joins the European Union** — Cyprus acceded to the EU as a divided island; EU law applies in full only in the south and is suspended in the north pending a political settlement. [britannica-cyprus-history]
- 2017: **Crans-Montana talks collapse** — UN-brokered reunification negotiations at Crans-Montana ended without agreement, after which Turkish-Cypriot leaders increasingly pressed for a two-state solution rejected by the Republic of Cyprus and the UN. [britannica-cyprus-history, un-unficyp-mandate]
- 2001: **The peacekeeping mandate rolls on** — The Security Council renews a mission begun in 1964; some 2,400 peacekeepers patrol the buffer zone between the two communities. [bbc-cyprus, un-unficyp-mandate]
- 3 April 2008: **Ledra Street reopens** — The crossing in the heart of walled Nicosia, closed since 1964, is reopened — the most symbolically charged of the openings along the buffer zone. [bbc-cyprus]
- 2013: **The eurozone crisis reaches the island** — The Republic of Cyprus negotiates a deal to extricate its financial sector from the eurozone debt crisis; the north, outside the EU and using the Turkish lira, is affected differently. [bbc-cyprus]
- 2018: **New crossings at Deryneia and Lefke** — The first new buffer-zone crossings in eight years open in the east and the west of the island. [bbc-cyprus]
