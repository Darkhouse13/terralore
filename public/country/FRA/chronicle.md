# The Chronicle of France: From the forests of Gaul to the Fifth Republic

History of France: From the forests of Gaul to the Fifth Republic. 5 eras and 31 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Founding: Treaty of Verdun — 843 CE. The Treaty of Verdun split Charlemagne's empire among his grandsons; the western portion, West Francia, is the territorial and political ancestor of France. The realm was consolidated under Hugh Capet from 987.

France was not founded in a single moment but assembled over two thousand years — from Celtic Gaul and Roman province, through the Frankish realms that gave the country its name, the long consolidation of the Capetian kings, the Revolution that abolished the monarchy, and five republics punctuated by two empires. Few nations have rehearsed so many ideas of what a state can be.

## Gaul & Rome — c. 600 BCE – 486 CE

Before France there was Gaul — a patchwork of Celtic peoples drawn into the Mediterranean world, then conquered and Latinised by Rome.

- c. 600 BCE: **Foundation of Massalia (Marseille)** — Greek colonists from Phocaea establish a trading port — the oldest city in France. [eb-marseille]
- 52 BCE: **Siege of Alesia** — Caesar defeats Vercingetorix, completing the Roman conquest of Gaul. [eb-alesia, eb-vercingetorix]
- 50 BCE: **Gaul becomes Roman** — Gaul is incorporated into the Roman world; Latin, Roman law and cities reshape the land. [eb-france]

Figures:

- Vercingetorix (c. 82–46 BCE) — Chieftain of the Arverni. United Gallic tribes against Rome and became, much later, a national symbol of resistance.

## The Franks & the Birth of France — 486 – 987 CE

A Germanic people, the Franks, built a Christian kingdom that grew into Charlemagne's empire — and on its division, France was born.

- c. 496–508: **Baptism of Clovis I** — The first Frankish king to adopt Catholic Christianity, aligning crown and Church. [eb-clovis]
- 800: **Charlemagne crowned Emperor** — Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne in Rome, reviving the Western imperial title. [eb-charlemagne]
- 843: **Treaty of Verdun** — Charlemagne's empire is split; West Francia becomes the seed of France. [eb-verdun]
- 987: **Hugh Capet elected king** — Beginning of the Capetian dynasty and the long consolidation of the French crown. [eb-hugh-capet]

Figures:

- Clovis I (c. 466–511) — King of the Franks. United the Franks and converted to Catholicism, founding the Merovingian kingdom.
- Charlemagne (747–814) — King of the Franks, Emperor. Forged a Western European empire and a cultural revival; crowned emperor in 800.

## The Medieval & Early-Modern Kingdom — 987 – 1789

Eight centuries of Capetian and Bourbon kings turned a small royal domain around Paris into Europe's most powerful absolute monarchy.

- 1337: **Hundred Years' War begins** — A dynastic struggle with England that would last until 1453 and forge French identity. [eb-hundred-years]
- 1429: **Joan of Arc at Orléans** — Joan lifts the siege of Orléans, reversing English fortunes; she is executed in 1431. [eb-joan]
- 1598: **Edict of Nantes** — Henry IV grants toleration to Protestants, ending the Wars of Religion. [eb-nantes]
- 1643–1715: **Reign of Louis XIV** — Absolutism and the court of Versailles make France the dominant power in Europe. [eb-louis-xiv]
- 1643: **Cayenne settled** — A Rouen company's expedition under Charles Poncet de Brétigny buys the hill at the mouth of the Cayenne river from a local leader, Cépérou, and builds a fort. Dutch and English forces take the town several times before France regains it for good in 1664. [outremers360-cayenne]
- 11 April 1713: **Treaty of Utrecht names the “Japoc or Vincent Pinson”** — Article 8 fixes the France–Portugal frontier in Guiana on a river of that name. Brazil later argued it was the Oyapock and France that it was the Araguari, a disagreement settled by arbitration in 1900. [riaa-1900]
- 1763–1765: **The Kourou expedition** — After the Treaty of Paris, France sends thousands of settlers to the Kourou coast in French Guiana with little prepared for them. Disease kills a large share of them within about two years; sources differ on the totals. [acad-outremer-kourou, mariners-kourou]

Figures:

- Joan of Arc (c. 1412–1431) — Military leader, saint. A peasant girl whose visions and victories helped save the French crown; canonised in 1920.
- Louis XIV (1638–1715) — King of France. The 'Sun King', emblem of absolute monarchy and builder of Versailles.

## Revolution & Empire — 1789 – 1870

The Revolution destroyed the monarchy and proclaimed the rights of man; from its turmoil rose Napoleon, and then a century of contested regimes.

- 14 July 1789: **Storming of the Bastille** — The opening act of the French Revolution; later France's national day. [eb-revolution]
- Aug 1789: **Declaration of the Rights of Man** — Proclaims liberty, equality and popular sovereignty — a founding text of modern democracy. [eb-rights-of-man]
- 1804: **Napoleon crowned Emperor** — Bonaparte founds the First Empire and exports the Napoleonic Code across Europe. [eb-napoleon]
- 1815: **Battle of Waterloo** — Napoleon's final defeat ends the Empire and the Napoleonic Wars. [eb-napoleon]
- 1852–1953: **Penal colonies in French Guiana** — From 1852 France sends convicts to penal camps in French Guiana, including the Îles du Salut. A decree of 1938 ends transportation; the last return convoy leaves in 1953. The Archives nationales d’outre-mer database covers nearly 100,000 convicts sent to French Guiana or New Caledonia between 1852 and 1953. [anom-bagnards, guyane-transportation]

Figures:

- Napoleon Bonaparte (1769–1821) — General, Emperor. Soldier-statesman whose conquests and legal code reshaped Europe; Emperor 1804–1814/15.

## The Republics & the Modern Nation — 1870 – present

Through two world wars, decolonisation and European integration, France settled into a durable republican order — its Fifth, founded in 1958.

- 25 May 1891: **Russian arbitration on the Maroni frontier** — Emperor Alexander III of Russia, as arbitrator, rules that the Awa is the border river between French Guiana and Suriname; land upstream of its confluence with the Tapanahoni goes to the Netherlands. The award does not settle every question on the Suriname frontier. [riaa-1891]
- 1895–1899: **Dreyfus held on Devil’s Island** — Captain Alfred Dreyfus, convicted of treason in 1894, is transferred in April 1895 to Devil’s Island off French Guiana to serve his sentence. He leaves the island in 1899; the Court of Cassation declares him innocent in 1906. [lehavre-dreyfus]
- 1 December 1900: **Swiss arbitration fixes the Brazil–French Guiana border** — The Swiss Federal Council rules at Bern that the “Japoc or Vincent Pinson” of the Treaty of Utrecht is the Oyapock, as Brazil had argued, not the Araguari as France had. The border follows the Oyapock and, inland, the Tumuc-Humac watershed. [riaa-1900, senat-brazil-border]
- 1905: **Separation of Church and State** — The 1905 law establishes laïcité — France's distinctive secularism. [eb-france]
- Aug 1944: **Liberation of Paris** — Allied and Free French forces liberate Paris after four years of occupation. [eb-france]
- 19 March 1946: **French Guiana becomes a département** — A law of 19 March 1946, championed by deputies including Aimé Césaire and French Guiana’s Gaston Monnerville, makes Martinique, Guadeloupe, Réunion and French Guiana départements of France. [elysee-1946]
- 1958: **Fifth Republic founded** — De Gaulle's new constitution creates the strong presidency that governs France today. [eb-fifth-republic]
- 1957: **Founding member of the EEC** — France signs the Treaty of Rome, beginning European integration. [eb-france]
- 1964–1968: **Guiana Space Centre** — On 14 April 1964 Prime Minister Georges Pompidou announces French Guiana as the site of France’s new space centre, chosen from fourteen sites studied; a Véronique sounding rocket launched on 9 April 1968 makes it operational. The first Ariane flies from it in 1979. [esa-csg50]
- 2002: **The euro in French Guiana** — French Guiana, a département of France, uses the euro; the Banque de France’s subsidiary IEDOM acts as its central-bank delegate in overseas territories whose currency is the euro. [iedom]
- Article 349 TFEU: **French Guiana an EU outermost region** — Article 349 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union names French Guiana among the outermost regions, whose remoteness justifies specific measures in the application of EU law there. It is one of nine such regions. [eur-lex-349, eu-outermost]
- 2017: **Oyapock bridge opens** — The bridge across the Oyapock between Saint-Georges-de-l’Oyapock and Oiapoque, finished in 2011, opens in 2017. France and Brazil share a border of more than 730 km, which the Sénat describes as France’s longest land border. [senat-brazil-border]

Figures:

- Charles de Gaulle (1890–1970) — General, President. Led Free France in WWII and founded the Fifth Republic, dominating its first decade.
