# The Chronicle of Madagascar: From Austronesian outriggers to the island republics

History of Madagascar: From Austronesian outriggers to the island republics. 5 eras and 31 sourced events, every claim traceable to a named reference.

Canonical: https://terralore.co/country/MDG/chronicle
Updated: 2026-08-10

Founding: Independence from France — 26 June 1960. Madagascar became a sovereign state on 26 June 1960, ending more than six decades of French colonial rule. Philibert Tsiranana led the new republic as its first president.

Madagascar's history begins with one of the most remarkable journeys in human settlement: seafarers from the region of Borneo who crossed the open Indian Ocean and, mixing over centuries with Bantu Africans, became the Malagasy people. Coastal kingdoms and a famous pirate era gave way to the highland Merina monarchy, which unified much of the island before French conquest abolished it in 1897. Independence came on 26 June 1960, followed by republics marked by socialist experiment and recurring political crisis.

## Settlement & the Making of the Malagasy — c. 5th century CE – c. 1500

Seafarers from across the Indian Ocean settled an empty island and, blending with African arrivals, forged a wholly new people and language.

- c. 5th–7th century CE: **Austronesian settlement of Madagascar** — Seafarers from the Borneo region cross the Indian Ocean and become the island's first lasting inhabitants. [eb-madagascar, wiki-history, wiki-malagasy-people]
- 1st millennium CE: **Bantu African arrivals** — Migrants from the East African mainland mix with Austronesian settlers, forming the Malagasy people and language. [wiki-malagasy-people, wiki-history]
- c. 10th–12th century: **Swahili and Arab trade contacts** — Muslim Swahili traders (the Antalaotra) settle the northwest coast, linking Madagascar to Indian Ocean commerce. [wiki-antalaotra, unesco-silkroad]
- c. 11th century: **Bantu and Tamil arrivals deepen the mix** — Bantu-speaking migrants from southeast Africa arrive in numbers alongside Tamil merchants from southern India, drawing the island's population from three continents. [bbc-madagascar]

## Coastal Kingdoms & the Pirate Coast — c. 1500 – 1787

On the coasts rose the Sakalava and Betsimisaraka kingdoms, while European pirates made the eastern shore one of the most notorious havens in the world.

- early–mid 17th century: **Rise of the Sakalava kingdom** — The Maroseraña dynasty builds the island's largest realm on the west coast, later dividing into Menabe and Boina. [lumen-kingdoms, eb-madagascar]
- late 17th century: **Île Sainte-Marie pirate haven** — The northeastern island becomes a major base on the pirate 'Round'; legend later names it the site of Libertalia. [goldenage-saintemarie]
- c. 1712: **Ratsimilaho unites the Betsimisaraka** — Ratsimilaho forges the eastern peoples into the Betsimisaraka confederation, becoming a dominant king by 1730. [lumen-kingdoms]
- 16th–17th centuries: **Portuguese and French trading posts** — European powers establish coastal posts but never hold ground inland; the island's kingdoms keep the terms of trade. [bbc-madagascar]

## The Merina Monarchy — 1787 – 1861

From the central highlands, the Merina kings unified most of the island, opened it to Christianity and foreign powers — then violently turned away.

- 1787–1810: **Andrianampoinimerina unifies Imerina** — From Ambohimanga and Antananarivo, the king ends civil war and begins the conquest of much of the island. [wiki-andrianampoinimerina, eb-madagascar]
- 1810s–1820s: **Radama I, Britain and the missionaries** — Radama I is recognised King of Madagascar by Britain and admits the London Missionary Society, bringing literacy and Christianity. [wiki-radama, eb-madagascar, unesco-ambohimanga]
- 1818 onwards: **The London Missionary Society arrives** — LMS missionaries open schools, render Malagasy in the Latin alphabet and translate the Bible — literacy arrives with a creed attached. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1835: **Ranavalona I bans Christianity** — Fearing foreign influence, the queen proscribes Christian practice; persecution and executions follow over decades. [wiki-ranavalona1, eb-madagascar]

Figures:

- Andrianampoinimerina (c. 1745–1810) — King of Imerina. Reunified the highland Merina kingdom and set in motion the unification of Madagascar from Ambohimanga and Antananarivo.
- Ranavalona I (c. 1778–1861) — Queen of Madagascar. Defended Malagasy independence through isolation, ending the British alliance and persecuting the early Christian movement.

## French Conquest & Colony — 1861 – 1947

France ended the monarchy, exiled the last queen and ruled Madagascar as a colony — until a 1947 uprising met catastrophic repression.

- 1881: **Treaty of peace, friendship and commerce with the United States** — Following a commercial convention in 1867, Washington concludes a full treaty with the Kingdom of Madagascar — a sovereign state in the diplomatic record fifteen years before annexation. [statedept-madagascar]
- 1883–1885: **First Franco-Hova War** — France invades and takes Antsiranana (Diego Suarez), the deep northern harbour it had wanted for a generation. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1890: **Britain accepts a French protectorate** — London concedes the imposition of a protectorate over the island; the government in Antananarivo, never consulted, refuses to accept it. [bbc-madagascar, statedept-madagascar]
- 1895: **French capture Antananarivo** — Franco-Hova war ends with the fall of the capital; Madagascar is forced into a French protectorate. [eb-madagascar, wiki-ranavalona3]
- 1897: **Monarchy abolished, Ranavalona III exiled** — France ends the Merina monarchy and exiles the last queen, ultimately to Algiers, where she dies in 1917. [wiki-ranavalona3, eb-madagascar]
- 1896–1911: **The 'pacification' campaign** — The formal declaration of a colony opens fifteen years of French military operations against a resistance that never conceded the point. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1942: **The Battle of Madagascar** — British forces invade the Vichy-administered island to deny its harbours to the Japanese navy; the colonial administration's prestige does not recover. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1946: **Madagascar becomes a French overseas territory** — Reorganisation gives Malagasy deputies representation in Paris; its failure to deliver independence sends nationalists to arms the following year. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1947–1948: **The Malagasy Uprising** — A nationalist insurrection from March 1947 is suppressed with great loss of life; estimates range up to roughly 90,000 dead. [wiki-uprising, globalvoices-uprising]

Figures:

- Ranavalona III (1861–1917) — Last Queen of Madagascar. The final Merina sovereign; deposed by France in 1897 and exiled, dying in Algiers in 1917.

## Independence & the Republics — 1960 – present

Free from France in 1960, Madagascar passed through a socialist revolution and recurring political crises in search of stable democracy.

- 26 June 1960: **Independence from France** — Madagascar becomes a sovereign republic; Philibert Tsiranana is its first president. [eb-madagascar, aaregistry-independence, statedept-madagascar]
- 1972: **The rotaka topples Tsiranana** — Economic stagnation triggers mass protests; the first president hands power to a military council, ending the First Republic. [bbc-madagascar]
- 1975: **Ratsiraka and the socialist republic** — Didier Ratsiraka takes power, founds the Democratic Republic and nationalises the economy under the 'Red Book'. [conversation-ratsiraka, eb-madagascar]
- 2002: **Ravalomanana and the 2002 crisis** — A disputed election triggers months of confrontation; Marc Ravalomanana takes power and Ratsiraka flees. [eb-ravalomanana, reliefweb-timeline]
- 2009: **The 2009 coup** — Andry Rajoelina ousts Ravalomanana with military backing, in a transfer widely condemned as unconstitutional. [reliefweb-timeline, eb-ravalomanana]
- 1992: **The Third Republic constitution** — Democratic reforms end the socialist republic and usher in a new constitution and multiparty politics. [bbc-madagascar]
- 2007: **Rainforests of the Atsinanana inscribed** — Six eastern national parks — home to species found nowhere else after 60 million years of isolation — join the World Heritage List. [unesco-atsinanana]
- 2010: **The rainforests are listed in Danger** — Three years after inscription, illegal logging and lemur hunting put the Atsinanana on the List of World Heritage in Danger. [unesco-atsinanana]
- 2021: **Drought and famine in the Grand Sud** — The worst drought in four decades leaves over 1.14 million people food insecure and some 14,000 in IPC Phase 5 — catastrophe — in the island's far south. [un-madagascar-drought]
- November 2023: **Rajoelina wins a third term** — An election boycotted by ten rivals, preceded by six weeks of protest, returns Rajoelina on the lowest turnout in the country's history. [bbc-madagascar]

Figures:

- Didier Ratsiraka (1936–2021) — President of Madagascar. The 'Red Admiral' who led Madagascar's socialist Second Republic from 1975 and dominated its politics for decades.
