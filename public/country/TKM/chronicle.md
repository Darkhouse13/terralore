# The Chronicle of Turkmenistan: From the Bronze Age oases of Margiana and Parthian Nisa, through Silk Road Merv and the Turkmen tribes, to Russian conquest, Soviet cotton and a neutral, gas-rich republic.

Founding: Independence of Turkmenistan — 27 October 1991. Turkmenistan dates its modern statehood to 27 October 1991, when its Supreme Soviet declared independence as the Soviet Union dissolved, with Saparmurat Niyazov — formerly first secretary of the Turkmen Communist Party — as its first president. The land it inherited carries an extraordinary depth of history: the Bronze Age oasis civilisation of Margiana, centred on Gonur Depe; Old and New Nisa, an early capital of the Parthian Empire; and Merv, one of the greatest cities of the Silk Road, made a capital by the Seljuk Turks before the Mongols destroyed it in 1221. After the southward consolidation of the Turkmen tribes, the Russian Empire conquered the region following the Battle of Geok Tepe in 1881, and in 1924 the Soviet authorities created the Turkmen SSR. Independent Turkmenistan declared a permanent neutrality recognised by the United Nations in 1995.

The deserts and oases of Turkmenistan hold one of Central Asia's deepest pasts: the Bronze Age oasis civilisation of Margiana centred on Gonur Depe, the Parthian royal city of Nisa, and Merv — for centuries one of the great cities of the Silk Road and the medieval Islamic world, made a capital by the Seljuk Turks before its destruction by the Mongols in 1221. From the late medieval period the Turkmen tribes consolidated across the Karakum, until tsarist Russia conquered the region in the bloody Battle of Geok Tepe in 1881. The Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic, created in 1924, was reshaped by forced collectivisation, cotton monoculture and the Kara-Kum Canal, before declaring independence on 27 October 1991. Modern Turkmenistan — holding some of the world's largest natural-gas reserves and a UN-recognised permanent neutrality — passed from the personality-cult rule of Saparmurat Niyazov ('Türkmenbaşy') to Gurbanguly and then Serdar Berdimuhamedow.

## Ancient Oases: Margiana and Parthian Nisa — c. 2300 BCE – 224 CE

The oases of the Karakum gave rise to a Bronze Age civilisation at Gonur Depe and, later, to Nisa, a royal city of the Parthian Empire.

- c. 2300 BCE: **The Margiana oasis civilisation (BMAC)** — The Bronze Age Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex flourished in the southern Karakum oases, its Margiana centre at Gonur Depe — monumental architecture, irrigation farming and fine metalwork later excavated by Viktor Sarianidi. [britannica-turkmenistan, loc-turkmenistan]
- c. 247 BCE: **Rise of the Parthian Empire** — The nomadic Parni founded the Parthian state, which grew into an empire ruling Iran and Central Asia for nearly five centuries and rivalling Rome, with Nisa among its early royal centres. [britannica-parthia, whe-parthia]
- 2nd century BCE: **Royal city of Nisa** — Old and New Nisa, near modern Ashgabat, served as an early capital and royal residence of the Parthian Empire, with palaces, temples and treasuries that fused Hellenistic and Iranian art. [unesco-nisa, britannica-parthia]
- 2007: **Nisa inscribed by UNESCO** — The Parthian Fortresses of Nisa were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007 as an exceptional record of the Parthian civilisation at one of its earliest and most important cities. [unesco-nisa]

Figures:

- Viktor Sarianidi (1929–2013) — Soviet–Greek archaeologist. Excavated Gonur Depe from the 1970s and defined the Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex, revealing a previously unknown Bronze Age oasis civilisation in the Karakum.

## Merv: Silk Road City and Seljuk Capital — 224 – 1221

Under Sasanians, the Arab caliphate and especially the Seljuk Turks, Merv grew into one of the largest cities on Earth — a Silk Road metropolis of science and faith.

- c. 651 CE: **Merv under the Arab caliphate** — After the fall of Sasanian Persia, Merv became a key garrison and administrative centre of the Arab caliphate in Khorasan, a base for the spread of Islam and for campaigns further into Central Asia. [unesco-merv, britannica-turkmenistan]
- 1118–1157: **Merv as Seljuk capital under Sultan Sanjar** — The Great Seljuk sultan Sanjar made Merv a capital; the city's population swelled and it became one of the largest and most cultured cities in the world, a hub of Silk Road science and faith. [britannica-seljuq, unesco-merv]
- c. 1157: **Mausoleum of Sultan Sanjar** — Built at the death of Sultan Sanjar, the great domed mausoleum at the centre of Sultan Kala stands as the finest surviving monument of Seljuk Merv. [unesco-merv, britannica-seljuq]
- 1221: **Mongol destruction of Merv** — A Mongol army under Tolui, son of Genghis Khan, besieged Merv during the conquest of the Khwarazmian Empire and massacred its population, ending the city's age as a great metropolis of the Islamic world. [unesco-merv, britannica-mongol]

Figures:

- Sultan Sanjar (c. 1086–1157) — Great Seljuk sultan (d. 1157). The last powerful sultan of the Great Seljuk Empire, who ruled from Merv and presided over the city's golden age; his mausoleum is the supreme surviving monument of Seljuk power.

## The Turkmen Tribes and the Russian Conquest — 1221 – 1885

The Turkmen tribes consolidated across the deserts and oases, found their poet in Magtymguly, and fell to tsarist Russia in the bloodbath of Geok Tepe.

- 18th century: **Magtymguly Pyragy and Turkmen poetry** — The poet Magtymguly Pyragy (1724–c. 1807) wrote in the Turkmen language and called for the unity of the tribes; he is revered as the father of Turkmen literature and national identity. [britannica-turkmen-history, loc-turkmenistan]
- 1869: **Russians found Krasnovodsk** — A Russian military force landed on the eastern Caspian coast and founded the port of Krasnovodsk (now Türkmenbaşy), opening the conquest of the Transcaspian region. [britannica-turkmen-history]
- January 1881: **Battle of Geok Tepe** — General Mikhail Skobelev stormed the Teke Turkmen fortress of Geok Tepe by detonating a mine under its walls; the assault and ensuing massacre of thousands broke Turkmen resistance and secured Russian control of Transcaspia. [britannica-gok-tepe, britannica-turkmen-history]
- 1885: **Russian annexation completed** — Following Geok Tepe, the Transcaspian region was annexed as part of the Russian Empire, completing the conquest of the lands of modern Turkmenistan. [britannica-turkmen-history, loc-turkmenistan]

Figures:

- Magtymguly Pyragy (1724–c. 1807) — Turkmen poet (1724–c. 1807). The most revered figure in Turkmen literature, who shaped Turkmen as a written language and called for the unity of the tribes; a lasting symbol of national identity.

## Soviet Turkmenistan — 1885 – 1991

Russian Transcaspia became the Turkmen SSR, remade by forced collectivisation, cotton monoculture and the Kara-Kum Canal.

- 1924: **Creation of the Turkmen SSR** — In the Soviet national delimitation of Central Asia, the Transcaspian and Turkmen lands were combined to form the Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic, with Ashgabat as its capital. [loc-turkmenistan, britannica-turkmenistan]
- late 1920s–1930s: **Collectivisation and cotton** — Forced collectivisation seized herds and land and ended Turkmen pastoral nomadism amid fierce resistance, while Soviet planning imposed cotton monoculture across the republic's irrigated land. [loc-turkmenistan, britannica-turkmenistan]
- 6 October 1948: **Ashgabat earthquake** — A devastating earthquake struck Ashgabat, destroying the city and killing tens of thousands of people in one of the deadliest seismic disasters in Soviet history. [loc-turkmenistan, britannica-turkmenistan]
- begun 1954: **The Kara-Kum Canal** — Construction began on the Kara-Kum Canal, which carried water from the Amu Darya across the desert to irrigate the cotton lands — a vast project that opened new farmland at heavy environmental cost. [britannica-turkmenistan, loc-turkmenistan]

Figures:

- Saparmurat Niyazov (1940–2006) — First secretary, Communist Party of Turkmenistan (1985–1991). Orphaned after the 1948 Ashgabat earthquake, he rose through the Soviet apparatus to lead the Turkmen Communist Party from 1985, positioning himself to take the republic into independence.

## Independence and the Neutral Republic — 1991 – present

Independent Turkmenistan declared a UN-recognised neutrality and built vast gas wealth under the personality cult of Niyazov, then a dynastic succession.

- 27 October 1991: **Independence declared** — Turkmenistan declared independence from the Soviet Union, with Saparmurat Niyazov as its first president; the United States recognised the new state in December 1991 and opened relations in 1992. [history-state-tkm, britannica-turkmenistan]
- 12 December 1995: **Permanent neutrality recognised by the UN** — The UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 50/80 welcoming Turkmenistan's declared permanent neutrality, the basis of its non-aligned foreign policy and a status unique in being recognised by a UN resolution. [un-neutrality, britannica-turkmenistan]
- 1999: **Niyazov declared president for life** — Parliament declared Saparmurat Niyazov ('Türkmenbaşy') president for life, the apex of a personality cult that included renaming the calendar and making his Ruhnama compulsory reading. [britannica-niyazov]
- February 2007: **Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow elected** — Following Niyazov's death in December 2006, Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow was elected president, beginning fifteen years of rule that retained tight one-party control and the neutral, gas-export policy. [britannica-gurbanguly, britannica-turkmenistan]
- March 2022: **Serdar Berdimuhamedow succeeds his father** — In an early election, Serdar Berdimuhamedow won the presidency over 70 percent of the vote, succeeding his father in a managed dynastic transfer of power, with the elder Berdimuhamedow remaining head of the People's Council. [britannica-serdar, britannica-gurbanguly]

Figures:

- Saparmurat Niyazov ('Türkmenbaşy') (1940–2006) — First president of Turkmenistan (1991–2006). Ruled independent Turkmenistan as an authoritarian president for life, building an extensive personality cult around himself and his book the Ruhnama while resting power on the country's vast gas wealth.
- Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow (born 1957) — President of Turkmenistan (2007–2022). A former dentist and health minister who succeeded Niyazov, ruled for three terms with continued one-party control, and engineered the 2022 succession of his son before chairing the People's Council.
