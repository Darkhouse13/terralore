# The Chronicle of Cuba: From the Taino and the fortified key to the Indies to a sugar colony, a contested republic, and a socialist revolution at the edge of the Cold War.

Founding: Republic of Cuba proclaimed — 20 May 1902. On 20 May 1902 the United States relinquished its occupation authority and the Republic of Cuba was formally installed, with Tomas Estrada Palma as its first president. Independence followed the defeat of Spain in the Spanish-American War of 1898 and four years of U.S. military government. Sovereignty was qualified from the outset by the Platt Amendment, incorporated into the Cuban constitution, which reserved to the United States a right of intervention and the lease of a naval base at Guantanamo Bay; the amendment was abrogated in 1934.

Cuba's history begins with the Taino, the Arawakan people Columbus encountered in October 1492, and continues through nearly four centuries as a Spanish colony in which Havana became the fortified key to the Indies and the island the world's foremost producer of sugar, worked by enslaved Africans. Three wars of independence, from the Ten Years' War of 1868 to Jose Marti's rising of 1895, ended only with U.S. intervention in the Spanish-American War of 1898; the Republic of Cuba was proclaimed on 20 May 1902, its sovereignty constrained by the Platt Amendment. The republic's turbulent decades of Machado and Batista gave way to the revolution of 1959, when Fidel Castro's movement took power and turned the island toward socialism and alliance with the Soviet Union, drawing the U.S. embargo, the Bay of Pigs, and the missile crisis of 1962. After the hardship of the post-Soviet Special Period, a brief thaw with Washington in 2014-15 and the passing of leadership beyond the Castro family carried Cuba into the present.

## The Taino and the Spanish Conquest — Pre-1492 - c. 1550

The Arawakan Taino, the island's most numerous people, met Columbus in 1492; within two generations conquest, forced labour, and disease had all but destroyed them.

- 27 Oct 1492: **Columbus reaches Cuba** — On his first voyage Christopher Columbus anchors off the northern coast of Cuba and claims the island for Spain, his first encounter with the Taino there. [britannica-taino, loc-cuba-study]
- 1511 CE: **Spanish conquest begins** — Diego Velazquez leads the conquest of Cuba, founding Baracoa as the first Spanish settlement; further towns follow within a few years. [loc-cuba-study, britannica-cuba]
- by c. 1520-1550: **Collapse of the Taino** — Forced labour, starvation, and Old World disease all but extinguish the indigenous Taino, prompting the import of enslaved Africans. [britannica-taino]

## Spanish Colony: Havana, Sugar, and Slavery — 1519 - 1868

Havana, founded in 1519 and ringed with stone fortresses, became the fortified key to the Indies, while in the nineteenth century Cuba grew into the world's foremost sugar colony, worked by hundreds of thousands of enslaved Africans.

- 1519 CE: **Founding of Havana** — The Spanish found Havana, whose harbour becomes an obligatory stop on the route to the New World and the most heavily fortified port of the Caribbean. [unesco-havana]
- begun 1558: **The fortifications of Havana** — Construction of the Castillo de la Real Fuerza and, later, the Morro and La Cabana fortresses makes Havana the fortified key to the Indies. [unesco-havana]
- 19th c.: **Sugar-and-slavery boom** — Cuba becomes the world's leading sugar producer, importing over 600,000 enslaved Africans; by 1860 it makes nearly a third of the world's sugar. [britannica-sugar-slavery]

## The Wars of Independence and 1898 — 1868 - 1898

From the Grito de Yara of 1868 to Jose Marti's rising of 1895, Cubans fought three wars to end Spanish rule; the last drew in the United States and ended Spanish dominion in the Spanish-American War of 1898.

- 10 Oct 1868: **Grito de Yara** — Carlos Manuel de Cespedes frees his slaves and proclaims independence at La Demajagua, beginning the Ten Years' War against Spain. [britannica-cespedes, britannica-cuba]
- 1895 CE: **Marti's War of Independence** — Jose Marti orders the resumption of war and lands in Cuba, dying at Dos Rios on 19 May; the rising continues under Gomez and Maceo. [britannica-marti, loc-marti]
- 15 Feb 1898: **Sinking of the USS Maine** — The U.S. battleship Maine explodes in Havana harbour, propelling the United States toward war with Spain over Cuba. [statedept-spanamwar]
- 10 Dec 1898: **Spanish-American War ends Spanish rule** — After Spain's defeat, the Treaty of Paris ends Spanish dominion over Cuba; the Teller Amendment had disclaimed U.S. annexation. [statedept-spanamwar, archives-platt]

Figures:

- Carlos Manuel de Cespedes (1819-1874) — Planter and independence leader. Lawyer and sugar planter who freed his slaves and proclaimed Cuban independence in the Grito de Yara of 1868, launching the Ten Years' War. Deposed as president of the Republic in Arms and killed in 1874, he is revered as the father of the Cuban nation.
- Jose Marti (1853-1895) — Poet and apostle of independence. Poet, essayist, and revolutionary who organised and unified the movement for Cuban independence from exile. He resumed the war in 1895 and was killed at Dos Rios that May, becoming the symbol of Cuba's struggle for freedom.

## The Republic: Occupation, Platt, Machado, and Batista — 1898 - 1958

After four years of U.S. occupation the Republic of Cuba was proclaimed on 20 May 1902, its sovereignty constrained by the Platt Amendment; the decades that followed were marked by prosperity, dependence, and the dictatorships of Machado and Batista.

- 1901 CE: **The Platt Amendment** — The U.S. Congress conditions withdrawal on the Platt Amendment, reserving a right of intervention and a naval base at Guantanamo Bay; Cuba writes it into its constitution. [archives-platt, britannica-platt]
- 20 May 1902: **Republic of Cuba proclaimed** — The United States ends its occupation and the Republic of Cuba is installed under Tomas Estrada Palma, its first president. [statedept-cuba, britannica-estrada-palma]
- Aug 1933: **Fall of Machado** — Amid depression and unrest the army forces dictator Gerardo Machado to flee, opening the era of Fulgencio Batista. [britannica-cuban-revolution, britannica-republic-1902]
- Mar 1952: **Batista's coup** — Anticipating electoral defeat, Fulgencio Batista seizes power in a bloodless coup and rules as dictator, provoking armed opposition. [britannica-26-july, britannica-republic-1902]

Figures:

- Tomas Estrada Palma (1835-1908) — First president of Cuba. Veteran of the independence struggle who became the Republic of Cuba's first president in 1902. His contested re-election in 1905 led to revolt and a renewed U.S. intervention.
- Fulgencio Batista (1901-1973) — President and dictator. Soldier who dominated Cuban politics from the 1930s, serving as elected president from 1940 to 1944 and seizing power again by coup in 1952. His authoritarian, corruption-tainted regime was overthrown by Castro's revolution in 1959.

## The Revolution and the Cold War — 1959 - 1991

Fidel Castro's movement took power on 1 January 1959 and turned Cuba toward socialism and the Soviet Union, drawing the U.S. embargo, the Bay of Pigs, and the missile crisis that brought the world to the brink of nuclear war.

- 1 Jan 1959: **Triumph of the Revolution** — Batista flees and the 26th of July Movement takes power; Fidel Castro soon leads the new government, which turns toward socialism. [britannica-cuban-revolution, britannica-26-july]
- Apr 1961: **Bay of Pigs invasion** — A brigade of U.S.-backed Cuban exiles lands at the Bay of Pigs in an attempt to overthrow Castro and is defeated within days. [statedept-bayofpigs, britannica-bay-of-pigs]
- Feb 1962: **U.S. embargo made comprehensive** — President Kennedy expands the trade embargo to cover nearly all commerce with Cuba and makes it permanent. [statedept-bayofpigs]
- Oct 1962: **Cuban Missile Crisis** — Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba trigger a thirteen-day confrontation; a U.S. quarantine and a Soviet agreement to withdraw the missiles avert nuclear war. [statedept-missilecrisis, britannica-missile-crisis]

Figures:

- Fidel Castro (1926-2016) — Revolutionary and head of state. Leader of the 26th of July Movement who took power on 1 January 1959 and ruled Cuba for nearly half a century. He transformed the island into a one-party socialist state allied with the Soviet Union and defined Cuba's confrontation with the United States.
- Che Guevara (1928-1967) — Argentine revolutionary. Argentine-born physician turned guerrilla who was a leading figure of the Cuban Revolution and held senior posts in its government. Killed in Bolivia in 1967 while seeking to spread revolution, he became an enduring icon of the radical left.

## Special Period, Thaw, and the Present — 1991 - present

The collapse of the Soviet Union plunged Cuba into the austere Special Period; later came cautious reform, a brief thaw with the United States in 2014-15, and the passing of leadership beyond the Castro family.

- 1991 CE: **The Special Period begins** — The Soviet collapse ends Cuba's subsidies and trade; Castro declares a special period of rationing and austerity as GDP contracts sharply. [britannica-cuba-1991]
- 2008 CE: **Raul Castro assumes the presidency** — An ailing Fidel Castro hands power to his brother Raul, who introduces gradual economic reforms. [britannica-cuba-1991]
- 17 Dec 2014: **Thaw with the United States** — Obama and Raul Castro announce normalisation; diplomatic relations are restored and embassies reopen in 2015, though the embargo remains. [statedept-relations-2015, britannica-cuba-1991]
- 2018-2021: **Leadership beyond the Castros** — Miguel Diaz-Canel becomes president in 2018 and Communist Party leader in 2021, the first non-Castro to hold the top posts since 1959. [britannica-diaz-canel]

Figures:

- Raul Castro (born 1931) — Revolutionary and head of state. Fidel Castro's brother and comrade from the revolution who served as president from 2008 and led the Communist Party until 2021. He oversaw cautious economic reform and the 2014-15 opening to the United States.
- Miguel Diaz-Canel (born 1960) — President of Cuba. Politician who became president in 2018 and first secretary of the Communist Party in 2021, the first person not named Castro to hold Cuba's leading posts since the 1959 revolution.
