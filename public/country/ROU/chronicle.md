# The Chronicle of Romania: From Roman Dacia to the European Union — a Latin island in a Slavic and Magyar sea, unified, dismembered and remade.

Founding: The Union of the Principalities under Alexandru Ioan Cuza — 1859. Modern Romania was born from the union of the two Danubian principalities, Moldavia and Wallachia. On 5 January 1859 the elective assembly of Moldavia chose Alexandru Ioan Cuza as its prince, and on 24 January 1859 the assembly of Wallachia elected the very same man, creating a personal union that bound the two states into a single nation. Cuza set in motion a sweeping programme of modernisation — reform of the justice and fiscal systems, an agrarian reform that gave land to some 400,000 peasant families, the metric system and a French-modelled civil code — and the United Principalities increasingly used the name Romania. Though Cuza was forced to abdicate in 1866, the state he founded endured: its full independence was recognised by the Congress of Berlin in 1878, and it was proclaimed a kingdom in 1881.

Romania's history is the story of a Latin-speaking people forged on the lower Danube and repeatedly contested by its larger neighbours. Its origins reach back to the kingdom of the Dacians, conquered by the Roman emperor Trajan in 101–106 CE, whose colonists left a Romance language and a sense of 'Romanness' that survived the empire's withdrawal. In the fourteenth century two Romanian principalities, Wallachia and Moldavia, emerged south and east of the Carpathians, producing rulers such as Vlad III the Impaler and Stephen the Great before falling under centuries of Ottoman suzerainty and the venal Phanariot regime. The two principalities chose the same prince, Alexandru Ioan Cuza, in 1859, forming the modern state; full independence was recognised at the Congress of Berlin in 1878 and a kingdom proclaimed in 1881. The First World War brought 'Greater Romania', crowned by the union of Transylvania on 1 December 1918 and confirmed by the Treaty of Trianon in 1920. In 1940 the country was torn apart — Bessarabia and northern Bukovina seized by the USSR, northern Transylvania awarded to Hungary, southern Dobruja ceded to Bulgaria. After four decades of communism and the dictatorship of Nicolae Ceausescu, a violent revolution in 1989 toppled the regime, and a democratic Romania joined NATO in 2004 and the European Union in 2007.

## Dacia and Rome: the Latin foundation — c. 1st century BCE – 271 CE

A powerful Dacian kingdom in the Carpathian arc was conquered by Trajan, and Roman colonists implanted the Latin tongue that would become Romanian.

- 1st century BCE: **The Dacian kingdom** — The Dacians built a powerful kingdom in the Carpathian arc, fortified by stone strongholds in the Orastie Mountains with their capital at Sarmizegetusa Regia, the nucleus of Dacian power. [dacia-britannica, dacian-fortresses-unesco]
- 101–102 CE: **Trajan's first Dacian war** — The emperor Trajan invaded Dacia and forced King Decebalus to terms, installing a Roman garrison in the Dacian capital at Sarmizegetusa. [trajan-britannica, decebalus-britannica]
- 106 CE: **Conquest of Dacia and the Roman province** — In a second war Trajan subdued the whole country; Decebalus killed himself, and Dacia became a Roman province whose colonists made Latin its language, the root of Romanian. [decebalus-britannica, dacia-worldhistory, history-romania-britannica]
- 113 CE: **Trajan's Column commemorates the conquest** — The Dacian wars were commemorated in Rome on Trajan's Column, a 38-metre marble monument whose spiral frieze of more than 2,600 figures narrates the two campaigns against the Dacians. [trajans-column-worldhistory]
- 271–275 CE: **Aurelian abandons Dacia** — Under mounting frontier pressure the emperor Aurelian withdrew the Roman army and administration from Dacia, yet a Latin-speaking population persisted, carrying forward the Daco-Roman heritage. [dacia-worldhistory, dacia-britannica]

Figures:

- Decebalus (died 106 CE) — Last king of Dacia. The Dacian king who united his people and resisted Rome through two wars with Trajan. Defeated in 106 CE, he took his own life rather than be captured, and his kingdom became a Roman province.
- Trajan (53 – 117 CE) — Roman emperor. The emperor who conquered Dacia in the wars of 101–106 CE, annexing it as a province and settling colonists whose Latin speech became the foundation of the Romanian language.

## Wallachia, Moldavia and the Ottoman centuries — c. 1300 – 1821

Two Romanian principalities arose on either side of the Carpathians, produced warrior-princes who defied empires, then endured long Ottoman suzerainty and the Phanariot regime.

- 1330: **Basarab I founds Wallachia** — The voivode Basarab I defeated King Charles I of Hungary at Posada, winning recognition of Wallachia's autonomy and establishing the first Romanian principality. [walachia-britannica]
- c. 1359: **Bogdan I founds Moldavia** — The Vlach voivode Bogdan I crossed the Carpathians from Maramures and wrenched Moldavia from Hungarian vassalage, founding the second Romanian principality. [walachia-britannica]
- 10 Jan 1475: **Stephen the Great's victory at Vaslui** — Stephen the Great of Moldavia crushed a much larger Ottoman army at Vaslui, one of his celebrated victories in a long resistance to the Turks that won him renown across Christian Europe. [stephen-britannica, moldavia-churches-unesco]
- c. 1456–1462: **Vlad III the Impaler resists the Ottomans** — Vlad III of Wallachia, remembered for impaling his enemies, fought a savage campaign against Ottoman power; his reputation later inspired the literary figure of Dracula. [vlad-britannica]
- 1711–1821: **The Phanariot regime** — Under Ottoman suzerainty the Porte appointed the princes of Wallachia and Moldavia from among the Greek Phanariot families of Constantinople, a period of heavy tribute and misrule that lasted until 1821. [walachia-britannica, history-romania-britannica]
- 1690: **Horezu Monastery and Brancovan art** — Prince Constantin Brancoveanu founded the monastery of Horezu in Wallachia, a masterpiece of the 'Brancovan' style whose school of mural and icon painting influenced religious art across the Balkans. [horezu-unesco]

Figures:

- Stephen the Great (c. 1433 – 1504) — Prince of Moldavia. Prince of Moldavia from 1457 to 1504, Stephen won European renown for his resistance to the Ottoman Turks, defeating a great Ottoman army at Vaslui in 1475 and endowing the monasteries of northern Moldavia.
- Vlad III the Impaler (1431 – 1476/77) — Voivode of Wallachia. A fifteenth-century prince of Wallachia whose ferocious resistance to the Ottomans and punishment of enemies by impalement earned him the name Vlad Tepes; his legend later shaped Bram Stoker's Dracula.

## Union, independence and the Kingdom of Romania — 1859 – 1914

The two principalities chose a single prince, won recognition of full independence at the Congress of Berlin and became a European kingdom.

- Jan 1859: **Union of the Principalities** — Moldavia (5 January) and Wallachia (24 January) elected Alexandru Ioan Cuza as their common prince, uniting the two principalities and founding the modern Romanian state. [union-actmedia]
- 1864: **Cuza's reforms** — Prince Cuza carried through agrarian reform giving land to some 400,000 peasant families, reformed law and taxation, introduced the metric system and a French-modelled civil code. [union-actmedia]
- 1878: **Independence recognised at the Congress of Berlin** — After fighting in the Russo-Turkish War, Romania's full independence was recognised by the great powers at the Congress of Berlin in 1878, though it ceded southern Bessarabia to Russia. [carol-encyclopedia, union-actmedia]
- 1881: **Kingdom of Romania proclaimed** — Romania was proclaimed a kingdom and Carol I, prince since 1866, became its first king, completing the country's emergence as a sovereign European state. [carol-encyclopedia]

Figures:

- Alexandru Ioan Cuza (1820 – 1873) — First prince of united Romania. Elected prince of both Moldavia and Wallachia in 1859, Cuza united the principalities and drove a programme of reform — land to the peasants, a modern civil code and the metric system — before being forced to abdicate in 1866.
- Carol I (1839 – 1914) — First king of Romania. Prince from 1866 and first king from 1881, the Hohenzollern Carol I led Romania to independence in the war of 1877–78, secured its recognition at the Congress of Berlin and modernised the new kingdom over a long reign.

## Greater Romania: the Great War and the union of Transylvania — 1914 – 1938

The First World War doubled Romania's size, uniting Transylvania, Bessarabia and Bukovina in 'Greater Romania' and confirming the transfer of Transylvania from Hungary at Trianon.

- 1916: **Romania enters the First World War** — Romania joined the Allies in 1916 seeking the Romanian lands of Austria-Hungary, especially Transylvania; despite early defeats, the collapse of the empires opened the way to union. [trianon-britannica]
- 1 Dec 1918: **Union of Transylvania with Romania** — A great national assembly of Romanians at Alba Iulia proclaimed the union of Transylvania with the Kingdom of Romania, the central act in the creation of Greater Romania. [trianon-britannica]
- 1918: **Greater Romania** — With Bessarabia, Bukovina and Transylvania joined to the kingdom, Romania nearly doubled in size, gaining large Hungarian and German minorities within its enlarged borders. [trianon-britannica, ushmm-romania]
- 4 Jun 1920: **Treaty of Trianon** — The Treaty of Trianon formally transferred Transylvania and part of the Banat from Hungary to Romania, stripping Hungary of about two-thirds of its territory and leaving a lasting dispute. [trianon-britannica]

Figures:

- Ferdinand I (1865 – 1927) — King of Greater Romania. King during the First World War, Ferdinand I led Romania into the Allied camp and presided over the union of Transylvania, Bessarabia and Bukovina, becoming the monarch of 'Greater Romania'.
- Queen Marie of Romania (1875 – 1938) — Queen consort and wartime advocate. Wife of Ferdinand I, Marie became a celebrated symbol of Romania during the First World War and lobbied the Allied powers at the Paris peace talks for recognition of Greater Romania's enlarged borders.

## Dismemberment, the Second World War and communist rule — 1938 – 1989

In 1940 Romania lost a third of its land to its neighbours; war, the Axis and a Soviet-backed takeover followed, ending in the dictatorship of Nicolae Ceausescu.

- 28 Jun 1940: **Soviet seizure of Bessarabia and northern Bukovina** — The Soviet Union demanded and took Bessarabia and northern Bukovina from Romania, the first of the 1940 territorial losses; Bessarabia, largely Romanian-speaking, became the core of the later Republic of Moldova. [ushmm-romania, moldova-romania-origins]
- 30 Aug 1940: **Second Vienna Award and Southern Dobruja** — Under German and Italian arbitration Romania ceded northern Transylvania to Hungary; in September southern Dobruja was ceded to Bulgaria, costing Romania about 30% of its territory and people. [ushmm-romania]
- 20 Nov 1940: **Romania joins the Axis** — After the loss of King Carol II's throne, General Ion Antonescu took power and brought Romania into the Axis, later joining the war against the Soviet Union and presiding over the murder of hundreds of thousands of Jews and Roma. [ushmm-romania, antonescu-britannica]
- 1947: **Communist takeover and the People's Republic** — After the war Romania was drawn into the Soviet bloc; the Paris treaty restored northern Transylvania, but Bessarabia stayed Soviet, the monarchy was abolished and a communist People's Republic proclaimed. [ushmm-romania, ceausescu-ebsco]
- 1965–1989: **The Ceausescu dictatorship** — Nicolae Ceausescu ruled Romania from 1965, pursuing a foreign policy independent of Moscow while imposing a personality cult, pervasive secret-police control and harsh 1980s austerity. [ceausescu-ebsco]

Figures:

- Michael I (1921 – 2017) — King of Romania. Romania's last king, Michael I led the August 1944 coup that overthrew Antonescu and brought Romania over to the Allies. He was forced to abdicate by the communists in 1947.
- Nicolae Ceausescu (1918 – 1989) — Communist dictator. Leader of communist Romania from 1965, Ceausescu built an autocratic regime around a cult of personality and harsh austerity. He and his wife Elena were overthrown and executed in the revolution of December 1989.

## Revolution, democracy and the European turn — 1989 – present

A violent revolution ended the Ceausescu dictatorship, and a democratic Romania joined NATO and the European Union, settling its Black Sea boundary with Ukraine at the world court.

- Dec 1989: **The Romanian Revolution** — Protests beginning in Timisoara spread to Bucharest; the army turned against the regime, the Ceausescus fled on 22 December and were executed on 25 December 1989, ending communist rule. [ceausescu-ebsco]
- 29 Mar 2004: **Romania joins NATO** — Romania formally joined NATO on 29 March 2004, depositing its instrument of accession in Washington alongside six other new members in the alliance's largest round of enlargement. [nato-2004]
- 1 Jan 2007: **Accession to the European Union** — On 1 January 2007 Romania, with Bulgaria, joined the European Union, raising it to twenty-seven member states and completing Romania's integration into the West. [eu-ecb-2007]
- 27 Aug 1991: **Moldova's independence and the Romanian bond** — As the Soviet Union dissolved, the former Bessarabia declared independence as the Republic of Moldova, immediately recognised by Romania, with which it shares the Romanian language and a common history. [moldova-britannica, moldova-romania-origins]
- 3 Feb 2009: **ICJ Black Sea boundary with Ukraine** — The International Court of Justice fixed the Romania–Ukraine maritime boundary in the Black Sea, ruling that Ukraine's Serpents' Island would have no effect beyond its 12-mile territorial sea. [icj-black-sea]

Figures:

- Ion Iliescu (born 1930) — First post-communist president. A former communist official who led the National Salvation Front that took power in December 1989, Iliescu became Romania's first post-communist president and a dominant figure of the 1990s transition.
- Klaus Iohannis (born 1959) — President of Romania. An ethnic German from Transylvania and former mayor of Sibiu, Iohannis served as president from 2014, embodying Romania's anchoring in the European Union and NATO and its multi-ethnic Transylvanian heritage.
