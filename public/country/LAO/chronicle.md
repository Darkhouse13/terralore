# The Chronicle of Laos: The Land of a Million Elephants, from the Mekong kingdom of Lan Xang through French Indochina and the most bombed nation on earth to a one-party socialist republic.

Founding: Franco-Lao Treaty of Amity and Association — 22 October 1953. On 22 October 1953 the Franco-Lao Treaty of Amity and Association transferred the last French powers to the Royal Lao Government, recognizing Laos as a fully independent and sovereign state. The act completed a path begun within the French Union, after Laos had been bound since 1893 into the colonial federation of French Indochina. Sovereignty did not bring peace: a three-cornered struggle among royalists, neutralists, and the communist Pathet Lao plunged the kingdom into civil war, compounded by the covert American bombing campaign of the wider Indochina conflict, until the monarchy itself was abolished and the Lao People's Democratic Republic proclaimed on 2 December 1975.

Laos is a landlocked Southeast Asian nation whose history flows along the Mekong River, settled by Tai-Lao peoples amid the older Mon and Khmer civilizations of the region. In 1353 the warrior-prince Fa Ngum unified the Lao principalities into the kingdom of Lan Xang, the "million elephants," which embraced Theravada Buddhism and reached its height before fracturing in the early 18th century into the rival kingdoms of Luang Prabang, Vientiane, and Champasak, all soon falling under Siamese suzerainty. France absorbed the Lao lands east of the Mekong into a protectorate from 1893 as part of French Indochina, and Laos achieved full sovereignty by the Franco-Lao Treaty of 22 October 1953. Decades of civil war and the covert American bombing of the "Secret War" ended only when the communist Pathet Lao abolished the monarchy and proclaimed the Lao People's Democratic Republic on 2 December 1975, the one-party socialist state that, opening cautiously to markets and regional trade, governs Laos today.

## Mekong Settlement and the Older Civilizations — 5th century - 1353 CE

Long before the Lao kingdoms, the middle Mekong was shaped by Mon and Khmer civilization; into this world Tai-Lao migrants descended from the north to settle the river valleys and uplands.

- from the 5th century CE: **The sacred landscape of Vat Phou** — Around the Champasak plain in the south, a planned Hindu landscape develops around the Vat Phou temple, growing over a millennium largely under Khmer influence; it is now a UNESCO World Heritage site. [unesco-vat-phou]
- 1st millennium CE: **Tai-Lao settlement of the Mekong** — Tai-speaking peoples migrating southward settle the middle Mekong valley, organizing into numerous small principalities (muang) such as Muang Swa, the future Luang Prabang. [laos-history, lan-xang]

## The Kingdom of Lan Xang — 1353 - 1707 CE

Fa Ngum forged the scattered Lao principalities into Lan Xang, the Land of a Million Elephants, a Theravada Buddhist kingdom that reached its golden age under Setthathirath before fracturing into rival states.

- 1353 CE: **Fa Ngum founds Lan Xang** — Fa Ngum unifies the Lao principalities of the Mekong by conquest and proclaims the kingdom of Lan Xang, the "million elephants," with its capital at Muang Swa (Luang Prabang). [fa-ngum, lan-xang]
- from the mid-14th century: **Theravada Buddhism established** — During Fa Ngum's reign Theravada Buddhism of the Sinhalese school is introduced into the Lao kingdom, likely from Cambodia, becoming the foundational religion of Laos. [fa-ngum, lan-xang]
- 1560 CE: **Setthathirath moves the capital to Vientiane** — Facing Burmese pressure, King Setthathirath transfers his capital from Luang Prabang to Vientiane, brings the Emerald Buddha, and builds the great stupa of That Luang. [setthathirath, vientiane]
- 1707 CE: **Lan Xang splits apart** — After a disputed succession, the kingdom of Lan Xang divides in 1707 into the separate kingdoms of Luang Prabang and Vientiane, ending its unity. [sai-ong-hue, lan-xang]

Figures:

- Fa Ngum (1316-1374 CE) — Founder and first king of Lan Xang. Raised at the Khmer court, he returned to conquer and unite the Lao principalities of the Mekong, proclaiming the kingdom of Lan Xang in 1353 and bringing Theravada Buddhism to the Lao.
- Setthathirath (1534-1571 CE) — King of Lan Xang. Reigning at the kingdom's height, he moved the capital to Vientiane in 1560, repelled two Burmese invasions, and built the That Luang stupa and other monuments that remain Lao national symbols.

## Three Kingdoms and Siamese Vassalage — 1707 - 1893 CE

Fractured into the rival realms of Luang Prabang, Vientiane, and Champasak, the Lao kingdoms slipped into the orbit of Siam, whose armies sacked Vientiane and reduced the others to tributaries.

- 1713 CE: **Champasak secedes** — The far south breaks away to form the kingdom of Champasak, completing the fragmentation of Lan Xang into three rival Lao kingdoms. [sai-ong-hue, laos-history]
- later 18th century: **The Lao kingdoms become Siamese vassals** — Divided and weakened, the rulers of Luang Prabang, Vientiane, and Champasak each fall under a Siamese commissioner and must pay tribute to Bangkok. [lan-xang, laos-history]
- 1828 CE: **Siam destroys Vientiane** — After King Anouvong's failed revolt against Bangkok, Siamese forces sack and raze Vientiane and deport its people, ending the kingdom of Vientiane. [vientiane, laos-history]

Figures:

- Auguste Pavie (1847-1925) — French explorer and diplomat. As vice-consul in Luang Prabang and then consul-general in Bangkok, he won Lao rulers to France and engineered the claim that brought the Lao lands east of the Mekong under French protection in 1893.

## The French Protectorate — 1893 - 1953 CE

From 1893 France absorbed the Lao lands east of the Mekong into Indochina; half a century of colonial rule, Japanese wartime occupation, and a nationalist awakening led at last to full independence in 1953.

- 1893 CE: **French protectorate established** — Following the Franco-Siamese crisis, Siam cedes the territory east of the Mekong and France makes the Lao states a protectorate within French Indochina, administered from Vientiane. [pavie, laos-foreign-rule]
- March-October 1945: **Japanese occupation and the Lao Issara** — Japan ousts the French administration in March 1945; after Japan's surrender, the nationalist Lao Issara (Free Lao) forms a provisional government to resist the return of French rule. [lao-issara, laos-foreign-rule]
- 1950 CE: **Founding of the Pathet Lao** — Prince Souphanouvong breaks with the Lao Issara to ally with the Viet Minh and proclaims the communist-oriented Pathet Lao ("Land of the Lao"), the future ruling movement. [souphanouvong, pathet-lao]
- 22 October 1953: **Full independence from France** — The Franco-Lao Treaty of Amity and Association transfers the last French powers to the Royal Lao Government, recognizing Laos as a fully independent and sovereign state. [laos-foreign-rule, history-state-laos]

Figures:

- Souphanouvong (1909-1995) — Leader of the Pathet Lao, the "Red Prince". A prince of Luang Prabang and half-brother of the neutralist premier Souvanna Phouma, he allied with the Vietnamese communists, founded the Pathet Lao in 1950, and became the first president of the Lao People's Democratic Republic in 1975.

## Civil War, the Secret War, and the Pathet Lao — 1953 - 1975 CE

Independence dissolved into a three-cornered civil war that drew in the superpowers; for nearly a decade American aircraft made Laos the most heavily bombed country in history before the Pathet Lao prevailed.

- 1962 CE: **Geneva agreement on Lao neutrality** — An international agreement declares Laos neutral and provides for a coalition government, but the country remains a contested corridor of the Vietnam War. [laos-history, indochina-wars]
- 1964-1973: **The Secret War bombing** — The United States drops more than two million tons of bombs on Laos to cut the Ho Chi Minh Trail, making it the most heavily bombed country per capita; vast unexploded ordnance remains a deadly legacy. [secret-war-loc, uxo-state, indochina-wars]
- 1973 CE: **Paris accords and U.S. withdrawal** — The Paris Peace Accords end direct American military involvement in Indochina, leaving the Lao factions to settle the civil war among themselves. [indochina-wars, laos-history]
- 2 December 1975: **Founding of the Lao PDR** — The Pathet Lao take power, abolish the 600-year-old monarchy, and proclaim the Lao People's Democratic Republic, with Souphanouvong as president and Kaysone Phomvihane as prime minister. [laos-history, kaysone, history-state-laos]

Figures:

- Kaysone Phomvihane (1920-1992) — Founding leader of the Lao PDR. Co-founder and general secretary of the Lao People's Revolutionary Party, he led the Pathet Lao to victory and became the first prime minister of the Lao People's Democratic Republic in 1975, dominating its politics until his death.
- Souvanna Phouma (1901-1984) — Neutralist prime minister of the Kingdom of Laos. Repeatedly prime minister between 1951 and 1975, the half-brother of Souphanouvong led the neutralist faction and sought, ultimately in vain, to hold a divided Laos together amid superpower rivalry.

## The Lao People's Democratic Republic — 1975 - present

A one-party socialist state closely tied to Vietnam, the Lao PDR turned from rigid central planning toward markets and regional integration, betting its future on hydropower, mining, and a new railway to China.

- 1986 CE: **New Economic Mechanism reforms** — Facing the failure of central planning, the ruling party launches market-oriented reforms, legalizing private enterprise and inviting foreign investment, echoing Vietnam's Doi Moi. [laos-history, worldbank-lao]
- 1995 CE: **Luang Prabang inscribed by UNESCO** — The former royal capital of Luang Prabang, a fusion of Lao and French colonial architecture at the meeting of the Mekong and Nam Khan, is added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. [unesco-luang-prabang]
- 23 July 1997: **Laos joins ASEAN** — The Lao PDR is admitted to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, cementing its integration into the region after decades of isolation. [asean-lao]
- December 2021: **Laos-China Railway opens** — The 1,000-km railway linking Vientiane to Kunming begins operating, aiming to turn landlocked Laos into a land-linked hub of regional trade while deepening its debt to China. [worldbank-lao]

Figures:

- Kaysone Phomvihane (1920-1992) — First prime minister and later president of the Lao PDR. The dominant figure of the republic's first generation, he kept Laos closely allied with Vietnam and, in his final years, opened the economy through the New Economic Mechanism before his death in 1992.
