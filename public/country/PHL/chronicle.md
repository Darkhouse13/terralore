Founding: Independence of the Republic of the Philippines — 4 Jul 1946. On 4 July 1946 the United States formally relinquished sovereignty over the Philippines and recognised the independence of the Republic of the Philippines under the Treaty of Manila, with Manuel Roxas as its first president. Filipinos count their independence from the 12 June 1898 declaration by Emilio Aguinaldo, and in 1962 moved the national Independence Day to that date; but full sovereignty came in 1946, after Spanish colonial rule, the Philippine-American War, the American period and the devastation of the Japanese occupation.

The Philippines is an archipelago of more than seven thousand islands whose history runs from Austronesian seafarers and the autonomous barangays of the datus, through the Indianised polities and the Islamic sultanates of Sulu and Maguindanao, into three and a half centuries of Spanish rule begun by Legazpi in 1565 and bound to the world by the Manila-Acapulco galleon trade. A national awakening led by Jose Rizal and the Katipunan of Andres Bonifacio erupted in revolution in 1896, and on 12 June 1898 Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed independence, only for Spain to cede the islands to the United States. After a brutal Philippine-American War, an American colonial period and the Japanese occupation, the Republic of the Philippines won full independence on 4 July 1946. The young democracy endured the Marcos dictatorship and martial law until the peaceful People Power Revolution of 1986 restored it, and the modern republic endures to 2026.

## Barangays, Indianised Polities and the Islamic Sultanates — c. 900 - 1565

Before Spanish ships arrived, the archipelago was a mosaic of autonomous barangays led by datus, linked into a wider Southeast Asian maritime world, with powerful Islamic sultanates rising in the south.

- 900 CE: **The Laguna Copperplate Inscription** — Dated to 900 CE, the earliest known Philippine document records a debt remission and reveals an organised society of datus and nobles already linked to the wider Malay and Javanese world. [britannica-philippines]
- mid-15th c.: **Founding of the Sultanate of Sulu** — Islam takes political form as the Sultanate of Sulu rises around Jolo, becoming a powerful maritime state controlling trade across the Sulu Sea and toward Borneo. [britannica-sulu]
- c. 1515: **Islam consolidates in Maguindanao** — The conversion of ruling families on Mindanao, associated with the missionary Sharif Muhammad Kabungsuwan, founds the Sultanate of Maguindanao, which expands through the 16th and 17th centuries. [britannica-maguindanao]

Figures:

- Sultan Kudarat (reigned c. 1619-1671) — Sultan of Maguindanao. The ruler under whom the Sultanate of Maguindanao reached the summit of its strength and influence on Mindanao, leading sustained resistance to Spanish encroachment in the south.

## Spanish Colonisation, Catholicism and the Galleon Trade — 1565 - 1815

From Legazpi's landing in 1565 Spain bound the archipelago into its empire, ruling for three centuries through the Church and the Manila-Acapulco galleon, the lifeline that linked Asia to the Americas.

- 1565: **Legazpi founds the first Spanish settlement at Cebu** — Miguel Lopez de Legazpi establishes Spain's first permanent settlement in the islands at Cebu, beginning more than three centuries of Spanish colonial rule. [britannica-spanish-period]
- 1571: **Founding of Manila** — Legazpi establishes Manila as the capital of the Spanish colony; by the century's end most lowland Luzon and the Visayas are under Spanish control. [britannica-spanish-period]
- 1565-1815: **The Manila-Acapulco galleon trade** — For 250 years Spanish galleons exchange Chinese silks for Mexican silver across the Pacific, making Manila a hub of global commerce and an economic lifeline for the colony. [britannica-galleon]

Figures:

- Miguel Lopez de Legazpi (c. 1502-1572) — Spanish conquistador and first governor-general. Led the expedition that founded the first permanent Spanish settlement at Cebu in 1565 and the capital of Manila in 1571, establishing Spanish rule over the archipelago.

## Rizal, the Katipunan and the Revolution of 1896-1898 — 1872 - 1898

A reform movement led by Jose Rizal gave way to the armed Katipunan of Andres Bonifacio; the 1896 revolution and Rizal's execution made Spanish rule untenable, and on 12 June 1898 Aguinaldo proclaimed independence.

- 1892: **Founding of the Katipunan** — Andres Bonifacio establishes the secret revolutionary society the Katipunan, dedicated to winning Philippine independence from Spain by armed force. [britannica-revolution]
- Aug 1896: **Outbreak of the Philippine Revolution** — The discovery of the Katipunan sparks open revolt; Katipuneros proclaim the 'Cry' of defiance, beginning the armed revolution against Spanish rule. [britannica-revolution]
- 30 Dec 1896: **Execution of Jose Rizal** — Spain executes the reformist writer Jose Rizal by firing squad in Manila; his martyrdom galvanises Filipinos and makes Spanish retention of the colony untenable. [nhcp-rizal, britannica-revolution]
- 12 Jun 1898: **Declaration of Philippine independence** — Emilio Aguinaldo proclaims independence from Spain at Kawit, Cavite, founding a provisional republic; this date later becomes the national Independence Day. [britannica-independence-day, loc-aguinaldo]

Figures:

- Jose Rizal (1861-1896) — National hero, reformist writer and physician. The ilustrado whose novels Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo exposed colonial abuses and awakened Filipino nationalism. Executed by Spain in 1896, he became the country's foremost national hero.
- Andres Bonifacio (1863-1897) — Founder and supremo of the Katipunan. Founder of the Katipunan and 'Father of the Philippine Revolution', who launched the 1896 revolt. He was executed in 1897 amid a power struggle with Aguinaldo's faction.

## Cession to America, the Philippine-American War and the Commonwealth — 1898 - 1941

Spain ceded the islands to the United States, and the betrayed Filipino republic fought a bloody war for independence; defeat ushered in an American colonial period that culminated in the self-governing Commonwealth.

- 10 Dec 1898: **Treaty of Paris: Spain cedes the Philippines to the U.S.** — By the Treaty of Paris ending the Spanish-American War, Spain cedes the Philippines to the United States without consulting Filipinos, who had already declared independence. [statedept-philamwar]
- 1899-1902: **The Philippine-American War** — Filipino nationalists under Aguinaldo fight the United States for independence; the war kills thousands of combatants and as many as 200,000 civilians before ending in 1902. [statedept-philamwar, britannica-philamwar]
- 1935: **Establishment of the Commonwealth** — The Philippines becomes a self-governing Commonwealth under U.S. sovereignty, with Manuel L. Quezon as first president and a ten-year transition toward full independence. [britannica-philamwar]

Figures:

- Emilio Aguinaldo (1869-1964) — Revolutionary leader and first president of the Philippine Republic. Led the revolution after Bonifacio's death, declared independence in 1898, and headed the Malolos Republic. He fought the United States in the Philippine-American War until his capture in 1901.

## Japanese Occupation, Independence and the Marcos Dictatorship — 1941 - 1986

The Japanese occupation laid the country in ruins; full independence came in 1946, but after a generation the republic was seized by Ferdinand Marcos, whose martial law became a fourteen-year dictatorship.

- 1942-1945: **Japanese occupation** — Japan occupies the Philippines during the Second World War; guerrilla resistance and the campaign to retake the islands leave over a million Filipinos dead and Manila in ruins. [ww2museum-1946]
- 4 Jul 1946: **Independence of the Republic of the Philippines** — The United States relinquishes sovereignty and recognises Philippine independence under the Treaty of Manila; Manuel Roxas becomes first president of the republic. [statedept-philippines, ww2museum-1946]
- 21 Sep 1972: **Marcos declares martial law** — Ferdinand Marcos imposes martial law under Proclamation No. 1081, arresting opponents and seizing one-man rule in a dictatorship that lasts until 1986. [britannica-martial-law, britannica-marcos]

Figures:

- Ferdinand Marcos (1917-1989) — President and dictator (1965-1986). Elected president in 1965, he declared martial law in 1972 and ruled as a dictator for fourteen years, presiding over repression and large-scale corruption before being ousted in 1986.

## People Power and the Modern Republic — 1986 - present

In 1986 a peaceful uprising on EDSA toppled Marcos and restored democracy under Corazon Aquino; since then the republic has weathered coups, charismatic and dynastic leaders, and the return of the Marcos name to power.

- Feb 1986: **People Power Revolution** — A peaceful uprising on EDSA, backed by defecting troops and the Catholic Church, drives Ferdinand Marcos into exile and installs Corazon Aquino as president, restoring democracy. [britannica-marcos]
- 1987: **New democratic constitution** — Under President Corazon Aquino a new constitution is ratified, re-establishing democratic institutions, presidential term limits and a bill of rights after the Marcos dictatorship. [britannica-philippines]
- 2022: **Return of the Marcos name to power** — Ferdinand 'Bongbong' Marcos Jr., son of the late dictator, wins the presidency, returning his family to the highest office 36 years after People Power. [britannica-marcos]

Figures:

- Corazon Aquino (1933-2009) — President (1986-1992); leader of People Power. Widow of slain opposition leader Benigno Aquino, she became the figurehead of the 1986 People Power Revolution and the first female president of the Philippines, restoring democratic rule.
