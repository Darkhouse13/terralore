# The Chronicle of Bhutan: The Land of the Thunder Dragon — from Guru Rinpoche's blessing to the world's first kingdom of Gross National Happiness.

Founding: Founding of the Wangchuck monarchy — 17 Dec 1907. On 17 December 1907 an assembly of monks, officials and people gathered at Punakha Dzong unanimously elected Ugyen Wangchuck, the powerful penlop (governor) of Tongsa, as the first hereditary Druk Gyalpo, or Dragon King. His enthronement ended centuries of unstable shared rule under the dual system and the destructive rivalries of regional lords, founding the Wangchuck dynasty that still reigns. Bhutan's deeper roots, however, run back to the 8th-century arrival of Buddhism and to the 17th-century unification of the state under Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal.

Bhutan traces its spiritual identity to the 8th century, when the tantric master Guru Padmasambhava is said to have carried Buddhism into the Himalayan valleys. A patchwork of feuding lords and monasteries was forged into a single theocratic state from 1616 by the Tibetan lama Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal, who built the great dzongs and a dual system of religious and secular rule. After civil strife and two treaties with British India, the penlop Ugyen Wangchuck was crowned the first hereditary king on 17 December 1907, founding the Wangchuck dynasty. Over the following century Bhutan opened to the world, joined the United Nations, championed Gross National Happiness, and in 2008 became a constitutional democratic monarchy through a transition handed down by its own kings.

## Buddhism and the Valleys of the Thunder Dragon — 7th – 12th centuries

Into a land of isolated Himalayan valleys came Buddhism — by tradition borne in the 8th century by the tantric saint Guru Rinpoche, whose blessing still defines Bhutan's sacred geography.

- 7th century: **First Buddhist temples founded** — Tibetan tradition credits King Songtsen Gampo with building the earliest Buddhist temples in the Bhutanese valleys, including Kyichu Lhakhang at Paro, as Buddhism spread south from Tibet. [loc-nepalbhutan, britannica-bhutan-history]
- 8th century: **Guru Rinpoche brings Buddhism to Bhutan** — The tantric master Guru Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) is said to have travelled into Bhutan, blessing sites such as Bumthang and the Taktsang cliff at Paro and marking the proper advent of Buddhism in the country. [britannica-padmasambhava, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 12th century: **Drukpa Kagyu tradition takes root** — The Drukpa ('Dragon') Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism spreads into the southern valleys and becomes the dominant religious tradition, lending Bhutan its name Druk Yul, the Land of the Thunder Dragon. [loc-nepalbhutan, britannica-bhutan-history]

## Unification under the Zhabdrung — 1616 – 1651

A Tibetan lama fleeing sectarian conflict forged the scattered valleys into a single state, ringed with fortress-monasteries and governed by a dual system of religious and secular authority.

- 1616: **Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal arrives in Bhutan** — Fleeing a sectarian dispute in Tibet, the Drukpa Kagyu lama Ngawang Namgyal enters western Bhutan and is recognised as the Zhabdrung, beginning the unification of the country. [britannica-bhutan-history, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 1637: **Punakha Dzong founded** — The Zhabdrung begins building Punakha Dzong at the confluence of two rivers as his capital, one of a network of fortress-monasteries that became the seats of Bhutan's temporal and religious power. [unesco-bhutan-dzongs, loc-nepalbhutan]
- c. 1651: **Dual system of government established** — The Zhabdrung institutes the chhoesi dual system, splitting power between a spiritual Je Khenpo and a secular Druk Desi; his death around this time is concealed to preserve the fledgling state. [britannica-bhutan-history, loc-nepalbhutan]

Figures:

- Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal (1594–1651) — Unifier of Bhutan. A Drukpa Kagyu lama from Tibet who unified the Bhutanese valleys, built the great dzongs, gave the country its laws and identity, and created the dual system of religious and secular government that defined Bhutan for centuries.

## Civil Strife and the British Treaties — 1651 – 1910

Behind the facade of the dual system, rival governors fought for control, while war with British India cost Bhutan its southern lands and drew the kingdom into the orbit of the Raj.

- 1720: **A Chinese imperial army enters Bhutan** — Qing forces invade and briefly establish control — the first of three outside powers to reach into the valleys within two centuries. [bbc-bhutan]
- 1772–1773: **The first British intervention** — A century before the Duar War, British forces intervene in Bhutan — the beginning of the Raj's long pressure on its southern frontier. [bbc-bhutan]
- 1864–1865: **The Duar War with British India** — After a failed British mission and disputes over the southern duars, Britain and Bhutan fight a five-month war that ends in Bhutanese defeat. [britannica-bhutan-history, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 11 Nov 1865: **Treaty of Sinchula** — Bhutan cedes the Bengal and Assam duars to British India in exchange for an annual subsidy, fixing its modern southern border and binding it to the Raj. [britannica-bhutan-history, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 8 Jan 1910: **Treaty of Punakha** — Revising the Sinchula terms, Britain increases Bhutan's subsidy and pledges non-interference in internal affairs while guiding its foreign relations, securing Bhutan's continued independence. [cambridge-punakha-1910, loc-nepalbhutan]

## Founding of the Wangchuck Monarchy — 1907 – 1952

On 17 December 1907 the warring penlops gave way to a single crown, as Ugyen Wangchuck was enthroned the first Dragon King and the Wangchuck dynasty began.

- 17 Dec 1907: **Ugyen Wangchuck crowned first king** — An assembly at Punakha Dzong unanimously elects Ugyen Wangchuck, penlop of Tongsa, as the first hereditary Druk Gyalpo, ending the dual system and founding the Wangchuck dynasty. [britannica-bhutan-history, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 1926: **Accession of the second king** — On Ugyen Wangchuck's death the crown passes peacefully to his son Jigme Wangchuck, confirming the stability of the new hereditary monarchy. [loc-nepalbhutan, britannica-bhutan-history]
- 1949: **The treaty with independent India** — Delhi guarantees non-interference in Bhutan's internal affairs while retaining influence over its foreign relations — the terms of British Punakha, inherited by a new republic. [bbc-bhutan]

Figures:

- Ugyen Wangchuck (1862–1926) — First Druk Gyalpo (King of Bhutan). Penlop of Tongsa who unified the country under his rule, cultivated ties with British India, and was elected first hereditary king of Bhutan on 17 December 1907, founding the Wangchuck dynasty.

## Opening to the World — 1952 – 1998

Two reforming kings ended serfdom, built roads, joined the United Nations and forged a special partnership with India — while the fourth king answered modernisation with Gross National Happiness.

- 1953: **National Assembly established** — King Jigme Dorji Wangchuck creates the Tshogdu, Bhutan's National Assembly, beginning the reform of the country's institutions alongside the abolition of serfdom and land redistribution. [britannica-jigme-dorji, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 21 Sep 1971: **Bhutan joins the United Nations** — Bhutan is admitted as the 128th member of the United Nations, a milestone in its emergence from isolation and its assertion of full sovereignty. [un-bhutan-admission, mfa-bhutan-un]
- 1972: **Gross National Happiness proclaimed** — On acceding to the throne, the young fourth king Jigme Singye Wangchuck advances Gross National Happiness as Bhutan's guiding measure of development, valuing well-being and culture over GDP alone. [mfa-bhutan-gnh, ophi-gnh]
- 1958: **Slavery abolished** — The third king abolishes slavery and serfdom, one of the first acts of a reign that would open a closed kingdom deliberately and slowly. [bbc-bhutan]
- 1974: **The first foreign tourists** — Bhutan admits its first tourists — a controlled opening, priced and permitted rather than free, and still the model today. [bbc-bhutan]

Figures:

- Jigme Dorji Wangchuck (1929–1972) — Third Druk Gyalpo, 'father of modern Bhutan'. Reforming third king who abolished serfdom, founded the National Assembly and a modern administration, launched Bhutan's five-year development plans and led the country into the United Nations.

## Democracy and the Modern Kingdom — 1998 – present

Bhutan's kings handed power to the people, founding a constitutional democracy in 2008 — even as the unresolved exodus of the Lhotshampa cast a shadow over the new era.

- 1988: **The census and the citizenship rules** — A census brands many ethnic Nepalis illegal immigrants, and measures stressing Tibetan-derived Bhutanese culture antagonise the Lhotshampa; violent unrest follows in the south in 1990. [bbc-bhutan, unhcr-bhutanese]
- early 1990s: **Exodus of the Lhotshampa** — Amid 'one nation, one people' measures and stricter citizenship rules, tens of thousands of southern Bhutanese of Nepali origin leave or are expelled, and more than 100,000 end up in UNHCR camps in eastern Nepal. [unhcr-bhutanese, loc-nepalbhutan]
- 2007–2008: **Resettlement of Bhutanese refugees** — A multinational programme led by UNHCR and IOM resettles over 100,000 Bhutanese refugees from Nepal to countries including the United States, Canada, Australia and Europe. [iom-bhutanese-resettlement, unhcr-bhutanese]
- 1999: **Television and the internet arrive** — Limited television and internet services are permitted and several dozen political prisoners released, a decade after the kingdom tightened. [bbc-bhutan]
- 2007: **The treaty with India revised** — A new agreement gives Bhutan more say over its own foreign and defence policy, loosening a constraint carried from 1910 through 1949. [bbc-bhutan]
- Jul 2008: **Constitution and democratic monarchy** — Following the country's first parliamentary elections, Bhutan's written Constitution comes into force, transforming the kingdom into a democratic constitutional monarchy with Gross National Happiness as a goal of the state. [britannica-bhutan-history, state-us-bhutan]
- Nov 2008: **Coronation of the fifth king** — Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck is formally enthroned as the fifth Druk Gyalpo, his coronation timed to the centenary of the Wangchuck monarchy and the inauguration of democratic rule. [britannica-bhutan-history, state-us-bhutan]
- 2013: **A prime minister questions Gross National Happiness** — Tshering Tobgay says the concept is overused and masks real problems — rising debt, chronic unemployment, poverty and corruption. [bbc-bhutan]

Figures:

- Jigme Singye Wangchuck (born 1955; reigned 1972–2006) — Fourth Druk Gyalpo. The fourth king, who articulated Gross National Happiness, opened Bhutan cautiously to the world, and voluntarily set in motion the country's transition to constitutional democracy before abdicating in 2006.
- Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck (born 1980; reigning from 2006) — Fifth Druk Gyalpo. Oxford-educated fifth king, enthroned in 2008, who oversaw Bhutan's first democratic elections and the coming into force of its Constitution, presiding over a constitutional monarchy.
