# The Chronicle of Andorra: Seven centuries of survival in the high Pyrenees, ruled by two princes and never conquered.

Founding: The Paréage of 1278 and the birth of the co-principality — 1278. Andorra's distinctive statehood was created not by conquest or unification but by a feudal compromise. A long dispute over the valleys between the Bishop of Urgell, on the Spanish side of the Pyrenees, and the Count of Foix, on the French side, was settled in 1278 by a charter known as the paréage, signed at Lleida on 8 September. It placed Andorra under the joint suzerainty of the two lords, each receiving an annual token tribute, and a second paréage in 1288 fixed the details. Together these documents became Andorra's first basic law and the foundation of its sovereignty for the next seven centuries. The arrangement produced a co-principality in which neither lord could absorb the territory, an enduring example of a successful diarchy that protected Andorran autonomy until a modern constitution was adopted in 1993.

Andorra is one of Europe's oldest surviving states, a cluster of valleys high in the eastern Pyrenees that has outlasted empires by belonging, in a sense, to no one. Legend traces its origin to Charlemagne, who is said to have granted the valleys their charter after his armies passed through against the Moors, a memory still sung in the national anthem 'El Gran Carlemany'. Its real political identity was forged in 1278, when a long feudal quarrel between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix was settled by a charter, the paréage, that placed Andorra under their joint suzerainty. From that compromise grew a unique co-principality whose two heads of state today are the President of the French Republic and the Bishop of Urgell, a Spanish prelate. For more than seven hundred years this divided overlordship, together with the country's isolation and a symbolic annual tribute, preserved Andorran neutrality and self-government through Europe's wars. In 1993 Andorra adopted its first written constitution, becoming a sovereign parliamentary democracy with the co-princes reduced to constitutional heads of state, and that same year it joined the United Nations.

## Charlemagne, the Marca Hispanica and the Bishops of Urgell — c. 790 – 1133

From a frontier of the Carolingian world, the high valleys passed under the lordship of the Bishop of Urgell, where the seeds of Andorra's separate identity were sown.

- c. 788–790: **The Charlemagne tradition and the Marca Hispanica** — Andorran tradition, recalled in the anthem 'El Gran Carlemany', credits Charlemagne with freeing the valleys from the Moors and granting their charter; the region lay within the Carolingian frontier zone of the Marca Hispanica. [anthem-andorra, andorra-britannica]
- 9th century: **Christianity and Carolingian order** — As part of the Carolingian frontier, the valleys were integrated into Latin Christendom, with the church of Urgell emerging as the dominant local authority over the centuries that followed. [andorra-britannica]
- early 12th c.: **Lordship of the Bishop of Urgell** — The Bishop of Urgell consolidated lordship over the Andorran valleys, entrusting their defence to secular protectors, among them the powerful counts of Foix. [andorra-britannica, statedept-andorra]

Figures:

- Charlemagne (c. 742 – 814) — Emperor of the Franks, legendary founder of Andorra. In Andorran tradition Charlemagne liberated the valleys from the Moors and granted their first charter, a memory enshrined in the national anthem. Historically, his frontier zone, the Marca Hispanica, drew the region into Carolingian Christendom.
- The Bishops of Urgell (from the 9th century) — Ecclesiastical lords of the valleys. From their see at La Seu d'Urgell the bishops accumulated lands and rights over Andorra, becoming its dominant lords. Their successors remain co-princes of Andorra to this day, the only such office held by a serving bishop.

## The Paréage of 1278 and the Making of the Co-Principality — 1278 – 1419

A feudal quarrel between bishop and count was settled by a charter of joint sovereignty, giving Andorra the divided overlordship that has defined it ever since.

- 8 Sep 1278: **The first paréage** — A feudal charter signed at Lleida ended the dispute between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix by placing Andorra under their joint suzerainty, creating the co-principality. [andorra-britannica, summary-britannica, statedept-andorra]
- 1288: **The second paréage** — A second charter settled questions of justice and defence left open in 1278; together the two paréages formed Andorra's first basic law and constitutional foundation until 1993. [andorra-britannica, statedept-andorra]
- from 1278: **The questia tribute and neutrality** — Andorra rendered a symbolic annual tribute, the questia, to each co-prince as an act of homage; the settlement was understood to recognise the valleys' neutrality. [andorra-britannica, coe-andorra]
- 14th century: **Self-government within divided lordship** — Because neither co-prince could rule alone, Andorrans gained latitude to govern their own valleys, developing assemblies of the parishes that prefigured a national council. [andorra-britannica, casadelavall-andorra]

Figures:

- Pere d'Urtx (fl. 13th century) — Bishop of Urgell, co-signatory of the paréage. As Bishop of Urgell, Pere d'Urtx was a party to the paréage of 1278 that established joint sovereignty over Andorra with the Count of Foix, fixing the ecclesiastical half of the co-principality that endures today.
- Roger-Bernard III, Count of Foix (1243 – 1302) — Co-signatory of the paréage. Count of Foix at the time of the 1278 settlement, he secured for his house the secular share of Andorran sovereignty. That claim later passed through the kings of Navarre to the crown, and then the presidency, of France.

## Two Princes, Neutrality and Endurance — 1419 – 1866

The valleys won their own council, the French half of the co-principality passed to the kings and then head of state of France, and Andorra slipped quietly through Europe's wars.

- 1419: **Founding of the Consell de la Terra** — The co-princes allowed Andorrans to create the Council of the Land, drawn from heads of households of the parishes, one of Europe's oldest representative assemblies and the ancestor of today's parliament. [casadelavall-andorra, andorra-britannica]
- 1607: **The French crown becomes co-prince** — After the rights of Foix passed through Navarre to Henry IV, an edict confirmed the King of France and the Bishop of Urgell as Andorra's co-princes; the French role later passed to the head of state. [statedept-andorra, andorra-britannica]
- 1702: **The Casa de la Vall** — The Andorran council established its seat and court at the Casa de la Vall in Andorra la Vella, long the symbolic home of the valleys' self-government. [casadelavall-andorra]
- 1789–1815: **Neutrality through revolution and war** — Through the French Revolution and Napoleonic Wars the co-principality survived; the French co-princely role transferred to each new French regime while Andorra guarded its neutrality. [andorra-britannica, coe-andorra]

Figures:

- Henry IV of France (1553 – 1610) — King of France and Navarre, co-prince of Andorra. Inheriting the Foix claim through the kingdom of Navarre, Henry IV brought the secular co-principality of Andorra into the French crown. His 1607 edict fixed the King of France and the Bishop of Urgell as co-princes.

## Reform, a Brief 'King', and the Strains of the Old Order — 1866 – 1992

The feudal co-principality was modernised by stages, weathered the bizarre episode of a self-proclaimed king, and slowly outgrew a constitution written for the Middle Ages.

- 1866: **The New Reform** — A reform led by syndic Guillem d'Areny-Plandolit and decreed by the episcopal co-prince broadened the Council and regularised elections, modernising Andorra's institutions while keeping the co-principality. [casadelavall-andorra, andorra-britannica]
- 1914–1918: **Neutral through the First World War** — Andorra remained outside the fighting of the Great War; an anomaly of its undefined status was that it was reportedly omitted from the peace settlement and stayed nominally at war for decades. [andorra-britannica, coe-andorra]
- Jul 1934: **Boris Skossyreff, 'King Boris I'** — A Russian-born adventurer briefly induced the Andorran council to proclaim him king during a period of unrest over voting rights; he was arrested and expelled within about two weeks. [andorra-britannica, statedept-andorra]
- 1939–1945: **Neutral in the Second World War** — Andorra preserved its neutrality through the Second World War, its mountain passes serving as a quiet crossing-point between Vichy France and Spain. [andorra-britannica, coe-andorra]

Figures:

- Guillem d'Areny-Plandolit (1822 – 1876) — Syndic and reformer. As syndic, Areny-Plandolit led the New Reform of 1866 that broadened Andorra's Council and regularised its elections, the first major modernisation of the medieval co-principality and a milestone toward representative government.
- Boris Skossyreff (1896 – 1989) — Self-proclaimed 'King Boris I of Andorra'. A Russian-born adventurer who, during the unrest of the 1930s, persuaded the Andorran council to proclaim him king in July 1934 on a platform of reform and a casino economy. His reign lasted only days before Spanish authorities arrested and expelled him.

## A Sovereign Democracy in the Heart of Europe — 1993 – present

A written constitution turned the medieval co-principality into a parliamentary democracy, the co-princes into constitutional heads of state, and Andorra into a recognised member of the international community.

- 14 Mar 1993: **The first written constitution** — Andorrans approved by referendum a constitution that made Andorra a sovereign parliamentary democracy, with the two co-princes reduced to constitutional heads of state and power vested in an elected government and council. [constitute-andorra, andorra-britannica, statedept-andorra]
- 28 Jul 1993: **Admission to the United Nations** — Andorra was admitted as a member state of the United Nations, taking its place in the international community as a fully recognised sovereign state. [un-members, coe-andorra]
- 1994: **Member of the Council of Europe** — Andorra joined the Council of Europe, committing the new democracy to European standards of human rights and the rule of law. [coe-andorra]
- 2004: **Madriu-Perafita-Claror Valley inscribed by UNESCO** — Andorra's only World Heritage Site, the Madriu-Perafita-Claror Valley, was inscribed as a cultural landscape, recognised for its high-Pyrenean pastoralism and a communal land-ownership system surviving from the 13th century. [unesco-madriu]
- 30 Jun 2011: **Monetary agreement and the euro** — Andorra signed a monetary agreement with the European Union allowing it to use the euro as its official currency and to issue its own euro coins. [ecb-andorra, eurlex-monetary]
- 2023–2024: **Association agreement with the EU** — After concluding negotiations in 2023, the European Commission in 2024 proposed an association agreement to give Andorra (with San Marino) deep access to the EU single market, comparable to the European Economic Area. [eu-association, ep-association]

Figures:

- Marc Forné Molné (born 1946) — Head of Government during the constitutional transition. Cap de govern through much of the 1990s, Forné led Andorra in the years after the 1993 constitution, guiding the new democracy into the United Nations and the Council of Europe and toward closer ties with Europe.
- The President of France (office since 1607/1870) — French co-prince of Andorra. Heir to the rights of the counts of Foix by way of the kings of Navarre and France, the French head of state serves as one of Andorra's two co-princes, an office unique in the world: a republican president who is also a constitutional prince of a foreign state.
