# The Chronicle of Kosovo: From ancient Dardania to a contested independence at the crossroads of the Balkans.

Founding: Declaration of independence from Serbia — 17 February 2008. On 17 February 2008 the Assembly of Kosovo, meeting in Pristina, adopted a declaration of independence from Serbia, proclaiming the Republic of Kosovo. The act followed nearly a decade of United Nations administration under Security Council Resolution 1244 of 1999 and the failure of status talks based on the plan of the UN envoy Martti Ahtisaari. The declaration was recognised the following day by the United States and several major European states, and Kosovo went on to win recognition from around a hundred countries. On 22 July 2010 the International Court of Justice, responding to a request from the UN General Assembly, advised that the declaration did not violate general international law or Resolution 1244. Serbia, however, continues to regard Kosovo as its autonomous province, and with the support of Russia and China and the non-recognition of several European Union members, Kosovo remains outside the United Nations and its statehood disputed.

Kosovo's history is layered and fiercely contested. In antiquity the region was the heartland of Dardania, a Paleo-Balkan kingdom absorbed by Rome and later held by Byzantium. In the high Middle Ages it became the core of the Serbian realm under the Nemanjic dynasty, studded with Orthodox monasteries, and the site of the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, an event that became central to Serbian national identity. Five centuries of Ottoman rule followed the conquest, during which the population gradually shifted toward an ethnic Albanian majority and the League of Prizren of 1878 gave birth to Albanian nationalism. Incorporated into Serbia and then Yugoslavia after the Balkan Wars, Kosovo was granted wide autonomy by the 1974 Yugoslav constitution, only to have it revoked by Slobodan Milosevic in 1989, prompting a decade of Albanian nonviolent resistance and then armed insurgency. The Kosovo War of 1998-99 brought a humanitarian catastrophe, a NATO air campaign, and UN administration under Security Council Resolution 1244. Kosovo declared independence on 17 February 2008; the International Court of Justice found in 2010 that the declaration did not violate international law, and around a hundred states recognise it, but Serbia, Russia, China and several EU members do not, leaving Kosovo outside the United Nations and its status unresolved.

## Ancient Dardania, Rome and Byzantium — c. 300 BCE – c. 1180 CE

The land of the Dardani became a Roman frontier district and then a Byzantine borderland, settled by Slavs in the early Middle Ages.

- 1st c. BCE: **Rome annexes Dardania** — The kingdom of the Dardani in the central Balkans was absorbed by Rome and incorporated into the province of Moesia, with towns such as Ulpiana, Scupi and Naissus as regional centres. [history-of-kosovo-britannica]
- late 3rd c. CE: **Province of Dardania** — In the administrative reorganisation of the later Roman Empire, the region was constituted as a province again bearing the ancient name of Dardania, within the diocese of Moesia. [history-of-kosovo-britannica]
- 6th–7th c.: **Slavic settlement of the interior** — As the Danube frontier collapsed, Slavic peoples settled widely across the Balkan interior under and after Byzantine authority, transforming the population of the future Kosovo. [history-of-kosovo-britannica]
- c. 850–1180: **Byzantine and Bulgarian borderland** — The region passed between the Byzantine Empire, the First Bulgarian Empire and rising Serbian principalities, a contested frontier of the medieval Balkans before the Nemanjic ascendancy. [history-of-kosovo-britannica, kosovo-history-britannica]

Figures:

- The Dardani — Ancient people of the central Balkans. A Paleo-Balkan people, variously classed as Illyrian or Thracian, who gave their name to the kingdom and Roman province of Dardania centred on the lands of modern Kosovo.
- Justinian I (c. 482 – 565) — Byzantine emperor born in Dardania. The great Byzantine emperor and codifier of Roman law was born at Tauresium in Dardania, near Naissus, illustrating the region's place within the late Roman and Byzantine world before the medieval Slavic states arose.

## The Medieval Serbian Heartland and the Battle of Kosovo — c. 1180 – 1455

Under the Nemanjic dynasty the region became the core of the Serbian realm, dense with Orthodox monasteries, until the Battle of Kosovo of 1389 entered Serbian memory forever.

- 13th c.: **Nemanjic rule and the Serbian Church** — The Nemanjic dynasty consolidated its hold over the region, which became a centre of the medieval Serbian state and of the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church seated at the Patriarchate of Pec. [history-of-kosovo-britannica, kosovo-history-britannica]
- 1321 / mid-14th c.: **Gracanica and Visoki Decani built** — The monastery of Gracanica was founded in 1321 and Visoki Decani built in the mid-14th century for King Stefan Decanski; with other churches they form the UNESCO Medieval Monuments in Kosovo, later placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger. [unesco-medieval-monuments, unesco-monuments-danger]
- 28 Jun 1389: **The Battle of Kosovo** — At Kosovo Polje, the Field of Blackbirds, the Serbian prince Lazar and his allies fought the Ottoman sultan Murad I; both rulers died and the Ottoman victory led to the eventual subjugation of Serbia. [battle-of-kosovo-britannica, battle-of-kosovo-historytoday]
- mid-15th c.: **Ottoman conquest completed** — By the mid-15th century the Ottomans had established direct rule over all of Serbia, including Kosovo, ending the medieval Serbian state in the region. [kosovo-history-britannica]

Figures:

- Prince Lazar Hrebeljanovic (c. 1329 – 1389) — Serbian prince and commander at Kosovo. Ruler of much of what remained of the Serbian lands, Lazar led the Christian coalition against Murad I at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389. Killed in the battle, he was venerated as a martyr and became the central figure of the Kosovo legend in Serbian tradition.
- Stefan Decanski (c. 1276 – 1331) — Serbian king and monastery founder. King of Serbia for whom the great monastery of Visoki Decani was built in the mid-14th century and where he is entombed; the monastery is among the finest of the medieval Orthodox foundations of Kosovo.

## Ottoman Rule and the Albanian National Awakening — 1455 – 1912

Five centuries of Ottoman rule reshaped the region's faith and population, and at Prizren in 1878 the cause of Albanian nationalism was born.

- 1455–1912: **Ottoman administration of Kosovo** — For roughly four and a half centuries Kosovo was ruled as part of the Ottoman Empire, governed through the Balkan provinces and, latterly, the vilayet of Kosovo. [kosovo-history-britannica]
- 17th–18th c.: **Migrations and a shifting population** — Over the Ottoman centuries many Orthodox Serbs emigrated and parts of the population converted to Islam, while the Albanian-speaking share grew, probably forming a majority by the 18th century. [kosovo-history-britannica]
- 1 Jul 1878: **The League of Prizren** — Albanian leaders founded the League of Prizren in Kosovo, the first Albanian nationalist organisation, seeking to unite Albanian-inhabited lands and promote the Albanian language amid the Congress of Berlin's partition plans. [albanian-league-britannica]
- 1881: **Suppression of the League** — After resisting territorial transfers to Montenegro and Greece, the League of Prizren was suppressed by the Ottoman authorities, but it had laid the foundation of the Albanian national movement. [albanian-league-britannica]

Figures:

- The League of Prizren (1878 – 1881) — First Albanian nationalist organisation. Founded at Prizren in 1878 in response to the Congress of Berlin, the League sought to unite the Albanian-inhabited vilayets into an autonomous unit within the Ottoman Empire and to foster the Albanian language and culture, becoming the cradle of Albanian nationalism.

## Yugoslav Kosovo: Autonomy, Revocation and Parallel State — 1912 – 1998

Won by Serbia in the Balkan Wars and folded into Yugoslavia, Kosovo gained wide self-rule in 1974, lost it under Milosevic in 1989, and answered with a nonviolent parallel state.

- 1912–13: **Balkan Wars bring Kosovo into Serbia** — The Balkan Wars ended Ottoman rule, and Kosovo was incorporated into the Kingdom of Serbia, later part of the Yugoslav state. [kosovo-history-britannica]
- 1974: **Wide autonomy under the Yugoslav constitution** — The 1974 Yugoslav constitution gave Kosovo broad self-government as an autonomous province within Serbia, with its own assembly, courts and a veto, making it nearly a republic in practice. [kosovo-govsociety-britannica]
- Mar 1981: **Pristina student protests** — Student demonstrations in Pristina demanding republic status for Kosovo grew into wider unrest, which the Yugoslav authorities suppressed, exposing deep tensions over the province's status. [kosovo-govsociety-britannica, kosovo-history-britannica]
- Mar 1989: **Milosevic revokes Kosovo's autonomy** — Under Slobodan Milosevic, the Serbian assembly stripped Kosovo of most of the autonomy granted in 1974, returning police, courts, education and language policy to Belgrade and igniting Albanian resistance. [kosovo-govsociety-britannica, kosovo-history-britannica, state-dept-kosovo]
- 1989–1998: **The Albanian parallel state** — Led by Ibrahim Rugova and the Democratic League of Kosovo, Kosovo's Albanians boycotted Serbian institutions and ran their own parallel schools, clinics and administration in a decade of nonviolent resistance. [rugova-sakharov-ep]

Figures:

- Ibrahim Rugova (1944 – 2006) — Leader of nonviolent resistance; first President of Kosovo. A literary scholar who founded the Democratic League of Kosovo in 1989, Rugova led a decade of nonviolent resistance and built a parallel Albanian state after the loss of autonomy. Awarded the Sakharov Prize in 1998, he later became the first president of post-war Kosovo.
- Slobodan Milosevic (1941 – 2006) — President of Serbia and of Yugoslavia. The Serbian leader whose revocation of Kosovo's autonomy in 1989 and crackdown on its Albanian population drove the province toward war. He was later indicted by the UN tribunal for the former Yugoslavia and died in custody during his trial.

## War, NATO Intervention and UN Administration — 1998 – 2008

Insurgency and a brutal counter-campaign brought a humanitarian catastrophe, a NATO air war, and nearly a decade of United Nations administration under Resolution 1244.

- 1998: **KLA insurgency and Serbian crackdown** — The Kosovo Liberation Army's insurgency against Serbian rule met an escalating campaign by Serbian and Yugoslav forces, killing many Albanians and displacing hundreds of thousands during 1998. [kosovo-conflict-britannica, nato-kfor, hrw-under-orders]
- 24 Mar 1999: **NATO air campaign begins** — After the failure of talks at Rambouillet, NATO launched Operation Allied Force against Yugoslav forces on 24 March 1999, a 78-day air campaign intended to halt the humanitarian catastrophe in Kosovo. [nato-kfor]
- Mar–Jun 1999: **Mass expulsion of Kosovo Albanians** — Serbian and Yugoslav forces forcibly expelled some 862,000 ethnic Albanians from Kosovo, uprooting more than 80 percent of the population, with thousands killed in a systematic campaign later prosecuted as war crimes. [hrw-under-orders]
- 10 Jun 1999: **UN Resolution 1244 and UNMIK** — The Security Council adopted Resolution 1244, ending the war, placing Kosovo under UN interim administration (UNMIK) with a NATO-led KFOR security force, while reaffirming Yugoslav sovereignty and deferring final status. [unmik-res-1244, nato-kfor]
- 2001: **Constitutional Framework for self-government** — UNMIK established a Constitutional Framework for Provisional Self-Government, creating an assembly, presidency and government for Kosovo while final status and ultimate authority remained with the UN administration. [unmik-constitutional-framework]

Figures:

- Martti Ahtisaari (1937 – 2023) — UN Special Envoy for Kosovo's status. The Finnish diplomat and later Nobel Peace laureate who led the UN status process for Kosovo and proposed a plan of internationally supervised independence; rejected by Belgrade and blocked at the Security Council, it nonetheless framed Kosovo's 2008 declaration.
- Hashim Thaci (born 1968) — KLA political leader; later Prime Minister and President. A political leader of the Kosovo Liberation Army during the war, Thaci became prime minister and, as such, a signatory of the 2008 declaration of independence; he later served as president before facing war-crimes proceedings at a special court in The Hague.

## Independence and the Contested Present — 2008 – present

Kosovo declared independence in 2008 and won the cautious backing of the International Court of Justice and around a hundred states, but Serbia's claim and the divided north keep its status unresolved.

- 17 Feb 2008: **Declaration of independence** — The Assembly of Kosovo declared independence from Serbia in Pristina, proclaiming the Republic of Kosovo; the United States recognised it the next day, followed by many other states. [kosovo-selfdeclared-britannica, state-dept-kosovo]
- 22 Jul 2010: **ICJ advisory opinion** — Responding to the UN General Assembly, the International Court of Justice advised that the 2008 declaration of independence did not violate general international law or Resolution 1244. [icj-advisory-summary, icj-case-141]
- 19 Apr 2013: **Brussels Agreement** — Under EU facilitation, Belgrade and Pristina reached the First Agreement of Principles Governing the Normalisation of Relations, addressing the integration of Serb-majority areas and a planned association of Serb municipalities in northern Kosovo. [kosovo-selfdeclared-britannica, eeas-belgrade-pristina-dialogue]
- 2023: **Ohrid agreement on normalisation** — At Ohrid the parties reached an EU-facilitated Agreement on the Path to Normalisation, committing to good-neighbourly relations and mutual recognition of documents and symbols, though Serbia did not recognise Kosovo's statehood. [eeas-ohrid-agreement, eeas-borrell-ohrid]
- Present: **An unresolved status** — Recognised by around a hundred states but not by Serbia, Russia, China or several EU members, Kosovo remains outside the UN, with the Serb-majority north around Mitrovica its most contested question. [kosovo-selfdeclared-britannica, state-dept-kosovo]

Figures:

- Hashim Thaci (born 1968) — Prime Minister at the declaration of independence. As prime minister, Thaci led the government that declared Kosovo's independence on 17 February 2008. A former KLA leader, he later became president and subsequently faced war-crimes proceedings before a specialist court in The Hague.
- Hisashi Owada (born 1932) — President of the International Court of Justice. The Japanese jurist who presided over the ICJ when, on 22 July 2010, the Court delivered its advisory opinion that Kosovo's declaration of independence did not violate international law.
