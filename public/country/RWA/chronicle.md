Founding: Independence from Belgium — 1 July 1962. Rwanda became a sovereign republic on 1 July 1962, ending more than four decades of Belgian administration of the trust territory of Ruanda-Urundi (and, before 1916, German rule). A United Nations–supervised referendum on 25 September 1961 had already abolished the monarchy and rejected the reign of Mwami Kigeri V, and the Hutu-led Parmehutu party of Grégoire Kayibanda had won the concurrent elections. At independence Kayibanda became the first president of the Republic of Rwanda, while the sister territory of Urundi became independent as the Kingdom of Burundi on the same day.

Rwanda is a small, densely populated country of the central African highlands whose people — Hutu, Tutsi and Twa — share a single language, Kinyarwanda, a common religion and a centuries-old monarchy. From at least the fifteenth century a kingdom centred on a Tutsi mwami (king) and his clans gradually extended its authority over the hills, reaching its greatest power in the late nineteenth century under Mwami Kigeri IV Rwabugiri. German and then Belgian colonial rule hardened the once-fluid distinction between Hutu and Tutsi into a fixed racial hierarchy, complete with ethnic identity cards, that the colonisers first used to govern through a Tutsi elite and then abandoned. The 1959 Hutu 'Social Revolution' toppled the monarchy and drove tens of thousands of Tutsi into exile; Rwanda became independent of Belgium on 1 July 1962 under Grégoire Kayibanda. Cycles of anti-Tutsi violence, the long rule of Juvénal Habyarimana, and the civil war launched by the exiles' Rwandan Patriotic Front in 1990 culminated, after Habyarimana's plane was shot down on 6 April 1994, in the genocide against the Tutsi: in roughly 100 days an estimated 800,000 to one million people, overwhelmingly Tutsi, were murdered. The genocide ended only with the RPF's military victory in July 1994, and Rwanda has since pursued justice through the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and the gacaca community courts, reconstruction, and the long, tightly controlled leadership of Paul Kagame.

## The Kingdom of the Mwami — c. 1400 – 1890s

Hutu, Tutsi and Twa shared one language and faith under a centralising monarchy whose Tutsi kings reached their height in the nineteenth century.

- c. 15th century: **Clans coalesce into kingdoms** — Across the highlands, clans containing Hutu, Tutsi and Twa form into small kingdoms; the Nyiginya dynasty of the Tutsi begins to extend its authority, founding the kingdom of Rwanda. [wiki-history-rwanda, wiki-kingdom-rwanda, ebsco-rise-rwanda]
- by the late 18th century: **A centralised Tutsi-led state** — A single kingdom under a Tutsi mwami controls much of present-day Rwanda, governing through clan chiefs and cattle-clientship arrangements such as ubuhake. [wiki-history-rwanda, un-historical-background]
- c. 1867–1895: **Reign of Kigeri IV Rwabugiri** — Mwami Kigeri IV Rwabugiri expands the kingdom through military conquest and administrative reform toward Lake Kivu, marking the high point of precolonial Rwandan power. [wiki-rwabugiri, wiki-history-rwanda]
- 1895: **Death of Rwabugiri** — Rwabugiri dies during a campaign, just as European powers reach the central African interior; a disputed succession leaves the kingdom weakened on the eve of colonial rule. [wiki-rwabugiri]

Figures:

- Kigeri IV Rwabugiri (reigned c. 1867–1895) — Mwami (king) of Rwanda. The great warrior-king under whom the kingdom of Rwanda reached its widest extent and most centralised power, through decades of military campaigns and reforms shortly before colonial rule.

## German and Belgian Rule — 1890s – 1959

Colonial powers ruling through the monarchy turned the fluid Hutu–Tutsi distinction into a fixed racial hierarchy, sealed by identity cards.

- 1894: **First European contact** — The German explorer Count Gustav Adolf von Götzen reaches the Rwandan court; Rwanda is incorporated into German East Africa and ruled indirectly through the mwami. [wiki-history-rwanda, ebsco-rise-rwanda]
- 1916: **Belgium occupies Rwanda** — Belgian forces take Rwanda from Germany during the First World War; from 1922 it is governed with Burundi as Ruanda-Urundi under a League of Nations mandate. [wiki-history-rwanda, ushmm-divided]
- 1931: **Belgians depose Mwami Musinga** — The Belgian administration removes the uncooperative King Yuhi V Musinga and installs his son Mutara III Rudahigwa, tightening colonial control over the monarchy. [wiki-musinga, wiki-history-rwanda]
- 1933: **Ethnic identity cards introduced** — Belgium issues identity cards classifying every Rwandan as Hutu, Tutsi or Twa, fixing a once-fluid social distinction into a rigid, inherited racial category. [ushmm-divided, un-historical-background, preventgenocide-idcard]
- 1957: **The Hutu Manifesto** — Hutu leaders publish the 'Hutu Manifesto' demanding an end to Tutsi domination; Belgian authorities and the Church begin to favour the Hutu majority over the Tutsi elite. [wiki-rwandan-revolution, ushmm-divided]

Figures:

- Mutara III Rudahigwa (1911–1959) — Mwami (king) of Rwanda. Installed by the Belgians in 1931, the first Rwandan king to be baptised a Catholic; he abolished the ubuhake cattle-clientship and pressed for independence before his sudden death in 1959 on the eve of revolution.

## Revolution, Independence and Exile — 1959 – 1973

A Hutu revolution overturned the monarchy, Rwanda became independent in 1962, and recurring violence drove waves of Tutsi into exile.

- November 1959: **The Hutu 'Social Revolution'** — A Hutu uprising, tolerated by the Belgian authorities, kills hundreds of Tutsi and drives many into exile, breaking the power of the Tutsi monarchy. [wiki-rwandan-revolution, un-historical-background, ushmm-divided]
- 25 September 1961: **Monarchy abolished by referendum** — A UN-supervised referendum abolishes the monarchy and rejects King Kigeri V; Grégoire Kayibanda's Parmehutu wins the concurrent elections. [wiki-1961-referendum, wiki-history-rwanda]
- 1 July 1962: **Independence from Belgium** — Rwanda becomes a sovereign republic with Grégoire Kayibanda as first president, ending Belgian rule of Ruanda-Urundi; Burundi gains independence the same day. [wiki-history-rwanda, ebsco-rise-rwanda]
- 1963–1964: **Reprisals against the Tutsi** — An incursion by Tutsi exiles is met with mass killings of Tutsi civilians and a further exodus, setting a pattern of cyclical anti-Tutsi violence. [wiki-history-rwanda, un-historical-background]

Figures:

- Grégoire Kayibanda (1924–1976) — First president of Rwanda. Leader of the Parmehutu and architect of the Hutu 'Social Revolution', he became independent Rwanda's first president in 1962 and ruled until he was overthrown in 1973.

## Habyarimana, Civil War and Arusha — 1973 – 1994

Juvénal Habyarimana's two decades in power ended in a civil war with the exiles' Rwandan Patriotic Front, a fragile peace, and the eve of catastrophe.

- 5 July 1973: **Habyarimana seizes power** — Defence minister Juvénal Habyarimana overthrows President Kayibanda in a coup and establishes a one-party state under the MRND. [wiki-history-rwanda, eb-habyarimana]
- 1 October 1990: **RPF invasion begins the civil war** — The Rwandan Patriotic Front, composed largely of Tutsi exiles from Uganda, invades northern Rwanda, igniting a civil war and intensifying official anti-Tutsi propaganda. [un-historical-background, ushmm-massacre]
- August 1993: **Arusha Accords signed** — Government and RPF sign the Arusha peace agreement promising power-sharing and refugee return; a UN force (UNAMIR) deploys, but extremists reject the deal and plan genocide. [un-historical-background, ushmm-massacre]
- 6 April 1994: **Habyarimana's plane shot down** — A surface-to-air missile downs the plane carrying President Habyarimana and Burundi's president over Kigali, killing all aboard; extremists seize on it to unleash the genocide. [ushmm-massacre, un-prevent-genocide, eb-habyarimana]

Figures:

- Juvénal Habyarimana (1937–1994) — President of Rwanda (1973–1994). Seized power in 1973 and ruled for over two decades; his refusal to allow refugees to return helped provoke the civil war, and his death when his plane was shot down on 6 April 1994 triggered the genocide.

## The Genocide against the Tutsi — April – July 1994

In roughly 100 days an estimated 800,000 to one million people, overwhelmingly Tutsi, were murdered in a planned campaign of extermination.

- 7 April 1994: **The killing begins** — Within hours of the plane crash, the Presidential Guard and Interahamwe militias begin murdering Tutsi and moderate Hutu in Kigali, using roadblocks and prepared lists; the violence spreads nationwide. [un-prevent-genocide, ushmm-massacre, ictr-genocide]
- April–July 1994: **Genocide against the Tutsi** — Over roughly 100 days an estimated 800,000 to one million people, overwhelmingly Tutsi, are systematically murdered, and an estimated 150,000–250,000 women are raped. [un-prevent-genocide, un-historical-background, ushmm-massacre, ictr-genocide]
- July 1994: **RPF victory ends the genocide** — Paul Kagame's Rwandan Patriotic Front captures Kigali and most of the country by mid-July, defeating the genocidal regime and halting the mass killing. [un-historical-background, ushmm-massacre, wiki-history-rwanda]

Figures:

- Paul Kagame (born 1957) — Commander of the RPF; later president of Rwanda. A Tutsi refugee raised in Uganda, he led the Rwandan Patriotic Front whose 1994 offensive ended the genocide; he became Rwanda's de facto leader and, from 2000, its president.

## Aftermath, Justice and Reconstruction — 1994 – present

From a shattered country came a refugee crisis, an international tribunal, the gacaca courts, reconstruction and the long, tightly controlled rule of Paul Kagame.

- 1994 onward: **Refugee crisis and the Congo wars** — Over a million Hutu flee to camps in Zaire and beyond; the presence of armed génocidaires there draws Rwanda into the Congo wars from 1996, which kill millions across the region. [un-historical-background, wiki-history-rwanda]
- November 1994: **International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda** — The UN Security Council establishes the ICTR in Arusha, which indicts 93 people and delivers landmark genocide convictions before closing in 2015. [ictr-genocide, un-prevent-genocide]
- 2002–2012: **The gacaca community courts** — Rwanda revives the traditional gacaca courts to try genocide cases locally; by their closure in June 2012 they have heard more than 1.9 million cases. [eb-gacaca, marquette-justice]
- 2000: **Kagame becomes president** — Paul Kagame formally assumes the presidency and consolidates power, presiding over reconstruction and growth alongside tight control of political life. [wiki-history-rwanda, ushmm-massacre]

Figures:

- Paul Kagame (born 1957) — President of Rwanda (from 2000). Leader of the RPF that ended the genocide and Rwanda's dominant figure ever since; credited with reconstruction and development but criticised for authoritarian rule and the repression of dissent.
