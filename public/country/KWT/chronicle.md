# The Chronicle of Kuwait: From the Bronze Age island of Dilmun and Alexander's Ikaros to a pearling port, an oil-funded welfare state, and the war that defined a nation.

Founding: Independence from Britain — 19 June 1961. On 19 June 1961 the ruler Sheikh Abdullah Al-Salim Al-Sabah and the British Political Resident in the Persian Gulf exchanged letters terminating the 1899 protectorate agreement, and Kuwait declared itself a fully sovereign and independent state. The new emirate's deeper roots lie in the founding of Kuwait town in the early 18th century and the choice of the Al Sabah family to rule around 1752, while the bay itself had been settled since the Bronze Age civilization of Dilmun on nearby Failaka island.

Kuwait occupies a sheltered bay at the head of the Persian Gulf, a crossroads where Bronze Age Dilmun traders and, later, the Greeks of Alexander's empire left their mark on Failaka island. The modern state grew from a small settlement founded in the early 18th century, where the Bani Utub clans chose the Al Sabah family to rule around 1752, building a prosperity on pearling, shipbuilding, and trade. A 1899 agreement with Britain shielded the sheikhdom from Ottoman absorption, and the discovery of oil transformed a poor port into one of the world's wealthiest states after full independence on 19 June 1961. Kuwait endured a seven-month Iraqi occupation in 1990 and was liberated in the 1991 Gulf War, after which it rebuilt and remains a constitutional emirate with the Gulf's oldest elected parliament.

## Failaka: Dilmun and the Greek Island of Ikaros — c. 2000 BCE - 2nd century CE

Long before Kuwait City existed, the island of Failaka in Kuwait Bay was a thriving outpost of the Bronze Age Dilmun culture and, after Alexander the Great, a fortified Greek settlement his sailors named Ikaros.

- c. 2000 BCE: **Dilmun settlement on Failaka** — Failaka island became a flourishing outpost of the Bronze Age Dilmun trading culture, with a town, temple, palace, and one of the largest concentrations of Dilmun seals known. [unesco-failaka, unesco-saad-saeed]
- late 4th century BCE: **Alexander's fleet names the island Ikaros** — During the exploration of the Gulf by Alexander the Great's navy, the admiral Nearchus is credited with naming Failaka 'Ikaros' after an Aegean island. [unesco-failaka, archaeology-ikaros]
- 3rd century BCE: **A Seleucid Greek fortress rises on Ikaros** — Under the Seleucid successors of Alexander, a fortified Greek settlement with stone walls and two temples was built on Failaka, the only standing Hellenistic stronghold in the Gulf. [unesco-failaka, archaeology-ikaros]
- 1970: **A Greek inscription confirms Ikaros** — A Danish archaeological expedition uncovered a forty-four-line Greek inscription on Failaka, settling the identification of the island with the Ikaros of ancient sources. [archaeology-ikaros]

## The Founding of Kuwait and the Rise of the Al Sabah — c. 1700 - 1896

In the early 18th century migrant clans known as the Bani Utub settled the bay, founded Kuwait town, and chose the Al Sabah family to rule, building a maritime economy on pearling, shipbuilding, and trade.

- early 18th century: **The Bani Utub settle the bay** — Clans of the Bani Utub, part of the Anizah confederation, migrated from the Arabian interior to the Gulf coast and founded the town of Kuwait around its sheltered bay. [britannica-sabah-dynasty, loc-persian-gulf-states]
- c. 1752: **Sabah I becomes the first Al Sabah ruler** — The Bani Utub chose Sabah ibn Jabir, founder of the Al Sabah dynasty, to be their sheikh, beginning a line of rulers that continues to the present day. [britannica-sabah-dynasty]
- 18th-19th century: **A pearling and trade entrepot** — Kuwait grew wealthy as a maritime port, its dhows trading to India and Africa, its shipyards renowned, and its pearling fleets diving the Gulf oyster beds each summer. [loc-persian-gulf-states, fanack-mubarak]

Figures:

- Sabah I bin Jabir Al-Sabah (reigned c. 1752 - 1764) — First ruler of Kuwait. Founder of the Al Sabah dynasty, chosen by the Bani Utub clans to lead the young settlement of Kuwait, beginning a ruling line that endures today.

## Mubarak the Great and the British Agreement — 1896 - 1934

Seizing power in 1896, Mubarak Al-Sabah secured Kuwait against Ottoman absorption through a secret 1899 agreement with Britain, anchoring the sheikhdom's autonomy for the century to come.

- 1896: **Mubarak Al-Sabah seizes power** — Mubarak took control of Kuwait in a palace coup in which the ruling sheikh and his brother were killed, beginning a reign that would shape the modern state. [fanack-mubarak]
- 23 January 1899: **The Anglo-Kuwaiti agreement** — Mubarak signed a secret treaty placing Kuwait's foreign relations under British control in exchange for protection, making the sheikhdom a British protectorate and shielding it from the Ottomans. [fanack-mubarak, britannica-kuwait]
- 1913: **Kuwait's separate status confirmed** — Anglo-Ottoman negotiations recognized Kuwait as an autonomous district under British protection, distinguishing it from the Ottoman territories that would later form Iraq. [britannica-kuwait, loc-persian-gulf-states]

Figures:

- Mubarak Al-Sabah (Mubarak the Great) (c. 1837 - 1915) — Ruler of Kuwait, 1896-1915. The ruler who secured Kuwait's autonomy by concluding the 1899 agreement with Britain, averting Ottoman absorption and laying the political foundations of the modern state.

## Oil, Independence, and the Welfare State — 1934 - 1989

The 1934 oil concession and exports from 1946 turned a pearling port into one of the world's richest states; independence came in 1961, followed by a written constitution and an elected parliament.

- 23 December 1934: **The oil concession is granted** — Sheikh Ahmad al-Jabir Al-Sabah granted a concession to the Kuwait Oil Company, a joint Anglo-American venture, opening the era of petroleum. [britannica-koc, britannica-kuwait]
- 1938: **Discovery of the Burgan field** — Drilling revealed the giant Burgan oil field, one of the world's largest, though development was delayed by the Second World War. [britannica-koc]
- 1946: **First oil exports** — Kuwait began exporting crude oil in 1946, launching the rapid transformation of the pearling port into a wealthy oil state. [britannica-koc, britannica-kuwait]
- 19 June 1961: **Independence from Britain** — Kuwait and Britain ended the 1899 protectorate agreement and Kuwait declared full sovereignty under Sheikh Abdullah Al-Salim Al-Sabah, despite an immediate Iraqi claim. [britannica-kuwait, mei-abdullah-salem]
- 11 November 1962: **Constitution and National Assembly** — A constitution was promulgated and elections in 1963 created the National Assembly, the Gulf's first and longest-serving elected parliament. [mei-abdullah-salem, britannica-kuwait]

Figures:

- Sheikh Abdullah Al-Salim Al-Sabah (1895 - 1965) — Ruler and first Emir of independent Kuwait, 1950-1965. Led Kuwait to independence in 1961 and presided over the 1962 constitution and the creation of an elected National Assembly, earning a reputation as the father of Gulf democracy.

## The Iraqi Invasion and the Liberation of 1991 — 1990 - 1991

On 2 August 1990 Iraq invaded and annexed Kuwait, triggering a seven-month occupation that ended only when a U.S.-led international coalition liberated the country in the Gulf War of 1991.

- 2 August 1990: **Iraq invades and annexes Kuwait** — About one hundred thousand Iraqi troops overran Kuwait in hours; Saddam Hussein declared the country annexed, and the ruling family fled to Saudi Arabia. [history-state-gulfwar, history-state-firstgulf]
- August-November 1990: **The UN condemns and authorizes force** — The Security Council passed Resolution 660 demanding withdrawal and Resolution 678 authorizing all necessary means to expel Iraq if it did not leave by 15 January 1991. [history-state-gulfwar]
- 16 January - 28 February 1991: **Operation Desert Storm liberates Kuwait** — A U.S.-led coalition launched an air campaign in mid-January and a ground assault on 24 February, expelling Iraqi forces and liberating Kuwait by late February. [history-state-gulfwar, history-state-firstgulf]
- February-November 1991: **Retreating Iraqis torch the oil fields** — As they withdrew, Iraqi forces set fire to and damaged over 700 Kuwaiti oil wells, causing a vast environmental disaster that took until November to fully extinguish. [adst-oil-fires]

## Reconstruction and Modern Kuwait — 1991 - present

After liberation Kuwait rebuilt at remarkable speed, restored its oil industry and parliament, and entered the 21st century as a wealthy emirate balancing dynastic rule with a contentious elected assembly.

- 1991-1992: **Rapid post-war reconstruction** — International teams capped the burning wells and Kuwait restored oil output to pre-war capacity within about a year, while the UN set up a commission for Iraqi reparations. [adst-oil-fires, britannica-kuwait]
- 2006: **Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad becomes emir** — The veteran foreign minister Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah acceded as emir, a reign noted for diplomacy and humanitarian engagement until his death in 2020. [carnegie-sabah, britannica-kuwait]
- December 2023: **Sheikh Mishal accedes as emir** — After the deaths of Sheikh Sabah in 2020 and Sheikh Nawaf in 2023, Sheikh Mishal Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah became ruler of Kuwait. [carnegie-suspension]
- May 2024: **Parliament dissolved and constitution suspended** — Emir Mishal dissolved the National Assembly and suspended several constitutional articles for up to four years, pausing the Gulf's oldest elected legislature. [carnegie-suspension]

Figures:

- Sheikh Sabah Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah (1929 - 2020) — Emir of Kuwait, 2006-2020. Long-serving foreign minister who became emir in 2006 and was widely respected as a mediator in Gulf and Arab disputes and an advocate of humanitarian aid.
