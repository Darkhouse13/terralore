# The Chronicle of Haiti: From the Taino island of Quisqueya and the slave colony of Saint-Domingue to the world's first Black republic.

Founding: Declaration of Independence — 1 January 1804. On 1 January 1804 at Gonaives, Jean-Jacques Dessalines proclaimed the independence of the former French colony of Saint-Domingue, restoring the Taino-derived name Haiti. It followed thirteen years of revolution (1791-1804) in which the colony's enslaved majority defeated French, Spanish, and British forces and a large expedition sent by Napoleon. Haiti became the second independent nation in the Americas after the United States and the first republic in history founded by formerly enslaved people.

Haiti occupies the western third of Hispaniola, the Caribbean island the Taino called Quisqueya, where Christopher Columbus landed in 1492 and where Spanish and then French colonisers built Saint-Domingue, the richest colony on earth, on sugar, coffee, and the mass enslavement of Africans. Between 1791 and 1804 the enslaved majority waged the only successful large-scale slave revolt in history, and on 1 January 1804 Jean-Jacques Dessalines proclaimed independence, making Haiti the second sovereign nation in the Americas and the first Black republic. Its sovereignty was burdened by a crippling 1825 indemnity demanded by France, chronic instability, a United States occupation (1915-1934), and the Duvalier dictatorships (1957-1986). The catastrophic 2010 earthquake and, since the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moise, a spiral of state collapse and gang violence have defined Haiti's recent history.

## Quisqueya: The Taino and Spanish Hispaniola — c. 600 - 1697

The Arawak-speaking Taino had settled the island they called Quisqueya long before Columbus's 1492 landing inaugurated Spanish colonisation and the rapid destruction of the Indigenous population.

- 6 Dec 1492: **Columbus lands on Hispaniola** — Christopher Columbus reaches the Taino island of Quisqueya and names it La Isla Espanola, beginning Spanish colonisation of the Americas. [loc-columbus-taino, britannica-hispaniola]
- early 16th c.: **Collapse of the Taino** — Forced labour, violence, and Old World disease devastate the Indigenous population within a generation of contact. [britannica-history-haiti, loc-columbus-taino]

## Saint-Domingue: The Richest Colony on Earth — 1697 - 1791

Spain ceded the western third of Hispaniola to France in 1697, and the colony of Saint-Domingue grew into the world's wealthiest plantation economy, built on sugar, coffee, and the mass enslavement of Africans.

- 1697 CE: **France gains Saint-Domingue** — The Treaty of Rijswijk transfers the western third of Hispaniola from Spain to France, creating the colony of Saint-Domingue. [britannica-rijswijk, britannica-saint-domingue]
- by 1789: **World's richest colony** — Saint-Domingue, with roughly half a million enslaved Africans, has become the leading producer of sugar and coffee and France's most valuable colony. [britannica-saint-domingue, britannica-haitian-revolution]

## The Haitian Revolution and Independence — 1791 - 1804

The only successful large-scale slave revolt in history overthrew French rule, defeated Napoleon's army, and on 1 January 1804 created the world's first Black republic.

- Aug 1791: **The slave uprising begins** — A massive revolt of the enslaved erupts on the northern plain of Saint-Domingue, opening the Haitian Revolution. [britannica-haitian-revolution]
- 1802 CE: **Napoleon's expedition; Toussaint deported** — Napoleon sends an army to restore slavery; Toussaint Louverture is seized and deported to France, where he dies in 1803. [britannica-toussaint, britannica-haitian-revolution]
- 1 Jan 1804: **Independence proclaimed** — Jean-Jacques Dessalines declares independence at Gonaives, founding Haiti as the world's first Black republic. [britannica-dessalines, britannica-history-haiti]

Figures:

- Toussaint Louverture (c. 1743-1803) — Revolutionary leader, governor-general. Formerly enslaved military genius who led the revolution to the brink of independence, abolished slavery in the colony, and governed Saint-Domingue before being deported by Napoleon to die in a French prison.
- Jean-Jacques Dessalines (c. 1758-1806) — Independence leader, first ruler of Haiti. Toussaint's lieutenant who led the final defeat of the French and proclaimed Haitian independence on 1 January 1804, ruling as Emperor Jacques I until his assassination in 1806.

## Kingdom, Republic, and the Indemnity — 1804 - 1915

After Dessalines's death the young nation split between Christophe's northern kingdom and a southern republic, while a crippling 1825 indemnity demanded by France and chronic instability shaped the nineteenth century.

- 1811 CE: **Christophe crowned King Henry I** — Henry Christophe proclaims a kingdom in northern Haiti and builds the Citadelle Laferriere; the south remains a republic under Petion. [britannica-christophe, unesco-national-history-park]
- 1825 CE: **The French indemnity** — France recognises Haitian independence only in exchange for an indemnity of 150 million francs, burdening Haiti with crippling debt. [britannica-history-haiti]
- 1821 CE: **Boyer unifies Hispaniola** — After Santo Domingo declares independence, Haitian forces move east and govern the whole island — a republic of former slaves administering the oldest European colony in the Americas. [bbc-haiti]
- 1844 CE: **Dominican Republic secedes** — The eastern, Spanish-speaking part of Hispaniola breaks from Haitian rule to form the independent Dominican Republic. [britannica-hispaniola]
- 1849-1859: **The Second Empire of Faustin Soulouque** — General Soulouque makes himself emperor and spends his reign in repeated attempts to reconquer the east. [bbc-haiti]
- 12 Jul 1862: **The United States recognises Haiti** — Fifty-eight years after independence — and only in the middle of its own civil war — Washington recognises the republic a slave revolt had founded. [statedept-haiti, bbc-haiti]

Figures:

- Henry Christophe (1767-1820) — King Henry I of northern Haiti. Revolutionary general who ruled the north as King Henry I from 1811, creating a court and nobility and ordering the building of the colossal Citadelle Laferriere before his death in 1820.

## Occupation and Dictatorship — 1915 - 1986

A United States military occupation lasting nineteen years was followed, after decades of instability, by the long and repressive Duvalier dictatorships of Papa Doc and Baby Doc.

- 1915-1934: **United States occupation** — U.S. Marines occupy Haiti for nineteen years, controlling its finances and building infrastructure amid widespread resentment and resistance. [history-state-occupation]
- 1957 CE: **Papa Doc Duvalier takes power** — Francois Duvalier becomes president and establishes a personal dictatorship enforced by the Tontons Macoutes, declaring himself president for life. [britannica-duvaliers]
- 1964 CE: **President for Life** — Duvalier proclaims himself president for life and crushes an uprising at Jeremie with hundreds killed; his anti-communism keeps American support flowing. [bbc-haiti]
- 7 Feb 1986: **Fall of Baby Doc** — A popular uprising drives Jean-Claude 'Baby Doc' Duvalier into exile, ending a 29-year family dictatorship in which an estimated 40,000-60,000 Haitians were killed. [britannica-duvaliers, bbc-haiti]

Figures:

- Francois Duvalier (Papa Doc) (1907-1971) — President-for-life of Haiti. Physician who seized dictatorial power in 1957 and ruled until his death through the Tontons Macoutes and a cult of personality, in a regime associated with widespread repression.

## Democracy, Disaster, and Collapse — 1986 - present

Turbulent transitions around Jean-Bertrand Aristide, the catastrophic 2010 earthquake, and the post-2021 collapse into gang rule have defined Haiti's most recent decades.

- Dec 1990: **Aristide elected** — Jean-Bertrand Aristide wins Haiti's first free presidential election; he is overthrown in 1991 and restored in 1994. [britannica-aristide]
- 1994 CE: **Twenty thousand US troops restore Aristide** — An American-led intervention reverses the 1991 coup and returns the elected president to office. [bbc-haiti]
- 2004 CE: **Aristide leaves again; the UN deploys** — An armed rebellion drives Aristide from the country a second time; US Marines land and a UN stabilisation force takes over, staying until 2019. [bbc-haiti]
- 12 Jan 2010: **Catastrophic earthquake** — A magnitude 7.0 earthquake devastates Port-au-Prince; death-toll estimates vary widely and remain disputed, with around three million people affected. [britannica-earthquake]
- Oct 2016: **Hurricane Matthew** — The strongest storm to strike the region in a decade kills hundreds and destroys thousands of homes. [bbc-haiti]
- 7 Jul 2021: **Assassination of President Moise** — President Jovenel Moise is assassinated at his home, accelerating Haiti's slide into gang rule and state collapse. [un-news-haiti-2025]
- 14 Aug 2021: **A second great earthquake** — Five weeks after the assassination, a magnitude 7.2 quake in the south-west kills some 2,200 people. [bbc-haiti]
- 2024-2026: **Transitional council and security mission** — A transitional presidential council and a Kenyan-led security mission seek to restore order as gangs control most of the capital and a strengthened UN-authorised force is approved. [un-news-haiti-2025]

Figures:

- Jean-Bertrand Aristide (born 1953) — President of Haiti. Former Catholic priest and Haiti's first freely elected president, twice ousted from power, whose turbulent career framed the country's fraught transition to democracy.
