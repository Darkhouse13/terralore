Founding: Proclamation of the Republic of Latvia — 18 November 1918. Modern Latvian statehood dates to 18 November 1918, when the People's Council of Latvia proclaimed an independent, democratic Republic of Latvia in Riga as the Russian and German empires collapsed at the end of the First World War. The new state had to fight a War of Independence against both Soviet and German forces before its sovereignty was secured; on 11 August 1920 Soviet Russia signed the Treaty of Riga, by which it renounced all sovereign rights over Latvia and its people. This first republic was destroyed by Soviet occupation and annexation in 1940, but Latvia's continuity as a state was never recognised as extinguished by much of the world, and independence was restored by declaration on 4 May 1990 and fully on 21 August 1991.

Latvia's history is shaped by its position on the eastern Baltic shore, a meeting point of Baltic and Finnic peoples, German crusaders, and the great powers that ringed the sea. In the thirteenth century the Northern Crusades brought German bishops and military orders who founded Riga in 1201 and forged the church-state of Terra Mariana, binding the Latvian and Livonian tribes to Latin Christendom. For centuries the land and its great Hanseatic port passed between rulers, the Livonian Order, Poland-Lithuania, Sweden, the Duchy of Courland and, from the early eighteenth century, the Russian Empire, while a Baltic German nobility dominated the countryside. A national awakening in the nineteenth century gave the Latvian people a modern sense of nationhood, and on 18 November 1918 an independent Republic of Latvia was proclaimed, its sovereignty recognised by Soviet Russia in the Treaty of Riga of 1920. That independence was extinguished in 1940 when the Soviet Union, acting under its secret pact with Nazi Germany, occupied and annexed the country, an act the Western powers never recognised; occupation, deportation, the Holocaust and decades of Soviet rule followed. Sustained by the doctrine of state continuity and by mass movements such as the Baltic Way, Latvians declared the restoration of independence in 1990, achieved it fully in 1991, and anchored the renewed state in the European Union and NATO in 2004.

## Baltic Tribes, the Northern Crusades and Terra Mariana — c. 1100 – 1561

German crusaders and missionaries reached the eastern Baltic, founded Riga and conquered the Latvian and Livonian tribes, binding the land into the church-state of Terra Mariana.

- 1201: **Foundation of Riga by Bishop Albert** — Bishop Albert of Buxhoevden founded Riga at the mouth of the Daugava as the base for the German mission and conquest, the city that became the centre of crusader Livonia and a great Hanseatic port. [latvia-history-britannica, riga-unesco, history-of-riga-rop]
- 1202: **Order of the Brothers of the Sword founded** — With papal sanction Bishop Albert established the Order of the Brothers of the Sword to subjugate and convert the Baltic tribes, launching decades of crusading warfare in Livonia. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]
- 1237: **Absorption into the Teutonic Order** — After heavy defeats the Sword Brothers were merged into the Teutonic Order as the autonomous Livonian Order; by this time the Latvian and Livonian tribal lands had largely been conquered and bound into Terra Mariana. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]
- 1282: **Riga joins the Hanseatic League** — Riga entered the Hanseatic League, the powerful association of northern European merchant towns, cementing its prosperity as the chief trading city between Russia and western Europe. [riga-unesco, mfa-history-latvia]

Figures:

- Bishop Albert of Riga (c. 1165 – 1229) — Bishop and founder of Riga. A canon of Bremen sent to Christianise Livonia, Albert founded Riga in 1201 and the Order of the Brothers of the Sword in 1202, becoming the chief organiser of the German conquest of the Latvian and Livonian lands.
- Meinhard of Üxküll (c. 1130 – 1196) — First missionary bishop in Livonia. An Augustinian monk who arrived on the Daugava around 1180 and was consecrated the first bishop of Üxküll, beginning the German Christian mission that the crusading orders would carry forward by force.

## Poland, Sweden, Courland and the Russian Empire — 1561 – 1795

After the fall of Livonia the land was fought over by Poland-Lithuania, Sweden and Russia, while the Duchy of Courland flourished and the whole region passed at last to the Russian Empire.

- 1561: **Dissolution of Livonia and the Duchy of Courland** — The collapse of the Livonian Order during the Livonian War split the land: Livonia passed under Polish-Lithuanian rule, while Gotthard Kettler founded the vassal Duchy of Courland and Semigallia. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]
- 1621: **Swedish conquest of Riga** — Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden captured Riga and most of Livonia from Poland-Lithuania, opening the period of Swedish rule remembered by Latvians as the 'good Swedish times'. [latvia-history-britannica, history-of-riga-rop]
- c. 1651–1660: **Courland's overseas colonies** — Under Duke Jacob Kettler the Duchy of Courland founded short-lived colonies on the Gambia River and on Tobago, a striking commercial venture by a small Baltic state before its decline. [courland-tandf]
- 1710: **Russia takes Riga** — During the Great Northern War the forces of Peter the Great captured Riga from Sweden; the cession of Swedish Livonia was confirmed at the Peace of Nystad in 1721, bringing the region into the Russian Empire. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]
- 1795: **Courland annexed by Russia** — In the third partition of Poland-Lithuania the Duchy of Courland was incorporated into the Russian Empire, completing Russian rule over all the Latvian lands. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]

Figures:

- Gotthard Kettler (1517 – 1587) — Last master of the Livonian Order, first Duke of Courland. Presiding over the dissolution of the Livonian Order in 1561, Kettler secularised its western lands and became the first duke of Courland and Semigallia, founding the dynasty that ruled the duchy for two centuries.
- Jacob Kettler (1610 – 1681) — Duke of Courland. Under Duke Jacob the Duchy of Courland reached its commercial peak, building a fleet and founding colonies on the Gambia and Tobago, before his capture by Sweden in the 1650s broke the venture.

## National Awakening, Independence and the First Republic — 1795 – 1940

A nineteenth-century national awakening gave the Latvian people a modern identity; out of the ruin of empire came an independent republic, recognised by treaty and then turned authoritarian under Ulmanis.

- c. 1850–1880: **The First National Awakening** — The Young Latvians led a cultural revival that championed the Latvian language and folklore against Baltic German dominance, creating the basis for a modern Latvian nation. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]
- 18 Nov 1918: **Proclamation of the Republic of Latvia** — As the empires collapsed at the end of the First World War, the People's Council of Latvia proclaimed an independent, democratic Republic of Latvia in Riga. [latvia-history-britannica, mfa-history-latvia, latvia-may4-latvia-eu]
- 11 Aug 1920: **Latvian-Soviet Treaty of Riga** — Concluding the War of Independence, Soviet Russia signed the Treaty of Riga, in which it voluntarily and irrevocably renounced all sovereign rights over the Latvian people and territory, recognising Latvia's independence. [mfa-treaty1920, latvia-history-britannica]
- 1922: **Adoption of the Satversme** — Latvia adopted its democratic constitution, the Satversme, in 1922, establishing the institutions of the parliamentary first republic. [mfa-history-latvia]
- 15 May 1934: **The Ulmanis coup** — Prime Minister Kārlis Ulmanis staged a self-coup, suspending the constitution, dissolving the Saeima and the parties, and establishing an authoritarian regime that lasted until 1940. [ulmanis-britannica, mfa-history-latvia]

Figures:

- Rainis (1865 – 1929) — National poet and writer. Pen name of Jānis Pliekšāns, Rainis was the foremost Latvian poet and dramatist of the awakening, associated with the New Current; his patriotic verse became central to the modern Latvian literary and national identity.
- Kārlis Ulmanis (1877 – 1942) — Statesman and authoritarian leader. Latvia's first head of government in 1918 and a dominant figure of the first republic, Ulmanis seized power in the coup of 1934 and ruled authoritatively until the Soviet occupation of 1940; deported to Russia, he died in captivity.

## Occupation, War and the Holocaust — 1939 – 1945

Assigned to the Soviet sphere by the Nazi-Soviet pact, Latvia was occupied and annexed in 1940, its people deported and murdered under successive Soviet and Nazi regimes.

- 23 Aug 1939: **The Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact** — The Nazi-Soviet non-aggression pact and its secret protocol assigned Latvia and the other Baltic states to the Soviet sphere of influence, sealing the country's fate in the coming war. [mrp-russias-periphery, occupation-museum]
- Jun–Aug 1940: **Soviet occupation and annexation** — The Red Army occupied Latvia in June 1940; after a staged election a puppet parliament requested incorporation, and the Soviet Union annexed the country in August 1940. [occupation-museum, mfa-history-latvia, welles-state-continuity]
- 13–14 Jun 1941: **The June 1941 deportations** — The Soviet regime deported some fifteen thousand people from Latvia to Siberia in a single operation, targeting the country's political, economic and intellectual elite; many never returned. [occupation-museum, mfa-history-latvia]
- 30 Nov & 8 Dec 1941: **The Rumbula massacre** — Under the Nazi occupation, SS units led by Friedrich Jeckeln shot about twenty-five thousand Jews, chiefly from the Riga ghetto, in the Rumbula forest, among the largest single massacres of the Holocaust. [rumbula-ushmm]

Figures:

- Friedrich Jeckeln (1895 – 1946) — SS commander, perpetrator. The senior SS and police leader who organised the Rumbula massacre of November-December 1941 and other mass killings in the occupied east; he was tried and executed in Riga in 1946.
- Paulis Lazda (1938 – 2020) — Historian, museum founder. A Latvian-American historian whose 1993 proposal created the Museum of the Occupation of Latvia, dedicated to documenting the fifty-one years of Soviet and Nazi occupation and remembering their victims.

## Soviet Rule, Restoration and the Return to Europe — 1945 – present

Through decades of Soviet rule and deportation Latvia's statehood survived in law and memory, until the Baltic Way and the restoration of 1990-1991 brought independence and a place in the European Union and NATO.

- Mar 1949: **The March 1949 deportations** — In Operation Priboi the Soviet authorities deported tens of thousands of Latvians to Siberia, many of them farmers resisting collectivisation, in the largest single act of repression of the postwar years. [occupation-museum, mfa-history-latvia]
- 23 Aug 1989: **The Baltic Way** — On the fiftieth anniversary of the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact, about two million people formed a human chain from Tallinn through Riga to Vilnius, demanding the restoration of Baltic independence. [baltic-way-baltdefcol, occupation-museum]
- 4 May 1990: **Declaration on the Restoration of Independence** — The Supreme Council of Latvia adopted the declaration restoring independence, reaffirming the 1918 constitution and declaring the 1940 Soviet occupation illegal. [latvia-may4-latvia-eu, occupation-museum]
- 21 Aug 1991: **Full restoration of independence** — After the collapse of the Moscow coup, the Supreme Council adopted a constitutional law fully restoring the independence of the Republic of Latvia, soon recognised internationally. [latvia-may4-latvia-eu, mfa-history-latvia]
- 29 Mar & 1 May 2004: **Accession to NATO and the European Union** — Latvia became a member of NATO on 29 March 2004 and of the European Union on 1 May 2004, anchoring the restored state firmly within Western institutions. [nato-latvia-mod, latvia-history-britannica]
- 2007: **Latvia-Russia border treaty** — The border treaty with Russia entered into force, fixing the frontier and renouncing Latvia's claim to the Abrene district transferred to the Russian SFSR in 1944, while the Constitutional Court reaffirmed state continuity. [abrene-baltictimes, satv-court-2007]

Figures:

- Vaira Vīķe-Freiberga (born 1937) — President of Latvia. A scholar who returned from exile to lead the restored republic, Vīķe-Freiberga served as president from 1999 to 2007 and was a leading advocate of Latvia's accession to the European Union and NATO in 2004.
- Anatolijs Gorbunovs (born 1942) — Chairman of the Supreme Council. As chairman of the Supreme Council he presided over the adoption of the 4 May 1990 Declaration on the Restoration of Independence, a central figure in Latvia's peaceful transition from Soviet rule.
