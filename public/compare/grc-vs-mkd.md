## Entangled histories (8 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 359–336 BCE: **Reign of Philip II of Macedon** — Philip II reformed the Macedonian army and won mastery over the Greek city-states at Chaeronea, laying the foundations for the empire his son Alexander the Great would build across Asia. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Philip II, king of Macedonia; Macedonia (region, Europe): Map, History & Facts)
- 336–323 BCE: **Conquests of Alexander the Great** — Alexander III led Macedonian and Greek forces in the conquest of the Persian empire, carrying Hellenistic civilization from Egypt to the Indus before his death at Babylon in 323 BCE. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Alexander the Great)
- 1912–1913: **The Balkan Wars and the partition of Macedonia** — The Balkan Wars ended Ottoman rule and divided historic Macedonia among Greece, Serbia and Bulgaria; the northern Vardar region passed to Serbia, becoming the core of the future republic. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Macedonia (region, Europe): Map, History & Facts; North Macedonia: History (Ancient, Ottoman, Yugoslav))
- 1993: **UN admission as 'FYROM'** — Owing to Greece's objection over the name, the country was admitted to the United Nations under the provisional designation 'the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia'. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: North Macedonia: Independence; A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: North Macedonia)
- 13 Sep 1995: **The Interim Accord with Greece** — Greece and the country signed an Interim Accord under which Greece agreed not to object to its membership of international bodies referred to by the provisional name. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Application of the Interim Accord of 13 September 1995 (the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia v. Greece), Judgment of 5 December 2011; A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: North Macedonia)
- 5 Dec 2011: **ICJ rules against Greece** — The International Court of Justice held that Greece had breached the Interim Accord by objecting to the country's admission to NATO at the 2008 Bucharest summit. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Application of the Interim Accord of 13 September 1995 (the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia v. Greece), Judgment of 5 December 2011)
- 17 Jun 2018: **The Prespa Agreement** — Greece and the country signed the Prespa Agreement, renaming it the Republic of North Macedonia (used erga omnes), terminating the 1995 accord and distinguishing the modern Slavic state from ancient Macedon. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: Prespa Agreement (Final Agreement for the Settlement of the Differences), 17 June 2018; Application of the Interim Accord of 13 September 1995 (the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia v. Greece), Judgment of 5 December 2011)
- 27 Mar 2020: **North Macedonia joins NATO** — After the Prespa Agreement lifted Greece's objection, North Macedonia deposited its instrument of accession and became NATO's thirtieth member on 27 March 2020. (from the North Macedonia chronicle; sources: North Macedonia joins NATO as 30th Ally, 27 March 2020; A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: North Macedonia)
