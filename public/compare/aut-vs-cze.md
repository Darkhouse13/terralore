## Entangled histories (4 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1526: **Bohemia under the Habsburgs** — The Bohemian estates elected Ferdinand I of Habsburg as king, binding the kingdom to the Habsburg monarchy and to Austria and Hungary for nearly four centuries. (from the Czechia chronicle; sources: Czechoslovak history)
- 8 Nov 1620: **Battle of White Mountain** — Imperial and Catholic League armies crushed the Bohemian rebels at White Mountain near Prague, ending Czech estates' power and ushering in Habsburg absolutism and forced Counter-Reformation. (from the Czechia chronicle; sources: Defenestration of Prague (1618); Czechoslovak history)
- 19th century: **The Czech National Revival** — From the late 18th century, scholars led by the historian Frantisek Palacky rebuilt and codified the Czech language and national history, transforming a cultural awakening into a political movement within Austria-Hungary. (from the Czechia chronicle; sources: Frantisek Palacky; Czechoslovak history: National awakening and the rise of constitutionalism)
- 28 Oct 1918: **Foundation of Czechoslovakia** — With Austria-Hungary collapsing, independence was proclaimed in Prague on 28 October 1918; Tomas Garrigue Masaryk became the first president of the new multi-ethnic democratic republic. (from the Czechia chronicle; sources: Tomas Masaryk; Foundation of Czechoslovakia)
