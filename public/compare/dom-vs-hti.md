## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1804 CE: **Independence of Haiti** — After defeating France, the formerly enslaved people of Saint-Domingue proclaim Haiti, the first nation founded by a successful slave revolt, reshaping the whole island. (from the Dominican Republic chronicle; sources: Hispaniola)
- 1821 CE: **Boyer unifies Hispaniola** — After Santo Domingo declares independence, Haitian forces move east and govern the whole island — a republic of former slaves administering the oldest European colony in the Americas. (from the Haiti chronicle; sources: Haiti country profile)
- 1822 CE: **Haitian unification of the island** — President Jean-Pierre Boyer occupies the east and unifies all Hispaniola under Haiti for twenty-two years, abolishing slavery but alienating much of the Dominican population. (from the Dominican Republic chronicle; sources: History of the Dominican Republic)
- 27 Feb 1844: **Dominican independence from Haiti** — The Trinitarios rise in Santo Domingo, expel the Haitian garrison, and proclaim the Dominican Republic, the date still marked as national independence day. (from the Dominican Republic chronicle; sources: History of the Dominican Republic; Juan Pablo Duarte)
- 1844 CE: **Dominican Republic secedes** — The eastern, Spanish-speaking part of Hispaniola breaks from Haitian rule to form the independent Dominican Republic. (from the Haiti chronicle; sources: Hispaniola)
- 2013 CE: **Citizenship ruling on Haitian descent** — A constitutional court ruling affecting the nationality of Dominicans of Haitian descent draws international criticism and underscores the fraught relationship with Haiti. (from the Dominican Republic chronicle; sources: History of the Dominican Republic)
