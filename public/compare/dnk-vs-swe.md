## Entangled histories (15 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1387–1412: **The rule of Margaret I** — Margaret I governed Denmark, Norway and Sweden as their effective ruler from the late 1380s until her death in 1412, the architect and guarantor of the Kalmar Union. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Margaret I, queen of Scandinavia; Denmark: Margaret I and the Kalmar Union)
- Jun 1397: **Formation of the Kalmar Union** — Queen Margaret united Denmark, Norway and Sweden under one monarch, her grandnephew Erik of Pomerania, creating a Scandinavian union intended to resist German power but soon dominated by Denmark. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Kalmar Union; Queen Margaret and the Union of Kalmar)
- 1397: **The Kalmar Union** — At Kalmar the magnates of Denmark, Norway and Sweden crowned Erik of Pomerania as common king under the regency of Margaret I, uniting the three Scandinavian kingdoms in a single monarchy. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Kalmar Union; Margaret I, queen of Scandinavia)
- 15th c.: **Swedish revolts and the Sture regents** — Resentment of Danish dominance and of taxes and offices given to royal favourites fuelled recurring Swedish risings, with regents of the Sture family ruling Sweden in defiance of the union crown. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Queen Margaret and the Union of Kalmar; Sweden: History)
- 1520: **The Stockholm Bloodbath** — King Christian II's execution of Swedish nobles after his coronation in Stockholm inflamed Swedish resistance and helped precipitate the collapse of the Kalmar Union. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Kalmar Union)
- 8–10 Nov 1520: **The Stockholm Bloodbath** — After capturing Stockholm and breaking his promise of amnesty, King Christian II of Denmark had some eighty-two Swedish nobles, clergy and citizens condemned as heretics and executed, including Gustav Vasa's father. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Stockholm Bloodbath; The Stockholm blood bath)
- 1521–1523: **The Swedish War of Liberation** — Gustav Vasa raised Dalarna in revolt against Christian II, leading a peasant army that expelled the Danes and was declared regent of Sweden, ending Danish control of the realm. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Gustav I Vasa; Sweden: The early Vasa kings (1523–1611))
- 1523: **Sweden leaves the union** — Gustav Vasa's rising drove out Danish rule and led to Sweden's secession from the Kalmar Union, leaving Denmark joined to Norway alone. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Kalmar Union; Denmark: Margaret I and the Kalmar Union)
- 1563–1720: **The long wars with Sweden** — From the Northern Seven Years' War onward, Denmark and Sweden fought repeatedly for control of the Baltic and the Sound, a rivalry that steadily eroded Danish power in the region. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Denmark: Reformation and war; Treaty of Roskilde (1658))
- 26 Feb 1658: **Treaty of Roskilde** — After Sweden's invasion across the frozen belts, Denmark ceded Scania, Halland, Blekinge, Bornholm and Trondheim; the loss of the eastern provinces was the largest in Danish history. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Treaty of Roskilde (1658); Treaty of Copenhagen)
- 26 Feb 1658: **Treaty of Roskilde** — After Charles X Gustav's invasion of Denmark, the Treaty of Roskilde forced Denmark to cede Scania, Blekinge, Halland and Bohuslän to Sweden, fixing its southern border and marking the empire's territorial peak. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Treaty of Roskilde)
- 1660–1661: **The establishment of absolutism** — In the crisis following defeat by Sweden, Frederick III secured a hereditary and absolute monarchy, a system later codified in the Royal Law (Kongeloven) of 1665. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Denmark: Reformation and war)
- 14 Jan 1814: **Treaty of Kiel and the union with Norway** — Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden by the Treaty of Kiel; Norwegian resistance produced not annexation but a personal union of the two kingdoms under one crown, lasting until 1905. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Treaty of Kiel; Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905)
- 14 Jan 1814: **Treaty of Kiel: the loss of Norway** — Defeated alongside Napoleon, Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden, ending a four-century union, while keeping Iceland, the Faroe Islands and Greenland. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Treaty of Kiel)
- Oct 1943: **Rescue of the Danish Jews** — Warned of a German round-up, Danes spirited more than 7,000 Jews across the sound to neutral Sweden, saving the great majority of the country's Jewish population from deportation. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: German occupation (1940-1945))
