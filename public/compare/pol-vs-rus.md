## Entangled histories (11 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1598-1613: **The Time of Troubles** — After the Rurikid line dies out, Russia descends into the Time of Troubles, a period of famine, civil war, pretenders, and Polish occupation of Moscow that nearly destroys the Russian state. (from the Russia chronicle; sources: Russia)
- 1762-1796: **The reign of Catherine the Great** — Catherine II expands the empire to the Black Sea and through the three Partitions of Poland, patronizes the arts and Enlightenment ideas, and founds the Hermitage collection, while serfdom reaches its most oppressive extent. (from the Russia chronicle; sources: Russia; Catherine the Great)
- 1772–1795: **The Partitions of Poland** — In three stages (1772, 1793, 1795) Russia, Prussia and Austria divided the Commonwealth among themselves, despite the reforming Constitution of 3 May 1791, ending Polish statehood for 123 years. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Partitions of Poland; Constitution of 3 May 1791)
- 1815: **Congress of Vienna and the 'Congress Kingdom'** — The post-Napoleonic settlement re-divided the Polish lands and created a nominally autonomous Kingdom of Poland under the Russian tsar, entrenching foreign rule. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: History of Poland (1795-1918); Partitions of Poland)
- 1830–1831: **The November Uprising** — Poles in the Russian partition rose against the tsar; after initial successes the rising was crushed, bringing reprisals, the end of the Kingdom's autonomy and a wave of political exile. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: History of Poland (1795-1918))
- 1863–1864: **The January Uprising** — A protracted guerrilla insurrection against Russian rule ended in defeat and severe repression, including executions, deportations to Siberia and intensified Russification. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: History of Poland (1795-1918))
- Aug 1920: **Battle of Warsaw** — Polish forces under Pilsudski halted the advance of the Soviet Red Army outside Warsaw, securing the independence of the young republic in the Polish-Soviet War. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Jozef Pilsudski; Partitions of Poland)
- 1 Sep 1939: **German and Soviet invasion of Poland** — Nazi Germany invaded on 1 September, starting the Second World War in Europe; the Soviet Union invaded from the east on 17 September, and the two powers again partitioned Poland. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Invasion of Poland, Fall 1939; Invasion of Poland (1939))
- 1947: **Establishment of communist rule** — Backed by the Soviet Union and rigged elections, a communist government consolidated power, founding the Polish People's Republic within the Soviet bloc. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Lech Walesa; Solidarity (Polish trade union))
- Aug 1980: **Birth of Solidarity** — Strikes at the Gdansk shipyard led by Lech Walesa produced Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc, which rapidly grew to about ten million members. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Solidarity (Polish trade union); Lech Walesa)
- 4 Jun 1989: **Semi-free elections and the fall of communism** — Round Table talks produced partly free elections that Solidarity won overwhelmingly, ending communist rule in Poland and helping trigger the collapse of the Soviet bloc. (from the Poland chronicle; sources: Solidarity (Polish trade union); Lech Walesa)
