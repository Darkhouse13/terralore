## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 30 Oct 1864: **Cession of the duchies** — By the peace ending the German-Danish War, Denmark surrendered the duchies to Prussia and Austria, placing tens of thousands of Danes under German rule and reshaping the southern frontier. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: German-Danish War: War and the final settlement of the Schleswig-Holstein question; The nation state (1848-1915))
- 1864: **The Second Schleswig War** — Prussian and Austrian armies invaded after Denmark sought to incorporate Schleswig; following defeat at Dybbol, Denmark ceded Schleswig, Holstein and Lauenburg, losing about a third of its territory. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: German-Danish War; German-Danish War: War and the final settlement of the Schleswig-Holstein question)
- 15 Jun 1920: **Reunification of Northern Schleswig** — Northern Schleswig came under Danish sovereignty on 15 June 1920, marked by King Christian X's ceremonial ride across the old frontier; the boundary then fixed remains the Danish-German border today. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: The reunification of Denmark in 1920)
- 10 Feb & 14 Mar 1920: **The Schleswig plebiscites** — Under the Treaty of Versailles, Zone 1 (Northern Schleswig) voted about 75 per cent for Denmark and Zone 2 about 80 per cent for Germany, determining where the new border would run. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: The reunification of Denmark in 1920; German-Danish War: War and the final settlement of the Schleswig-Holstein question)
- 9 Apr 1940: **German occupation of Denmark** — Germany invaded on 9 April 1940 and Denmark surrendered within hours; a policy of negotiation gave way in August 1943 to direct German control and an active resistance movement. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: German occupation (1940-1945); A Guide to the History of Recognition, Diplomatic, and Consular Relations: Denmark)
- Oct 1943: **Rescue of the Danish Jews** — Warned of a German round-up, Danes spirited more than 7,000 Jews across the sound to neutral Sweden, saving the great majority of the country's Jewish population from deportation. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: German occupation (1940-1945))
