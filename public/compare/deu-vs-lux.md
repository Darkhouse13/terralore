## Entangled histories (10 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1815: **Congress of Vienna creates the Grand Duchy** — Luxembourg was raised to a Grand Duchy under William I of the Netherlands in personal union, placed within the German Confederation with a Prussian garrison, and lost an eastern slice to Prussia in a second partition. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: The history of the Nassau dynasty)
- 1839: **Treaty of London: the cession to Belgium** — The 1839 Treaty of London partitioned the Grand Duchy a third time, ceding its larger French-speaking western half to the new kingdom of Belgium while confirming the independence of the German-speaking remainder. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: The history of the Nassau dynasty; History of the city of Luxembourg)
- 1842: **Entry into the Zollverein** — The reduced Grand Duchy joined the German customs union in 1842, binding its economy closely to the neighbouring German territories for the rest of the century. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: Luxembourg (1914-1918))
- 11 May 1867: **Treaty of London: neutrality and the razing of the fortress** — After the Luxembourg Crisis between France and Prussia, the powers guaranteed the Grand Duchy's perpetual neutrality, withdrew the Prussian garrison and ordered the fortress dismantled, a demolition completed in 1883. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: The history of the Nassau dynasty; History of the city of Luxembourg)
- 2 Aug 1914: **German invasion in the First World War** — German forces occupied neutral Luxembourg on 2 August 1914 to control its railways for the advance on France, holding the country until November 1918 despite its formal protest. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: Occupation of Luxembourg (1914-1918); Luxembourg (1914-1918))
- Jan 1919: **Abdication of Marie-Adelaide** — Criticised for her conduct under the German occupation, Grand Duchess Marie-Adelaide abdicated in January 1919 in favour of her sister Charlotte, who reigned until 1964. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: Occupation of Luxembourg (1914-1918); The history of the Nassau dynasty)
- 10 May 1940: **Second German invasion and annexation** — The Wehrmacht overran Luxembourg on 10 May 1940; the government and Grand Duchess fled into exile, and the territory was placed under a German civil administration aimed at its full integration into the Reich. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: Occupation and Annexation during the Second World War: the Case of Luxembourg; Luxembourgers general strike against Nazi occupation, 1942)
- 31 Aug 1942: **Conscription and the general strike** — When the occupier imposed German citizenship and Wehrmacht conscription in August 1942, a general strike spread from 31 August, the first in Nazi-occupied Europe; the Germans declared martial law and executed twenty-one strikers. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: Luxembourgers general strike against Nazi occupation, 1942; Occupation and Annexation during the Second World War: the Case of Luxembourg)
- 16 Dec 1944: **Liberation and the Battle of the Bulge** — Allied forces liberated Luxembourg in September 1944, but the German Ardennes offensive launched on 16 December 1944 overran the north of the country, which had to be liberated a second time before war's end. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: The Battle of the Bulge; Luxembourgers general strike against Nazi occupation, 1942)
- 1951: **Founding member of the ECSC** — Following the Schuman Declaration of 9 May 1950, Luxembourg joined Belgium, France, Italy, the Netherlands and West Germany as a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community, operating from 1952. (from the Luxembourg chronicle; sources: The Schuman Declaration, 9 May 1950; The history of the European Union, 1945-59)
