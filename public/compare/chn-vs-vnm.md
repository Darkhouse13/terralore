## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 111 BCE: **Han conquest begins Chinese rule** — The Chinese Han dynasty conquers the region, opening more than a thousand years of direct Chinese administration, taxation, and cultural influence. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Vietnam)
- 40-43 CE: **Revolt of the Trung sisters** — Trung Trac and Trung Nhi lead a rebellion that briefly drives out the Chinese before being defeated in 43 CE; they become lasting symbols of Vietnamese resistance. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Trung Sisters; Vietnam)
- 938 CE: **Battle of Bach Dang and independence** — Ngo Quyen destroys a Southern Han fleet at the Bach Dang River using hidden iron-tipped stakes, ending Chinese rule; he proclaims himself king in 939. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Ngo Quyen; Vietnam)
- 1407 CE: **Ming China occupies Dai Viet** — The Chinese Ming dynasty conquers Dai Viet and imposes two decades of harsh direct rule, provoking widespread resistance. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Le Loi; Vietnam)
- 1771-1789 CE: **Tay Son rebellion** — A peasant-led uprising overthrows the Nguyen and Trinh lords and the Le dynasty, and in 1789 routs an invading Chinese Qing army near Thang Long. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Gia Long; Vietnam)
- 1804 CE: **The name Vietnam adopted** — The unified realm is recognized under the name Vietnam, with its capital at Hue and a centralized administration modeled on Qing China. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Nguyen dynasty; Vietnam)
- 1979 CE: **Sino-Vietnamese War** — China invades northern Vietnam in response to Vietnam's ousting of the Khmer Rouge in Cambodia; the brief but bloody border war deepens Vietnam's isolation. (from the Vietnam chronicle; sources: Vietnam)
