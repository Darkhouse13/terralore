## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1813: **Treaty of Gulistan** — Ending the Russo-Persian war of 1804–13, Persia ceded to Russia the eastern Caucasus khanates from Lankaran to Derbent, including Baku, Shirvan, Ganja and Karabakh. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Treaty of Golestān (Gulistan), 1813; Azerbaijan: Russian suzerainty and the road to independence)
- 1828: **Treaty of Turkmenchay** — The second Russo-Persian war ended with Turkmenchay, confirming Russian rule and adding Nakhichevan and Yerevan, fixing the Aras-river border that still divides the two Azerbaijans. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Treaty of Turkmenchay, 1828; Azerbaijan: Russian suzerainty and the road to independence)
- 1920: **Azerbaijan SSR established** — Following the Red Army's entry into Baku, the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic was formed, becoming a full union republic of the USSR from 1936. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Azerbaijan: Russian suzerainty and the road to independence; Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Georgia: Country Studies (ed. Glenn E. Curtis))
- April 1920: **Soviet annexation** — The Red Army entered Baku in April 1920 against little resistance, ending the democratic republic and bringing Azerbaijan under Soviet rule. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Azerbaijan: Russian suzerainty and the road to independence)
- 1923: **Nagorno-Karabakh autonomous oblast created** — The Armenian-majority mountainous region of Nagorno-Karabakh was constituted as an autonomous oblast within Soviet Azerbaijan, freezing a contested status that would later erupt. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Nagorno-Karabakh: conflicts, map and people; Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Georgia: Country Studies (ed. Glenn E. Curtis))
- 1988: **Karabakh dispute reignites** — Under perestroika, Nagorno-Karabakh's Armenian majority demonstrated for transfer to Armenia, triggering intercommunal violence that grew into open conflict as the Soviet Union collapsed. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Nagorno-Karabakh: conflicts, map and people)
- 1989: **Azerbaijan declares sovereignty** — Azerbaijan declared its sovereignty in 1989 as Soviet authority weakened, a first step toward the restoration of independence two years later. (from the Azerbaijan chronicle; sources: Azerbaijan: Russian suzerainty and the road to independence)
