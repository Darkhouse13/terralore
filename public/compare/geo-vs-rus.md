## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 24 July 1783: **Treaty of Georgievsk** — King Erekle II of Kartli-Kakheti placed his kingdom under Russian protection, ceding control of foreign affairs in exchange for guarantees of its integrity and dynasty against Persia and the Ottomans. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: Treaty of Georgievsk (1783); Georgia: Turkish and Persian domination)
- 1801: **Russia annexes Kartli-Kakheti** — The Russian Empire unilaterally annexed eastern Georgia and abolished the Bagrationi monarchy, beginning the absorption of the Georgian lands that was completed over the following decades. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: History of Georgia; Treaty of Georgievsk (1783))
- 26 May 1918: **Democratic Republic of Georgia proclaimed** — Amid the collapse of the Russian Empire, Georgia proclaimed an independent democratic republic governed by Mensheviks, with a democratic constitution and land reform. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: Georgia: Independent Georgia; History of Georgia)
- Feb–Mar 1921: **Red Army conquest** — The Soviet Red Army invaded, took Tbilisi after heavy fighting and proclaimed the Georgian SSR on 25 February 1921, incorporating Georgia into the Soviet Union. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: Georgia: Independent Georgia; History of Georgia)
- 9 April 1989: **The April 9 tragedy in Tbilisi** — Soviet troops violently broke up a peaceful pro-independence demonstration in Tbilisi, killing about twenty people and galvanising the Georgian national movement. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: Georgia: Independent Georgia)
- Aug 2008: **Russia–Georgia war over South Ossetia** — A five-day war around South Ossetia drew in the Russian military; estimates put the combined dead at about 850 with over 100,000 displaced. Russia then recognised Abkhazia and South Ossetia, a step most states rejected. (from the Georgia chronicle; sources: Georgia: Independent Georgia; South Ossetia)
