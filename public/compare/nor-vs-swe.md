## Entangled histories (10 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- Jun 1397: **Formation of the Kalmar Union** — Queen Margaret united Denmark, Norway and Sweden under one monarch, her grandnephew Erik of Pomerania, creating a Scandinavian union intended to resist German power but soon dominated by Denmark. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Kalmar Union; Queen Margaret and the Union of Kalmar)
- 1397: **The Kalmar Union** — Under Queen Margaret I of Denmark, Denmark, Norway and Sweden were united in the Kalmar Union, with Erik of Pomerania crowned common king; Norway became the weakest of the three partners. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: Kalmarunionen)
- 1537: **Norway reduced to a Danish province** — After Sweden left the union, Norway remained tied to Denmark and was governed from Copenhagen, becoming in effect a Danish province while keeping its own laws and church. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: Kalmarunionen; Norway: The Treaty of Kiel)
- 1810: **Bernadotte chosen as heir** — The French marshal Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte was elected Swedish crown prince, founding the Bernadotte dynasty; he ruled from 1818 as Charles XIV John, king of Sweden and Norway. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Treaty of Kiel; Sweden: History)
- 14 Jan 1814: **The Treaty of Kiel** — Defeated in the Napoleonic Wars, Denmark-Norway was forced to cede Norway: by the Treaty of Kiel the Danish king renounced all claims to Norway in favour of the king of Sweden. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: Kieltraktaten; Treaty of Kiel; Norway: The Treaty of Kiel)
- 14 Jan 1814: **Treaty of Kiel and the union with Norway** — Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden by the Treaty of Kiel; Norwegian resistance produced not annexation but a personal union of the two kingdoms under one crown, lasting until 1905. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Treaty of Kiel; Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905)
- 4 Nov 1814: **Union with Sweden** — After a short war, Sweden accepted the Eidsvoll constitution and the Storting voted to enter a personal union with Sweden under a shared king, with Norway keeping its parliament and laws. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: The story of 1814 in 1-2-3; Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905)
- 1884: **Parliamentarism and the consular dispute** — The introduction of parliamentary government strengthened the Storting, while the conflict with Sweden over Norway's right to its own consular service drove the two countries apart. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905)
- 7 Jun 1905: **Dissolution of the union with Sweden** — The Storting declared the union with Sweden dissolved; confirmed by referendum and settled peacefully at Karlstad, it made Norway a fully sovereign state for the first time in centuries. (from the Norway chronicle; sources: Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905; Haakon VII)
- 1905: **Peaceful dissolution of the union with Norway** — After Norway's parliament declared the union dissolved, Sweden agreed at Karlstad to a peaceful separation, recognising Norwegian independence without war in a settlement held up as a diplomatic model. (from the Sweden chronicle; sources: Norway: The union conflict, 1859–1905)
