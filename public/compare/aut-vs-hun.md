## Entangled histories (13 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 29 Aug 1526: **Battle of Mohács** — Süleyman the Magnificent destroyed the Hungarian army and killed King Louis II, ending the medieval kingdom and opening Hungary to Ottoman and Habsburg domination. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Battle of Mohács (1526))
- 1686: **Reconquest of Buda** — A Habsburg-led Christian coalition retook Buda from the Ottomans, part of the campaign that drove the Turks from Hungary after more than a century. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Battle of Mohács (1526))
- 1697–1718: **Prince Eugene and the conquest of Hungary** — After Vienna's deliverance, Prince Eugene of Savoy won victories such as Zenta (1697) and the capture of Belgrade (1717); the Peace of Karlowitz (1699) secured Habsburg control of Hungary and Transylvania. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: Prince Eugene, the 'noble knight')
- 1699: **Treaty of Karlowitz** — The Ottomans ceded almost all of Hungary to the Habsburgs, reuniting the country but binding it firmly within the Habsburg monarchy. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Battle of Mohács (1526))
- 15 Mar 1848: **The Revolution of 1848** — A bloodless rising in Pest, with Sándor Petőfi among its voices, won the March Laws abolishing censorship and serfdom and establishing a responsible Hungarian government against Habsburg absolutism. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Hungarian Revolution (1848-1849))
- Aug 1849: **Defeat of the war of independence** — After Hungary proclaimed independence under Kossuth, the revolution was crushed by a combined Austrian and Russian army; the surrender brought executions and Kossuth's exile. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Hungarian Revolution (1848-1849))
- 1867: **Austro-Hungarian Compromise (Ausgleich)** — The Ausgleich transformed the empire into the Dual Monarchy, granting Hungary full internal autonomy with its own parliament while keeping defence and foreign affairs common to Austria-Hungary. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Ausgleich (Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867); Hungary: The Dual Monarchy, 1867-1918)
- 1867: **The Austro-Hungarian Compromise** — The Ausgleich of 1867 reorganised the empire into the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary, two states sharing a common monarch, foreign policy, finance and army; Franz Joseph was crowned king of Hungary on 8 June. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: The Compromise with Hungary, 1867; Franz Joseph I)
- 28 Jun 1914: **Assassination at Sarajevo** — Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the throne, and his wife Sophie were assassinated in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip, the spark that led Austria-Hungary to war. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: Sarajevo and the July Crisis)
- 28 Jul 1914: **Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia** — After the July Crisis, Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, triggering a chain of alliances that plunged Europe into the First World War. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: Sarajevo and the July Crisis)
- 1918: **Collapse of Austria-Hungary** — Defeat in the First World War destroyed the Dual Monarchy; amid revolution Hungary passed through a democratic republic and a brief communist regime before the conservative order of Regent Horthy. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Treaty of Trianon; Hungary: The Dual Monarchy, 1867-1918)
- 12 Nov 1918: **Collapse of Austria-Hungary and the First Republic** — As the war was lost the empire disintegrated; Emperor Karl I renounced participation in government on 11 November and the Republic of German-Austria was proclaimed on 12 November 1918, ending Habsburg rule. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: The End of Monarchy, the Birth of New States)
- 10 Sep 1919: **Treaty of Saint-Germain** — Signed for Austria by Karl Renner, the treaty confirmed the breakup of Austria-Hungary, forbade union with Germany without League of Nations approval, limited Austria's army and ceded South Tyrol to Italy. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: 1919: Treaty of Saint-Germain)
