## Entangled histories (8 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- c. 2600 BCE: **Mohenjo-daro flourishes** — Mohenjo-daro, on the Indus in modern Pakistan, becomes one of the civilisation's largest cities and is today a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its Great Bath and citadel. (from the India chronicle; sources: Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro; Indus Valley Civilization)
- 1906: **Founding of the All-India Muslim League** — The All-India Muslim League is founded to represent the political interests of India's Muslims; it would become the engine of the Pakistan Movement. (from the Pakistan chronicle; sources: Pakistan: A Country Study; Mohammed Ali Jinnah)
- 15 Aug 1947: **Independence and Partition** — India becomes independent from Britain under Prime Minister Nehru as the subcontinent is partitioned into India and Pakistan, unleashing mass migration and communal violence. (from the India chronicle; sources: Partition of India; Republic Day, India)
- 14 Aug 1947: **Independence of Pakistan** — Pakistan becomes independent under the Indian Independence Act, with Jinnah as Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan as prime minister, comprising West and East Pakistan. (from the Pakistan chronicle; sources: Mohammed Ali Jinnah; Indian Independence Act)
- 18 Jul 1947: **Indian Independence Act passed** — The British Parliament passes the Indian Independence Act, partitioning British India and providing for the creation of the dominions of India and Pakistan. (from the Pakistan chronicle; sources: Indian Independence Act)
- 1947–1948: **Partition violence and mass migration** — Up to 12–20 million people are uprooted and an estimated one to two million die in massacres and forced migration along the new India–Pakistan border. (from the India chronicle; sources: Partition of India)
- 16 Dec 1971: **Loss of East Pakistan and birth of Bangladesh** — After a brutal war and Indian intervention, Pakistani forces surrender at Dhaka and East Pakistan becomes the independent state of Bangladesh, dismembering the country. (from the Pakistan chronicle; sources: Bangladesh Liberation War; 1971 India-Pakistan War)
- 28 May 1998: **Pakistan conducts nuclear tests** — Following India's tests, Pakistan detonates nuclear devices in Balochistan, openly joining the ranks of nuclear-armed states. (from the Pakistan chronicle; sources: Pakistan: A Country Study)
