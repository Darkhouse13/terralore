## Entangled histories (9 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- c. 1000: **Incorporation into the Kingdom of Hungary** — The Slovak lands were absorbed into the Kingdom of Hungary founded by Stephen I, beginning roughly a thousand years under the Hungarian crown as the region known as 'Upper Hungary'. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: History of Slovakia; Slovakia)
- 1467: **Universitas Istropolitana at Pressburg** — Slovakia's first university, the Universitas Istropolitana, was founded at Pressburg, an early sign of the towns' wealth and learning within the Hungarian kingdom. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: History of Slovakia)
- 1536: **Pressburg made capital of Hungary** — As the Ottomans overran central Hungary, the parliament promoted Pressburg (now Bratislava) to capital of the Kingdom of Hungary, a secure stronghold close to Habsburg Vienna. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: History of Slovakia; Bratislava: Coronation City)
- 1563–1830: **Pressburg the coronation city** — Between 1563 and 1830, eleven Hungarian kings and eight royal consorts were crowned at St Martin's Cathedral in Pressburg, including Maria Theresa in 1741, making the city the ceremonial heart of the kingdom. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: Bratislava: Coronation City)
- 1848–1849: **Slovaks in the revolutions of 1848** — During the revolutions of 1848 Slovak leaders, Štúr among them, set out national demands and raised volunteers, asserting the Slovaks as a distinct people within the Hungarian kingdom. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: Ľudovít Štúr; History of Slovakia)
- 1867: **Magyarization under the Compromise** — After the Austro-Hungarian Compromise the Hungarian government intensified Magyarization, pressing the Magyar language on Slovak schools and public life and denying the Slovaks recognition as a nation. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: Slovakia; Slovak literature)
- 4 Jun 1920: **The Treaty of Trianon** — Hungary lost about two-thirds of its territory and population, leaving roughly three million ethnic Hungarians outside the new borders in Romania, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and elsewhere; the trauma shaped Hungarian politics for a century. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Treaty of Trianon)
- 2 Nov 1938: **First Vienna Award** — Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy awarded Hungary parts of southern Slovakia from Czechoslovakia, the first of the revisionist gains that drew Hungary toward the Axis. (from the Hungary chronicle; sources: Treaty of Trianon)
- 25 Sep 1997: **ICJ judgment on Gabčíkovo–Nagymaros** — The International Court of Justice ruled in the Gabčíkovo–Nagymaros Project case (Hungary/Slovakia) that both states had breached their obligations over the Danube dam treaty of 1977, ordering them to negotiate in good faith. (from the Slovakia chronicle; sources: Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros Project (Hungary/Slovakia); Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros Project, Judgment of 25 September 1997)
