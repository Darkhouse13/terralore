## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 7 Nov 1550: **Execution of Bishop Jón Arason** — Jón Arason, bishop of Hólar and the last Catholic bishop in Iceland, was beheaded with his two sons at Skálholt for resisting the Danish-imposed Reformation, after which Iceland became fully Lutheran. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: Jón Arason; Iceland: History)
- 1602–1786: **The Danish trade monopoly** — The Danish crown confined all Icelandic trade to a small group of licensed Danish merchants, depressing prices and returns and impoverishing the island for nearly two centuries. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: Iceland: History; History of Iceland, 1840s to the Second World War)
- 14 Jan 1814: **Treaty of Kiel: the loss of Norway** — Defeated alongside Napoleon, Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden, ending a four-century union, while keeping Iceland, the Faroe Islands and Greenland. (from the Denmark chronicle; sources: Treaty of Kiel)
- 1874: **Constitution on the settlement millennium** — On the thousandth anniversary of the settlement, Denmark granted Iceland a constitution and increased control over domestic affairs, though executive power remained with the crown. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: History of Iceland, 1840s to the Second World War; Þingvellir and the Independence Campaign)
- 1 Dec 1918: **The Act of Union and the Kingdom of Iceland** — The Danish-Icelandic Act of Union made Iceland a sovereign state in personal union with the Danish crown, with Denmark retaining foreign affairs and the union open to termination after twenty-five years. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: Act of Union (Iceland, 1918); History of Iceland, 1840s to the Second World War)
- May 1940: **British occupation of Iceland** — After Germany occupied Denmark, British forces occupied strategically vital Iceland in May 1940 to forestall a German move, severing Iceland's last constitutional ties to its monarch. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: History of Iceland from 1944; Iceland: History)
- 17 Jun 1944: **Founding of the Republic at Þingvellir** — After an overwhelming referendum, Icelanders proclaimed a republic at Þingvellir, ending the union with Denmark; Sveinn Björnsson became the first president and 17 June became the national day. (from the Iceland chronicle; sources: Iceland Becomes a Republic; History of Iceland from 1944; Sveinn Björnsson)
