## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1731: **The Little Horde accepts Russian protection** — Abu'l-Khayr Khan of the Little Horde swears allegiance to the Russian Empire for protection against the Dzungars; the Middle Horde follows in 1740 and part of the Great Horde in 1742, opening the steppe to Russian power. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: History of Kazakhstan; Kazakhstan)
- 1771: **Ablai Khan elected ruler of the Kazakhs** — Ablai (Abylai) is elected khan of the Kazakhs and balances between the Russian and Qing empires to preserve a degree of independence; he endures as a national symbol of Kazakh sovereignty. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: Abylai the King: A Warrior, Diplomat and Patriot; History of Kazakhstan)
- 1822–1848: **Russia abolishes the Kazakh khanates** — To end Kazakh autonomy, Russia abolishes the khanate of the Middle Horde in 1822, the Little Horde in 1824, and the Great Horde in 1848, dismantling the political structure of the Kazakh Khanate. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: History of Kazakhstan; Kazakhstan)
- 1837–1847: **Kenesary Kasymov's revolt** — Kenesary Kasymov, a grandson of Ablai Khan, leads the largest Kazakh uprising against Russian rule, fighting to restore the independent khanate until he is killed in 1847. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: History of Kazakhstan; Kazakstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan: Country Studies)
- 5 December 1936: **Kazakh S.S.R. becomes a union republic** — Kazakhstan, an autonomous republic since 1920 and renamed the Kazak A.S.S.R. in 1925, is raised to a full constituent republic of the Soviet Union under the 1936 constitution. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: History of Kazakhstan; Kazakstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan: Country Studies)
- 1949–1989: **Semipalatinsk nuclear test site** — The Soviet Union conducts some 456 nuclear tests at the Semipalatinsk site in northeastern Kazakhstan between 1949 and 1989, exposing local populations to radioactive fallout with severe long-term health effects. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: Kazakhstan; Kazakstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan: Country Studies)
- 16 December 1991: **Kazakhstan declares independence** — Kazakhstan proclaims itself a sovereign, independent state, the last of the Soviet republics to declare independence, with Nursultan Nazarbayev as president; the USSR formally dissolves days later. (from the Kazakhstan chronicle; sources: Kazakhstan: Independent Kazakhstan; History of Kazakhstan)
