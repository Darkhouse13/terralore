## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 14 September 1641: **Treaty of Péronne** — Honoré II and Louis XIII signed the Treaty of Péronne, shifting Monaco from Spanish to French protection while recognising the principality's independence and the prince's sovereignty. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: French protection (Treaty of Péronne, 1641); Monaco: 1215 to 1662; Monaco)
- from 1642: **French titles for the Grimaldi** — Under the Péronne settlement the prince of Monaco received French titles and estates, including the duchy of Valentinois, binding the dynasty to the French crown. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: French protection (Treaty of Péronne, 1641))
- 1793: **Revolutionary France annexes Monaco** — Revolutionary France abolished the Grimaldi sovereignty and annexed the principality, expelling the dynasty and scattering the palace's art collections. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: Secession of Menton and Roquebrune (Treaty of 1861); Monaco - Prince's Palace)
- 2 February 1861: **Cession of Menton and Roquebrune** — By the Franco-Monégasque Treaty, Prince Charles III ceded Menton and Roquebrune to France for four million francs; France recognised Monaco's sovereignty over its remaining territory. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: Secession of Menton and Roquebrune (Treaty of 1861); Monaco: 1815 to Present; Monaco)
- 17 July 1918: **Franco-Monégasque Treaty of 1918** — Signed amid a succession crisis, the treaty aligned Monaco's policy with French interests and provided that the principality would fall under French protection should the Grimaldi line end; it was noted in the 1919 Treaty of Versailles. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: The history of Monaco's accession; Monaco)
- 24 October 2002: **Treaty of 2002 guarantees independence** — A new Franco-Monégasque treaty, with the revised constitution and succession law, ended the clause returning Monaco to France if the Grimaldi line failed, guaranteeing the principality's continued independence. (from the Monaco chronicle; sources: Albert II, prince of Monaco; The history of Monaco's accession; Monaco: 1815 to Present)
