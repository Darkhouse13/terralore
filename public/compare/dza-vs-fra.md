## Entangled histories (9 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1827: **The "fly-whisk" incident** — A dispute over French debts ends with the dey of Algiers striking the French consul with a fly-whisk, a slight France later uses as a pretext for invasion. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: France Conquers Algeria; History of Algeria)
- 5 July 1830: **France captures Algiers** — A French expeditionary force takes Algiers, ending the Ottoman regency and beginning 132 years of French rule in Algeria. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: France Conquers Algeria; History of Algeria)
- 1832–1847: **Abd al-Qadir's resistance** — Proclaimed amir in 1832, Abd al-Qadir builds a state across much of Algeria and leads armed resistance to the French until his surrender in 1847. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: Abdelkader; France Conquers Algeria)
- 1848: **Algeria annexed to France** — Algeria is organised into French départements and declared an integral part of France, opening the way to large-scale European settlement and land expropriation. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: History of Algeria; French occupation of Algeria)
- 1 November 1954: **Toussaint Rouge: the war begins** — The FLN launches coordinated attacks across Algeria on 1 November 1954, opening the war of independence against French rule. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: Algerian War; History of Algeria)
- 1956–1957: **Battle of Algiers** — A fierce urban campaign in the capital pits FLN cells against French paratroopers; the army's use of torture turns world opinion against the war. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: Algerian War; The Algerian War of Independence)
- 1958: **The war topples the Fourth Republic** — The Algerian crisis brings down France's Fourth Republic and returns Charles de Gaulle to power, opening the way to eventual negotiations. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: History of Algeria)
- 18 March 1962: **Évian Accords** — France and the FLN sign the Évian Accords, agreeing a ceasefire and a self-determination referendum that will lead to Algerian independence. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: Algerian War; The Algerian War of Independence)
- 5 July 1962: **Independence proclaimed** — After a referendum on 1 July and French recognition on 3 July, Algeria proclaims its independence on 5 July 1962, ending 132 years of French rule. (from the Algeria chronicle; sources: A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: Algeria; Ahmed Ben Bella)
