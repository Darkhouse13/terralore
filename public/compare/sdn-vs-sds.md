## Entangled histories (9 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1821: **Turco-Egyptian conquest reaches Sudan** — Muhammad Ali's forces conquer the Funj lands and extend Egyptian rule up the Nile, seeking gold, ivory and slaves and making Khartoum the seat of administration. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: Turco-Egyptian conquest of Sudan (1820–1824); Khartoum, Sudan (1821– ))
- 1885: **Mahdist revolt and the fall of Khartoum** — The Mahdist uprising overruns Sudan and captures Khartoum, ending Turco-Egyptian rule and bringing further upheaval to the southern Nile peoples. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: Anglo-Egyptian Condominium)
- 1898: **Mahdist state crushed at Omdurman** — An Anglo-Egyptian army defeats the Mahdist forces at Omdurman, clearing the way for the reconquest of Sudan, including the south. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: Anglo-Egyptian Condominium)
- 1899: **Anglo-Egyptian Condominium established** — Britain and Egypt establish joint rule over Sudan, with the largely Christian and traditionalist south administered separately from the Arab-Muslim north. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: Anglo-Egyptian Condominium; History of South Sudan)
- 1947: **Juba Conference** — Northern and southern representatives meet at Juba to discuss the south's place in a future Sudan, but southern delegates have little leverage over the outcome. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: History of South Sudan)
- 1 Jan 1956: **Sudan's independence; the south sidelined** — Sudan becomes independent under a northern-dominated government, with southern demands for federalism largely unmet. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: History of South Sudan)
- 27 Feb 1972: **Addis Ababa Agreement** — The Addis Ababa Agreement ends the seventeen-year first war, granting the south a single autonomous region and integrating Anyanya fighters into Sudan's forces. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: Sudan: The Addis Ababa Agreement; Addis Ababa Agreement (1972))
- Jul 2005: **Death of John Garang** — Weeks after becoming Sudan's first vice-president, John Garang dies in a helicopter crash; Salva Kiir Mayardit succeeds him as SPLM leader and southern president. (from the South Sudan chronicle; sources: South Sudan: The 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement, Conflict, Independence; Ten Years Later: Taking Stock of Sudan's Comprehensive Peace Agreement)
- 9 July 2011: **South Sudan secedes** — After a referendum, South Sudan becomes independent, taking most of the former Sudan's oil with it. (from the Sudan chronicle; sources: Sudan; Independence of South Sudan)
