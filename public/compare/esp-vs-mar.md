## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1086: **Almoravids enter al-Andalus** — Yusuf ibn Tashfin leads the Almoravids into Spain, checking the Christian Reconquista and bringing Muslim al-Andalus under Moroccan rule. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: North Africa: The Maghrib under the Almoravids and the Almohads; The Art of the Almoravid and Almohad Periods (ca. 1062–1269))
- 1212: **Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa** — A Christian coalition decisively defeats the Almohads in Spain, beginning the dynasty's decline and reopening al-Andalus to the Reconquista. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: The Art of the Almoravid and Almohad Periods (ca. 1062–1269); North Africa: The Maghrib under the Almoravids and the Almohads)
- 30 March 1912: **Treaty of Fez establishes the protectorates** — Sultan Abd al-Hafid signs the Treaty of Fez, making Morocco a French protectorate; a Spanish zone in the north and south and an international zone at Tangier are settled the same year. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: Treaty of Fès (1912); A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: Morocco)
- 22 July 1921: **Battle of Annual and the Rif War** — Abd el-Krim's Riffian fighters destroy a Spanish army at Annual and proclaim a Republic of the Rif; a Franco-Spanish coalition suppresses the rebellion by 1926. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: Rif War)
- 2 March 1956: **Restoration of independence** — France ends its protectorate by agreement with Morocco; Spain relinquishes most of its northern zone in April, restoring Moroccan independence under Sultan Mohammed V. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: French Colony to Sovereign State: Moroccan Independence; A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition: Morocco)
- 6 November 1975: **The Green March** — King Hassan II leads the Green March, in which about 350,000 unarmed Moroccans enter the Western Sahara to assert Morocco's claim as Spain withdraws; the territory's status remains disputed. (from the Morocco chronicle; sources: Moroccans march into Western Sahara in the Green March, 1975; Dispute over the Western Sahara Erupts in the Green March)
