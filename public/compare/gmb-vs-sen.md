## Entangled histories (5 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- mid-14th c.: **Rise of the Jolof Empire** — A Wolof confederation unites provinces between the Senegal and Gambia rivers, drawing tribute and trade across the western savanna. (from the Senegal chronicle; sources: Wolof Empire; Jolof kingdom)
- 1444: **Portuguese reach the Senegambian coast** — Portuguese ships arrive off Senegal and the Gambia, opening Atlantic maritime trade — and the first European slave raids — along the coast. (from the Senegal chronicle; sources: The Roots of African Trade (World History Vol. 2); French conquest of Senegal)
- 1889–1890s: **Anglo-French agreements draw the enclave** — Boundary agreements between Britain and France fixed The Gambia's frontiers as a narrow band following the river, leaving the territory almost entirely surrounded by French Senegal. (from the Gambia chronicle; sources: History of the Gambia; The Gambia (country overview and history))
- 1 February 1982: **The Senegambia Confederation formed** — The Gambia and Senegal established the Senegambia Confederation to integrate their militaries, security and economies while retaining sovereignty, with Senegal's Abdou Diouf as president and Jawara as vice-president. (from the Gambia chronicle; sources: Senegambia Confederation; The Gambia (country overview and history))
- 30 September 1989: **The confederation dissolves** — Lacking popular support and strained by disputes over economic and customs integration, the Senegambia Confederation was formally dissolved after Senegal withdrew its troops, and the two states resumed separate paths. (from the Gambia chronicle; sources: Senegambia Confederation; The Gambia (country overview and history))
