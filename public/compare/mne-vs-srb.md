## Entangled histories (8 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1912–1913: **The Balkan Wars** — Montenegro joined Serbia, Bulgaria and Greece in the First Balkan War against the Ottoman Empire and besieged Scutari, but was forced by the Great Powers to give up the captured city. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Montenegro: History; Nicholas I, king of Montenegro)
- 1 Dec 1918: **Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes proclaimed** — Prince Regent Alexander proclaimed the South Slav kingdom in Belgrade, uniting Serbia and Montenegro with former Habsburg lands under the Serbian crown; it was renamed Yugoslavia in 1929. (from the Serbia chronicle; sources: Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes; Serbia in the Yugoslav kingdom)
- Nov 1918: **The Podgorica Assembly** — An assembly in Podgorica deposed King Nikola I and the Petrovic-Njegos dynasty and proclaimed the unconditional union of Montenegro with Serbia in the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Montenegro (First World War); Montenegro: History)
- 1992: **Federal Republic of Yugoslavia** — As the other republics broke away, Montenegro joined Serbia in forming the rump Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, remaining bound to Belgrade through the wars and sanctions of the 1990s. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Montenegro: History; Montenegro: federation, State Union and referendum)
- 2002: **Unilateral adoption of the euro** — Montenegro made the euro its sole legal tender from March 2002, a unilateral euroisation undertaken without European Central Bank agreement that distanced its economy from Serbia's. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Euroisation: Montenegro's adoption of the Deutsche Mark and then the euro (sole legal tender from March 2002))
- 2003: **State Union of Serbia and Montenegro** — European mediation transformed the federation into the loose State Union of Serbia and Montenegro, whose Constitutional Charter allowed either republic to hold an independence referendum after three years. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Consequences of the referendum in Montenegro; Montenegro: History)
- Jun 2006: **Montenegro's independence and the end of the State Union** — Following a referendum on 21 May 2006, Montenegro declared independence; Serbia accepted the result and declared itself the successor state, dissolving the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro. (from the Serbia chronicle; sources: Montenegro: Independence; A Guide to the United States' History of Recognition, Diplomatic, and Consular Relations, by Country: Serbia)
- 3 Jun 2006: **Restoration of independence** — The parliament in Podgorica formally declared Montenegro an independent sovereign state, recognised by Serbia, restoring the statehood lost at the Podgorica Assembly of 1918. (from the Montenegro chronicle; sources: Consequences of the referendum in Montenegro; Montenegro: Independence)
