## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 14th century: **Timbuktu and Djenné flourish** — Under Mali's mansas the Niger cities grow into major centres of trans-Saharan trade and Islamic scholarship, attracting merchants and scholars. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: Timbuktu — World Heritage Centre; The Mali Empire)
- 1468: **Sonni Ali takes Timbuktu** — Sonni Ali captures Timbuktu, and in 1473 Djenné, bringing the Niger trade cities under Songhai control and laying the foundation of the empire. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: The Songhai Empire (HIST103); Sonni Ali)
- April 1591: **Battle of Tondibi ends the Songhai Empire** — Moroccan forces under Judar Pasha, armed with firearms, rout the Songhai army at Tondibi near Gao, collapsing the last of the great Niger empires. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: Fall of the Songhai Empire; Moroccan invasion of the Songhai Empire)
- c. 1712: **Bitòn Coulibaly consolidates the Bambara kingdom of Segou** — Mamari (Bitòn) Coulibaly transforms a youth society into an army, founding the centralised Bambara state of Segou on the middle Niger. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: Bamana Empire; Bamana (Ségou) Empire)
- 1861: **Umar Tal's Tukulor empire conquers Segou** — Al-Hajj Umar Tal, leading the Tijaniyya jihad begun in the 1850s, overthrows the Bambara kingdom of Segou and extends the Tukulor empire across the upper Niger. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: ʿUmar Tal — West African Tukulor Leader & Jihadist; Bamana (Ségou) Empire)
- early 20th century: **Colony of French Soudan** — The conquered upper Niger is administered as French Soudan within French West Africa, its economy reoriented toward the colonial metropole. (from the Mali chronicle; sources: Modibo Keita — 1st President of Mali; Mali Federation)
