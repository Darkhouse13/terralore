## Entangled histories (11 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1772-1795: **Partitions of Poland and Russian annexation** — In three partitions Russia, Prussia and Austria divided the Commonwealth; Russia took most of the Belarusian lands, and after 1795 all of Belarus was ruled by the Russian Empire. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: History of Belarus; Belarus)
- 1839: **Suppression of the Uniate Church and Russification** — The Uniate (Greek Catholic) Church was abolished and absorbed into Russian Orthodoxy, part of a sustained imperial policy of Russification that discouraged the Belarusian language and name. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: History of Belarus)
- 1 Jan 1919: **Founding of the Byelorussian SSR** — As the Germans withdrew, the Bolsheviks announced the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic; in 1922 it became one of the four founding republics of the Soviet Union. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Belarus: The emergence of the Belorussian S.S.R.)
- 18 Mar 1921: **Treaty of Riga partitions Belarus** — The treaty ending the Polish-Soviet War divided Belarus between Poland and Soviet Russia along the line of the first partition; western Belarus went to Poland and the east became the Byelorussian SSR. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Belarus: The emergence of the Belorussian S.S.R.)
- 1937-1941: **Stalinist terror and Kurapaty** — Amid forced collectivisation, the NKVD carried out mass executions of Belarusians at Kurapaty near Minsk between 1937 and 1941; the mass graves, revealed in 1988, became the emblem of Soviet repression in Belarus. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Kurapaty (1937-1941): NKVD Mass Killings in Soviet Belarus)
- 17 Sep 1939: **Reunification of western Belarus** — Following the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact and the German invasion of Poland, Soviet forces entered eastern Poland and western Belarus was annexed to the Byelorussian SSR, reuniting the Belarusian lands. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Belarus: The emergence of the Belorussian S.S.R.; History of Belarus)
- 8 Dec 1991: **Belavezha Accords dissolve the USSR** — In the Belavezha forest in Belarus, part of the UNESCO-listed Belovezhskaya Pushcha, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus, including Stanislau Shushkevich, signed accords declaring that the USSR had ceased to exist and founding the Commonwealth of Independent States. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Belavezha Agreement (1991); Bialowieza Forest (Belovezhskaya Pushcha); Belarus: The emergence of the Belorussian S.S.R.)
- 25 Aug 1991: **Independence of Belarus** — After declaring sovereignty on 27 July 1990, the Supreme Soviet gave the declaration constitutional force on 25 August 1991 in the wake of the Moscow coup, proclaiming the independent Republic of Belarus. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Declaration of State Sovereignty of Belarus (1990); Belarus: The emergence of the Belorussian S.S.R.)
- 1994: **Lukashenka elected president** — Alyaksandr Lukashenka won Belarus's first presidential election on an anti-corruption platform and went on to consolidate authoritarian rule and forge ever-deeper ties with Russia. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Alexander Lukashenko)
- 8 Dec 1999: **Treaty on the Union State with Russia** — Lukashenka and Yeltsin signed a Treaty on the Creation of a Union State, a blueprint for deep Russian-Belarusian integration that has bound the two countries ever closer. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Treaty on the Creation of a Union State; Alexander Lukashenko)
- Aug-Sep 2020: **Disputed election and mass protests** — Lukashenka's claimed landslide re-election sparked the largest protests in Belarusian history; the EU declared the vote neither free nor fair and refused to recognise his mandate, while he held on with Russian support. (from the Belarus chronicle; sources: Belarus: Declaration by the High Representative on the so-called 'inauguration' of Lukashenko; Alexander Lukashenko)
