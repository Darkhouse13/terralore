## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1806: **Britain seizes the Cape** — British forces occupied the Cape Colony during the Napoleonic Wars, beginning lasting British rule confirmed by the Congress of Vienna in 1815. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: South Africa (country, history and government); History of slavery and early colonisation in South Africa)
- 1 December 1834: **Abolition of slavery at the Cape** — Slavery was abolished across the British Empire, freeing enslaved people at the Cape and deepening Boer discontent that helped spark the Great Trek. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: History of slavery and early colonisation in South Africa; South Africa (country, history and government))
- from 1836: **The Great Trek** — Thousands of Voortrekkers left the Cape Colony for the interior, seeking independence from British rule and founding new Boer communities on lands occupied by African peoples. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: Origins of the Battle of Blood River 1838; South Africa (country, history and government))
- 1899–1902: **The South African War** — War between Britain and the Boer republics featured guerrilla campaigns and British concentration camps in which tens of thousands of Boer and Black civilians died. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: The South African War 1899–1902; South African War; Treaty of Vereeniging)
- 31 May 1902: **Treaty of Vereeniging** — The peace signed at Pretoria ended the South African War, bringing the Transvaal and Orange Free State under British sovereignty with promises of eventual self-government. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: Treaty of Vereeniging; South African War)
- 31 May 1910: **Union of South Africa** — The four British colonies were united into the self-governing Union of South Africa under the South Africa Act, a white-controlled state that excluded the Black majority from political power. (from the South Africa chronicle; sources: The Union of South Africa 1910; South Africa Act; South Africa (country, history and government))
