## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 1815: **Congress of Vienna and the German Confederation** — After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna created a loose confederation of German states dominated by Austria and Prussia, leaving national unity unfulfilled. (from the Germany chronicle; sources: Germany)
- 12 Nov 1918: **Collapse of Austria-Hungary and the First Republic** — As the war was lost the empire disintegrated; Emperor Karl I renounced participation in government on 11 November and the Republic of German-Austria was proclaimed on 12 November 1918, ending Habsburg rule. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: The End of Monarchy, the Birth of New States)
- 1919: **South Tyrol ceded to Italy** — Under Saint-Germain the German-speaking southern part of historic Tyrol was awarded to Italy, placing a large German-speaking population under Italian rule and creating a lasting territorial grievance. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: 1919: Treaty of Saint-Germain; De Gasperi-Gruber Agreement and the First Statute of Autonomy)
- 10 Sep 1919: **Treaty of Saint-Germain** — Signed for Austria by Karl Renner, the treaty confirmed the breakup of Austria-Hungary, forbade union with Germany without League of Nations approval, limited Austria's army and ceded South Tyrol to Italy. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: 1919: Treaty of Saint-Germain)
- 12–13 Mar 1938: **The Anschluss** — German troops entered Austria on 12 March 1938 and on 13 March a law proclaimed reunification with the Reich; Austrian independence ended and a manipulated April plebiscite recorded near-total approval. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: German Annexation of Austria)
- 5 Sep 1946: **Gruber-De Gasperi Agreement on South Tyrol** — Austrian foreign minister Karl Gruber and Italian premier Alcide De Gasperi signed an accord committing Italy to grant autonomy and language and education rights to South Tyrol's German-speaking population. (from the Austria chronicle; sources: De Gasperi-Gruber Agreement and the First Statute of Autonomy)
