## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 20 Mar 1602: **Founding of the Dutch East India Company (VOC)** — The Dutch Republic charters the VOC with sweeping powers to trade, wage war and govern, launching nearly two centuries of company rule in the Indies. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: Dutch East India Company)
- 31 Dec 1799: **Dissolution of the VOC** — Bankrupt after decades of war, debt and corruption, the VOC is dissolved and its possessions pass to the Dutch state, ushering in direct colonial rule. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: Dutch East India Company)
- 1830: **The Cultivation System** — The Dutch impose the Cultuurstelsel, forcing Javanese peasants to grow export crops for the colonial state; it enriches the Netherlands while causing widespread hardship and local famines. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: History of Indonesia (Islamic influence; Dutch rule); Indonesian National Awakening)
- 1942: **Japanese occupation of the Indies** — Japan conquers the Dutch East Indies, ending Dutch rule; its occupation brings forced labour and hardship but also mobilises and arms Indonesian nationalists. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: Indonesia (toward independence; Japanese occupation; after Suharto); Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies)
- 1945–1949: **The Indonesian National Revolution** — Indonesians wage a four-year armed and diplomatic struggle against Dutch attempts to reimpose colonial rule, enduring two major Dutch offensives and heavy losses. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: Indonesia Regains Its Independence (Research Starters))
- 27 Dec 1949: **Indonesian independence** — After a violent four-year war, the Netherlands transfers sovereignty over the Dutch East Indies to the Republic of Indonesia. (from the Netherlands chronicle; sources: Netherlands; Independence, Decolonization, Violence and War in Indonesia, 1945–1950)
- 27 Dec 1949: **Recognition of sovereignty** — Following the Round Table Conference in The Hague, the Netherlands formally transfers sovereignty and recognises the independence of Indonesia. (from the Indonesia chronicle; sources: Indonesia Regains Its Independence (Research Starters); Proclamation of Indonesian Independence)
