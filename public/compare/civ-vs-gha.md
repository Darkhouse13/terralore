## Entangled histories (3 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 11th–14th centuries: **The savannas join the trans-Saharan trade** — As the Ghana and Mali empires expanded southward, the northern peoples of the region — Senufo and Mandé — were drawn into trans-Saharan commerce, exchanging forest kola nuts for salt, cloth, and gold. (from the Ivory Coast chronicle; sources: History of Côte d'Ivoire; History of Ivory Coast; Background Notes: Cote d'Ivoire)
- c. 1690: **Akan states form on the eastern frontier** — As the Asante Empire rose in present-day Ghana, Akan groups such as the Abron founded states near the eastern edge of modern Côte d'Ivoire, beginning a long era of Akan migration into the region. (from the Ivory Coast chronicle; sources: History of Ivory Coast; History of Côte d'Ivoire)
- c. 1710: **Seku Watara founds the Empire of Kong** — Seku Watara united the Dyula of Kong into a state, often called the Wattara Empire, that grew rich controlling the gold and kola trade and reached into present-day Burkina Faso, Mali, and Ghana. (from the Ivory Coast chronicle; sources: Kong Empire; History of Ivory Coast)
