## Entangled histories (7 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- July 1915: **South Africa conquers the colony** — South African forces invaded German South West Africa during the First World War and forced the German garrison to surrender, beginning seventy-five years of South African rule. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Namibia (country, history and government); History of Namibia)
- 1920: **League of Nations mandate** — The League of Nations granted South Africa a mandate to administer the territory as a trust for its inhabitants; South Africa instead governed it as a near-province, settling white farmers and suppressing African political life. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Namibia (country, history and government); Namibia)
- 1946: **South Africa refuses UN trusteeship** — After the founding of the United Nations, South Africa refused to place the territory under UN trusteeship and sought to annex it, beginning a long international dispute over its status. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Namibia (country, history and government); Namibia)
- 26 August 1966: **Armed struggle begins at Omugulugwombashe** — SWAPO's military wing, PLAN, fought South African forces at Omugulugwombashe, marking the start of the armed liberation war and of South Africa's long Border War. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: South West African People's Organisation (SWAPO); History of Namibia; South African Border War)
- 27 October 1966: **UN revokes the mandate** — The UN General Assembly terminated South Africa's mandate over the territory, declaring its continued administration illegal, and later renamed the territory Namibia in 1968. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Namibia (country, history and government); Namibia)
- 21 June 1971: **ICJ rules the occupation illegal** — The International Court of Justice held that South Africa's continued presence in Namibia was illegal and that it was obliged to withdraw its administration immediately. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Legal Consequences for States of the Continued Presence of South Africa in Namibia (1971 Advisory Opinion); Namibia)
- 1 March 1994: **Reincorporation of Walvis Bay** — South Africa transferred the port enclave of Walvis Bay and the offshore islands to Namibia, completing the country's territory and giving it sovereignty over its only deep-water harbour. (from the Namibia chronicle; sources: Namibia; Namibia (country, history and government))
