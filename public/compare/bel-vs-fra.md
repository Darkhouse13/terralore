## Entangled histories (6 events)

Events in which either nation's sourced chronicle names the other — including under earlier names of the same state. Each is the record exactly as its own chronicle carries it, with that chronicle's sources.

- 11 Jul 1302: **Battle of the Golden Spurs** — A Flemish town militia, mostly craft guildsmen, destroyed a French army of knights near Courtrai, collecting the golden spurs of the slain nobles and asserting the independence of the Flemish towns. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: Battle of the Golden Spurs; Belgium: History)
- 1795: **French annexation of the southern Netherlands** — Revolutionary France annexed the Austrian Netherlands and the prince-bishopric of Liège, ending Habsburg rule and governing the region directly from Paris for twenty years. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: Belgium: History)
- 1815: **Union with the Netherlands** — After Napoleon's defeat at Waterloo on Belgian soil, the Congress of Vienna merged the southern provinces with the Dutch into the United Kingdom of the Netherlands under William I, as a buffer against France. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: Belgium: History; The Treaty of London, 1839 (primary text))
- 19 Apr 1839: **Treaty of London guarantees Belgian neutrality** — Austria, Britain, France, Prussia, Russia and the Netherlands recognised Belgium as an independent and perpetually neutral state and guaranteed its neutrality, the treaty later cited by Britain to enter the First World War. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: The Treaty of London, 1839 (primary text); Belgium: History)
- 10 May 1940: **Second German invasion** — Germany again invaded neutral Belgium on 10 May 1940, this time thrusting through the Ardennes, and rapidly overwhelmed the defending Belgian, French and British forces. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: German Invasion of Western Europe, May 1940; Belgium: History)
- 1962–1963: **The linguistic frontier fixed** — Language laws drew a fixed border between Dutch-, French- and German-speaking areas and created four language regions, entrenching the divide between Belgium's communities. (from the Belgium chronicle; sources: Belgium: History)
